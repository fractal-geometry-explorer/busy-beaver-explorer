Mark Rule Stepper

Give a starting tape as a list of marks, then step the proven round rules of 1RB0RE_1LC0RA_1LA1LD_1LA---_0RF0LB_0LD1RE and watch where the marks go. One step is one round: the gap leaves cell 1, works its way right, a packet comes back, and the tape shifts. This runs the mark rules, not the bare transition table.

Starting tape

A width line, then one mark per entry: a letter and its cell, like M100. G gap, M marker, W wall, B block. A round starts with the gap at cell 1, and the end marker usually sits at width − 2.

What happens in a round
  • The gap starts at cell 1 and meets the next mark to its right.
  • Marker at distance d (cells strictly between them, minus 3). If d is a multiple of 4: swap, the marker moves to g + d/2 − 1 and the gap to g + d/2 + 1, and the gap carries on. Otherwise: merge, the marker becomes a wall at g + d/2 + 3 and a packet goes left.
  • Wall: gap and wall vanish and a shift runs right to the next mark. A marker there becomes the gap; a wall becomes a block; a block becomes a wall 2 cells further on; blank tape gets a new end marker (width + 2). Except for the marker case, a packet then goes left.
  • Block: it turns into a wall one cell to its left while the gap sleeps, and a shift runs right as above.
  • Nothing to the right: a new end marker appears (width + 3) and a packet goes left.
  • Packet going left skips markers. At a gap it wakes it 2 cells on; at a wall it leaves a marker 1 cell left and a new gap 1 cell right; at the left end the round is over; at a block the machine halts.
  • Then every mark moves 2 cells right (4 if a marker sat at cell 0, which becomes a wall) and a new gap appears at cell 1.
  • The proven rules need stripes between marks. Before every move the stepper checks that the cells it is about to use match a proven rule, and after every round that the tape is a real tape. When they don't, it stops and says why:
    • Halts: a packet reached a block.
    • At the end: the gap came within 2 cells of the end marker, where the real machine can halt.
    • Not covered: marks touch in a way no proven rule handles, such as a packet reaching a marker right after a wall, a wall or gap right before a gap, or a round ending on a tape whose stripes don't fit.
    • Rule not built in: a packet reaching a marker right after a gap. A proven rule covers this, but the mark rules here have no step for it.
    • Can't continue: two marks would overlap, or a gap meets a gap.
    • Real machine differs: on tapes up to 4,096 cells each round is also run on the bare transition table, and its marks are compared.
