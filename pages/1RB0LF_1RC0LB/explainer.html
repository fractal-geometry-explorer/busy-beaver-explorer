Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LF_1RC0LB_1RD1RB_1RE1RF_1LA---_0RC1LA

Nobody knows. Started on a blank tape, this Turing machine has run for ten billion steps without stopping, and in that time it has visited only 642 cells. This page runs the real machine in your browser and lists what is known, each result marked by what it rests on. It grows as the work does.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in E reading 1. E is entered only from D0 (writes 1, moves R): D>0 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

Macro structure

Passes, and the one way to halt

Watch the simulator for a while and the head settles into a rhythm. Moving right, states C and B fill a sparse stretch of tape, single 1s with short runs of 0s between them, into solid 1s: C>1 0 1 0 becomes 1 1 1 1 C>. Moving left, A and F turn a solid block back into alternating cells, 1 1 1 1 A>1 into A>1 1 0 1 0, and B erases blocks. Left of the head the tape is solid, right of it sparse.

Call a run of 0s between two 1s a gap. A rightward run, a pass, starts when F or B reads the first cell of a gap and moves right. C crosses every gap of length 1 or 2, and the first gap of another length ends the pass:

  • a gap of 3: C and D fill two cells and E reads the 1 that ends the gap, which halts;
  • a gap of 4 or more: C, D and E fill three cells and E turns the head left;
  • two 1s side by side: B or F turns left.

This is proved in Lean, checked by the kernel, for any number of crossed gaps (pass_outcome in lean/Pass.lean). So the machine halts exactly when some pass meets a gap of 3 before any other gap longer than 2.

It never has. In ten billion steps every run of 0s or of 1s of length 3 or more, between cells of the other symbol, has had even length at every pass start. A pass that ends at a gap of 4 or more uses up 4 of its cells, which keeps the parity; B erases an even block and a few cells beside it, and the pass that follows refills four of them, which again leaves an even run. If that parity can be proved to last forever, the machine never halts. That proof is the open step.

Most passes end at a gap of 6, half as many at 10, a quarter at 14, and so on: gaps of 4j + 2, each about half as common as the last. That is how the digits of a binary counter behave, and the tape grows about as slowly as a counter does: 199 cells after a million steps, 642 after ten billion.

Macro structure

From pass to pass: a machine on lists of numbers

Stop the simulator whenever a pass is about to start and the tape always looks the same way. Left of the head is a stack of solid blocks of even length, 1^(2s+2), separated by single 0s. The head stands on a gap of 2h + 2 cells. Right of it is a word: segments (0 1)^m separated by gaps of 2n + 2 cells, where n = −1 means two 1s side by side.

The states alternate. A pass from B fills four cells of its gap and sweeps back over the top block of the stack, which becomes a segment in front of a gap four cells shorter. A pass from F runs right over every gap with n = 0, pushing each segment it crosses onto the stack as a block, and stops at the first other gap. If that gap has n ≥ 1 it shrinks by 2, and the last block pushed is erased again and becomes B's next gap. If it has n = −1, the segment after it loses one 0 1.

Lean proves these three rules for every value of the parameters (mstep_sound in lean/Macro.lean), and the C simulator checks them against all 344,806,676 pass starts of the first ten billion steps. The rules fail only when two 1s side by side touch an empty segment; then a gap of odd length appears, and only a gap of odd length can turn into the fatal gap of 3. So the machine halts only if this macro machine gets stuck (no_halt_of_macro).

If every number is even, nothing can go wrong: each rule turns even numbers into even numbers, and a gap counting down by 2 from an even value reaches 0 and is crossed, never −1. Lean proves this for all values (even_run in lean/Parity.lean), and also that odd numbers are born only at the left end of the tape, one each time the stack runs empty, and otherwise disappear in pairs or at the right end (oddc_le). In the run there have never been more than four of them at once, and only 546 of 344,806,676 passes have met two 1s side by side. Proving that the odd numbers never line up badly is the open step.

The ruler-like tally of pass endings is visible here too: B's head gap is h = m + 2 for the last segment m the previous pass crossed, and h = 2, 4, 6, 8 occur with frequencies that halve exactly. The first nonzero gap is counted down by 2 per round, like the lowest digit of a counter.

Open question

Safe by design, or lucky so far?

Write the macro machine as two stacks and its right stack becomes a call stack: each entry is a frame, a loop with a few turns left that pushes its value when it is done. Odd numbers are the only danger. When a frame's own countdown is odd it ends one step early, and that step takes 1 from the frame that called it, a flip. A frame created with value 2 can take two flips; the third gets the machine stuck, and that is the only way the machine can ever halt.

In a hundred billion steps of this two-stack form the run has made 1,181 flips and has twice left a frame at 0, both times on a frame with no turns left, so nothing happened. To test whether something deeper protects it, take snapshots of the run, change one to three even numbers by 2 (every parity stays the same), and let each copy run. About one copy in seventy-five gets stuck within 108 to 109 steps, always in the same way: a value-2 frame near the bottom of the call stack is flipped twice and then hit once more.

Measured per flip, the run is indistinguishable from these copies. The same share of flips land on value-2 frames, second flips are as common, and the copies' stuck rate predicts a 17 % chance that the run would already have got stuck. The copies also stay risky for as long as they run, and the run keeps making about 200 flips per decade of steps. So there is no sign yet of the exact-value invariant a proof of non-halting would need. If the run behaves like its copies, it gets stuck with chance 2 to 3 % per decade: a halt that is likely but astronomically far away, perhaps around 1035 steps. That is a statistical model, not a proof, and a steady decline in the risk over time, which the copies hint at but do not show clearly, would change it.

Kernel
pass, pass_outcome, start_F, start_B, lean/Pass.lean
Which passes halt

A pass starts when F or B reads a 0 and moves right; C then crosses every gap of length 1 or 2 (by induction over any list of them). The first gap of another length ends the pass: a gap of 3 halts, a gap of 4 or more turns left at E, two 1s side by side turn left at B or F. So a pass halts exactly when the first gap it meets that is not 1 or 2 has length 3.

Kernel
sweep, sweep_end_odd, sweep_end_even, erase, erase_end, lean/Pass.lean
The leftward pieces

A and F turn 1^(2k) A>1 into A>1 (1 0)^k; B erases 1^k; at the 0 left of a block an odd block starts a pass and an even block starts B erasing the block before it.

Kernel
mstep_sound, blank_to_M0, no_halt_of_macro, lean/Macro.lean
The macro machine

At every pass start the tape is a stack of solid blocks 1^(2s+2) separated by single 0s, the head on a gap of 2h+2 cells, and a word of segments (0 1)^m and gaps 0^(2g) 1. A function mstep on these numbers gives the next pass start, and Lean proves the machine goes there, for all values of the parameters. mstep fails only at a gap g = 0 (two 1s side by side) next to an empty segment. So if the macro machine never gets stuck from the configuration after 12 steps, the machine never halts.

Kernel
phi_interior, phi_end, phi_bottom, lean/Counter.lean
A conserved counter

Read as a recursive program (the right stack of the two-stack form is a call stack), the macro machine keeps a sum Φ: the stack, plus the head gap, plus m + g − 1 for every segment m and gap g of the word. A pass that ends inside the word and a B step with a nonempty stack leave Φ unchanged; a pass that runs into the last segment adds 2 or 1; B with an empty stack takes 1 off. So Φ counts the passes that reach the right end, up to the rare steps at the left end. It does not by itself rule out getting stuck.

Kernel
even_not_stuck, even_step, even_run, oddc_le, oddc_run, lean/Parity.lean
Odd numbers are the only danger, and come from the left end

If every stack entry, segment and gap n = g − 1 is even (the last segment may be anything), the macro machine is not stuck and stays all-even until B has an empty stack, for all values. The number of odd numbers goes up only when B has an empty stack, by exactly 1; otherwise a step keeps it, takes 2 off (two odd numbers cancel), or loses 1 at the last segment. So after t steps there are at most 1 + (visits to the left end) odd numbers; in the first 3·106 macro steps there are 38 such visits and never more than 3 odd numbers at once.

c/fastrun.c, c/twostack.c, py/accel.py, tests/test_accel.py, tests/test_macro.py
The two-stack form does not get stuck in 5·1012 steps

The macro machine written as two stacks (each step one F pass and one B pass) does not get stuck in its first 5.1·1012 steps (of the order of 1014 to 1015 machine steps), run by a plain C simulator at about 4 s per 109 steps; a second C program that follows every frame by identity agrees up to 2·1011. Two flips leave a frame at value 0 (steps 204,719,421 and 2,084,922,784), both on frames with no turns left, and none happen after that; there are 1,923 R2 steps by 5.1·1012. The fast simulator equals the Python rules step by step for 20,000 steps and an exact memoized Python simulator after 2.5·108 steps; the frame-tracking one prints the same rows as the Python rules every 1000 steps for 3·105 steps; those rules are tested against mstep.

Computed
c/pds.c, py/pds.py, tests/test_pds.py, notes/LAB_NOTES.md
No small DFA closes the tape

One side of the tape abstracted by a DFA, the other kept exactly as a pushdown stack, reachability computed exactly by post* saturation: every DFA with at most 7 states, on either side, lets the abstraction reach a halt. Run-window languages on the tape do not close either. Tested sound on random small machines.

Heuristic
py/blank.py records, notes/LAB_NOTES.md
The tape grows very slowly

The visited width is 199 cells after 106 steps and 642 after 1010, so the time per new cell grows fast, far faster than for the other machines studied here. The left part of the tape holds blocks of 1s of even length separated by single 0s; the head reaches new cells on both sides.

Heuristic
py/macro.py stats, notes/LAB_NOTES.md
Pass endings look like a binary counter

Almost every pass ends at a gap of length 4j + 2, and each j is about half as common as j − 1. In macro terms half of all passes are B passes, which end at their own head gap h = m + 2, where m is the last segment the F pass before crossed; h = 2, 4, 6, 8, … occur with frequencies halving exactly. In the macro machine the first nonzero gap is counted down by 2 per round and a segment just before it becomes a gap of its own size: a counter whose digits are the gaps. Not a proved description of the run.

Heuristic
c/macro.c, notes/LAB_NOTES.md
Only a few odd numbers

Odd numbers are born only at the left end of the tape (proved, above), and the left end is rare: 38 visits in the first 3·106 macro steps. In the run there are at most 4 odd numbers at a time, and F meets two 1s side by side only 546 times in 1010 steps (of 344,806,676 passes).

Heuristic
py/perturb.py, c/twostack.c perturb, notes/LAB_NOTES.md
Stuck configurations next to the run

Changing one to three even numbers of a configuration of the run by 2, with every parity kept, gets the macro machine stuck: 6 of 1,980 tries in Python, 20 of 1,500 in C with longer runs (108 to 109 steps). One example changes a single entry deep in the stack from 0 to 2. So whatever keeps the run from getting stuck depends on exact values, not on parities. Every stuck copy examined ends the same way: a frame created with value 2, a few frames above the end, flipped twice and then hit once more: a third flip, or its own odd countdown running out at value 0.

Heuristic
c/twostack.c, notes/LAB_NOTES.md
The run is no safer than its perturbed copies

Per flip (an R2 step taking 1 from its caller) the run and its perturbed copies agree: 61–63 % of flips hit frames created with value 2, about 1 % are second flips, and the run's near-miss rate (2 in 1,181 flips by 1011 steps) is close to theirs (0.29 % over 124,075 flips). The copies get stuck about once per 6,200 flips (20 of 1,500 copies), so the run would have got stuck by 1011 steps with probability about 0.17. The copies do not relax back to safety (their rate stays flat for 108–109 steps after the change), their risk falls at most slowly with the run's age, and the run's flips do not thin out (about 200 per decade). If the run behaves like its copies, it gets stuck with chance 2 to 3 % per decade of steps: a halt is likely but astronomically late. This is a model, not a proof.

Open
notes/LAB_NOTES.md
The macro machine never gets stuck

A description of where the odd numbers can be that the rules preserve and that excludes the two stuck cases. That would prove the machine never halts. An odd number has over a thousand distinct small neighbourhoods in the run, so the description is not tiny; window and pushdown languages over the macro numbers are the next tries. Any such description has to separate the run from configurations one even number away, which per flip behave like it and do get stuck.
