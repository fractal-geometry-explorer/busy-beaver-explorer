What is known about 1RB1RA_1RC1LB, and what is open

1RB1RA_1RC1LB_1LD0RA_---0LE_1LC0LF_1RE0LB

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape. This page tracks what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

The work has just started: the shared tools are in place and the run has been looked at. Nearly everything about its structure is still observation.

Status as of 29 October 2026.

In short

The question is open. Lean proves that the machine does not halt in its first 10,000,000 steps (the first 20,000 checked by Lean's kernel alone), and two shift rules that hold for every block length. A direct simulation finds no halt in the first 1010 steps.

The only halting transition is D reading 0, and D is entered only from C reading 0, so a halt needs C to meet two 0s in a row. The run keeps its tape as blocks of ones separated by single 0s during its rightward sweeps (and by 00 when the head is back at the left end): finding the rules that act on these blocks is the next step.

kernel Proved in Lean, kernel-checked

No halt in 20,000 steps

From blank tape the machine makes 20,000 steps without halting. Evaluated by Lean's kernel on a list simulator proved to agree with the machine's semantics.

blank_runs_20000, lean/Blank.lean

Two shift rules

State A runs right over a block of n ones in n steps; state B runs left over a block of n ones to the 0 before it in n + 1 steps; for every n and any surrounding tape.

a_sweep, b_sweep, lean/Rules.lean (generated by lean/rules.py)

No halt in 1010 steps

A direct simulation in C runs 1010 steps without a halt (137,072 ones on the tape then). The simulator is tested against the Python reference simulator and on machines with known step counts.

No simple conserved quantity
