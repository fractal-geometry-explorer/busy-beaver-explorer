A six-state Busy Beaver holdout · status as of 1 November 2026

Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1RA_1LC1LD_1LD0LB_1RE0RF_---1RD_1RA1RB

Nobody knows. Started on a blank tape, this Turing machine has run for more than a trillion steps without stopping. This page shows how its run is organised, what has been proved about it in Lean, what has only been computed, and exactly where the two gaps are that stand between the current results and an answer.

1 · The machine

Six states, two symbols, one halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The only missing entry is E reading 0, so the machine halts exactly when it is in E on a 0. E is entered only from D reading a 0 (write 1, move right), so a halt is the moment D reads a 0 with another 0 to its right.

A sweeps right over ones and turns at the first 0. B and C carry the head left. That asymmetry is the shape of everything below: long sweeps to the right edge, and work done on the way back.

Kernel The machine stops exactly in E on a 0, and D>0 0 halts on the next step whatever the rest of the tape.

Kernel A closed tape language with windows of three cells (245 abstract configurations, checked by decide +kernel) covers every configuration of the run from blank tape. Past the head, two 0s in a row occur only in the blank region, so the machine halts if and only if E reaches the blank region at the right edge. D at the left end always has a 1 to its right, but that is forced by the step that brings it there.

Kernel The closed form above is what the machine does for every block length and every tape further left. When the last block is a multiple of 3, the pass is followed by a machine on lists of numbers (astep, 25 kinds of step, each proved sound). Together they give pass, one pass for every last block: whatever it returns, next pass start or halt, the machine does. That pass always finishes is not proved.

5 · The recursive pass

A last block 3j becomes 2j + c

When the last block is a multiple of 3 the converter does not stop at its left end: it runs on into the blocks further left, and on the way back it handles a stream of short words 0 1^k. The pass then ends in a regular loop over the words 0 1 1: every two words add 4 to one block and 2 to its neighbour, and each word left over adds 2 to the last block.

The irregular part before that loop never reads past the words in front of it, so it is the same for every j. Lean runs it once (pre), taking words 0 1 1 from an unbounded supply as needed. It takes u of them and stops with p still ahead and a block a under the head. Then for every j ≥ u the next last block is 2j + c, with c = a + 2p − 2u depending only on the blocks to the left.

Kernel The final loop, pre once for all j, and the formula 2j + c (recur_sound). For x = 2, in closed form for every list to the left: c = −1, so …, 2, 3 becomes a last block 1 and halts, and a last block of 6 or more after a 2 keeps a good start good.

tail_end, pre_sound, recur_sound, recur_two, recur_two_three_halts, recur_two_good · lean/Formula.lean

Kernel, if pre stops For every list of blocks ≥ 2, of any length: c ≥ −1, and c ≥ 0 unless x = 2; u ≤ 2; the blocks left behind are ≥ 2. The proof is an abstract interpretation of the block machine (numbers up to 3 exact, larger ones by residue mod 3) whose 4,463 reachable descriptions the kernel checks in 127 pieces. So for j ≥ 2, a good list with last block 3j passes to blocks ≥ 2 with last block ≥ 2j − 1.

offset_bound, recur_next, check_ok · lean/Offset.lean, lean/OffsetData.lean

Kernel pre never gets stuck on a good list, and the cells right of the head, read from the right end, go down in dictionary order at every step. So if the run keeps at most N cells, pre stops within 4·3N steps. The bound N itself is the first open gap (section 9).

6 · Good and safe starts

Where a halt could still come from

A pass start is good if every block is at least 2 and the last block is neither 1 nor 4. Covered passes keep good starts good (Kernel next_good), so trouble can only come from recursive passes. But good is not enough: 2 2 3 and 3 6 are good and halt.

A pass start is safe if it is good, has at least two blocks, and has a last block 3 only after a block of at least 6. The run reaches the safe start 10 3 (blank_safe). Then, assuming pre stops, every route out of safety is accounted for:

Kernel, if pre stops From a safe start with last block other than 3 and 6, the next start is safe, its last block is at least two thirds of the old one less 5/3, and it is 6 only if the old one was 9, or 2 after a block 3. So case B is entered only at the end of a descent through 9, falling by at most a factor 3/2 a pass.

Every halting start found ends this way (all 63 safe starts x b with x even below 400 and b below 300 that halt within 12 passes go through case B, Computed). To get to 9 the last block must come down a staircase: each covered pass from a block 1 mod 3 takes b + 5 to ⅔(b + 5), and k such steps in a row need 3k to divide b + 5. 314 54 shows a full descent: 54 → 36 → 24 → 16 → 9 → 6 → 4 → 1.

So the question of halting has become a question about digits: does the run ever produce a small last block whose base-3 digits line up for a long enough staircase? That is the Collatz-like core of this machine.

9 · Inside a recursive pass

The carry, and how the tape grows at the left end

Watch pre only at the moments A stands with no ones under the head. Between two such moments it eats exactly the first two words. Call a word other than 2 irregular. Only the pair 1 2 writes new words: the converter carries left over the blocks that are 1 mod 3, turning each block 3jᵢ + 1 into a word 1 followed by jᵢ words 2, and stops at the first block that is not 1 mod 3.

Left-end growth

The tape grows at the left only when the carry runs off the left end, after every block between the head and the end has become words. Each such growth step can leave the tape a cell longer, and they can come several in a row. If they are finitely many, the cells stay bounded between them and pre_stops applies, so bounding them is the whole of the first gap.

Kernel The two-block cycle shrinks. A growth through row_two leaves blocks 3, 6; if 2t words 2 and then 1 2 follow with t = 3s + 1, the loop makes the blocks 4t+3, 2t+6, the carry grows the tape again, and the state returns to 3, 6 with t replaced by s = (t − 1)/3. So that cycle ends within log₃ t + 1 rounds.

cycle36 · lean/Tokens.lean

Computed The number of growth steps in a pass is at most the starting token count plus 2 (20,000 random lists and three hill climbs). The head's drift past the left end grows with the number of blocks: a hill climb finds 6 cells with 4 blocks, 9 with 8, 11 with 12, 13 with 24. On the run, the 582 recursive passes to 1010 steps drift at most 6 cells.

10 · The open gaps

What stands between these results and an answer

Gap 1 · Bounded left-end growth

Show that inside a recursive pass the head does not drift left without bound, equivalently that there are finitely many growth steps. With it, every recursive pass finishes, pre always stops, and every result marked "if pre stops" becomes unconditional: c ≥ −1 for every list, the safe-start reduction, and safe_pass_shrink. The computed bound "growth steps ≤ starting tokens + 2" is the candidate, and cycle36 is the first piece of it.

Gap 2 · The Collatz-like descent

Case B: does the run ever bring the last block down to 9 and then to 6 with offset 0? No condition on a bounded number of blocks decides it (16 16, 314 54), sizes alone cannot (no_total_bound), and residues mod 54 cannot. On the run the chance of such a descent falls fast enough that its sum converges, which is evidence, not proof.

Case A (a last block 3 after a block ≥ 6 followed by an unsafe start) is also open in Lean, but never happens in 55,700 lists tried; a proof would need the abstraction to keep x ≥ 6 and separate c = 0 from c ≥ 3.

Open Whether the machine halts from blank tape. If gap 1 is closed and case A is proved, the run halts if and only if it reaches case B.
