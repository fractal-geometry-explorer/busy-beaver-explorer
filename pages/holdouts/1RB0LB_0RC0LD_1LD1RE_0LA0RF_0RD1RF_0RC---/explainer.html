A 6-state Busy Beaver machine · Status as of 8 November 2026.

Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_0RD1RF_0RC---

Nobody knows. Started on a blank tape, this machine builds two binary counters that tick together, one on each side of an engine. The left one runs with clockwork regularity; the right one has stretches of junk between its digits, and that is where the open question lives. It would halt only if a carry ever found a 1 where it has always found a 0.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from D1 (writes 0, moves R): D>1 1; E1 (writes 1, moves R): E>1 1. So a halt is the moment one of these patterns appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The machine

Two counters and an engine

Most of the time the tape is L · engine · R. Each visit of the head to the engine is an event, and every event adds 1 to both counters. In the reference machine of the family (1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_0RD1RF_0RC---), L is written in blocks of four cells, 0101 for a 1 and 0001 for a 0, lowest digit next to the engine, and R keeps one cell per digit, three cells apart (the cells 9, 12, 15, … right of the head), lowest digit nearest the engine. The rhythm

The left side is clockwork

When L overflows, the carry runs to the left end of the tape, the tape grows by two cells there, and L starts again. The lowest digit of L is the cell where the head turns most often, and the number of its turns between two consecutive left-end extensions follows a strict pattern, without a single exception in the first 1.5·109 steps (ymax.c):

64 64 128 128 256 256 512 512 … 223 223 224

So the periods of the left side are powers of two in events, each length twice (the counter gains one digit every two periods). The time per event is not constant, because a carry on the right costs more when it crosses junk, so the steps between left-end extensions grow by factors between about 1.35 and 2.05 instead of exactly 2.

The right side

A counter with junk between its digits

The cells that change between events are the digits of the counters: each digit changes about half as often as the one before (chg.c counts, over a whole period, how often each cell changes from one event to the next). On the right, between some digits of R, there are stretches of fixed cells (0101010101 00 1 00 1 00 1 and the like) that the carries cross without changing. They are written when a carry runs into the end of the tape, at irregular event counts, and their layout is what makes the run irregular. How they are produced is not decoded yet.

Where it could stop

The dangerous moments

The machine halts only in state F reading a 1, and F is entered from D1 (0RF) and E1 (1RF). After D1, used about 4·107 times in 109 steps, the head has read a 0 every time: part of the counters' ordinary carry. E1 is the rare one: 306 uses in the first 109 steps; in the reference machine E1 fires when a carry of the right counter reaches a digit for the first time since the last left-end visit: right after the visit at step 2,188,604 it fires at steps 2,188,675, 2,188,767, 2,188,882, 2,189,117, 2,189,592, 2,190,547, 2,192,462, three cells further right each time and after twice the wait each time, as a binary counter reaching new digits. So one way to halt is a carry meeting a fresh digit followed by a 1, the other an ordinary carry finding a 1 after D1. None of this has happened; whether the junk can ever arrange it is the open question.

Proved

The increments, proved for every size

Every visit of the head to the engine (A on cell 3) adds 1 to one of the two counters, and the theorems in lean/Events.lean prove each kind of visit for every size: the left increment with a carry over any number of ones, the right increment with a carry over any number of ones, and carries across any number of junk units 1010000. They are the reference machine's theorems, unchanged: they never use E1, the only transition where this machine differs. In the first 108 steps 95.2% of the 1,048,574 events are instances of them.

Next

What would settle it

Decode what a carry writes when it reaches the end of the tape, as a function of the counters, so that the layout of the junk after each visit is a formula. Then the question becomes whether a carry can ever find a 1 where it has always found a 0, which may be provable by an invariant of the junk, or may reduce to an arithmetic question about the two counters.

Related machines

Other holdouts of the list of 608 that are tied to this one. The comparison of tables is exact; everything about runs was checked on the first 107 steps of each machine and is an observation, not a proof (holdouts/related.py, holdouts/related.json).

Same run as 1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_0RD1RB_0RC--- (observed, not proved). At every one of the first 107 steps from blank tape the two machines have the same tape and the head on the same cell. Their tables differ, and no renaming of the states turns one into the other. While they run, the states pair up as A–A, B–B, C–C, D–D, E–E, F–B, F–F (this machine first), which is not one to one. Transitions not used in these steps: this machine never uses F1 and the other never uses F1. Lean proves (lean/Twin.lean in the other machine's folder, lock_or_break, not_halts_of_noBreak) that the two runs have the same tape, the same head position and paired states up to the first break, a configuration where the paired states act differently, and that neither machine halts if no break ever comes. A halt of either machine is itself a break, and the halting transitions of the two do not correspond, so the two are not known to halt together.

Same tapes at another pace (observed, not proved). Leave the states aside and compare only the tape and the place of the head. Then a large part of the first 107 configurations of this machine are configurations of the machines below, in the same order and up to the end of the run. The steps at which they occur differ, the tables are not renamings of each other, and equal tapes with different states may be continued differently, so this does not tie the halting questions together.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. It halts only if F reads a 1, i.e. only if after D1 (0RF) and E1 (1RF) the head finds a 1; it never has, and the layout of the junk, which would decide it, is not decoded.
