Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1LB_1RC0RD_0LA1RE_0RF1LC_1LA0RB_---1LC

Open here; an outside proof awaits reviewA Rocq proof that it halts is in an open busycoq pull request, not merged and not compiled here. This page's own ledger treats halting as open.

Nobody knows yet. The machine builds a long block of identical words and, beside it, a binary counter that ticks once every two trips of the head. Each time the counter overflows it eats a little of the leftover cells beyond it; when they are gone, the whole block turns into new leftovers and everything starts again, each time on a vastly larger scale. Only at the end of such an epoch can the machine stop.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from D0 (writes 0, moves R): D>0 0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.496). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

After 109 steps the tape spans 28,354 cells (20,782 left of the start, 11 right), with a density of 1s of 0.75.

The tape

A block of words, a counter, and leftovers

Everything happens left of the start. Write x for the word 1011. Each time the head reaches a new cell on the left in state B, the tape reads

0^inf B> 0 10 x^N | J | counter | junk | 10 1011 1011 ...

The junction J is 1010 x and 11 x in turn. A round is one trip of the head from the left end into the counter and back. The block of x's grows by one word every two rounds, so the tape grows by two cells a round and a round costs time proportional to the tape length: the visited part grows like the square root of the time.

The left end

Crossing the block in one stroke

The head crosses the block of x's twelve steps a word on the way left (state A) and eight a word on the way right (state E), rewriting each word while it is on it and restoring it after. Lean proves the full bounce at the left end for every block length (left_turn, right_sweep). Because of this, the length of the block matters only for the time; the reduced simulator keeps it at 40 words and was checked against the full machine for 24,522 rounds; it is faithful until the first restart, where the real block becomes junk.

The right of the block

A binary counter

How deep into the tape right of the block a round's changes reach, over 2,000,000 rounds. Each depth occurs half as often as the one before, exactly the pattern of carries in a binary counter that goes up by one every two rounds, with a bit about every three cells.

The open question

Overflows, epochs and restarts

Beyond the counter lies junk: a run of 1110 words and a second, slow counter. Each overflow of the main counter (the visited region widened at rounds 349,558, 8,738,166 and 25,515,382) takes one or two of the words, and every second overflow ticks the slow counter. When the words are gone the main counter swallows the slow one, and at the next overflow either the head reads 0 in state F, which halts, or the machine restarts: the whole block of 1011 words is rewritten as 1110 words, the counter becomes the new slow counter, and a new epoch begins with an empty counter.

The blank-tape run restarts at round 25,515,382 with 12,757,674 words of junk. An epoch with a words of junk lasts about 42a/3 rounds, and its block, the next epoch's junk, grows as large. Jumping from overflow to overflow, the second epoch ends in a restart with a junk of about 217,010,257 words, and so does the third. Halting starts exist (for some junk lengths divisible by 3, in a Cantor-like pattern), so whether the machine ever halts depends on the digits of these huge numbers.

Kernel
inc_even, inc_odd (lean/Rules.lean, from lean/rules.py); count, lean/Counter.lean
The counter's carries, and a full count, for every width

Write the counter with digit i as 1010/1111 (0/1) for even i and 10/11 for odd i, least significant first, right after the junction 1010 1011. Two rounds turn B> 0 10 (1011)^n 1010 1011 (111111)^m 1010 R into the same with (1011)^(n+1) and (101010)^m 1111 R (26m+40n+122 steps), and (111111)^m 1111 10 R into (101010)^m 1010 11 R (26m+40n+128 steps). By induction on m, a counter of 2m digits goes from all zeros to all ones while the block gains 4m−1 words, for every n and every tape R right of it.

Computed
py/macro.py, py/epoch.py
What an overflow does

Right of the counter lies junk (1110)^a T, T a second counter in the same digit code. When the main counter is all ones at even width w, the next two rounds leave it at width w+2 with value 2w+1 and take 1 and 2 words of 1110 in turn (three every two overflows), and every second overflow adds one to T. Simulated for small w and a (w ≤ 8, a from 6 to 9, 30 overflows in a row): the result does not depend on w or a. The overflows predicted this way fall on rounds 349,559, 8,738,167 and 25,515,383, one after the observed widenings of the visited region.

Computed
py/closure.py, py/epoch.py
Restarts

When the 1110 words are used up the main counter absorbs the slow counter. If a leftover terminator remains, the next overflow restarts the machine: within three rounds the block (1011)^N becomes (1110)^N, the main counter of width W becomes the new slow counter 1111 1110 (1010)^(3W/4) 11 (or ... 1011 when W ≡ 2 mod 4) and a new, empty main counter starts. If nothing remains, the next overflow reads 0 in F and halts. From fresh epochs with a ≤ 40 words of junk and slow counters of widths 4, 6 and 8 (py/closure.py 40 4,6,8): 107 of the 120 runs restart in exactly this form (leftover 11), 6 restart with other leftovers and a variant tail (the second epoch's is 1111 1110 (1010)^(3W/4+1) 1011 1011), and 7 halt.

Computed
py/shortcut.py, py/gen.py
Halting configurations exist in the family

Starting from a fresh epoch (1110)^a plus the slow counter of width w, some junk lengths lead to a halt: for w = 6, a = 15, 18, 39, 42, 99, 102, 105, 108, 117, 120, 123, 126, ...; for w = 8, a = 51, 54, ..., 72, 87, 90, 147, 150, .... All are multiples of 3, in a Cantor-like pattern set by the digits of the slow counter. For the tail family left by the second epoch (... 1011 1011) no halts were found for widths 8, 12, 16 and a < 150.

Heuristic
py/epoch2.py, py/epoch3.py, py/ep3real.py
The second and third epochs end in restarts

Skipping the 2k+2 overflows that only count the slow counter up (a shortcut checked against full runs at small sizes), the second epoch, junk 12,757,674 words, ends after its last overflows in a restart at counter width 17,010,256, with a block of N2 words, N2 a number of 17,010,257 bits (N2 ≡ 1 mod 3). The third epoch, junk N2, also ends in a restart: its last overflows absorb a slow counter of 17,010,257 digits and leave the terminator 11, simulated at the real width (17,010,270, then 17,010,272 counter digits). Extrapolated from small sizes, hence heuristic.

Heuristic
Where the open question sits

Each epoch's junk length is about the number of words the block grew in the epoch before, so the lengths grow like a tower: 12,757,674, then N2 ≈ 217,010,257, then a number of about (4/3)N2 bits. The machine halts if and when an epoch's junk length falls in the halting set of its tail family, which is decided by residues and binary digits of these numbers. The fourth epoch needs the digits of a number of about 217,010,257 bits.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Open. A proof of non-halting by a family of configurations closed under carry, overflow and restart is not possible as such: the family that the restarts produce contains halting members, so any such proof has to carry the exact arithmetic of the junk lengths from epoch to epoch. Not proved in Lean: the overflow round (the head sweeping into the junk, in two alignments), the slow counter's increment (symtm stops with NeedSplit on (11)^k), the absorption and the restart, and counters given as arbitrary bit lists rather than all zeros or all ones.
