What is known about 1RB0RE_0RC---_1LD1LE_1LE1LD_1RF0LC_1RA1RF

1RB0RE_0RC---_1LD1LE_1LE1LD_1RF0LC_1RA1RF

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 22 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Lean proves that one bounce of the head turns the zone, a word in the 4-cell letters u = 1110 and v = 1111, into the next row of Pascal's triangle mod 2 (the running XOR of the word with a 1 appended), for every word, and solves the rows in closed form by Lucas' theorem. It also proves that the machine halts as soon as a right turn meets a frozen block of odd length followed by 0 1, whatever the zone. In 1010 steps that never happens: at every right turn the tape right of the head is a word in the 2-cell letters 11 and 01 (observed). Proving that invariant would settle the machine; it is not proved.

The halting configurations

The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (B1); the generated statement covers every configuration.

step_none_iff, lean/Blank.lean

No halt in the first 10,000 steps

The list simulator of the shared library, proved to agree with the machine's semantics, runs 10,000 steps from blank tape without a halt, evaluated by Lean's kernel.

blank_runs_kernel, lean/Blank.lean

The leftward sweep is a running parity

With the head in D (even) or C (odd) on the last cell of a 4-cell word 111x: D on u = 1110 leaves 0101 and goes to C; D on v = 1111 leaves 1111 and stays D; C on u leaves 1111 and goes to D; C on v leaves 0101 and stays C. So the word written behind the head is the running XOR (from the right) of the u's, as 1111 (even) or 0101 (odd). Plus the runs (1111)n for D, E and F.

d_u0 ... c_v1, d_run, e_run, f_run, lean/Rules.lean (from py/rules.py)

The machine on blocks of 1s

Going right, F crosses a block; at a separator followed by a block of one cell it fills the separator, erases the cell and fills the next separator (the blocks on both sides gain a cell); at a separator followed by a longer block it turns, moving two cells from that block to the one on its left; three 0s before a 1 are filled; at four 0s it writes 1011 and turns. Going left, D crosses a block and fills the separator after it; E erases every second cell of the next block and, if the block's length is odd, the pass goes on in D, otherwise the head turns. Each statement holds for any tape around the pattern.

f_block, f_single, f_turn, f_three_zeros, f_end, d_block, d_sep, e_pairs, e_last, c_last, c_sep, e_turn, turn_two, lean/Blocks.lean (from py/blocks_rules.py)

Where it can halt

Only B reading 1 halts; B is entered only from A reading 0, and A only from F reading 0. So the machine halts exactly on F> 0 0 1: F on that pattern halts within two steps (halt_F), and two steps before any halt the machine is in F on 0 0 1 (halt_route). An earlier version of this page gave the pattern as F> 1 0 1, which was wrong.

halt_F, lean/Blocks.lean; halt_route, lean/Route.lean (from py/route.py)

One bounce is one row of Pascal's triangle mod 2

At a right turn the head is in E just right of the zone, a word w in u = 1110 (1) and v = 1111 (0) after a prefix that is empty or one 1. If the frozen tape starts 1 1 1, then for every word the machine reaches the next right turn with the zone T(w), the running XOR from the right of w 1, the prefix kept or toggled by the parity of w, and the frozen tape two cells shorter. A block of 2k + 1 frozen cells gives k such bounces.

bounce, bounces, with sweep, lend, rsweep, regroup, lean/Pascal.lean

The zone after j bounces, by Lucas' theorem

Number the letters from the right end of the zone and flip letter 0. After j bounces letter i is the XOR of the starting letters dk with C(i − k − 1, j − 1) odd, and that binomial coefficient is odd exactly when i − k ≥ j and j − 1, i − k − j share no binary 1 (Lucas). Proved for every word and every j, with Pascal's rule for the bit test proved from scratch.

closed_form, diagram, pascal_rule, Q_rule, lean/Lucas.lean

A right turn at an odd frozen block halts

Whatever the zone and prefix, a right turn with the frozen tape 12k+1 0 1 ... leads to a halt: k bounces, then F reads 0 0 1.

halts_of_odd, halt_turn, lean/Pascal.lean

Even head blocks: absorption and walls

For every zone: a head block of 2 cells followed by a block of 4k+1 cells is absorbed and leaves a clean zone (tmap w, vk, u) with the third block one cell shorter under the head; with 4k+3 cells the zone gets a wall 1 0 next to the head. A bounce against a walled zone is the running XOR with a 0 appended, and the wall stays; an odd head block still halts with a wall. The case of an even head block meeting a walled zone is settled by the 2-cell letters below.

absorb, absorb3, wbounce, halts_of_odd_wall, lean/Absorb.lean

Walls are letters: one bounce for every zone

Cut the tape into 2-cell letters: a = 11, b = 10 in the zone, c = 01 in the frozen tape. A clean zone is a word in ab and aa, a wall is one b, so walls anywhere in the zone are just letters. For every zone in a and b and every frozen word in a and c: the left sweep is the running XOR from the right and always reaches the left end; the right sweep is the running XOR from the left and turns at the first place where it is odd twice in a row. The next right turn has the same shape, so an even head block meeting a walled zone is one instance (wall_even), and the machine cannot halt before the right sweep runs past the end of the frozen word: any set of shapes closed under the list map nxt gives a proof of non-halting.

bounce2, wall_even, turn_next, not_halts_of_closed, lean/Wall.lean

The left sweep of a crossing

For a zone with one lone 1 between a near part N and a far part O (2-cell letters), the left sweep takes the running XOR over N. If the parity at the lone 1 is even it goes on over O to the left end. If it is odd, E turns the as of O before its first b into 1 0 and turns right on that b. This holds for every N, O, prefix and frozen word.

cross_even, cross_odd, lean/Crossing.lean

Crossing bounces; a clean far part stays clean

For every zone with one lone 1 (near part, far part), prefix and frozen word, the next right turn of a crossing is given on lists (turn2_odd, turn2_even). If the far part is a clean 4-cell word u ws (as at every crossing turn observed), an even bounce turns it into u (sx true ws) and an odd bounce removes its first u; it never stops a bounce. A crossing can therefore only go wrong through its near part.

clean_even, clean_odd, far_even, lean/Crossing.lean

The holdout as a question about lists

A list machine astep on three kinds of right-turn states (ordinary, crossing, E on the last 1) covers the right end of the tape too. Each list step is at least one machine step (astep_sim), and the first right turn is at step 12 (start). If the list machine never fails from there, the machine never halts from blank tape (not_halts_of_arun). It can fail in only two ways: F reaching the blank end during a crossing, or an even crossing bounce blocked by its far part. Neither has happened: the list machine matches all 17,758 right turns of the first 2·108 steps.

not_halts_of_arun, lean/End.lean; py/listmachine.py

Odd runs in the frozen word rule out both failure cases

A right sweep from F reads a word to the end and finishes in F only if the word is in (0|11)*. Call a 0 followed by an odd run of 1s, which is followed by a 0 or by the end of the word, a witness. A crossing turn whose far part is clean and whose frozen word has a witness always has a next list state (astep_T2_witness).

lean/Race.lean

The ten crossing turns without a witness are safe for every clean far part

The 10 crossing turns with blank end and no witness in the first 40,000 list steps all come right after E reads the last 1 during a crossing, in six windows with three near parts: b a b a6 (four windows), b a b a12 (one) and a 43-letter word (list step 28,450). For each, Lean proves that for every prefix and every clean far part (for the third, every far part starting u v v) the list machine does not fail for 2, 3 or 6 steps and then reaches a safe state: the crossing is over, or the frozen word has a witness and the far part is clean, so the next step exists too. The proofs keep the far part symbolic: the near side only reads the run of as it absorbs after an odd bounce, and an even bounce turns the far part into its running XOR. Lean also proves that the near part alone is not enough: EO (b a b a2) fails at once for every far part, and the run avoids it only because the far part's first run is u v v.

window_W3, window_W6, window_W43, eo_W1_fails, witnessed_step, lean/Window.lean (the 43-letter window from py/window43.py)

Every turn of F is at a block of odd length, except at the right end (1010 steps)

In the first 1010 steps F turned 139,833 times. The block it turned at had odd length every time (3 cells 36,463 times, 5 cells 46,436, 7 cells 19,254, 9 cells 20,280, and longer), except 22 turns at a block of exactly two cells, each of which was the last block of the tape. A turn at a block of two with another block after it would leave 0 0 1 (turn_two). F met two 0s in a row 42 times, each time at the right end with only blank cells beyond. No halt.

c/turns.c

40,000 list steps, and 106 in C++

The list machine runs 40,000 steps from the first right turn without failing: 17,897 T, 22,084 T2 and 19 EO turns. The far part is clean at every T2 turn, and the zone grows to 21,395 letters. Of the 18,062 crossing turns with blank end, 18,052 have a witness in the frozen word. The other 10 come in the first five bounces after E reads the last 1 during a crossing, and the near part gets them through. The C++ port runs 106 list steps without failing (zone 620,913 letters, of the order of 1011 machine steps by an estimate from the zone size). E reads the last 1 during a crossing 8 times there, always with one of the three near parts that lean/Window.lean covers, and all 12 crossing turns without a witness are in those windows.

py/listmachine.py run 40000, py/race.py 40000, c/listmachine.cpp

With a free far part the near side can fail

During a crossing the near part, the frozen word and the end evolve without looking at the far part, except for the run of as the near part takes from it after each odd bounce. Letting those run lengths be anything, a breadth-first search finds failures of the list machine, both from crossing starts am (m = 1, 2, 3, the values the run has) and from real states of the run two bounces after E reads the last 1: a long run turns the near part into a zone that eats the whole frozen word. So a proof must use which run lengths the far parts of real crossings have, that is, the structure of the zone that the growing phase leaves.

py/game.py

The next crossing depends on a short deep window

The next crossing start (near part and the first 30 letters of its far part) depends only on the last 200 or so letters of the frozen word: randomizing everything else gives the same next crossing in 13 of 13 runs; keeping only 30 letters does not.

c/locality.cpp

Crossings as one map

A crossing depends only on the swept zone, the prefix and the end cell, so the machine is an iterated map on crossing starts; the simulated map matches the run at all 13 crossings of the first 2·107 steps. The 17 crossing starts up to 2·108 steps have an odd last run of as and odd inner runs apart from at most two near the head, but small words of that kind can still break a crossing, depending on run lengths modulo higher powers of 2.

py/crossing.py

An even frozen block: absorption

With a block of 2 under the head the zone swallows it and the next block, and the block after that loses one cell (frozen blocks 2, b, c, ... become c − 1, ...; if c = 1 the next pair goes too). Checked on 20,000 random zones (up to 8 letters, both prefixes) and frozen tapes; the zone never changes the outcome.

py/frozen.py

Right of every right turn: 2-cell letters (1010 steps)

At all 139,833 right turns in E in 1010 steps, the tape from the head to the last 1 is a word in 11 and 01 starting at the head, so the block under the head has even length and every later block odd length. The block under the head had 2 to 4, 6 to 8, 10 to 16 or 18 to 32 cells (dyadic bands; once 62 and once more than 64). The same holds for 109 steps of this table started in C, D and E (the three holdouts with this table).

c/twoletter.c

Every right turn has one of three shapes; halting needs a crossing (2·108 steps)

In 2-cell letters plus a lone 1 o, all 17,758 right turns of the first 2·108 steps are a zone in a, b with at most one o inside it, E on a, a frozen word in a, c and then one lone 1 or nothing; or, 18 times, E on a lone 1 at the right end. At every turn with no o inside the zone the next right turn is exactly the one Lean's nxt predicts (8,988 times), or nxt is none and a crossing starts (17 times). Turn-to-turn n-gram closures (k = 3 to 6) do not close, and no bounded number of zone letters decides where a crossing goes.

holdouts/triage.json

Regular-language deciders tried (F11)

None of these closes: each reaches a halting configuration in its abstraction, which says nothing about whether the machine halts. n-gram CPS (tools/c/ngramcps.c), equal widths on both sides: a halting local configuration is reached with plain up to width 24, history 1 up to width 24, history 2 up to width 24, history 3 up to width 24, LRU history up to width 12 (larger widths ran out of the 150 s or 60 million configuration budget). n-gram CPS with every cell tagged by its position mod p: fails for p = 2 up to width 20, p = 3 up to width 20, p = 4 up to width 20, p = 6 up to width 20. n-gram CPS where each side also carries the state of a small automaton that has read that side from the far end (every automaton with up to 3 states on both sides, or 4 states on one side), with position mod 1..4 and widths 4, 6, 8: no closed set without a halt in all 120 parameter sets. Run-length windows (the K nearest runs of equal macro cells on each side, run lengths kept exactly below T and mod M above): fails for all 72 parameter sets (cells of 1 to 4 bits, K = 2, 4, 6, T = 2, 4, M = 1, 2, 4). Two-automaton closed tape language by SAT (a deterministic automaton reading each side inward, n states each, CaDiCaL): no solution for n = 2..8; n = 9 not finished in 150 s.

heuristic Evidence only

Two phases in an epoch

Followed turn by turn through one epoch (steps 4,104 to 7,890): first the head bounces between the left end and a turning place that moves right, eating the blocks beyond it two cells at a time; at each right turn the zone is a list of blocks 14k+3, at each left turn blocks 14k+1 separated by odd numbers of one-cell blocks. When the turning place reaches the right end the head crosses back to the left end in short bounces, leaving blocks of 3 and pairs 1, 5 (the words 1110 and 1011) for the next epoch to eat. Observed on that epoch, not proved.

py/blocks.py

Perturbed orbits halt

1000 orbits that agree with the real one on the last 10 letters of the frozen word at list step 186 give 67 astep failures in 9 crossings, 47 of them real halts of the machine within 109 steps: about 0.5 to 1% per crossing. From list step 12240 the rate is lower (1 failure in 476 crossings). The machine looks like a probabilistic halter; a non-halting proof would need structure that makes the real far parts special.

c/perturb.cpp

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.501). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.064).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

open Open

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. Halting needs F> 0 0 1. In the run so far two 0s in a row appear in front of F only at the right end of the tape, with blank tape beyond. A proof would need an invariant that keeps every block at which F turns odd, and a description of what the right end does once per epoch.
