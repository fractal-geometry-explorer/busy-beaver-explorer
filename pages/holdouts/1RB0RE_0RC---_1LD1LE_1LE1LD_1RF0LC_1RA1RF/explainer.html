The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in B reading 1. B is entered only from A0 (writes 1, moves R): A>0 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The run at a glance

Space-time diagram and records

The diagram stacks the tape at many moments. Sampling at each new cell shows the shape of the run as the visited region grows; sampling evenly in time shows where the head spends its time. The records list every step at which the head reaches a cell no earlier step reached, with the side, the width so far and the whole tape: the first place to look for a repeating macro structure.

What the tape looks like

A word in two 4-cell letters

Between sweeps the tape is a long run of blocks 14k+30, that is a word in two letters, u = 1110 and v = 1111. At step 2,003,981 the tape begins

u^3 v u v^2 u v u v u^3 v^2 u v u v u v^2 u^2 v^3 u v^2 u v^3 u v u^3 ...

about 650 letters in all, plus a short end on the right. Read u as 1 and v as 0: the tape holds a binary string about 650 bits long (the side that grows like a square root), next to a short fixed part on the right (the side that grows like a logarithm).

The rule

Every leftward sweep computes a running parity

Going left, the head is in state D ("even so far") or C ("odd so far"). Each time it crosses a letter: if the letter is u the parity flips. It writes 1111 behind it when the new parity is even and 0101 when it is odd. These four cases (and the long runs over 1111) are proved in Lean.

Worked example: the word v u u v u (bits 0 1 1 0 1) swept from the right, starting even: u → odd, v → odd, u → even, u → odd, v → odd: the new bits, left to right, are 1 1 0 1 1. The next rightward sweep turns 0101 back into a letter; repeating "replace by running XOR" is the rule that generates Pascal's triangle modulo 2, which is why the space-time diagram is full of Sierpinski triangles.

The emergent behavior

Epochs, each about 1.75 times longer

The head does not always cross the whole tape. Inside an epoch it bounces between the left end and an inner wall; the wall moves left a little on each bounce, and the zone between them is rewritten by the parity rule while the tape right of the wall stays frozen. When the zone has shrunk away, a full crossing starts a new epoch. Epochs began at steps 47,799, 145,962, 487,979, 1,427,948, 4,277,179, 13,419,889, 40,483,837, 124,076,294, 383,276,545 and 1,162,585,978, with the tape's left end at 374, 703, 1,319, 2,265, 4,020, 7,237, 12,641, 22,085, 38,918 and 68,091 cells.

Followed turn by turn, an epoch has two phases (observed on the epoch from step 4,104 to 7,890). In the first the head bounces between the left end and a turning place that moves right by 2 to 20 cells per bounce, eating the blocks beyond it two cells at a time. When the turning place reaches the right end of the tape, the head crosses back to the left end in short bounces 8 to 28 cells wide, leaving blocks of 3 and pairs 1, 5 behind it, which the next epoch eats.

Can it stop?

Only on F> 0 0 1

The machine stops only when B reads a 1; B is entered only from A reading 0, and A only from F reading 0. So it stops exactly when state F stands on 0 0 1 (proved in Lean, both directions). An earlier version of this page said 1 0 1, which was wrong.

Read the tape as blocks of 1s separated by single 0s. Going right, F crosses blocks; a block of one cell is erased and its two separators filled; at a longer block F turns, moving two cells from that block to the block on its left. A turn at a block of exactly two cells erases it, and if another block follows, the tape now reads 0 0 1 there: the next visit of F would stop the machine. All of these rules are Lean theorems.

In the first 1010 steps F turned 139,833 times, always at a block of odd length, except 22 times at a block of two that was the last block on the tape, with nothing but blank cells after it. F met two 0s in a row 42 times, always at that right end. So far, then, the question is decided at the right end of the tape, about twice per epoch. This is what the run shows, not a proof: nothing yet shows that a block of even length can never be turned at in the middle of the tape.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.501). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.064).

After 109 steps the tape spans 64,917 cells (43,020 left of the start, 130 right), with a density of 1s of 0.823.

d_u0 ... c_v1, d_run, e_run, f_run, lean/Rules.lean (from py/rules.py)
The leftward sweep is a running parity

With the head in D (even) or C (odd) on the last cell of a 4-cell word 111x: D on u = 1110 leaves 0101 and goes to C; D on v = 1111 leaves 1111 and stays D; C on u leaves 1111 and goes to D; C on v leaves 0101 and stays C. So the word written behind the head is the running XOR (from the right) of the u's, as 1111 (even) or 0101 (odd). Plus the runs (1111)n for D, E and F.

Kernel
f_block, f_single, f_turn, f_three_zeros, f_end, d_block, d_sep, e_pairs, e_last, c_last, c_sep, e_turn, turn_two, lean/Blocks.lean (from py/blocks_rules.py)
The machine on blocks of 1s

Going right, F crosses a block; at a separator followed by a block of one cell it fills the separator, erases the cell and fills the next separator (the blocks on both sides gain a cell); at a separator followed by a longer block it turns, moving two cells from that block to the one on its left; three 0s before a 1 are filled; at four 0s it writes 1011 and turns. Going left, D crosses a block and fills the separator after it; E erases every second cell of the next block and, if the block's length is odd, the pass goes on in D, otherwise the head turns. Each statement holds for any tape around the pattern.

Heuristic
py/blocks.py
Two phases in an epoch

Followed turn by turn through one epoch (steps 4,104 to 7,890): first the head bounces between the left end and a turning place that moves right, eating the blocks beyond it two cells at a time; at each right turn the zone is a list of blocks 14k+3, at each left turn blocks 14k+1 separated by odd numbers of one-cell blocks. When the turning place reaches the right end the head crosses back to the left end in short bounces, leaving blocks of 3 and pairs 1, 5 (the words 1110 and 1011) for the next epoch to eat. Observed on that epoch, not proved.

Kernel
halt_F, lean/Blocks.lean; halt_route, lean/Route.lean (from py/route.py)
Where it can halt

Only B reading 1 halts; B is entered only from A reading 0, and A only from F reading 0. So the machine halts exactly on F> 0 0 1: F on that pattern halts within two steps (halt_F), and two steps before any halt the machine is in F on 0 0 1 (halt_route). An earlier version of this page gave the pattern as F> 1 0 1, which was wrong.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. Halting needs F> 0 0 1. In the run so far two 0s in a row appear in front of F only at the right end of the tape, with blank tape beyond. A proof would need an invariant that keeps every block at which F turns odd, and a description of what the right end does once per epoch.
