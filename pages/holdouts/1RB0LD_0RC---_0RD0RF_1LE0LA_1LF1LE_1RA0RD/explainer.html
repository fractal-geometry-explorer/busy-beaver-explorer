Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LD_0RC---_0RD0RF_1LE0LA_1LF1LE_1RA0RD

Nobody knows. This machine bounces between a wall and the right end of its tape, converting the tape between two periodic patterns, in epochs that grow by about 9/4 each. At the end of each epoch the wall nibbles at counters it left behind earlier; now and then it builds a much larger one and starts over. The bouncing is proved in Lean; the nibbling is where the open question lies.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in B reading 1. B is entered only from A0 (writes 1, moves R): A>0 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.512).

After 109 steps the tape spans 30,464 cells (52 left of the start, 23,179 right), with a density of 1s of 0.545.

What the tape is made of

A wall, two patterns and some counters

Write a = 10 and b = 0110. Almost all of the tape is made of these two pieces. Right of a wandering wall the tape is a run of one 8-cell word, P = a b a = 10011010 after the head has swept right, or Q = 11110001 after it has swept left. Left of the wall lies a run of an 18-cell word (a a a b a a b, in another phase while it is being built), and further left runs of a 36-cell word that act as counters, and some debris.

The head goes back and forth between the wall and the right end of the tape. Each round trip makes the tape two cells wider, which is why the right side grows like the square root of the step count.

One round trip

The bouncer part is exact

Going left the head turns every P into Q, zig-zagging 36 steps per word; going right it turns every Q back into P, 32 steps per word. At the right end 0 D>1 becomes 1 0 E>0 1 in 5 steps: two new cells. Lean proves all three for every number of words, and their composition: 0 D> Q^n 1 comes back as 1 0 E>0 Q^n 1 in exactly 68n + 5 steps (round_right).

Everything interesting happens at the wall, where the head turns back to the right.

The wall

Two phases, epochs growing by 9/4

Phase A. The wall slowly eats the 18-cell run: per round trip 2 cells of it become 2 cells of the 8-cell run, in a pattern that repeats every 36 round trips (the wall moves 72 cells left, the 18-cell run loses 4 words, the 8-cell run gains 18). With x words of 18 cells this takes about 9x round trips and leaves about 4.5x words of 8 cells.

Phase B. When the 18-cell run is used up the wall turns around: every round trip it moves exactly 18 cells to the right, building one 18-cell word out of two 8-cell words. With y words this takes about y/2 round trips and leaves about y/2 words of 18 cells. Then phase A starts again.

So one epoch takes x to about (9/2)(1/2)x = 9x/4. The number of round trips in phase B, measured with the plain simulator:

The hard part

Counters used up irregularly, and restarts

When phase A ends, before phase B can start, the head runs further left and works on whatever lies there. At the start this is a counter: 10 copies of the 36-cell word 101001100110010011101001101010100110. Epoch by epoch it loses 0, 1 or 2 copies with no visible rule (6, 4, 4, 4, 3, gone, at the ends of the first epochs).

At about 1.7·1014 steps something new happened: the whole 18-cell run (691,448 words) became a counter of 345,724 copies of the 36-cell word, a fresh small counter of 5 copies appeared next to it, and the bouncer started over from small sizes, replaying exactly the epoch sizes it had after the first restart. After the small counter was used up the wall nibbled at the big one (345,724 → 345,722 → 345,721 → 345,720 → 345,718 → 345,717 over about 25 epochs). At about 4.96·1021 steps it restarted again, building a counter of 1,710,343,625 copies and a small one of 38. At about 2.57·1072 steps it restarted a third time, with a counter of 38,924,542,121,409,275,697,275,846,796,041,586 copies, and a fourth counter of 732,592,001,087,343,578 copies came soon after. The counters now read [345,717; 1,710,343,616; 3.9·1034; 7.3·1017].

This looks like a recursion: each level's counter is as large as the bouncer was when it was built, and lower levels use it up a little at a time. How much is removed at each epoch depends on the exact sizes, which grow by 9/4: residues of such numbers are what Collatz-like problems are made of. A proof either way would need the exact rule for the wall's work at the end of phase A, as a function of the sizes and of the debris.

Where it could halt

Only at the wall

The machine halts when state B reads a 1. The proved sweeps and the turn at the right end never do that, so a halt can only come from the wall, during the transitions between phases or while it works on the counters and debris. Closed tape languages with windows of 2 to 6 cells do not exist for this machine (they were tried before), which fits the picture of counters that must be read exactly.

What is proved

Proved, computed, observed

Lean, kernel: the halting configurations; no halt in 10,000 steps; the sweeps, the turn at the right end and the 68n + 5 round trip for every n (lean/Sweep.lean). Lean, native_decide: no halt in 107 steps.

Computed (trusted): no halt in 2.5·1072 steps on the segment simulator with cycle jumps, which agrees cell for cell with the plain simulator where both can run. Observed: the 9/4 epochs, the counters and the restarts.

Heuristic
py/run.py epochs
Epochs grow by about 9/4

Phase A: per round trip the 18-cell run loses 2 cells and the 8-cell run gains 4 (2 from the wall, 2 from the right end), so x copies of the 18-cell word last 9x round trips and leave about 4.5x copies of the 8-cell word. Phase B: per round trip one 18-cell word is made from two 8-cell words, so y copies give about y/2 new 18-cell words. One epoch maps x to about 9x/4. Read off the simulator, not proved.

Heuristic
py/run.py epochs
Nested counters, used up irregularly

Left of the 18-cell run the tape holds runs of the 36-cell word 101001100110010011101001101010100110 (10 copies at first). When phase A ends the wall works on the nearest such run and removes 0, 1 or 2 copies, in no visible pattern. Three times so far the whole 18-cell run was turned into such a counter and the bouncer restarted from small sizes: at 1.7·1014 steps (345,724 copies, plus a new small counter of 5) and at 4.96·1021 steps (1,710,343,625 copies, plus 38) and at 2.57·1072 steps (38,924,542,121,409,275,697,275,846,796,041,586 copies, followed by one of 732,592,001,087,343,578). Between restarts the epochs replay the same sizes.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The bouncer part is proved; the wall's work at the end of each phase A (on the counters and the debris between them) is the open part. It changes the counters by amounts that depend on residues of the epoch sizes, which is the kind of question (like the Collatz-type BB(6) cryptids) that has no known method.
