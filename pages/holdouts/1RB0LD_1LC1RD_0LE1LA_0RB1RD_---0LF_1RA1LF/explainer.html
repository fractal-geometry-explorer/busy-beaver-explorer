Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LD_1LC1RD_0LE1LA_0RB1RD_---0LF_1RA1LF

Nobody knows yet, but the question has been boiled down. This machine keeps its tape as a row of blocks of 1s with a single gap, and the gap sweeps leftwards like a carry in a counter, splitting even blocks in half between their neighbours. Lean proves it halts exactly when a sweep carries a power of two all the way to the left end of the tape. In 1011 sweeps that has never happened.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in E reading 0. E is entered only from C0 (writes 0, moves L): 0 C>0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.531).

After 109 steps the tape spans 42,664 cells (18 left of the start, 30,165 right), with a density of 1s of 1.0.

Structure

What the tape is made of

Look at the tape whenever the head is about to turn and it is always the same kind of thing: blocks of 1s, each followed by a single 0, except at one place where two 0s stand side by side. Call that place the gap. The head, in state B, sits on the second 0 of the gap. Here is the tape after 417 steps, blocks 3, 8, 6, 4 with the gap at the right end:

We write such a moment as (b, ls, rs): b is the block just left of the gap, ls the blocks further left, rs the blocks to the right of the gap (the first of them may be empty). Everything the machine does is to move this gap around and shuffle 1s between neighbouring blocks. Far to the left, beyond the first block, the tape is blank.

The basic move

One round: the gap steps one cell left

From the gap the head runs left over the block b: state E erases its last 1, F runs over the rest, writes a 1 into the 0 in front of the block, and A and D come back right to the gap, which is now one cell further left. Net effect: the block left of the gap loses two 1s and each of its neighbours gains one:

(a, b, c) → (a + 1, b − 2, c + 1)   in 2b + 5 steps

So rounds repeat until the block is down to 0 or 1 cells, giving half of it to each side. In Lean this is round_gap (one round, every b, every surrounding tape) and rounds_gap (m rounds at once, in m(2c + 2m + 7) steps, c the block at the end).

One sweep

Even blocks split, the first odd block closes the gap

A sweep starts with the gap at the right end of the tape (the right neighbour is the blank tape, an empty block). The gap works on the block to its left:

  • even block 2m: after m rounds it is empty, its two neighbours have m more each, and the gap simply moves on to the next block left (even_gap);
  • odd block 2m + 1: after m rounds one 1 is left; then the gap closes: 1 0 0 becomes 1 1 1, merging that 1 into the right neighbour, D runs right across the whole tape, and a new gap opens at the right end (odd_gap, drun).

Each sweep adds exactly two 1s to the tape (the two 0s of the gap) and keeps the number of blocks, except when it reaches the left end, where it adds new blocks. The tape grows by about one cell per sweep while a sweep costs about as many steps as the tape is long, which is why the width grows like the square root of the step count.

The emergent behaviour

A carry that rarely gets through

Read the sweeps as a counter. A sweep stops at the first odd block it meets, so it only gets far when the blocks near the right end happen to be even, like a carry rippling through the digits of a binary counter. Near the right end the blocks are small and the pattern is exactly periodic (the sweep depths go 3 1 2 5 3 1 2 6 ...); further left the blocks are huge (tens of millions of 1s after 108 sweeps) and the parities they present to the sweep look irregular.

Only rarely does a sweep get through every block and reach the left end of the tape. In the first 1011 sweeps that happened 17 times:

The danger

Where it could halt: a power of two at the left end

The machine halts on E reading a 0. That happens in only two situations: a gap at an empty block (three 0s in a row, which the invariant rules out), or an odd block closing its gap with nothing at all to its left: then D falls off the tape into the blank, and B, C, E read three blank cells.

At the left end of the tape, an even block 2m gives half to a brand-new leftmost block, and the gap moves onto that new block, which again has nothing to its left. So a block 2k·q (q odd) halves k times, and then:

  • q ≥ 3: the odd block gives (q − 1)/2 to yet another new block on its left and closes safely: the tape has grown by k + 1 blocks;
  • q = 1: it closes onto blank tape and the machine halts.

For example the sweep that reached the left end at sweep 893,513 carried 281,084 = 22 · 70,271: it halved twice and stopped safely at 70,271. The machine halts if and only if some sweep reaches the left end carrying a power of two (Lean: halts_iff, mstep_halt_iff, wall_chain, wall_odd).

Nothing seen so far forbids that. The carried value is fixed by the exact parities of all blocks, a Collatz-like dependence; if it behaves like a random number of its size, the chance of a power of two is about log2v / v per visit, tiny for v around 1010, and the visits are rarer and rarer. So it almost certainly never halts, but a proof would need to understand which values the carry can bring.

Status

What is proved

Lean, kernel (lean/Macro.lean, lean/Reduce.lean): the round, the even and odd block rules and D's run to the right end, for all sizes and all surrounding tapes; the block machine mstep and that the machine follows it (mstep_sound, mstep_halt); from blank tape the first gap after 7 steps; the invariant that keeps every block nonempty, so the rules cover the whole run; and halts_iff: the machine halts iff the block machine does, which (mstep_halt_iff, wall_chain, wall_odd) is iff a power of two reaches the left end.

Lean, native_decide (lean/MacroRun.lean): 20,000,000 block steps, no halt: the machine runs at least 221,196,908,934,683 steps.

Computed: the block machine checked against the machine, tape for tape, at every gap in the first 108 steps (py/macro.py); the block machine in C for 1011 sweeps (c/sweeps.c), with the left-end visits listed above.

Open: whether a power of two ever reaches the left end.

The rules of a gap

With the tape a row of blocks of 1s separated by single 0s and one gap 0 B>0: a round takes two 1s off the block left of the gap and gives one to each neighbour (2b + 5 steps), for every b and every surrounding tape; m rounds at once; an even block is used up and the gap moves on to the next block left; an odd block keeps one 1, the gap closes (1 0 0 becomes 1 1 1), D runs to the right end of the tape and opens a new gap there.

The block machine

mstep maps one gap configuration (b, ls, rs) to the next, with its step count mcost; the machine follows it (mstep_sound), and where it says halt the machine halts (mstep_halt). From blank tape the first gap 1^3 0 B>0 comes after 7 steps (init_gap).

Kernel
mstep_halt_iff, wall_chain, wall_odd, lean/Reduce.lean
Where it halts: a power of two at the left end

Under the invariant the block machine halts exactly at gap 1 [] rs: an odd block that kept a single 1 with nothing left of it. A block 2k·q (q odd) reaching the left end halves k times, each half becoming a new leftmost block (wall_chain), and then q halts iff q = 1 (wall_odd). So the machine halts iff some sweep reaches the left end carrying a power of two.

Heuristic
README.md
Why it probably never halts

A sweep reaches the left end only when every block it meets is even, like a carry running through every digit of a counter; such sweeps come ever more rarely (each between 1.4 and 30 times as many sweeps after the one before), and the block they carry grows like the number of sweeps. If its size behaves like a random number, the chance that it is a power of two is about log2v / v, and the expected number of halts still to come, with v already above 1010, is about 4·10-9.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Open: equivalent (in Lean) to whether a sweep ever carries a power of two to the left end. The carried values depend on the parities of all blocks, a Collatz-like question with no visible invariant that excludes powers of two.
