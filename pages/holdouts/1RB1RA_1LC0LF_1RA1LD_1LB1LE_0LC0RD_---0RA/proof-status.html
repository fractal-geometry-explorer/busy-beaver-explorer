What is known about 1RB1RA_1LC0LF_1RA1LD_1LB1LE_0LC0RD_---0RA

1RB1RA_1LC0LF_1RA1LD_1LB1LE_0LC0RD_---0RA

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 24 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The behaviour is decoded completely: during a visit of the left end the tape is a stack of frames (a block of 1s and a block of 0s each) and a word over the tokens 110 and 111, and twelve transitions on these reproduce the run from blank tape step for step (that the machine follows them is proved in Lean). One of them is the halt. It can only happen in the cascade that follows a visit reaching the right end of the tape, and there it looks like a matter of chance: random words halt at a few in a thousand such visits, and the run has had 31 of them in its first 2,097,152 visits (1.3·1013 steps) without halting. The earlier conjecture that every word over the two tokens leads to such a word again is false: a word of 19 tokens halts (Lean).

Shift rules

For every length n of the run and any tape around it: L (111110110)^n D> 1 R reaches L D> 1 (101101101)^n R; L (011011111)^n E> 0 R reaches L E> 0 (110110110)^n R; L (110)^n D> 1 R reaches L D> 1 (101)^n R. These are the moves the head makes across the long uniform stretches of the tape.

lean/Shifts.lean (written by bbtools.symtm.prove)

The machine follows the model

The model is a Lean function mstep on states (a list of frames and RET, Z or CALL with a word), and den gives the tape a state stands for. mstep_next: when mstep s is a state s' with a step count k, the machine goes from the tape of s to the tape of s' in exactly k steps. mstep_halt: when mstep s is the halt, the machine halts. mrun_sound, blank_runs_of_mrun: a run of the model from RET(1, c) on the empty stack is a run of the machine from blank tape. That the model is never stuck on the run is proved in lean/Inv.lean; the C and Python programs are not proved to be the same function as mstep, but Lean's own run of the model reproduces their numbers (lean/Bound.lean, lean/Drive.lean).

lean/Sim.lean

The model never gets stuck, and halts exactly when the machine does

On every state of the run the model either halts or moves on, by at least one step, to a state of the run again; so the machine halts from blank tape if and only if the model does.

inv_step, halts_iff_model, lean/Inv.lean

A faster model, proved

The call through the periodic block, the return through its frames and runs of returns done as tight loops; each transition of the faster model is a run of the machine.

qstep_next, fvrun_sound, blank_runs_of_fvrun, lean/Fast.lean

130,661 steps from blank tape through the model

20,000 transitions of the Lean model, run by the kernel, are 130,661 steps of the machine from blank tape without a halt: a check that the whole chain from the model to the machine closes.

blank_runs_model_kernel, lean/Sim.lean

Every line of the transition table is a theorem

Frames and the two ways a word is written on the tape are defined in Lean, and each transition of the model is proved for the machine at any depth of the stack: the tape of the left-hand side reaches the tape of the right-hand side in the stated number of steps (ret_stride, ret3, ret2, ret2_pop, ret1, ret1_pop, ret1_one, z_call, z_call_end, call_word, call_end, and the versions at the bottom of the stack); ret1_halt: RET(1) on a frame of one 1 and one 0 above a 0 halts; blank_to_model: two steps from blank tape the machine is in RET(1, c) on the empty stack; visit_of_ret1: RET(1, v) on the empty stack is two steps before the head stands in B on a blank cell with blank tape on its left and v on its right.

lean/ModelWords.lean

The tape moves of the model

Every transition of the model is one of these moves, each proved for every repeat count and any tape around it: the strides of the returning head (stride, stride1, stride_q2), the turns at a frame (ret1_fill, ret1_split), the turn on a zero and the run to the right (z_turn, a_run), the calls (call_cb, call_c; b_eat) and the halt: L 0 1 0 C>1 R halts on its fourth step (halt_pattern).

lean/Model.lean (written by py/model_lean.py)

Words do not always lead to words: a word that halts

Started on the visit of the 19-token word b c c c b b b b c c b b b b b b c b c (b = 110, c = 111; head in B on the blank cell left of it) the machine halts on its 6,323rd step, evaluated by Lean's kernel. This refutes the earlier conjecture that every word over b and c leads to such a word again (it had been checked on 81,918 words of at most 16 tokens). The configuration is not one of the run from blank tape.

haltingWord_halts, word_can_halt, lean/HaltingWord.lean

Where a halt can come from

If the machine halts on its third step from a configuration, that configuration is one of: D with 0 two left, 1 left, 0 under the head. So a run can reach a halt only through one of these patterns, and a proof that the machine never halts only has to rule them out. The proof splits on the state and the five cells around the head.

tools/c/tmrun.c (at D L), README

The tape is a word over two tokens

With b = 110 and c = 111: at each of the 74,198 moments in the first 2·1010 steps when the head stands in B on the blank cell left of the tape, the whole tape is a word over b and c (the longest has 83,005 tokens). The periodic block is a = c b b.

c/wcheck.c

A visit is a recursion on the word

The next word is b V(X) for w = b X, with V(b X) = c c X, V(c c b c^n b Y) = c b c c b^n V(Y), V(c b c^n b Y) = c b b b^n V(Y). These rules give the next word at 2,239 of 2,361 visits up to 3·107 steps and never a wrong one; at the other 122 the rest starts with c c c, a case not decoded. These are special cases of the stack model below.

py/words.py run 3e7

The complete recursion: a stack model

During a visit the tape is a stack of frames 1^n 0^z followed by one of RET(q, v) (the head coming back, 1^(q-1) C>1 and the word v), Z(v) (C on the last zero of the top frame) or CALL(m, X) (1^m B> and the word X); twelve transitions with exact step counts (README, py/model.py). Each time the returning head finds three zeros in the top frame it runs a whole visit on the word to the right: visits inside visits. The model agrees with direct runs of the machine: the next word and the step count of the visit on each of the 131,070 words of at most 16 tokens, and the word and step number of each of the 74,198 visits of the first 2·1010 steps from blank tape (byte-identical output of c/dvis.c and c/wrun.c dump).

py/model.py all 17, c/wrun.c dump 74198 against c/dvis.c 2e10

Where the halt sits in the model

The machine halts exactly when the head returns in phase (RET(1, v)) to a top frame of one 1 and one 0 (tape 0 1 0 C>1). As long as no call runs off the right end of the word, every frame has a number of zeros that is 2 mod 3 and this cannot happen; the model is then a recursion on words alone, F(c^a b X) = b^(a+1) T(X) for the visit and T(c^j b Y) = c F^j(c Y) for the call (j visits inside the visit). Only a visit that reaches the right end brings frames out of phase.

Short words: the first halting ones have 19 tokens

The first visit on every word of at most 18 tokens (524,286 words) ends with a word again. Two words of 19 tokens halt, one of 20, five of 21, eleven of 22. Of 20,000 random words of 1,000 tokens, each run for 300 visits, 35 halted, among 20,230 visits that reached the right end; of 3,000 random words of 10,000 tokens, 1,000 visits each, 5 halted, among 1,287.

What happens in those 2,097,152 visits

The right end was reached in 31 visits (the latest: 113,145, 118,809, 231,897, 232,177 and 1,095,353), each followed by a cascade of tens of thousands of out-of-phase transitions; the head came back twelve times to one of seven frames with a single 1, always with 2, 5 or 8 zeros (a halt needs 1).

By the transition table the machine halts exactly when C reads a 1 and the three cells to its left are 0 1 0. In 2·1010 steps the run was one cell from this 12,171 times (C reads 1, D reads 0, B reads 1), each time with a 1 in place of the first 0.

Regular-language deciders tried (F11)

None of these closes: each reaches a halting configuration in its abstraction, which says nothing about whether the machine halts. n-gram CPS (tools/c/ngramcps.c), equal widths on both sides: a halting local configuration is reached with plain up to width 20, history 1 up to width 16, history 2 up to width 15, history 3 up to width 14, LRU history up to width 5 (larger widths ran out of the 150 s or 60 million configuration budget). n-gram CPS with every cell tagged by its position mod p: fails for p = 2 up to width 16, p = 3 up to width 16, p = 4 up to width 16, p = 6 up to width 16. n-gram CPS where each side also carries the state of a small automaton that has read that side from the far end (every automaton with up to 3 states on both sides, or 4 states on one side), with position mod 1..4 and widths 4, 6, 8: no closed set without a halt in all 120 parameter sets. Run-length windows (the K nearest runs of equal macro cells on each side, run lengths kept exactly below T and mod M above): fails for all 72 parameter sets (cells of 1 to 4 bits, K = 2, 4, 6, T = 2, 4, M = 1, 2, 4). Two-automaton closed tape language by SAT (a deterministic automaton reading each side inward, n states each, CaDiCaL): no solution for n = 2..8; n = 9 not finished in 150 s.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: fixed/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.539). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

It probably halts, far beyond any run

Visits that reach the right end keep coming at about 1.5 per doubling of the number of visits. In the run the returning head met a frame with a single 1 in 4 of the 31 such visits (12 meetings); on random words of 10,000 tokens there are about as many such meetings per right-end visit (583 in 1,287) and one meeting in a hundred is a halt (5 of 583; for words of 1,000 tokens 35 of 2,959). The words of the run look random behind the periodic block (in one long visit the token statistics of 1.4·108 calls matched fair coin flips to three digits; the visits inside a visit form a critical branching process). At these rates a right-end visit halts with probability about 0.4%, and a halt becomes likely only around visit 2150 or later, when the tape would hold about 2150 tokens. The events of one deterministic run are not independent trials, so this is an estimate only.

README

No shortcut to the halt in sight

A dedicated pass tried to skip whole cascades and reach the halt. The words of the run are repetitive but not simple: the greedy LZ77 parsing, a lower bound for every grammar that produces the word and so for every Hashlife-style memo of shared sub-words, has 164 phrases at visit 210, 4,988 at 218 and 13,839 at 220 (1,060,886 tokens). Extrapolated to visit 2150 this is about 2100 phrases on a power law and about 233 on a power of log V; ten doublings cannot tell which. Even a working accelerator that reached visit 240 would meet a halt with a chance of only about 10% by the estimate above. No accelerator was found.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. In the stack model the halt is one explicit case, a returning head meeting a frame of one 1 and one 0, which can only arise in the cascade after a visit reaches the right end of the tape. No invariant excludes it (words over the two tokens can halt), and a run long enough to see it is out of reach if the estimate of about 2150 visits is right.
