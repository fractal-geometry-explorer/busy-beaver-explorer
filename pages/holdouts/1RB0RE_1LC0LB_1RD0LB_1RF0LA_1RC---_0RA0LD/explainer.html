Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0RE_1LC0LB_1RD0LB_1RF0LA_1RC---_0RA0LD

Nobody knows, but the run is now followed exactly for 30 epochs: a small engine eats a stack of identical half-units in one of three exact cycles while a block grows one word per round trip; when the stack is gone the block becomes the next stack, 8.72 to 8.85 times larger. What is missing is a proof that every epoch, for every residue of its size, ends the same way.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in E reading 1. E is entered only from A1 (writes 0, moves R): A>1 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The structure

Epochs: a stack of units becomes a block, the block becomes the next stack

The head sweeps from end to end. At every visit to the right end the tape has three parts:

partcellswhat it is
engine P71–222a short, ever-changing word at the left end
stack U^N40 NN copies of the unit U = (10101010101010101011)^2
block V^m10 mm copies of V = 1101110101, between 1101110 and the fixed tail 1101110010110

Within an epoch the stack shrinks: about every 23.5 round trips the unit at its right end is used up, while the block grows by about 1.5 words per round trip, so each 40-cell unit turns into about 35 words, 350 cells. When the stack is empty the block is converted, 4 words per unit, into the next stack, over about a hundred round trips. So the epochs grow by a factor of about 8.8:

Exact

Three engine cycles

Cut every round trip just after the leftward sweep has crossed the stack. From there the engine part depends only on the engine and the first few half-units of the stack, and the rest only lengthens the block by one word. The symbolic simulator proves both parts for every count, and the engine turns out to be periodic: it falls into one of three cycles.

The units are eaten at the left end of the stack. When the stack is gone, one sweep complements the block, and about 30 round trips later it is regrouped in pairs of words into the next stack, leaving a short junction.

Halting

Where a halt would have to come from

E1 is the only halting transition and E is entered only from A on a 1 (writing 0, moving right): the machine halts exactly when A reads two 1s in a row. The stack and the block are crossed by the shift rules above, which never do that; so a halt would have to come from the engine at the left end, from the right end, or from a conversion. In the first 109 steps E is entered 101,661,462 times and reads 0 every time; no halt in 4·1010 steps.

Because the engine stays short (at most 222 cells seen), it has finitely many possible contents, and the rest of the tape is described by two counters N and m. That is the shape of a provable statement: enumerate the engine's contents at the visits to the right end by symbolic simulation with N and m as variables, and check that the set closes up without a halt. It has not been done yet: the engine took 2,322 different values in the first 2,521 visits.

Epochs

Within an epoch one unit disappears about every 23.5 round trips (the units are in fact eaten at the left end of the stack; see the exact cycles) and m grows by about 1.5 per round trip, so a unit yields about 35 copies of V. When N reaches 0 the block of V's is turned into the next stack, 4 V's (40 cells) per unit, over about a hundred round trips. py/decide/decide.py, py/decide/fs.py
Exact round trips: three engine cycles

The symbolic simulator py/decide/fs.py (blocks with linear-form counts, shift rules found by one-copy simulation) splits each round trip into an engine lemma (engine and stack, symbolic in the stack size, re-run at its threshold) and a right-part lemma (symbolic in every block count). Within an epoch the engine falls into one of three cycles: 1,744 round trips eating 100 half-units 10101010101010101110, 704 eating 40, or 2,336 eating 132; every round trip adds exactly one word 1011101110 to the block.

Heuristic
py/decide/epochsim30.log
The epoch map is Collatz-like

n' = (R/2C)·n + O(1), where R/C is the cycle of the epoch; the cycle of the next epoch and its junction (01000, 0001000101000 or 1010101010001000101000, then (1010001000)^j with j from 4 to 631) depend on residues of n modulo the consumption of a period and on parities. In epochs 3 to 29 the cycles go A A B B A A B B A A A B B B A A B A A C A B A B B A (A = 1,744/100, B = 704/40, C = 2,336/132) with no visible pattern.

Heuristic
py/decide/bfs_partial.log
A closure search did not close

py/decide/bfs.py follows epoch classes symbolically (every residue of the stack size, the block size up to parity). A partial run handled 121 transitions and 118 classes without a halt, but the queue grew faster than it was processed, and it used an earlier, unsound threshold for the engine lemmas, so it proves nothing.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The epochs are followed exactly, but a proof needs every epoch class: show that the classes (engine content at the stack end, residue of the stack size, junction, parity of the block) form a finite closed set under the epoch map with no halting transition. The map is affine per residue class and the next class depends on residues, so this may be a Collatz-type problem; the first search did not close.
