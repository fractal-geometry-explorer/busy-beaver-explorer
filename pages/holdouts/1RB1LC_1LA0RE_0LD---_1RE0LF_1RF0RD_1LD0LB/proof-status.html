OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Left of the start the tape is a list of blocks of a = 10 separated by s = 01100, with one junction cell j somewhere. Lean proves three kinds of rounds, each for all block lengths: the junction in the first block moves a word over (two more on the left, one fewer on the right); the junction in a later block makes the first block pay one word, the block before the junction gain three and the block after it lose one, the blocks in between being crossed unchanged (proved with up to two blocks between); and with no junction, the first block pays one word into a run of 1001100 words at the right end. The second kind is the tripling: a block of c words that the junction passes comes back as 3c plus a few. Jumping over these rounds, the run is followed exactly (checked against the machine to 1011 steps) to 1012 steps without a halt. What remains is the bounded work where the junction meets a separator or the right end.

An accelerated simulator, checked against the machine

c/m5fast.c applies the three kinds of rounds in one stroke wherever the tape has their form (with any number of blocks between; the step counts follow the same pattern), and simulates the rest. Run beside the plain machine it agrees in tape and state at all 13,356 records in D on the left (every jump included) up to 1.015·1011 steps.

No halt to 1012 steps

From blank tape c/m5fast reaches 1012 steps without a halt (26 minutes; the visited tape is then about 2·106 cells wide). The rounds it jumps over are the long ones; the steps it still simulates are those where the junction crosses a separator or meets the tail, a bounded number of rounds each, every one costing a sweep of the tape.

Why the blocks triple

While the junction sits in a block after a separator, every round moves one word from the block after the junction to the block before it and adds two more there (b → b+3, c → c−1). So a block of c words that the junction passes leaves 3c words plus what was there before, which matches the pairs seen earlier (3, 11 → 9, 41 → 35, 123, ...); the first block pays for it, one word a round. What happens when the junction reaches a separator, or the first block runs out, is still simulated, not decoded.

open Open

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The only halting transition is C reading 1; none of the proved rounds can halt, so a halt could only come in the short stretches where the junction crosses a separator, meets the tail, or the first block runs out.
