Nobody knows whether this six-state machine halts from blank tape. This page records what is established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

The tape is two binary counters written in short words. A counter at the left end, with digits 1010 (0) and 1000 (1), counts through all its values once per round; between two of its increments the head goes right and decrements a second counter whose digits are the words 1100 (1) and 1010 1010 (0); each borrow of the second counter moves one word 110 from one run of them to another. When the first run is used up the configuration changes in a way that is not yet understood.

Status as of 19 November 2026.

In short

Decided by an outside Rocq proof: it never halts. Busycoq pull request 103 (Alex Havrilla of DeepMind, 2026-11-19) proves this in Rocq, and we compiled it here with Coq 8.20.1 on busycoq's BB6 branch: it uses no axioms (Print Assumptions), and its transition table is this machine. It is their proof, on busycoq's definitions of a machine and of halting, and busycoq's maintainer has not yet reviewed it. Everything else on this page is what this repository found on its own, which by itself leaves the question open.

The question is open. No halt in the first 1010 steps (C simulator). The regular rounds follow an exact law, checked on all 39 of them; the phase changes between runs of regular rounds (24 seen) have no rule yet, and a halt (F reading a 1) could only come from one of them.

The left counter: increment and overflow, for every length

Digits of four cells, 1 0 1 0 = 0 and 1 0 0 0 = 1, least significant on the right, followed by 1 0, with the head in E on the 0 after it. incr: L 1010 (1000)^n 1 E>0 R reaches L 1000 (1010)^n 1 0 C> R in 10n + 7 steps, for every n, L and R. overflow: from 0^inf (1000)^n 1 E>0 R the counter becomes n + 1 zero digits, 0^inf (1010)^(n+1) 1 0 C> R, in 10n + 13 steps (the head visits a new cell on the left in state F on the way).

The round law, on all 39 regular rounds up to 1010 steps

At the visits of the head, in state F, to the leftmost cell, the tape right of it reads, in words a = 1100, b = 110, c = 10, c^s H X_1 bb X_2 bb ... bb X_w b^m cc0 b^j, with a header H (ab, ababcc or abccbcc) and right-counter digits X = a (1) or cc (0), X_1 least significant, value v. With D = 2s/2 and t the number of borrows of D decrements from v (t = (D − v − 1) div 2w + 1), a round with m ≥ t (m > t when H is not ab) goes to (s + 2, H, v − D mod 2w, m − t, j + t). All 39 such rounds from step 1,756 to 1010 follow it exactly; within a phase t doubles, which is the doubling map (m, j) → (m − j − k, 2j + k) seen before.

The phase changes, and the question

When m is too small for the next round (m < t) the round ends differently: from H = ab the left counter loses two digits (s − 4) and the header becomes ababcc or abccbcc (four times it stays ab with m + j growing by 4); from those headers the next visit has s + 2, header ab, one more right digit and m + j + 1. The new digits are not yet predicted. Whether the machine halts is open.
