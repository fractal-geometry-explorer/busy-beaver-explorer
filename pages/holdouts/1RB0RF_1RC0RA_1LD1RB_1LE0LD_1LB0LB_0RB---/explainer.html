The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from A1 (writes 0, moves R): A>1 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.071).

The structure

At every record, a word

This machine and 1RB1RF_1RC0RA_1LD1RB_1LE0LD_0RA0LB_0RB--- differ only in A1 and E0, and they behave the same way. Each time the head reaches a new cell on the right (a record), the tape is a word: blocks of 1s separated by single 0s, ending in 1^n B>0. For this machine, the word starts at the left end of the visited tape. The blocks are odd, except that the first may be 2.

From one record to the next the last block grows by 2. The rest of the word changes by one small, width-preserving rewrite, at a place that moves one block to the left per record. The rewrites seen are 3 1 1 → 1 1 3, b → (b−2) 1, 5 1 1 → 3 1 3, 3 3 3 1 1 1 → 1 1 3 5 3 and a few others. The time between records tends to double. In the first 2·1010 steps the last block reaches 51 (record at step 1.57·1010).

Between records

Two counters

Right after a record, state D erases the last block from right to left. Only a single marker 1 remains, n+1 cells out. This is the Lean theorem rec_erase: W 0 1^3 0 1^(2k+1) B>0 0 becomes W 0 1 0 1 0 1 1 C> 0^(2k) 1 (the head on the first of the 2k 0s) in 2k+11 steps. The head then zigzags. Going left, it turns the single-1 blocks at the end of the word into units 1 1 0 0. Going right, B sweeps them back, turning 10 into 01 and 01 into 11 (b_sweep10, b_sweep01). Meanwhile the empty stretch fills with gaps 1 0^odd.

Between two records of 1RB0RF_1RC0RA_1LD1RB_1LE0LD_1LB0LB_0RB---, the turning points follow a ruler sequence on both sides of a fixed cell. On the right they go 88, 90, 88, 92, 88, 90, 88, 94, …. On the left they dig 55, 65, 41, 65, 55, 65, 45, … cells deep into the word. This is the signature of two binary counters running against each other: one written in the end of the word, one in the gaps. A full count takes about 2n/2 steps, and it ends at the next record.

Where it could stop

Rebuilds and F events

F is reached only from A reading a 1, and F halts on a 1. So a halt needs A to read 1 1. In the runs, A reads a 1 only when the moving rewrite reaches the left end of the word. There the machine rebuilds the left end. Some of these rebuilds also extend the word, and the last block starts again small (a restart). Records during a rebuild need not be words, and the F events come in bursts there. In the first 2·1010 steps there are 133 F events, and each is followed by a 0.

Rebuilds are rare. For 1RB0RF_1RC0RA_1LD1RB_1LE0LD_1LB0LB_0RB--- the restarts come at about 5·103, 1.4·104, 3.1·105, 1.5·107, 9.7·107 and 7.0·109 steps. They are rare because the rewrite moves one block per record and records come ever more slowly, so direct simulation sees only a handful. Deciding the machine needs the exact record-to-record map on words, and from it the map of the left-end rebuilds. Neither is written down yet.

Status: open. Lean has the halting configurations, no halt in 107 steps, and three shift rules of the sweeps. The record structure is observed in direct runs, not proved.
