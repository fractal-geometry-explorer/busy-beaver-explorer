1RB0LA_0LC0RA_1LA1LD_1LC0LE_1RF0LF_0RB---

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The tape is a sequence of domains, each a periodic pattern (mostly built from 10, 0111 and blocks of 1s), separated by walls that barely move. At the left end a block of 1s grows by about two cells per visit of the head until it is frozen into a domain of 10s, and a new block starts. The right end grows by one cell at rare events (62 in 3·109 steps). No macro map is known.

The halting configurations

The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (F1); the generated statement covers every configuration.

Where a halt can come from

If the machine halts on its third step from a configuration, that configuration is one of: D with 1 two left, 1 left, 1 under the head. So a run can reach a halt only through one of these patterns, and a proof that the machine never halts only has to rule them out. The proof splits on the state and the five cells around the head.

Domains with frozen walls

In the defect picture (period 4) the tape is cut into regions by vertical, i.e. stationary, walls; new regions are born at the left end with lengths that grow from one to the next.

the picture pages/holdouts/1RB0LA_0LC0RA_1LA1LD_1LC0LE_1RF0LF_0RB---/defects.png

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.512). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.09).
