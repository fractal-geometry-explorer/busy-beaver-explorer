OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 13 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The machine is a base-5 counter, incremented once per sweep, whose digits drift and collide; Lean proves the counting rule for all configurations, and an exact simulator of that structure finds no halt in the first 1017 steps. Halting needs a crowded collision that the run has never produced, and nothing proves it never will.

The piece rules of a visit

Nine rules, each for all counts and all surrounding tape: the crossing of the left gap, the carry over a d, the turn at a, b, c or e, the return over a gap and over a passed d, and the growth at the left end.

start, carry, bounce_a … left_end, lean/Pieces.lean

One visit is one base-5 increment

From a sample configuration 0 0 W^g X₁ W^g₁ … Xₖ W^gₖ T with digits a, b, c, d, e (values 1, 2, 3, 4, 0), whenever the increment bump is defined (the carry stops at a digit with a nonempty gap after it), the machine reaches the sample configuration of the incremented counter in exactly 12g + 11 + cost steps; iterated in run_sound.

visit, run_sound, lean/Counter.lean

The merge of two adjacent e

The visit that meets two e side by side, the place where the four machines of the family differ: the pair becomes one digit d (value 4), three W further right.

merge_ee, lean/Merge.lean

Collisions are where it could halt

99.990% of the visits are plain increments. The others are overflows (all digits d) and collisions of a digit with the next. Hand-made tapes with crowded collisions reach A>0 1 1 and halt; the run has met only isolated collisions so far.
