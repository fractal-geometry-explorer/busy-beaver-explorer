What is known about 1RB---_1RC1RF_1LD1RA_1LB0LE_1LF1LF_0RB0LD

1RB---_1RC1RF_1LD1RA_1LB0LE_1LF1LF_0RB0LD

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The machine is a base-4 counter whose digits are defects in a periodic background. Lean proves its rounds for all block lengths; the collisions of digits, which happen because the blocks between them run out at a critical rate, are only described empirically, and a tape of the same shape is proved to halt, so a proof has to follow the block lengths exactly.

Other proofs and outside reports

Community label

In the community's BB(6) tracker the machine's class is Skelet 1-like Class 1. These are labels read off the run by others, not results.

The halting configurations

The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (A1); the generated statement covers every configuration.

step_none_iff, lean/Blank.lean

No halt in the first 10,000 steps

The list simulator of the shared library, proved to agree with the machine's semantics, runs 10,000 steps from blank tape without a halt, evaluated by Lean's kernel.

blank_runs_kernel, lean/Blank.lean

The counter's rounds

Seven rounds from the head in state D at the left end to the next such moment, for all block lengths: the first digit going q→r (its block loses a period), r→s, p→q; a carry into the second digit (q, r or p); and the overflow of a one-digit counter into END, which creates a new first digit.

round_q … overflow, lean/Rules.lean (generated by py/rules.py)

The four sweeps

The pass to the right (state B) turns a block of the background into 1s; the pass back writes the background again in one of three phases, by its state: D writes 010, E writes 001, F writes 100. For all block lengths. The state of the pass back is how a carry (or its absence) travels to the left.

sweep_right, sweep_left_D, sweep_left_E, sweep_left_F, lean/Rules.lean

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: fixed/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.547). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

What an overflow does to the blocks

Between two overflows with the same digits and no collision, the block before END loses 1 period and the j-th block from the right loses 4j (1, 4, 16, 64, 256 at the overflows of steps 1.6·107 to 4.4·107). The block before END went 8, 7, …, 1 and END then absorbed the last digit over six overflows (clusters 1,1,1 → 3,1 → 1,0,1 → 3 → 1,0 → 2 → 1), during which no new digit is made and the first block keeps growing; the next block before END went 42, 41, …, 29 by 1010 steps and is at 9 after 3.16·1015.

py/counter.py, py/far.py

open Open

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. No halt in 3.16·1015 steps. Missing: the rules of every collision (the compound defects seen so far form a small set) and of END absorbing a digit, as local rules of the two passes, and an invariant on the block lengths that keeps the digits from wrecking END; the blocks are spent at a critical rate, so the invariant has to be exact. The next absorption of a digit by END (the block before it is at 9 and loses 1 per overflow) is due around 1016–1017 steps, within reach of py/far.py in a few hours.
