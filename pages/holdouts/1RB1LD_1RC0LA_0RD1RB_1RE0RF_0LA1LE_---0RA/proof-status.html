OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 13 November 2026.

In short

Open. The tape is a block (100)^n followed by blocks of ones separated by 00, almost all of length 6j+1 (7, 13, 19, ...) and grouped by length: a counter with one digit per length, rewritten as the head sweeps the tape. The rule of one sweep has not been extracted, so there is no map to iterate and no heuristic. Direct simulation: no halt in 1011 steps.

In the community's BB(6) tracker the machine's class is Chaotic Bell Class 1. These are labels read off the run by others, not results.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.514). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.065).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

No halt in the first 3.07×1014 steps of an accelerated run

Observed, not proved: the accelerated simulator rsim.py ran 3.07×1014 steps from blank tape without reaching a halting transition. Its tape, state and head position were equal to those of a plain C simulator at 6 checkpoints, the last at 1.01×1010 steps. Of these steps, the part after the last checkpoint rests on the simulator alone, including 1,600 trip-level jumps: a jump applies many identical round trips at once after seeing the same tape shape recur with constant count changes, which is validated against direct simulation but not proved.
