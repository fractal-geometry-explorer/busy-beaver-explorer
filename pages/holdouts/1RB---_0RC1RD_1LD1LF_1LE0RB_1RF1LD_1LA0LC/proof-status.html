What is known about 1RB---_0RC1RD_1LD1LF_1LE0RB_1RF1LD_1LA0LC

1RB---_0RC1RD_1LD1LF_1LE0RB_1RF1LD_1LA0LC

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. The head sweeps across the whole tape and back, and each sweep rewrites the tape cell by cell, so the tape grows by a few cells at each end per sweep (square-root growth on both sides). What the sweeps do to the lengths of the blocks looks irregular. This page lists what has been established, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Lean proves where and how a halt would have to happen (an isolated 0 in front of B, or two 0s in front of E) and the sweep rules for any length; the C simulator finds no halt in the first 1011 steps, and at every sweep end so far every block of 1s has odd length and every 0 is isolated. No invariant that rules the halt out is known.

The halting configurations

The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (A1); the generated statement covers every configuration.

step_none_iff, lean/Blank.lean

How a halt comes about

A is entered only from F reading 0, so the machine halts exactly when F reads a 0 with a 1 on its left (1 F>0). F is entered only from C reading 1 and E reading 0, and the two short ways in are 1 B>0 1 (B meets an isolated 0; halt 4 steps later) and E>0 0 (E meets two 0s; halt 3 steps later).

enter_A, enter_F, halt_F, halt_B, halt_E, lean/Halt.lean

The sweep rules

For every n and every surrounding tape: moving right, B turns (1 1)^n into (1 0)^n in 2n steps; moving left, F does the same to a solid block; D and E, moving left, fill an alternating stretch (0 1)^n or (1 0)^n back into solid 1s.

bsweep, fsweep, dfill, efill, lean/Rules.lean

No halt in the first 1011 steps

The C simulator (tools/c/tm.h) runs 1011 steps without a halt; the tape then spans 454,373 cells.

Odd blocks and isolated 0s at every sweep end

At every moment in the first 109 steps at which the head reaches a new cell on either side, every run of 1s on the tape (except the outermost ones and the runs at and next to the head) has odd length, and every run of 0s has length 1.

c/runhist.c

F never reads an isolated 0

F reads a 0 about 4.8 million times in the first 108 steps and every time the cell to its left is also 0, so A reads 0 and the run goes on. A halt is exactly the first time this fails.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: sqrt/sqrt

Both ends of the visited tape grow like the square root of the step count (fitted exponents 0.501 and 0.499): the head crosses the whole tape every sweep and adds a few cells at each end.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The odd-block shape is the natural candidate for an invariant, but it is not closed under single steps as it stands: the leftward fill carries a pair of 0s along behind the head, so a proof needs a finer language (window CTL up to 7 cells and n-gram CPS (widths up to 9, see the README for the larger runs) fail).
