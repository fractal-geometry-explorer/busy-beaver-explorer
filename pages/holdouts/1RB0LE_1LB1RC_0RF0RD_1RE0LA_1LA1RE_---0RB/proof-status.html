What is known about 1RB0LE_1LB1RC_0RF0RD_1RE0LA_1LA1RE_---0RB

1RB0LE_1LB1RC_0RF0RD_1RE0LA_1LA1RE_---0RB

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

Open, probably never halting. The tape is always a row of blocks of 1s separated by 00, and the machine treats it as a stack. An exact model of what each sweep does, proved in Lean for all block sizes, shows: from P(n) = E> 00 1^n 01 an odd n leads straight to P((3n+7)/2); an even n starts a recursion that halves the number and pushes blocks on the stack, and ends in one of three ways: a halt (a block pattern (q, r) = (2, 2)), a provable infinite loop ((q, r) = (2, 3)), or a new P(n′), sometimes enormously larger. Lean checks that the run from blank tape survives 80 P-rounds by the kernel (10100 steps) and 650 with native_decide (109,700 steps), with no halt and no loop. For a random start of d digits the chance that the next round halts falls quickly with d, so a halt later on looks unlikely; nothing proves it.

The block model, for every stack and block size

With E(S, p, q, r) = 1^S_k 00 … 1^S_1 00 1^p 00 1^q 0 1^r E> 0 (head at the right end, S a stack of blocks) and P(n) = E> 00 1^n 01: q ≥ 4 gives (p+2, q−2, r+1) in 8q+4r−9 steps (and j such rounds at once, by induction); q = 3 gives (p+2, 1, r+1) in 4r+15; q = 2 and r = 1 gives (p+2, 3, 1) in 31; q = 2 and r = 2 halts after 20 steps; q = 2 and r ≥ 3 pushes p+2 and continues with (1, r−2, 1) in 8r; q = 1 merges p, q, r into a block of p+r+4 and pops the stack in 8r+22 (with an empty stack: E([], 0, p+r+4, 1), which reaches P(p+r+4) in 3(p+r+3)+4 more); P(n) goes to E([], 2, n−2, 2) in 5n−6 steps (n ≥ 4). Proved from 12 local rules generated by bbtools.symtm and two crossing lemmas by induction (the head reads one cell beyond each copy, which symtm cannot cross by itself).

The two endings

From (p, 2, 3) the machine pushes p+2, merges back to (p+2, 6, 1) and returns to (p+6, 2, 3) in 2 sweeps: once (q, r) = (2, 3) occurs it never halts (a block-level translated cycler). (q, r) = (2, 2) halts after 21 steps. Nothing else ends a round.

Halting gets rarer as n grows

Fraction of random starts P(n) whose next round halts or loops forever, by number of digits of n: 6 digits: 4.4% halt, 1.5% loop; 8 digits: 2.7%, 0.8%; 10 digits: 2.0%, 0.6%; 12 digits: 1.0%, 0.35%; 15 digits: 0.55%, 0.3%; 20 digits: 0.25%, 0.03% (4,000 random starts each). The fraction drops quickly with the size of n, and n has thousands of digits after 650 rounds, so a halt after that looks very unlikely. No probability model behind this is worked out yet.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. It halts if and only if some round of the orbit from P(5) reaches (q, r) = (2, 2); it provably never halts if one first reaches (2, 3).
