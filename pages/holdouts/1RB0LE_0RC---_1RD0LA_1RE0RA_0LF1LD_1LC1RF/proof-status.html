Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The head shuttles out from the left end over the background word 100110001001, turning back or passing at each defect depending on its shape (so the defects act as the digits of a counter), and rewriting the background as (01)^4 1^4 going right and back going left (proved in Lean). Defects of the background move as gliders from a gun at the left end to a few nearly stationary defects near the right end, which grows in rare bursts. No macro map is known.

Gliders, merges and a pile

Gliders move right, then slow down and merge; three or four nearly stationary defects sit within about 300 words of the right end. Around 2·108 steps the head, leaving the left end, turns at the first defect on most excursions and passes it on 3 of every 12 (pattern 000010001010, 1 = passes).

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.484). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.071).
