Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0RC_0RC1RC_0LD0RA_1LE0LF_0LA1LC_---1LA

Decided: it never haltsProved in this repository, checked by Lean and by Coq. The ledger below says what each part rests on.

It never halts, and Lean proves it. Two blocks of the tape trade places, about seven times longer each round; now and then the left block is poured into a third block at the far right. After 1.8·1015 steps the whole pattern repeats exactly, with only that third block longer each time.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from D1 (writes 0, moves L): 0 D>1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

Four levels

From six states to one number

The question "does this machine halt?" can be asked at four levels of description. Each level keeps less detail than the one above it, and each step down is a theorem saying that the answer is the same. The page walks down the ladder: the six-state machine on its tape; the tape read as blocks of repeated words at the moments state E reaches the left end; whole cycles of the long phases, each a rule that holds for every size of the blocks; and finally one number r, the length of the last block, which grows by 321,042,928 every period and never leads to a halt.

Level 1 to level 2

Seven-cell words on the left, three-cell words on the right

Look at the tape each time the head, in state E, reaches the left end of the visited tape: an event. The tape is then a block of seven-cell words 1011001, a short irregular zone, and then blocks of the three-cell word 011 separated by a few zeros. The last block, (011)^r, ends the tape.

Going right, the head shifts a block of three-cell words by one cell (3 steps a word). Going left, it crosses two words at a time (18 steps). So what happens at the end of a block depends only on whether its length is even or odd: every cycle rule the proof needed has the form "for all lengths of this parity".

The Lean proof works at this level with a symbolic executor: a program whose tape is a list of cells and blocks (w)^n, where n may be c + a x with x unknown. One move of the executor either crosses a whole block with a shift rule, or makes one ordinary step (opening a block only when its count is surely at least 1). A shift rule says: in state q, with the word u just behind the head, the head crosses k copies of a word w and leaves the word v behind it, in s steps, without looking at anything else. Each of the 31 shift rules is a Lean statement about the machine for every tape around those cells, checked by the kernel (shRTbl_ok, shLTbl_ok in lean/ExecData.lean), and the soundness theorem run_sound (lean/Exec.lean, kernel) says that whatever the executor does in some number of moves, the machine does, for every value of the unknowns. That is the theorem that makes level 2 answer the same as level 1.

The browser writes u, then n crossings' worth of copies of w, between twelve random cells on each side, puts the head in state q where the rule says, and runs the real machine for n·s steps. The rule holds if the head never reads a random cell, ends in state q at the far side, and the cells read u vn as the rule says. Words are written nearest cell first, as in the Lean file, so on the left of the head they appear reversed.
Level 2 to level 3

Collect, expand, drain

Three regular phases take almost all the time. p counts the seven-cell words, q or m the three-cell words of the middle block, r those of the last block.

phasetape when state E reaches the left endone cycle
collect0 (1011001)^p (101)^5 10 (011)^q 000 (011)^r, q even, r odd2 events: p + 2, q − 2
expand0 (1011001)^p 1011 0^5 110110011010 (101)^m 1000 (011)^r, m even, r odd4 events: p − 2, m + 14
drain0 (1011001)^p 1011 0^5 1101100110110 (011)^16 001101100110 (011)^r, r odd72 events: p − 52, r + 414

Collecting turns three-cell words into seven-cell words one for one; expanding gives seven three-cell words back for each seven-cell word. So each round the blocks are about seven times longer: about 600, 4,370, 30,750, 215,000, 1,506,000 words. Between the phases there are short irregular stretches where the separators collide, and trains of cars (011)^4 0 are built and taken apart. Usually the next round starts. Sometimes the stretch ends in the drain pattern: then the whole left block is poured into the last block (414 words of three for 52 words of seven), and the left part starts again from a small state, in front of a much longer last block.

At level 3 a whole cycle of a phase is one statement. A cycle rule is a template of the whole tape whose counts are b + 2x before and b' + 2x after, one x for each block that changes. It is proved for all values of the x by one run of the executor itself, without cycle rules, from the template before to the template after (ruleOK_of_run, kernel; the run is evaluated by native_decide: rule0_ok … rule11_ok in lean/ExecData.lean). The executor then applies a rule N times in one move, N as large as the shrinking counts allow; the induction over N is in Lean (rule_instN, kernel), so the choice of N is not trusted. Twelve rules were enough: eleven are collect or expand, plain or with inert trains in between, and one, rule 8, is the drain. Separately, lean/Phases.lean proves the plain collect and expand cycles by hand for any number of cycles, with the kernel only (collect2, collect_n, expand4, expand_n); the replay does not need them.

Every block that has a variable gets the same x here. The browser writes the template on a blank tape, puts the head on its first cell in state E, runs the real machine until state E has reached the left end the rule's number of times, and compares the tape with the template after. The drain (rule 8) takes about five million steps. lean/Phases.lean writes the blocks of three-cell words with the six-cell word 110110 and allows any count b + x, not only b + 2x.

Level 3 to level 4

The left part comes back to the same state

The left part of the tape never needs more of the last block than the parity of its length, and a drain adds an even number of words. After the second drain the left part reaches a state that it meets again, exactly, two drains later:

Events and steps computed by the simulators (py/closure/accel.py, py/replay/engine.py, verify/sim3.cpp, which agree); the Lean statement does not count them.

One period is two epochs, two drains. To prove that it goes on for ever, one period is run with r replaced by 65 + 2x, x unknown. Whenever a step would depend on the value of x the executor stops; it never had to. It ends in the same configuration with 65 + 321,042,928 + 2x, after 5,581,714 moves (period_run, lean/Period.lean, evaluated by native_decide). With run_pos this is the theorem grow_cyc: for every x, C(65 + 2x) reaches C(65 + 2(x + 160,521,464)) in at least one step. The run from blank tape reaches C(16,797,709) = C(65 + 2·8,398,822) (reach_cyc: 5,037,044 moves, lean/Arrive.lean). And the kernel proves the last step, nonhalting_of_growth_cycle in lean/Cycle.lean: if the run reaches some C(65 + 2x) and every C(65 + 2x) reaches a later one in at least one step, then the run from blank tape is at such a configuration at later and later steps, so it is still running at every step: the machine never halts.

Why only the parity of r matters

The claim behind the symbolic period can be watched on the real machine for small r: start from C(r) for two odd values of r and compare the two runs event by event. Up to the last block the two tapes are the same, and the last blocks keep their difference. Start one of them from an even r and the tapes part at once; C(r) with r even halts, at event 21,362,421 (computed, py/closure/classes.py).

The left part is the tape from the head to the start of the last block (011)^r; the plot shows its length (in cells) and the last block's count, event by event, for the first r. 3,000 events are about 1.5·108 steps for each run.

A narrow escape

Almost the same tape halts

The same node with other counts is not safe. With 61 words instead of 59 in the left block,

E> 0 (1011001)^61 1011 0^5 1101100110110 (011)^16 001101100110 (011)^r, r odd ≥ 65,

halts after 1,772,276 + 5,220 (r − 65) steps (computed by py/closure/classes.py, confirmed by plain simulation for r = 65, 67, 71). C(r) itself with r even halts too, much later, at event 21,362,421. So there is no simple description of "safe tapes" that contains the run: it is safe because it sits on one exact orbit. That is why the automatic deciders, which look for such descriptions, left this machine open, and why the proof follows the orbit itself.

The browser can run the halting neighbour: pick r and the number of seven-cell words, and the real machine runs from that tape.

With 59 words the browser stops after 2·107 steps; that the run from C(r) never halts for odd r ≥ 65 is the Lean theorem grow_cyc, not something a bounded run can show.
The bottom line

It never halts

It never halts.

Started on a blank tape, 1RB0RC_0RC1RC_0LD0RA_1LE0LF_0LA1LC_---1LA runs for ever. From step 1,818,198,163,340,705 on its run is the growth cycle: C(r) comes back as C(r + 321,042,928) after 116,320,154 arrivals of state E at the left end, and so on without end.

The Lean theorem is nonhalt : ¬ HaltsFromBlank in lean/Replay.lean, ledger level native_decide. What it rests on, exactly:

  • The Lean kernel checks the definitions of the machine, the soundness of the symbolic executor (run_sound, run_pos, rule_instN, ruleOK_of_run in lean/Exec.lean), the 31 shift rules (lean/ExecData.lean, each by rfl) and the last implication, nonhalting_of_growth_cycle (lean/Cycle.lean).
  • Fourteen evaluations by native_decide, which trust Lean's compiler as well as its kernel: the twelve cycle rules (rule0_ok … rule11_ok, from 422 to 201,007 executor moves each), the arrival from blank tape (arrive_run, 5,037,044 moves) and the period (period_run, 5,581,714 moves). #print axioms nonhalt lists propext, Quot.sound and these fourteen evaluations; there is no sorry.
  • Not used: any simulator outside Lean. The executor's strategy (which rule, how many times, how to re-bracket the tape) is not trusted; only its soundness is, and that is proved.
  • Not stated in Lean: the numbers of steps and events, such as the step 1,818,198,163,340,705 at which C(16,797,709) is first reached; Lean says only "reaches, in at least one step". Those counts come from py/closure/accel.py, py/replay/engine.py and verify/sim3.cpp, which agree (computed, not proved).

The short cycle rules can be checked by the kernel alone (decide +kernel was tried on rule 4, 422 moves, and rule 11, 3,255 moves); the drain rule and the two long runs have not been, so there is no kernel-only proof of nonhalt yet. A second proof of the same statement, in Coq on top of busycoq (coq/H65826.v, nonhalt : ~ halts tm c0), evaluates all its runs inside Coq's kernel with vm_compute and reports no axioms; the ledger lists it as kernel level.

A symbolic executor, proved sound

Tapes are lists of cells and blocks (w)^n with n = c + a0x0 + a1x1 + …, the x free. A move is a shift rule from a table (a block crossed at once, one or two copies at a time), or one machine step, and at each arrival of state E at the left end a re-bracketing of the tape and a cycle rule applied N times at once. Any step that would depend on a variable fails. Theorem: if the executor goes from S to S′ in k moves and its tables are valid, the machine goes from S to S′ for every value of the variables. The 31 shift rules are proved by evaluation in the kernel; the N-fold application of a cycle rule by induction.
