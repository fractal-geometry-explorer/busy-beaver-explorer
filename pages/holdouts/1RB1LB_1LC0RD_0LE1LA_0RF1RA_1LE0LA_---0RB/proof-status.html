What is known about 1RB1LB_1LC0RD_0LE1LA_0RF1RA_1LE0LA_---0RB

1RB1LB_1LC0RD_0LE1LA_0RF1RA_1LE0LA_---0RB

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 22 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. On single cells one round trip is a cellular automaton: c'[p] = 1 xor c[p] xor c[p+3] from the left end to the first 00, proved in Lean for every tape with no lone 1 before that point (lean/Trip.lean), walls included. So the tape is three interleaved copies of rule 102 and the only thing that can go wrong is a lone 1 before the turn (token mode). A fast simulator built on this reaches 5.2·1013 steps with no halt and no lone 1 after 8.5·106 steps. Earlier results: The sweep rule is proved: writing x = 111 and y = 110, one round trip of the head replaces each word by the XOR of it and its right neighbour (rule 102), in Lean for every length, with its Lucas closed form. A halt needs a 10 defect with y in front of it and only ys up to the next wall. Lean now proves what a deep sweep does at a defect: with x in front it is rule 102 on the whole line, with y in front the defect jumps to the end of the gap's first x block, and after a jump over m slots the next jump comes exactly m sweeps later. So a defect's whole life is one map on its gap, and the danger pattern is a jump with nothing to land on. Lean also proves which gaps can get there: under shielded sweeps exactly the rows of Pascal's triangle mod 2, and at the next jump exactly one rest C for each jump length L (word k is y iff k and L share no binary 1). The map does not rule that out by itself; a proof also needs to show that no new bulk 10 defect is born, which in the run has not happened since 8.5 million steps. No closed tape language has been found.

The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (F0); the generated statement covers every configuration.

Where a halt can come from

If the machine halts on its third step from a configuration, that configuration is one of: B with 1 under the head, 0 right, 0 two right. So a run can reach a halt only through one of these patterns, and a proof that the machine never halts only has to rule them out. The proof splits on the state and the five cells around the head.

halt_route, lean/Route.lean (written by py/route.py)

blank_runs_kernel, lean/Blank.lean

Shift rules over 3-cell words

Ten rules, each for every n and every surrounding tape: for example D turns (101)n into (110)n going right, B turns (110)n into (011)n, A runs left over (110)n turning it into (111)n.

lean/Rules.lean (from py/rules.py)

One round trip is rule 102, for every length

For every word string w ending in y and followed by a 0, started in B on its first cell: the right sweep turns x into 000 and y into 011, B on the 0 writes 1 and turns, and the left sweep (C or E remembering the word to the right) writes back word i as w[i] xor w[i+1], with y after the end. The last word becomes x and the 0 after the string a 1.

epoch, x102_getD, lean/Xor.lean

One round trip is a cellular automaton on cells

From the head on a new leftmost (blank) cell in A, with runs of at least two 1s each followed by one 0 up to a second 0 (that is, no lone 1 before the first 00): the cells from the start cell to the first 0 of the pair become c[p] == c[p+3] (= 1 xor c[p] xor c[p+3]) with 1 1 0 imagined after them, the second 0 becomes 1, nothing further right is read, and the head ends one cell left, in A, after 4(Q − lo) − 6z + 15 steps. The left sweep spends one step per cell, its state naming the three original cells to its right. This covers every round trip without token mode, walls included; the word rule of Xor.lean is the special case of clean words.

trip, sweep, lstep, out_ca3, ca3_getD, trip_steps, lean/Trip.lean

Where a lone 1 can come from

If no three neighbouring cells hold two 0s, the automaton writes 0 1 0 at p, p+1, p+2 only when the cells p … p+5 were 0 1 1 1 1 0. A lone 1 before the turn is the only thing a round trip cannot cross (token mode, where the halt lives).

iso_birth, lean/Trip.lean

Many round trips: Lucas' theorem

Iterating rule 102 with a fixed word after the end, word i after t sweeps is the XOR of the starting words i + k over the k ≤ t with C(t, k) odd, i.e. the binary digits of k and t − k disjoint. For a gap of n x's next to the fixed y, word j from the y is y iff C(t, j) is odd, so the whole gap is y first after 2b − 1 sweeps, 2b > n.

closed_form, allx, lean/XorLucas.lean

The danger pattern halts; an x in front shields the defect

B at the start of y 10 yn 0 halts for every n: the defect puts B in token mode, F crosses the y words turning each into 000, and F reads the wall's 0. With x before the defect, x 10 is the single run 11110, which B rewrites to 00011 and leaves at the start of the gap the ordinary way.

danger_halts, token_y, shield, lean/Danger.lean

The defect's two deep sweeps: shielded, or a jump

For all word strings and lengths. Shielded (x before the defect T): P x T G y 0 becomes x102(P, x) T x102(y G y, y) 1, the defect one slot further left; counting T as a y this is rule 102 on the whole line (x102_shielded). Jump (y before T): P y T yj xa+1 C y 0, with C empty or starting with y, becomes x102(P y, x) xj+a+1 T x102(C y, y) 1: the defect jumps to the last slot of the gap's first x block. With no x before the wall this is the danger pattern, which halts.

shielded, jump, x102_shielded, lean/Hop.lean

The jump clock

After a jump over m = j + a + 1 slots the words before the defect are Q y xm; each shielded sweep turns Q y xk into Q' y xk−1. So exactly m shielded sweeps follow and then the word before the defect is y again: the next jump (or merge, or the danger pattern) comes m sweeps later, whatever the words further left.

clock, lean/Hop.lean

Which gaps shielded sweeps can turn all y: Pascal rows

Read the gap with the defect as constant term, W = 1 + g0 z + … over GF(2). t shielded sweeps multiply it by (1 + z)t, and the first r coefficients of the result are all y iff they were those of mask t, the series whose coefficient k is 1 exactly when k and t share no binary 1 (it is (1 + z)−(t+1); Pascal's rule is the whole proof). mask has period 2b below 2b, so a gap of fewer than 2b words ever becomes all y iff it is one of the 2b series mask t, t < 2b: the rows of Pascal's triangle mod 2 found earlier (checked before in py/pascal_gaps.py for every gap up to 14 words), now proved for all lengths. The Lean file also proves that the shielded sweep of Hop.lean is multiplication by 1 + z on every coefficient the old gap determines (ser_shielded).

reach_ones, mask_period, pascal_reach, ser_shielded, lean/Gaps.lean

Which gaps a jump can make dangerous

At a jump over yj xa+1 with rest C (L = j + a + 2), the series at the next jump is F(W) = (1 + z)L C, and its first r coefficients are all y iff C agrees with mask L on its first r words: word k of C must be y exactly when k and L share no binary 1. So the run of y words at the next jump is one less than the length of the common prefix of C and mask L, and the danger pattern at the next jump (the run reaches the wall) needs C = mask L on every word: for each (j, a) exactly one gap, so at most about n2/2 of the 2n gaps of n words (190 of 1,048,576 for n = 20). ser_jump ties this to the words of jump in Hop.lean: the gap it leaves is (1 + z) C on every coefficient the old gap determines.

next_jump_run, Fj_ones, ser_jump, ser_rest, lean/Gaps.lean

The next-jump rule, against the real run

py/jump_gaps.py checks the agreement rule on 163,800 cases (every C of up to 12 words, L up to 40) and reads the real run to 1.2·107 steps: at all 10 jumps that have a successor in the same lifetime, the run at the next jump is the agreement with mask L minus one. The closest calls are at 2.53·106 steps, where C = yyxx is mask 6 on every word the old gap holds (the next run is 3 and the gap had grown to 7, so the words that entered at the wall decided it), and at 5.44·106 steps (agreement 4 of 5, run 3 where 4 would have halted). So the jump map pins down the dangerous gaps exactly, but the words fed in at the wall between jumps are not yet controlled.

Regular-language deciders tried (F11)

None of these closes: each reaches a halting configuration in its abstraction, which says nothing about whether the machine halts. n-gram CPS (tools/c/ngramcps.c), equal widths on both sides: a halting local configuration is reached with plain up to width 15, history 1 up to width 20, history 2 up to width 20, history 3 up to width 20, LRU history up to width 6 (larger widths ran out of the 150 s or 60 million configuration budget). n-gram CPS with every cell tagged by its position mod p: fails for p = 2 up to width 16, p = 3 up to width 16, p = 4 up to width 16, p = 6 up to width 16. n-gram CPS where each side also carries the state of a small automaton that has read that side from the far end (every automaton with up to 3 states on both sides, or 4 states on one side), with position mod 1..4 and widths 4, 6, 8: no closed set without a halt in all 120 parameter sets. Run-length windows (the K nearest runs of equal macro cells on each side, run lengths kept exactly below T and mod M above): fails for all 72 parameter sets (cells of 1 to 4 bits, K = 2, 4, 6, T = 2, 4, M = 1, 2, 4). Two-automaton closed tape language by SAT (a deterministic automaton reading each side inward, n states each, CaDiCaL): no solution for n = 2..8; n = 9 not finished in 150 s.

One sweep pair is the XOR rule

Write x = 111 and y = 110. For every word string w over {x, y} ending in y, up to 16 words (65,535 strings): started in B on its first cell the machine sweeps right without turning, rewriting each run 1m0 as 0m−111; started in C at the end of that it sweeps left without turning; the result is the word string v with vi = wi xor wi+1 (x = 0, y = 1), last word x. This is the linear rule 102 cellular automaton, so every word string can occur and the left string looks random.

py/xor_epoch.py, exhaustive per length, run with the machine's own table

Closed tape language searches (16 November 2026)

None closes; each reaches a halting configuration in its abstraction or runs out of budget. Abstract regular model checking over whole-tape automata, merging states by k-step Moore equivalence (forward, reversed, both), k = 1..6, forward from blank and backward from F reading 0. Run-length windows with lengths mod 3 (the F11 sweep tried M = 1, 2, 4 only): K = 2..16 nearest runs with exact lengths below 3 halt; exact lengths up to 4..8, or runs near the blank ends kept exact, exceed 5 to 30 million abstract configurations. The reason found: the bulk is a full shift (any word string), so windows of K runs grow like 2K, while the right end needs exact lengths.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.505). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.067).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

How a bulk 10 is born, in cells

Between two walls the 0s of one copy of rule 102 are multiplied by (1 + u) at each pass (u = three cells left), so after a plain pass their number is even, and a turn at either wall deletes one. A bulk 10 is born when a segment's 0s run out at a turn at its left wall; that needs, first, a trip whose turn ends a segment of y words only (0s every three cells) with a 111 wall on its left. The run has 5 such trips (at 92, 108, 1,904, 2,608 and 3,403 round trips); each is followed by the token-mode trips of one 10 lifetime, and there is none in the next 106 trips. The y-only segments that keep forming sit 10, 17, 24 and 53 cells from the right end, behind walls of 6, 9 or 18 cells, as part of the right end's periodic cycle.

c/trips.c (PRE lines), argument in the README

A defect's lifetime is one map on the gap

Read the gap with the defect as constant term, W = 1 + g0 z + … over GF(2). Shielded sweeps multiply W by 1 + z; a jump strips the leading run of ones and the run of zeros after it (L = j + a + 2 slots) and, with the clock, gives F(W) = (1 + z)L C at the next jump. The left words fix only the first jump. Every deep sweep of the real run to 1.2·107 steps (54 shielded, 10 jumps, 3 merges) follows the rules, the gap agrees with the model on every word the old gap determines (63 of 63), and all 10 jumps and merges after a first jump came on the clock. For random gaps the run of ones at a jump is geometric (py/fmap.py), so the map does not by itself prevent a halt; the real run had j = 0 or 1 at every jump.

c/turns.c, py/defect_life.py, py/fmap.py

A 10 defect is a pinned y in a rule-102 line

In the run to 9·106 steps, every sweep with an x before a bulk 10 defect (50 of them) moves the words on both sides by w'[i] = w[i−1] xor w[i] in the defect's frame, with the defect acting as a fixed y for the gap. So the word before the defect is a Lucas sum of the words left of it (checked for 36 sweeps). The other 9 sweeps are exactly those with y before the defect: the defect jumps (see the jump rule above). A halt needs such a sweep to meet an all-y gap; the two never coincided.

c/defect10.c, py/defect_frame.py

The string is rewritten every sweep

At successive visits of the head to the left end (steps 2,918,884 … 2,994,219) the left string is different each time, not shifted copies of one another, and the visited width grows by one or two cells per visit (2,366 → 2,395 in 29 visits). Read as words of 3 cells it is a string over 110 and 111.

Defects are walls emitted at the right end

Where the word alignment shifts (a run of 1s of length 0 or 1 mod 3, or a double 0) there is a wall between two XOR domains. A single wall survives each epoch and cycles z2 → 13k → 13k−3 → … → 13 → z2, drifting left. A right sweep stops at the first double-0 wall (B reads 0), so walls act like digits of a counter. Walls are created by a 5-phase cycle at the right end, which grows by 2 cells per cycle. A halt needs the token parser (state B) at 1 0 0: a single 1 (seen only within about 30 runs of the right end) followed by 110 words and then a double 0; it was not seen in 3·108 steps.

simulation, 16 November 2026 (project folder ctl-696/NOTES.md)

Halting needs a short gap that the XOR rule turns all y

Every left-visit tape up to 3·107 steps parses as words 111 and 110 with inserted defects 0 and 10. The halting read 1 0 0 needs the pattern 0 10 (110)* 0, which never occurs. In mutation tests on real tapes, random words between defects stay clear in 99 of 100 runs over 60 more left visits; every halting run, shrunk to its smallest change, alters the words W in a gap 10 W 0, which the XOR rule then turns into all 110 (for example 10 xyxyx 0 becomes 10 yyyyy 0), giving the pattern. So what keeps the machine from halting is a condition on the XOR orbit of these gaps, of any length, which a fixed-window closed tape language cannot express.

mutation tests, 16 November 2026 (project folder ctl-696/tools/mut*.py, trace.py)

No new 10 defect after 8.5 million steps

Reading the defects as particles with charge mod 3 (0 = 1, 10 = 2): a 10 in the bulk forms when two 0 walls with only 111 words between them meet and the word before the left wall is 110 (110 0 111…111 0; with 111 before the wall, such gaps are common and never merge), and it ends by cancelling with the next 0 wall. In the real run there are only four such lifetimes, the last ending at 8.5·106 steps, and none forms after that up to 1011 steps (21,812 tapes checked, no halt). While one lives, the words after it change by vi = wi−1 xor wi with 110 fed in from the left. They became all 110 131 times, and each time the word before the 10 was 111, so the halting pattern never formed. The trigger 110 0 111…111 0 was last seen at 4.07·106 steps in the same census.
