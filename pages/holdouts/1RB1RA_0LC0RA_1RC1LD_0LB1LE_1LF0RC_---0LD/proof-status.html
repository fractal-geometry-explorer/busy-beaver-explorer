Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The run is a sequence of rounds, each starting from W (100)k 10 (110)N 111 B> with a short word W. Lean proves the sweep rules the rounds are built from; a symbolic run in Python checks the inner period of a round for all sizes. The round map itself is found by simulation only, and no rule that decides it is known.

Other proofs and outside reports

An outside proof is waiting for review: it never halts

busycoq pull request 44 (Alk, 2026-11-05) contains a Rocq proof that this machine never halts (verify/M_1RB1RA_0LC0RA_1RC1LD_0LB1LE_1LF0RC_HHH0LD.v). As of 12 November 2026 it was open: not merged, not reviewed by the maintainer, and not compiled here unless the ledger below says so. The verdict on this page does not rely on it.

c/round.c

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.488). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.052).
