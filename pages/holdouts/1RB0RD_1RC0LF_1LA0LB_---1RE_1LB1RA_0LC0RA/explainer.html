Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0RD_1RC0LF_1LA0LB_---1RE_1LB1RA_0LC0RA

Decided: it never haltsProved in this repository, checked by Lean and by Coq. The ledger below says what each part rests on.

It never halts, and Lean proves it. Two long blocks on this machine's tape act like two buckets: at every visit of the left end a little is poured from the left one into the right one, which grows about twice as fast as the left one shrinks. When the left one is empty the right one takes its place. A short stretch at the right end, the tail, decides how much is poured; at the end of a round it is always one of 62 words, and from none of them does the machine stop.

Four levels

From six states to 1,144 classes

The question "does this machine halt?" can be asked at four levels of description. Each level keeps less detail than the one above it, and each step down is a theorem saying that the answer is the same. The page walks down the ladder: the six-state machine on its tape; the tape read as two long blocks of pairs 01 and a short tail, at the moments state A reaches the left end; whole periods of the tail's cycles, each a rule that holds for every size of the two blocks; and finally the ends of rounds, sorted into 1,144 classes that lead only into each other and never to the halting transition.

Level 1 to level 2

Two blocks and a tail

Seen at the moments when the head, in state A, arrives at the left end (an event), the tape is, for most of the run,

010 (01)^a 000 (01)^b T

Here (01)^a means the pair 01 written a times. The left block has a pairs, the big block has b pairs, and T, the tail, is a short word made of a few small blocks of pairs separated by 000 or 111. Example, step 9,932,715,748: a = 2,110, b = 56,419, and the tail has blocks of 4, 13, 16 and 20 pairs.

One visit of the left end: the head crosses the whole tape to the right and back, twice. On the way it takes exactly 3 pairs from the left block, and the tail changes a little: its small blocks shrink by 3 pairs each, a block that is used up merges into its neighbour, and from time to time a block is absorbed into the big block. In the long stretches the events come in pairs six steps apart, first 110 … and then 010 …, so one visit is two events. The five crossings of the long blocks in a visit are proved in Lean for blocks of any length, three pairs at a time, by the kernel alone (c_right6, a_left6, b_right6, b_left6, f_left6 in lean/Rules.lean).

The Lean proof works at this level with a symbolic executor (adapted from the one of 1RB0RC_0RC1RC_0LD0RA_1LE0LF_0LA1LC_---1LA): a program whose tape is a list of cells and blocks (w)^n, where n may be c + a x with x unknown. One move either crosses a whole block with a shift rule (in state q, with the word u just behind the head, k copies of w are crossed and the word v is left behind, in s steps), or makes one ordinary step. Each of the 15 shift rules is a statement about the machine for every tape around those cells, checked by the kernel (shRTbl_ok, shLTbl_ok in lean/ExecData.lean), and the soundness theorem run_sound (lean/Exec.lean, kernel) says that whatever the executor does, the machine does, for every value of the unknown. That is the theorem that makes level 2 answer the same as level 1.

The browser writes u, then n crossings' worth of copies of w, between twelve random cells on each side, puts the head in state q where the rule says, and runs the real machine for n·s steps. The rule holds if the head never reads a random cell, ends in state q at the far side, and the cells read u vn as the rule says. Words are written nearest cell first, as in the Lean file, so on the left of the head they appear reversed.
Level 2 to level 3

The tail runs in a cycle

The tail does not grow. After a start-up of some dozens of visits it repeats with a fixed period, and in one period fixed amounts leave the left block and enter the big block. Four such cycles occur:

Each line is a cycle rule in lean/ExecData.lean: a template of the whole tape, A> 110 (01)^a 000 (01)^b T with a = a0 + 3x and b = b0 + 3y, that comes back with a smaller and b larger by the amounts in the table, for all x and y. Each is proved by one run of the executor itself, without cycle rules, from the template before to the template after, 9,143 to 25,749 moves, evaluated by native_decide (ruleOK_of_run is the kernel theorem that turns such a run into the rule). Inside a round the executor applies a rule N times in one move, where N may itself be N0 + q x: the left block is r0 + m x and each period eats d = 27, 45, 48 or 66 pairs of it, so N = N0 + (m / d) x. The N-fold rule is proved by induction (rule_instN, kernel), and the subtraction is checked coefficient by coefficient (appTok, app_inst).

Both blocks get the same x here. The browser writes the template on a blank tape, puts the head on its first cell in state A, runs the real machine until state A has reached the left end the rule's number of times, and compares the tape with the template after.

Level 3 · Try it

Drain the left block yourself

Within a round the tail runs in one of four cycles, and each period of a cycle moves a fixed number of pairs out of the left block and a larger number into the big block. Pick a cycle and apply it until the left block is nearly empty: the big block ends up 2.06 to 2.2 times as long as the left block was, which is why each round is that much longer than the last. In the real run the tail decides which cycle applies; here you choose, to see the arithmetic.

Level 3 to level 4

When the left block is empty

A round ends when the left block is down to one pair: the tape is then

node B T = A> 01001000 (01)^B T

and during the next two visits the big block (B pairs) becomes the new left block, of B + 3 pairs, with a new small big block. So each round is 2.06 to 2.2 times longer than the one before. Sizes B at the round ends: 586, 1,291, 2,827, 6,280, 12,967, 28,531, …

What the next round end looks like depends on two things only: the tail T, and at which point of its cycle the tail is when the left block runs out. The second is decided by the remainder of B when divided by the amount a period eats (45, 48, 66 or 27). The size of B beyond that plays no role, and that is what makes a finite analysis possible.

The browser can find the round ends of the real run. The first button lists those of the first 1.2·108 steps; the second runs on to the round end where the closed set takes over, which Lean reaches with 527,489 executor moves (arrive_ok, lean/Arrive.lean) and the simulators place at step 2,340,515,329.

The long run takes the browser some 10 to 30 seconds; it runs in slices, so the page stays usable. "Tail" gives the position of the round end's tail in the list of 62 tails of the closed set (lean/ClassData.lean), or "none".
Level 4

A closed set of 1,144 classes

Call a class all round ends with a given tail T and a given remainder of B (and B at least 20,000). For one class the symbolic executor runs one whole round with B = r0 + m·x, where x is an unknown. The result is the next round end: a new tail T' and a new size B' = k + c·x. Whatever x is, B' has one of a few remainders; all of them must be classes of the list. Nothing about x is remembered, so the list over-counts what the real run can do.

Starting from the round end the real run reaches after 2,340,515,329 steps (B = 28,531), the search (py/closure/closure.py bfs) stops by itself: 62 tails, 1,144 classes, and every class leads only to classes already on the list. In none of the 1,144 symbolic rounds does the machine reach its halting transition (state D reading 0). The remainders are not a hidden Collatz problem here: the search never asks which remainder comes next in the real run, it accepts all of them.

In Lean (lean/Classes.lean, lean/ClassData.lean) each class is a record (tail, r0, m, K, next tail, k, c), and two tests are evaluated for every class by native_decide, 143 classes to a chunk (chunk0_ok … chunk7_ok): clsRun, the executor's round of K + 1 moves from node (r0 + m x) tail with x free must end at node (k + c x) next; and covers, for every j below a common multiple P of the moduli of the next tail's classes, some class of that tail must contain B' = k + c·j, with c·P a multiple of its modulus, so that the same class serves j, j + P, j + 2P, and so on. The kernel proves what the two tests mean (clsRun_sound, covers_sound) and that a list passing both is closed (closed_of_checks); with the arrival, nonhalting_of_closed_set (lean/Closed.lean, kernel) gives the theorem.

The panel below holds the 1,144 records as they are in lean/ClassData.lean. The browser runs the coverage test, as written in Classes.lean, and follows the run from B = 28,531 through the classes. The rounds themselves (clsRun) take about 4·109 machine steps each, too many for the browser; they are what Lean evaluates.

Each round: find the class whose tail is T and whose r0 ≤ B ≡ r0 (mod m), set x = (B − r0) / m, then B becomes k + c·x and T the next tail. Exact integer arithmetic (BigInt). How sure

What this rests on

The closed set was found by our own symbolic simulator. It is now also a Lean proof: a second program of the same kind (a symbolic executor: it crosses whole blocks at once and applies a proved cycle many times in one go, a number of times that may depend on the unknown x) is written in Lean and proved correct once: whatever it does, the machine does, for every x. Lean then runs it on each of the 1,144 classes with x unknown, 133 million moves in all, and on the run from blank tape, and checks by a proved test that every round end it arrives at is on the list. Those runs are done by compiled code (native_decide), the one thing trusted beyond Lean's kernel.

What was done before that to test the first simulator, and still supports the step counts quoted here:

  • 1,302 consecutive rounds of the real run, with block sizes up to 437 digits, were compared with the classes: next tail, next size and number of steps agree exactly.
  • Ten symbolic rounds were replayed cell by cell by a plain simulator (about 4 billion steps each): same tape, same head position, same step count.
  • 20 deliberate errors were planted in the certificate and in the simulator; the checker reported all 20.
  • A separate checker re-runs all classes and tests that the set is closed without using the search.

Lean's kernel alone proves the five sweeps for blocks of any length, the executor's correctness, and the last step of the argument: a closed set of round ends that the run enters implies that it never halts.

A warning

Nearby tapes do halt

The shape 01001000 (01)^B T alone does not keep the machine running. Take one of the 62 tails and make one of its small blocks one pair longer or shorter: of 1,141 such classes tried, 1,136 fall back into the closed set after one round, but 5 halt, for every size B. One of them, checked cell by cell: with the tail 0000 (10)^10 000 (10)^4 000 (10)^12 111 0000 101 (the last block has 12 pairs; the closed set has this tail with 11) and B = 20,002 + 3x, the machine halts after 815,565 + 122x steps (computed by py/closure/validate.py tails, confirmed bit by bit at x = 0, 1, 3).

So the argument really needs the exact list of 62 tails; a statement about all tails would be false. Other remainders of B for the 62 tails are harmless: all 1,722 classes tried fall into the closed set.

The browser can run the halting neighbour, and the tail of the closed set next to it.

The tape is A> 01001000 (01)^(20,002 + 3x) T. With the tail of the closed set the browser stops after 2·107 steps; a whole round from there takes about 4·109 steps. These round ends lie in classes of the list, so that the run from them goes on from class to class for ever follows from the Lean theorem closed_set, not from a bounded run.
The bottom line

It never halts

It never halts.

Started on a blank tape, 1RB0RD_1RC0LF_1LA0LB_---1RE_1LB1RA_0LC0RA runs for ever. From step 2,340,515,329 on, every round end of its run lies in one of 1,144 classes, every class leads after one round only to classes of the same list, and no round of any class reaches the halting transition.

The Lean theorem is nonhalt : ¬ HaltsFromBlank in lean/Replay.lean, ledger level native_decide. What it rests on, exactly:

  • The Lean kernel checks the soundness of the symbolic executor (run_sound, run_pos, rule_instN, ruleOK_of_run in lean/Exec.lean), what the two class tests mean (clsRun_sound, covers_sound, closed_of_checks, arrives_sound, inSetB_sound in lean/Classes.lean), the 15 shift rules (each by rfl), and the last implication, nonhalting_of_closed_set (lean/Closed.lean).
  • Sixteen evaluations by native_decide, which trust Lean's compiler as well as its kernel: the six cycle rules (rule0_ok … rule5_ok), the eight chunks of 143 classes (chunk0_ok … chunk7_ok, a round with x free and the coverage test for each class), the arrival from blank tape (arrive_ok, 527,489 moves) and its class (start_ok). #print axioms nonhalt lists propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound and these sixteen evaluations; there is no sorry.
  • Not used: the Python search that found the classes, or any simulator outside Lean. The executor's choices (which rule, how many times, how to re-bracket) are not trusted; only its soundness is, and that is proved.
  • Not stated in Lean: step counts, such as the step 2,340,515,329 of the first round end in a class; Lean says only "reaches, in at least one step". Those come from the simulators (computed).

There is no kernel-only proof yet: the long runs would have to be cut at checkpoints, which has not been done. A second proof of the same statement, in Coq on top of busycoq (coq/H11019.v, nonhalt : ~ halts tm c0), evaluates all its runs inside Coq's kernel with vm_compute and reports no axioms; the ledger lists it as kernel level.

A closed set of round ends implies that it never halts

If the run from blank tape reaches a round end A> 01001000 (01)^B T that belongs to a set S, and every round end in S reaches a round end in S after at least one step, then the machine does not halt. Its two hypotheses are proved by the replay below.

Kernel
lean/Exec.lean, lean/Classes.lean; shRTbl_ok, shLTbl_ok in lean/ExecData.lean
A symbolic executor and the two class tests, proved sound

The executor of 1RB0RC_0RC1RC_0LD0RA_1LE0LF_0LA1LC_---1LA, adapted: tapes of cells and blocks (w)^n with n = c + a·x; shift rules with a carried word, one to three copies at a time; one machine step; at each arrival of state A at the left end a re-bracketing and a cycle rule applied N times, where N may itself be a count N0 + q·x (the subtraction is checked coefficient by coefficient). Any step that would depend on x fails. Theorems: whatever the executor does, the machine does, for every x (run_sound); a class whose run ends at the announced round end does so for every x, in at least one step (clsRun_sound); the coverage test, a finite check over one period of the remainders, implies that every announced B lies in a listed class (covers_sound); a list that passes both is closed (closed_of_checks). The 15 shift rules are proved by evaluation in the kernel.

The tail cycles

Within a round the tape is 010 (01)^a 000 (01)^b T. After a start-up the tail T repeats with a fixed period: 15 visits of the left end (a − 45, b + 99; three different tails), 16 visits (a − 48, b + 99), 22 visits (a − 66, b + 141) or 9 visits (a − 27, b + 57). Each cycle is established by one symbolic run with a and b free (steps affine in both) and re-checked on numbers.

py/closure/closure.py, py/closure/cert.json
The closed set: how it was found, step counts

A class is a tail T (62 of them) and a remainder of B (modulo 66: 682 classes; 45: 300; 48: 144; 27: 18), with B ≥ 20,000. For each class one symbolic round with B = r + m·x, x free, ends at 01001000 (01)^B' T' with B' = k + c·x (c = 141, 99 or 57, k ≥ 41,224); every remainder B' can have lies in a class again. The simulator refuses any step whose outcome depends on x. closure.py check re-runs all classes and tests the coverage independently of the search (5 minutes). That the set is closed is now a Lean theorem (above); this tool found it and supplies the step polynomials.
