A 6-state Busy Beaver machine · Status as of 8 November 2026.

Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LD_0RC1RA_1RD1RE_1LA0LE_0RA0RF_1RC---

Nobody knows. This machine keeps a stack of counters on its tape. It works on the top one with a simple proved rule, and each time that counter runs out it rebuilds it about 10/3 times larger, either borrowing from the next counter or making the next counter take a step, depending on the counter's size modulo 3. Followed exactly with the proved rules, it has not halted in 1039,417 steps.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from E1 (writes 0, moves R): E>1 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

What the tape is made of

A stack of levels

Every time the head is at the right end of the tape in state C, the tape left of it reads as a stack of levels, each a run of ones followed by a run of 01, separated by single zeros:

0^∞ [1^nk (01)^mk 0] … [1^n2 (01)^m2 0] 1^n1 (01)^m1 C> 0^∞

Level 1 is the one next to the head. It is huge; the levels above it are small and change rarely. A level never has exactly one 1: 0 1 (01)^m is just (01)^(m+1) on the level above.

The fast part

Trips: three ones become five 01

From 1^(n+3) (01)^(m+1) C> the head runs left to the ones, turns three of them into 01 pairs, runs back and adds two more at the right end: 1^n (01)^(m+6) C>, in 6m + 45 steps. Lean proves this for every m and every tape further left (trip), and k trips in a row by induction (trips). So level 1 = 1^n (01)^m becomes, after n/3 trips, 1^r (01)^(m + 5n/3) with r = n mod 3.

The end of an epoch

Rebuilding level 1, by n mod 3

With M copies of 01 left and r ones:

  • r = 0. The head rebuilds level 1 as 1^(2M+7) (01)^3, taking the last 01 of level 2; if level 2 has no 01 it joins instead: 1^(2M+6+n2) (01)^3 (zero_dec, zero_merge, zero_blank).
  • r = 1. Nothing happens: 0 1 (01)^M is level 2 with M + 1 more copies of 01. Level 1 is popped and level 2 carries on with its ones (by trips, now at the right end).
  • r = 2. Level 2 takes one step and level 1 becomes 1^(2M-4) (01)^3 after a trip of level 2, 1^(2M-8) (01)^3 otherwise. A level-2 step is a trip 1^(n+3) (01)^q → 1^n (01)^(q+7), or, with no ones, a rebuild to 1^(2q+5) (01)^6 that takes from level 3; on blank tape a new level 1^4 (01)^6 is pushed (two_trip, two_dec, two_merge, two_blank).

All of these are proved in Lean for every size. Each epoch multiplies level 1 by about 10/3 (M is about 5n/3 and the new n is about 2M), so the step count grows about elevenfold per epoch. The first epochs:

The recursion

Every level is the same counter, run slower

A level k ≥ 2 moves only when the level below it ends an epoch with two ones left, and then it takes one step of the same kind: a trip (adding 7 copies of 01 instead of 5), a rebuild to 1^(2m+5) (01)^6 when it has no ones, or, when it has two ones, a carry: level k+1 takes a step and level k is rebuilt to 1^(2m-6) (01)^6 or 1^(2m-10) (01)^6. A level left with one 1 pops into the level above. This is a multi-level counter in which each digit is itself a counter of the same kind.

In practice the stack stays shallow: level 1 is popped whenever its size is 1 mod 3, which takes level 2 with it. In 37,692 epochs from blank tape a carry into level 3 happened once, at epoch 30,402 (level 2 = 1^2 (01)^72, rebuilt as 1^134 (01)^6 with a new level 3 1^4 (01)^6).

Where it could halt

Not in any proved rule

The machine halts when state F reads a 1. None of the proved rules does, so a halt would have to come from a case they do not cover: a carry, or a level whose run of 01 is very short. Simulating every event with level 1 large and up to two small levels above it (8,064 cases) found no halt. Whether the run ever reaches a bad case depends on the sizes of level 1 modulo 3, epoch after epoch, which nobody knows how to predict.

The trip and the start

Kernel
lean/Rules.lean
The epoch ends, in the common cases

With level 1 = 1^r (01)^M, r = 0 or 2: zero_blank, zero_dec, zero_merge (r = 0: level 1 becomes 1^(2M+6) (01)^3 or 1^(2M+7) (01)^3, taking a 01 or the ones of level 2), two_blank, two_trip, two_dec, two_merge (r = 2: level 2 takes one step and level 1 becomes 1^(2M-4) (01)^3 or 1^(2M-8) (01)^3). Each holds for every M, the sizes of the other levels and the rest of the tape, with the exact step count. Not covered: a carry from level 2 into level 3, and level-2 runs of 01 shorter than 4.

Heuristic
py/stack.py
The rule at every level

A level k ≥ 2 changes only when the level below it ends an epoch with two ones left. It then takes one step: a trip, 1^(n+3) (01)^m to 1^n (01)^(m+7); or, with no ones, a rebuild to 1^(2m+5) (01)^6 (or 1^(2m+4+p) (01)^6), taking from level k+1; or, with two ones, a carry into level k+1 and a rebuild to 1^(2m-6) (01)^6 after a trip there, 1^(2m-10) (01)^6 otherwise. A level reduced to a single one is the same tape as one more 01 on the level above (a pop). Observed in every simulated case with m ≥ 5; smaller m behaves differently and is not tabulated.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The machine halts only when state F reads a 1; no proved rule does that, and plain simulation of all 8,064 event configurations with up to two small extra levels found no halt. A proof of non-halting would need rules for every case (the carries and small levels too) closing over all stacks that can arise; the order in which the cases come is set by sizes modulo 3.
