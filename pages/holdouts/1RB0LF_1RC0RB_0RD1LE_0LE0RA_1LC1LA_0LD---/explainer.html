Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LF_1RC0RB_0RD1LE_0LE0RA_1LC1LA_0LD---

Nobody knows. Started on a blank tape, this machine has run 1011 steps without stopping. Its tape is written in four-cell words and holds a long list of numbers that survives from sweep to sweep. This page runs the real machine in your browser and shows the one pattern that would make it stop.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from A1 (writes 0, moves L): 1 A>1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The run at a glance

Space-time diagram and records

The diagram stacks the tape at many moments. Sampling at each new cell shows the shape of the run as the visited region grows; sampling evenly in time shows where the head spends its time. The records list every step at which the head reaches a cell no earlier step reached, with the side, the width so far and the whole tape: the first place to look for a repeating macro structure.

The shape of the tape

A list of numbers in four-cell words

Stop the simulator each time the head reaches a fresh cell at the left end. The tape reads

C>0 (1000)^k  ...  (0100)^13 (0111)^25 (0100)^58 (0111)^46 (0100)^25 (0111)^34 ...   (active zone)

A counter of words 1000 sits at the left end, then a long list of runs of the words 0100 and 0111. The same numbers come back in the same order sweep after sweep: the sweeps rewrite each word (into 0001 / 1101 and back) but keep the counts. The list changes only next to the left counter and in a zone near the right end.

How it moves

Crossing the words

Lean proves these for any number of words (lean/Rules.lean):

D> 1000 1000 1000      →   0110 0110 0110 D>     (4 steps a word)
C> 0100 0100           →   0011 0011 C>
0001 0001 E>0          →   E>0 0001 0001         (8 steps a word)
Where it could stop

The one way to halt

The only missing transition is F reading 1. F is entered only from A reading 1, which writes 0 and steps left; A is entered from D reading 1 (then F reads the 0 that D just wrote, which is safe) or from E reading 1, moving left. So the machine stops exactly when

1 1 E>1   →   1 A>1 1   →   F>1 0 1   halt

This is proved in Lean (lean/Halt.lean). The tape is sparse in 1s where E works, and in 169 million visits the two cells left of E were never both 1.

Which family it belongs to

The 22 machines of family F09 (“bouncers over lists of counters, not yet classified”) were screened in one way. The machine runs from blank tape for 2·1010 steps; each time the tape reaches a new cell after a threshold, the tape is compressed twice: first repeats of one word, then repeats of groups of words. If the tape is a few growing blocks and a bounded rest, the number of level-2 tokens stays bounded while the tape grows. Everything in this section is observed in one run. None of it is proved.

Proposed family: F06, block-list counters (a periodic block of long period, a frozen list of growing runs and a list of counters at the right).

With repeats of single words only, the number of pieces is proportional to the tape (18,641 level-1 tokens for 195,502 cells at 1.6·1010 steps). Most of them are one block of long period: [(0100)^13 (0111)^10 (0100)^4 (0111)^13 (0100)^10 (0111)^4]^N with N = 69 at 4.1·109 steps and N = 363 at 1.6·1010 (left-end records; a period of 216 cells). With such groups counted once, the left-end records have 2,772 and 2,817 level-2 tokens at 4.1·109 and 1.6·1010 steps and the right-end records 4,033 and 4,135: the rest of the tape hardly grows over a factor of 4 in steps.

Right of the block the tape is a frozen list of runs of growing lengths (10, 37, 16, 28, 19, 34, … up to 202) and then a list of units (1110)^a (1000)^3 111 (0100)^b (0111)^3 01 000 with a and b between 3 and 42. The two runs are different and nothing is claimed about one from the other.

The halting configurations

The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (F1); the generated statement covers every configuration.

Kernel
halt_F, halt_A, halt_E, halt_C, halt_D, enter_F, enter_A, enter_E, lean/Halt.lean
How a halt comes about

F reading 1 is the only halt. F is entered only from A reading 1 (enter_F), which writes 0 and steps left, so the machine halts exactly at 1 A>1. A is entered from D reading 1 and from E reading 1 (enter_A); the D way is harmless (via_D_safe), so a halt needs 1 1 E>1 (halt_E), reached from 1 1 1 C>1 or 1 1 1 D>0.

Kernel
enter_A, via_D_safe, lean/Halt.lean
The way in through D is harmless

A is entered from D reading 1, moving right; D writes 0 there, so when A reads 1 and steps back left, F reads that 0 and goes on. For every surrounding tape.

Kernel
dword, aword, cword, bword, berase, eshift, dshift, efill, cfill, lean/Rules.lean
Crossing the words

For every n and every surrounding tape, moving right: D turns (1000)^n into (0110)^n, A turns (0001)^n into (1100)^n, C turns (0100)^n into (0011)^n, B turns (0010)^n into (1001)^n and erases 1^n. Moving left: E carries a 0 across (0001)^n, D across (0010)^n, and E and C fill (0 1)^n and (1 0)^n into 1s.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. A proof would describe how a sweep pair acts on the list of counters (where values are consumed and new ones are written) and show that three 1s in a row never meet E or C or D in the halting way. Window CTL up to 7 cells and n-gram CPS (widths up to 9, see the README for the larger runs) fail.
