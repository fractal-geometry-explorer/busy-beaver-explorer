What is known about 1RB0RF_1LC---_1LF1LD_1RE0LD_0RC0RE_1LC1RA

1RB0RF_1LC---_1LF1LD_1RE0LD_0RC0RE_1LC1RA

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 13 November 2026.

In short

Open, probviously halting. The run alternates between two kinds of sweep that multiply a count k of copies of 10 by about 3/2 per half-phase (the zigzag is proved in Lean). A short word w at the left end is a memory: the next w and the exact correction depend only on w and the parity of k, by a table read off simulation. The machine halts exactly when w reaches a pattern with four ones in a row together with the wrong parity of k. The orbit avoids it for 2,000,005 half-phases; with random parities it is reached after about 240 half-phases.

Other proofs and outside reports

Community label

In the community's BB(6) tracker the machine's class is Bell-like. These are labels read off the run by others, not results.

The zigzag that turns (10)k into ones

For every j, x, y and every tape L on the left: L (10)^(x+2j) 1 1 E> 0 1 1^(2y+1) 0^inf reaches L (10)^x 1 1 E> 0 1 1^(2y+6j+1) 0^inf. Each round uses up two copies of 10 at the junction and adds six ones at the right end of the tape, the head sweeping over the whole block of ones and back.

The exact half-phase map on (w, k)

At a half-phase end the tape is w (10)^k F> (or w (01)^k A>). The next end is w′ (10)^k′ with k′ = (3k + c)/2, where w′ and c depend only on w and the parity of k. Each entry of the table is read off direct simulation for 12 values of k of that parity between 60 and 140 and is the same for all of them; the 50 entries the orbit uses so far give c ∈ {5, 7, 8, 9, 11, 14, 17}. Along the run from blank tape the table reproduces all 21 half-phases simulated directly (k up to 61,267) and the 34 of the macro simulator (k up to 7,950,370).

2,000,005 half-phases without a halt

Run with the table, the orbit from blank tape makes 2,000,005 half-phases without meeting a halting entry; k then has 352,184 digits (about 10704,000 machine steps; others report direct simulation to 1.1·1079). It visits only 42 word states, most often w = '', '1', '1000', '101', '11000' (each about half as often as the one before), and never one where the other parity of k would have halted. The shortest way to a halting entry needs 41 prescribed parities in a row, so a halt is expected only after about 240 half-phases (the estimate below), when k has about 1012 bits: out of reach of this method.

Where it halts: words with four ones in a row

A half-phase halts (B reads 1 near the left end) only from a few (w, parity) pairs, all with 1111 in w: ('1111', odd), ('111101', odd), ('11111', even), … Among the 2,958 word states reachable from the start under some sequence of parities (|w| ≤ 18) there are 45 halting entries. The shortest way there takes 41 half-phases with prescribed parities: w counts up through '1', '1000', '101', '11000', '1101', '1000000', …, '1111000', '111101' and then needs an odd k.

Rounds between end events, by remainder class

Sample the run whenever the head is in state A reading 0 with only 0s to its right. Read leftwards from the head, the tape is then A U^m B: one long periodic block between two short context words. For each class (A, U, B, m mod 2) the next sample is simulated for six values of m and extended as an affine map of m (with a cubic step count). Along the direct run from blank tape to 3·109 steps (21 events) the class map predicts the next event in all 15 rounds it applies to. This is an extrapolation checked by computation, not a proof.

heuristic Evidence only

Probviously halting, after about 240 half-phases

If the parities of k behave like fair coins, the word state is a Markov chain on these states, and the chance of a halt within 2e half-phases is 6·10-8 for e = 16, 4·10-3 for e = 32, 0.62 for e = 40 and 0.9996 for e = 43. So the machine halts with probability 1 in this model, but only after about 1012 half-phases, when k has about 2·1011 digits.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Given the table (sampled, not proved), exactly when the orbit of (w = '', k = 4) meets a halting entry, i.e. a word with four ones in a row and the wrong parity of k. That is a question about the parities of the ×3/2 sequence k, coupled to the word: Collatz-like.
