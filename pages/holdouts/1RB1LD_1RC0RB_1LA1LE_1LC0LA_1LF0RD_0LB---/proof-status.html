What is known about 1RB1LD_1RC0RB_1LA1LE_1LC0LA_1LF0RD_0LB---

1RB1LD_1RC0RB_1LA1LE_1LC0LA_1LF0RD_0LB---

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

It is a counter in disguise. Each time the head reaches the left end, a solid block there has grown by six cells, and the next time takes four times as many steps. The head's excursions in between follow the carry pattern of a counter. A word on the right side of the tape changes a little at each overflow; when it is used up, the left block shrinks and a new epoch starts.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Lean proves no halt in the first 10,000 steps by the kernel alone and in the first 107 with native_decide; the C simulator finds no halt in the first 2·1010 steps.

Where a halt would come from. The machine halts only in state F reading 1, and F is entered only from E reading a 0 (1LF), which is rare: 16 times in the first 108 steps, each time at the left end or in the run of 0s just left of the solid block, so F reads a 0.

What a proof needs. An exact description of one overflow (an induction over the counter's digits) and of the epoch change, showing that E reads a 0 only where the cell to its left is 0.

blank_runs_kernel, lean/Blank.lean

A run-length frame map, every case proved

At every head turn write the tape as two lists of runs (blocks of equal cells), left and right of the head: a frame with list parameters. A table of 310 cases, each a pattern on the first few runs of both sides (lengths exact or at least 4, the rest of each side any list), sends each frame to the frame of the next head turn; every case is proved for all values of its parameters and all lists by one symbolic run (RLCase.sound), so the frame map next = rlNext cases is sound (rlNext_sound). Between two head turns this machine crosses only a few run boundaries, which is why a finite table covers a tape whose number of runs grows without bound.

Overflows quadruple

At the times state A is at the left end the tape starts A>0 1^n b … (b = 110), n growing by 6 per event; the gaps between events grow by a factor tending to 4: the events at steps 20,060,883, 78,781,139, 313,662,151, 1,253,186,235 and 5,011,282,607 have n = 60, 66, 72, 78, 84.

tools/c/tmrun.c (at A L)

Epochs

The left block falls back from 1^39 to 1^18 at step 488,707 and from 1^84 to 1^75 at step 7,842,487,335, while the word on the right is rebuilt; between these the right word changes by a few cells per overflow.

tools/c/tmrun.c

E reads 0 only next to a 0

F, the state with the halting transition, is entered only from E reading 0. In the first 108 steps that happens 16 times: 12 at the left end of the tape and 4 in the run of 0s left of the solid block, always with a 0 to the left, so F reads 0.

A counter

Between overflows the head's rightward excursions from its home position have lengths 2, 4, 2, 6, 2, 4, 2, 8, …, and its leftward excursions grow in the same ruler pattern, the carries of a counter.

head reversals after step 3·107

No finite frame map with scalar parameters

The frame-map method (stop at head turns, match the tape against finitely many templates with block-length parameters, fit affine cases) needs the tapes at head turns to fall into a bounded number of shapes. Here they do not: tokenizing the tape at every 7th head turn into maximal powers of words of length at most 4, the first 107 steps give 617,359 distinct shapes (exponents of 3 and more merged), 553,191 of them new in the last decade, and up to 34 tokens per tape; the number grows tenfold per decade of steps. For comparison the same count saturates at 32 shapes of at most 5 tokens for 1RB1LC_1LC1RD_1LA0LB_0RB1RE_1RF0RA_---0RC and at 89 for 1RB1RF_1LC1LF_0RE1LD_0LB1LD_---1RC_1RA0RD, where frame maps were proved. The cause is the counter itself: its digits form an arbitrary word, and the word of higher digits on the right grows too, so a frame needs a list parameter; the run-length frames above are such frames.
