Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1RB_1LC0RE_---1LD_0RA1LB_1RA1LF_0LE1LE

Nobody knows, but the question has been boiled down. This machine keeps two blocks of 1s on its tape and, round after round, takes the shorter one off the longer one and triples it, like Euclid's algorithm gone wrong. Lean proves that it halts exactly when the two blocks come out equal (or off by one) at the end of a round. This page runs the real machine, shows the rule, and lists what is known.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in C reading 0. C is entered only from B0 (writes 1, moves L): 0 B>0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The tape

Two blocks of ones and a flag

At the start of every round the head sits at the right end of the tape in state B, and the tape is

R1(i, j) = …0 12i 0 12j 0 11 B> 0…
R2(i, j) = …0 12i 0 12j 0 11 0 11 B> 0…

Two blocks of 1s of even lengths 2i and 2j, and a short tail that works as a one-bit flag J. From blank tape the first round starts at step 74 in R1(3, 1).

One round

A three-block bouncer

The head runs to the left end, where the round becomes three blocks E> 0 1a 0 1b 0 1c with a = 2i, b = 2j − 3 and c = 6 (after R1) or 9 (after R2). A four-cell zig-zag 0110 walks right across the first two blocks and the head walks back: each such pass takes (a, b, c) to (a − 2, b − 2, c + 6) and moves the left end of the tape two cells further out. When the first or second block runs out, the round ends with the third block as the new second block, and the parity of c choosing the next tail.

In the half-lengths (i, j) and the flag J the round is this map (next in lean/Round.lean, proved for all i and j):

So the shorter block is taken off the longer one and the shorter one is tripled: Euclid's algorithm with a tripling that keeps the numbers growing, by about 0.31 bits a round along the orbit.

The left side of the tape grows like the square root of the step count (two cells per pass, and a round of size n costs about n2 steps); the right side grows by one or three cells per round, and the number of rounds grows like the logarithm of the step count.

Halting

Why it would halt, and why it probably never does

The machine halts exactly when the two blocks run out in the same pass: i = j or i + 1 = j. Then the zig-zag finds a blank where it expects the third block and the head reads a 0 in state C, the halting transition. Lean proves both directions: halts_iff : HaltsFromBlank ↔ ∃ n, orb n = none.

Along the orbit the difference i − j behaves like a random number as large as i and j themselves, so with n-bit numbers the chance of hitting one of the two halting values is about 2−n. The orbit has been followed for 106 rounds, to numbers with tens of thousands of digits, and the closest approach is at round 547,658 the difference i − j still has 167,823 bits, against 167,848 bits for i and j. Ruling out a hit forever is a problem of the Collatz type, and no invariant is known: congruences alone cannot do it, because i = j is excluded only by size.

Heuristic
tools/py/bbtools/triage.py
Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.497). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.126).

Kernel
round_ok, lean/Round.lean; pieces in lean/Pieces.lean
Every round, for all block sizes

From R1(i, j) or R2(i, j) (two blocks of 1s of lengths 2i and 2j, then a tail 0 11 or 0 11 0 11, head at the right end in state B) the machine reaches the next round configuration given by next, or halts when next is none (i = j or i + 1 = j). Proved for all i, j from 26 symbolic runs and an induction on the inner passes.

Heuristic
Probviously non-halting

Along the orbit i - j looks like a random number of the size of i and j, so a round with n-bit numbers hits the two halting values with chance about 2-n; the expected number of future hits is negligible. No invariant is known that excludes them (congruences cannot: i = j is excluded only by size).

Open
Does the orbit ever reach i = j or i + 1 = j?
