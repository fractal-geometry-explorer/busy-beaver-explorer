A 6-state Busy Beaver machine · Status as of 8 November 2026.

Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0RD_1RC0LF_0LA0RB_1RE---_1LB1RA_0LE0LF

Nobody knows. But the machine can be cut in two: a wall of seven-cell words that grows by three words per cycle and does nothing else, and a short data word at the fixed end that behaves like the odometer of a car with wheels of 5, 3 and 2 positions. The wall part is proved in Lean; the odometer is where the open question lives.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in D reading 1. D is entered only from A1 (writes 0, moves R): A>1 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.501). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

The structure

A wall that only counts, and a data word

From step 2,035 on, the tape always has the same layout. On the left a wall of n copies of the word 1001111. Then twelve cells that are almost constant, 0100 111111 and two more. Then, from cell 13 on (cell 0 is where the head started), a data word. Call Z(n, R) the moment the head stands on the first data cell in state E, with 0 1 just left of it, a wall of n words and data R.

From Z(n, R) the head works on the data for a while, a few hundred steps. Sooner or later it comes back over the cut in state B or F. Then it sweeps the whole wall seven times, there and back, rewriting every word on each pass, and returns to the cut. At that moment the tape is Z(n+3, R'): three more words in the wall, and a data word R' that the seven sweeps did not touch. That is one cycle.

The seven sweeps take exactly 168(n−1)+575 steps. Lean checks this for every n and every data word at once (cycle_B, cycle_F), using one shift rule per pass of the head over the wall. So the wall is a clock and nothing more. Whether the machine halts depends only on the sequence of data words R → R' → R'' …

Almost all the running time goes into the wall: a cycle costs 168 n steps there and a few hundred in the data. Jumping over the wall with the Lean rule, c/edge.c runs ten million cycles in a few seconds. That is 2.5·1016 steps of the real machine.

The emergent behaviour

An odometer with wheels of 5, 3 and 2

In each cycle the head goes into the data up to some cell and turns back. How far it goes is very regular. Counted over ten million cycles:

This is how an odometer behaves: the first wheel moves every time, and passes a tick to the next wheel once per turn. Here the first wheel has 5 positions, so one cycle in five the head stops at cell 16 and the rest of the data is left alone. The next wheels have 3 and 2 positions, and so on.

The same shows in the data itself. Look only at the first k cells of the data word, cycle after cycle. They repeat with an exact period:

Each new stretch of cells multiplies the period by 2, 3 or 5. The far end of the data is reached only when all wheels roll over together, which gets rarer and rarer. Then the data grows by a few cells and a new wheel appears. The data length grows like the logarithm of the number of cycles, about 42 cells for every factor of ten.

Why it is hard

No rule for the next wheel

A real odometer has identical wheels, and a machine with identical wheels is a counter that the standard methods prove to run forever. Here the wheels differ: the periods multiply by 5, 3, 2, 2, 5, 3, 3, 5, 3, … with no pattern found so far. What a new wheel looks like depends on the state of all the old ones at the moment it is made.

The machine can halt in one way only: state D reading a 1, which needs a pattern like 1 0 1 1 1 under the head in state A. By the wall cycle, that could only happen inside the data word. A proof that it never happens needs a description of every data word that can occur, and for that one needs the rule for the wheels.

The reduced machine has run 750,000,000 cycles, 1.4·1020 steps of the real machine, without halting. The tables above come from the same runs. They are measurements, not theorems.

The wall cycle, for every n

Cut the tape between cells 12 and 13. Left of the cut: n words 1001111, then 0100 111111 and two more cells; right of the cut: the data R. If the head comes back from the data onto cell 12 in state B or F, reading 1 with a 1 on its left, then 168(n−1)+575 steps later the head is on the first data cell in state E, the wall has n+3 words, the two cells are 0 1 again and R is untouched. This is Z(n) → Z(n+3).

No halt in 750,000,000 cycles (1.4·1020 steps)

c/edge.c runs the machine step by step on the data and jumps over the seven sweeps with the Lean rule. It stops if the head ever touches the wall in a way the rule does not cover; that never happened. Its step counts agree with the plain machine on the first 106 cycles (3·106 steps, py/check_edge.py) and on 123 cycles between steps 6·108 and 7·108. After 750,000,000 cycles the wall has 2,250,000,007 words and the data 423 cells.

README.md
A mixed-radix odometer

How deep the head goes into the data in a cycle follows the same pattern: it turns at cell 16 in exactly one cycle out of 5, at each of 22, 25, 28, 31 in one out of 15, at each of 33, 34, 35, 40 in one out of 30, and so on (counted over 107 cycles). The data length grows like the logarithm of the cycle number, about 42 cells per factor 10. No rule is known for the period of the next digit, and nothing here is proved.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The machine halts only on A> 1 0 (1 1 0)k 1 1 1-like patterns (state D reading 1), and by the wall cycle such a pattern can only arise inside the data word. A proof of non-halting needs an invariant for the data word, i.e. a description of the odometer digits that covers the new digits made at the far end.
