What is known about 1RB0RA_0LC0RE_1LE1LD_0LE---_1LF0LC_1RA1LE

1RB0RA_0LC0RE_1LE1LD_0LE---_1LF0LC_1RA1LE

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. Its run is a clock: a block of 1s at the left end grows by two cells per tick, and tick k rewrites the word to the right of the block by applying one fixed map P to it exactly 2k times. P is a single trip of the head into the word and back. This page lists what has been established, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 9 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The clock is proved in Lean for every k and every word: if P takes the word through 2k trips, the tick happens, in an exactly known number of steps. What P does to the word over many trips, and what happens when a trip does not come back (the end of a phase), is not proved. An exact accelerated simulator, checked against the direct one at every visit of the left end in the first 2·1010 steps, follows the run through 30 phases to 2.363·10194 steps without a halt.

Where it can halt

The only halting transition is D on 1, and D is entered only from C on 1 (1LD): the machine halts exactly when C reads a 1 whose left neighbour is a 1, 1 C>1. Both directions are proved in Lean, by a case split on the cells the head reads: a configuration halts one step later if and only if it is 1 C>1 (halt_iff_1), so a halting run from blank passes through it (halts_blank_pred_1).

The sweep and the merge

rule_sweep: 0 0 F> 1^(3+2n) 0 becomes 1 0^(4+2n) 1 B> (the block is swept into 0s); rule_merge: 1 0 F> 1 becomes F> 1 1 1, for every n and all tapes around.

The clock, for every k

tick: for every k, every word W and all tapes L, if W reaches W' by 2k P-steps (each one an excursion L 0^3 1 B> W → L F> 1^3 0 W') of total cost E, then L 0 0 F> 1^(3+2k) 0 W reaches L F> 1^(3+2k+2) 0 W' in exactly E + 12·2k − 2 steps. Strong induction on k: the tick is the sweep, one P-step, ticks 0 … k−1, and the merge. tick_left_end: the same at the left end of the tape.

What a phase does to the word

At almost every phase end from step 2,985 to 1060 steps the new word has |W| + L + 2 cells (L the last block; exceptions +3, +4, +14), keeping only a short tail of the old word; over a phase the word changes at the front and in a group of cells near its end. No simple phase-to-phase map was found.

What the word does

In the word, read as blocks of 1s, the front is a gadget of period 8 under P, followed by a binary counter whose digits are the 4-cell words 1 0 1 0 (blocks 1 1: digit 0) and 1 1 1 0 (block 3: digit 1), least significant first; every 8 P-steps the counter goes down by one (seen from a phase start: 12, 11, 10, …, 5 at P-steps 8, 16, …, 64). Deeper blocks (5, 7, 9, …) change only when a borrow reaches them. A phase ends when the front reads 1 0 0 or 1 1 0: the counter has run out.

Does it halt from blank tape?

A normal tick cannot halt (the clock theorem). Halting needs a P-step that does not come back — that happens only at the end of a phase — followed by C on a 1 with a 1 to its left (1 C>1). Open: what P does over 2k trips (a counter in the word), what a phase end builds, and whether one ever reaches the halting configuration.
