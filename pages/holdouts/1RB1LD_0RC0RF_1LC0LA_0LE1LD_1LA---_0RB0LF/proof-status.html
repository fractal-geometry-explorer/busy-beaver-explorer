What is known about 1RB1LD_0RC0RF_1LC0LA_0LE1LD_1LA---_0RB0LF

1RB1LD_0RC0RF_1LC0LA_0LE1LD_1LA---_0RB0LF

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The tape is always a handful of blocks of 1s separated by short runs of 0s. One elementary move is proved in Lean (a 0 travelling through a block). The way block lengths change from sweep to sweep looks chaotic: blocks merge, and large blocks are cut in a ratio close to 1:2. No rule on the lengths is known, so nothing is known about halting (E reading a 1).

Growth shape: power/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.353). Right of the start it grows like a power of the step count other than 1/2 (fitted exponent 0.287).

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The machine halts only when E reads a 1. The block lengths change chaotically, and no invariant that keeps E away from a 1 has been found. CTL (windows of 2 to 5 cells) and NGramCPS (widths 2 to 8, with history) fail.
