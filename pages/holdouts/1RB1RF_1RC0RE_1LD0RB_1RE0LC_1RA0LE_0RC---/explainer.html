Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1RF_1RC0RE_1LD0RB_1RE0LC_1RA0LE_0RC---

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows, but probably not. The tape is a queue of blocks 0101… separated by a five-cell marker, and single odd cells (cursors) travel through it, making every block four times longer. The machine can only stop if three such cursors pile up at exactly the moment a new one is born, next to a block of a single copy, with a short queue after them. In 105000 steps the pile-up happened nine times, never at that moment, and never next to such a block.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from A1 (writes 1, moves R): A>1 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The macro picture

Blocks, separators and cursors

Write a for 01 and b for 00110. Each time the head arrives at the right end of the tape (a visit), the tape is a row of blocks a^k separated by b, with a few single cells, 0 or 1, in between: the cursors. A cursor is a place where the regular beat 0101… skips; the rightmost one is the active one.

One visit moves the active cursor one a to the left and flips it. The block behind it grows by 4 copies of a, and the block at the very end of the tape pays 2. So a cursor that walks through a block of k copies leaves 4k behind: with the 6 extra copies it picks up when it crosses a separator, sizes go 2 → 14 → 62 → 254 → …, each 4k + 6, that is 4j − 2.

Crossing a separator takes two visits (b 0 → 0 a 1 1 a b → 0 a^6 b). And when the end block has nothing left to pay, the last separator itself turns into a new cursor (a birth); the cursor that was active simply stays where it is until a later cursor runs into it.

All of this is proved in Lean for all block sizes (lean/Rules.lean). After 1018 steps the tape holds, among others, 1,299,680 blocks of 14, 47,575 blocks of 1022 and 258 blocks of 1,048,574.

Where it could stop

Three cursors in a row, at the wrong moment

The machine stops only if state A reads two 1s in a row. Inside the queue that happens in one situation: three cursor cells 1 1 1 stand side by side and the next cursor arrives directly to their right as a 0. Lean proves that this tape, which has exactly the form the run keeps, halts after 213 steps:

1 a b a 0 a 1 1 1 0 a^22 0 B>

How could the run get there? Three things must line up:

  1. a birth happens while a cursor is half way across a separator, leaving 0 a 1 1 a b behind;
  2. a later cursor reaches that place: for exactly one visit the tape reads 1 1 1 there, and one visit later 0 a 1 a^14, harmless;
  3. a birth happens at exactly that one visit, so the three 1s are left standing for the next cursor.

In the first 1018 steps step 1 happened once, at step 7.08×108, and step 2 followed at step 1.057×1011. Step 3 did not: the end block, which pays 2 copies at every visit and whose emptying is what triggers a birth, still held 14,076 copies, so the next birth was 7,038 visits away.

A faster simulator, written for this machine, follows the run to 105000 steps, a number with five thousand digits. It does not simulate every step: it recognizes stretches where the same thing happens over and over (a cursor walking through a long block) and does them in one arithmetic move, and every shortcut it takes is checked against the step-by-step machine. Three 1s in a row turned up nine times. Each time, the next birth was far away:

The miss is always about as large as the tape is long, which is about the square root of the step count. If the moment of birth were a roll of the dice, the first near miss had a 1 in 7,000 chance of ending the run and every later one less than 1 in 10196: by that count the machine runs forever. But the schedule is not dice; the machine's sibling (same kind of queue, sizes 4j) has an outside proof, in review, that it does halt. The question stays open until someone works out the exact timetable of the cursors.

Which tapes halt

Which queues lead to a halt

A triple left standing by a birth is not a halt yet. The part of the tape right of it now runs like a machine of its own, with its own births and its own cursors meeting and cancelling, until the first cursor reaches the triple. Three things can happen then: the machine halts, or a cursor arrives and the triple dissolves, or no cursor arrives at all (they all cancel, the triple's own 1 becomes the active cursor again and walks on).

Left of the triple. An arriving 0 halts the machine only if the cursor stranded in front of the triple sits right after a single copy of a, as in b a 0 a 1 1 1. The stranded cursor is the one a birth caught half way across a separator, and the block left of it is the one it was about to walk into. In the run that block always has 2 copies (114 of the 124 such births up to 107500 steps) or 14 (the other 10). With two copies there, no queue on the right gives a halt.

Right of the triple, with the single copy on the left. One block b a^n: the machine halts for every even n ≥ 12, proved in Lean for all such n. A run of q equal blocks of size 4j − 2 (254 or more): it halts for q = 1 and for q = 9 to 19; for q = 2 to 8 a cursor arrives without a halt; from q = 20 on, the cursors cancel before any of them gets 20 blocks deep. Four blocks of 254 weigh about as much as one of 1022 (the upper edge of the band drops by one column every four rows), sixteen as much as one of 4094. Further down the band does not close: with 5 blocks of 1022, even 5,000 blocks of 254 before them are crossed. A few large blocks carry a cursor across many small ones; twenty large ones stop it. (Exact accelerated simulation, py/haltq.py; its shortcuts are checked against the plain machine, not proved.)

The run's own near misses. For each of the nine triples of the first 105000 steps, the simulator was rerun from the tape a birth at exactly that visit would have left. None halts: the left of every one of them is wrong (a^2 or a^14 before the stranded cursor), so the coincidence the dice count above would not have been enough. Which queue sizes reach the halting form? With the run's left end, none; with a single copy left of the triple, the ones in the map.

README.md; py/qsim.py prints the tape
Tape structure

With a = 01 and b = 00110, the tape at each visit of the right end is 1 a (b a^2)^q followed by a list of blocks a^k separated by b, with single cursor cells in between (q = 127 at 1011 steps, 16,418,839 at 1018: a visit with no cursor on the tape inserts one b a^2 at the left). The block sizes that persist are 14, 62, 254, 1022, 4094, …, that is 4j − 2, each four times the previous plus 6.

The local rules of the queue

With a = 01, b = 00110 and the head in state B at the right end. Feeding: L a^(k+1) c a^m b a^(n+2) 0 B> becomes L a^k c' a^(m+4) b a^n 0 B> (the cursor c moves one a left and flips, the block after it gets 4, the end block pays 2; without a separator the end block gets 2). Crossing a separator in two visits: b 0 → 0 a 1 1 a b → 0 a^6 b, and b 1 a^m → 0 0 a^(m+5) → 1 a b a^m. Birth: when the end block is empty the last separator becomes a new cursor and the old one stays where it is. All for all sizes and every tape to the left.

A queue of blocks of size 4^j - 2

A cursor that walks through a block of k copies leaves 4k (plus 6 at a separator) behind it, which gives the persistent sizes 2, 14, 62, 254, 1022, ... The numbers of equal blocks persist from one size to the next (1264, 127 and 258 copies sit at sizes 254, 1022, 16382 at 2×1015 steps and at 16382, 65534, 1048574 at 1018): a self-similar counter. Heuristic
py/qsim.py, py/qsim_check.py
No halt in the first 1018 steps of an accelerated run

py/qsim.py is an exact simulator with macro rules for a^n, b and (b a^n)^q, plus jumps: when the same tape shape recurs with constant changes of the counts for 4 periods, many periods are applied at once and the next one is checked. Without jumps it agrees with a cell-by-cell simulator at every visit of the right end tested (6,000 visits, 5.3×107 steps); with jumps it agrees with the run without jumps at every visit up to 3×1010 steps. It reaches 1018 steps in 160 s without a halt (3,304,842 visits simulated one by one, 127,033 jumps). The jumps are validated, not proved. (An earlier accelerated run, rsim.py, reached 4.61×1014 steps.)

go/fsim.go is the same exact rope engine in Go with big integers, plus exact bulk feeding (all visits of one feeding run at once, with their step count) and periodic jumps over whole visits, each validated by the period after it. py/births.py (on the accelerated simulator); go/fsim.go (MARGIN lines)
How close the run from blank tape came

The halting shape needs (1) a birth while a cursor is half way across a separator (0 a 1 1 a b), (2) a later cursor reaching that place, which gives 1 1 1 for one visit, and (3) a birth at exactly that visit. In the first 1018 steps there are 124 births; (1) happened once (birth 22, step 7.08×108), (2) followed at step 1.057×1011, the only visit among 3,304,842 simulated one by one with three cursor cells 1 side by side; (3) did not happen: the end block held 14,076 copies, not 0. With of the order 109 visits and 124 births in 1018 steps, a random visit is a birth with chance about 10−7; the real schedule is determined, not random, and not understood. To 105000 steps (go/fsim.go): 51,981 births seen, 74 of them half way across a separator, and nine visits with 1 1 1. At each of the nine the end block b a^M 0 was far from empty, so the next birth was at least M/2 visits away: 7,038 visits at the first, then numbers of 197, 867, 1359, 1636, 1766, 1784, 1860 and 2496 digits (steps about 10394 to 104994); M mod 8 takes the values 0, 2, 4 and 6.

For every j, the tape 0^inf 1 a b a 0 a 1 1 1 a^10 b a^(2j+12) 0 a 1 B> halts: a triple left standing by a birth, with one block of any even size ≥ 12 after it (so every size 4i − 2 ≥ 14). py/haltq.py left; fsim log, BIRTH mid-hop lines
The cells left of a triple decide too

An arriving 0 halts the machine only if the cursor stranded in front of the triple follows a single copy of a after a separator or a cursor 0 (b a 0 a 1 1 1 and similar: 12 of 400 small contexts tested). That stranded cursor is the one a birth caught half way across a separator, and the block left of it is the one it was about to enter: in all 124 such births up to 107500 steps that block has 2 copies (114 times) or 14 (10 times), never 1. With two copies, no queue tested gives a halt.

README.md
A probabilistic estimate: it does not halt

A triple ends in a halt only if a birth falls on its visit, that is if the active cursor's end block, which holds M copies (M of the order of the square root of the step count), runs out exactly there. If that moment fell at random within the cursor's life the chance would be about 2/M: about 1/7000 for the first triple, below 10-196 for each later one. Triples come about once in 550 decades of the step count (nine in 5000), so the chance that any later one ends in a halt is below 10-2490. The schedule is deterministic, so this is an estimate, not a proof; a halt by a structured coincidence that the random model does not see is not excluded. It is also too generous: at the nine triples of the run a birth at the right moment would not have been enough (see the reruns above).

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. A proof of non-halting has to show that a birth never falls on the visit at which three cursor cells 1 stand together (or that the next cursor then arrives as a 1); a proof of halting has to find when it does. Both need the global schedule of the cursors: the counter of equal blocks as an exact recurrence. The sibling 1RB1RF_0RC0RE_0RD1RA_1LE0LA_1LB0LD_0RD--- has an outside proof in review that it halts; that proof is not in this repository, so its mechanism could not be compared.
