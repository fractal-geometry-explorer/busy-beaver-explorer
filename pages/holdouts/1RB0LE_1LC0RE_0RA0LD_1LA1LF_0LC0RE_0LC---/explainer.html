Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_0LC---

Nobody knows whether it halts. Its engine is now decoded and proved: a base-3 counter on the left and two binary counters on the right tick together, N and M by one per event and R by three each time M wraps. Only the overflows of R, where the machine reaches its rare transitions, remain a mystery.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from D1 (writes 1, moves L): 1 D>1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

Decoded

Three counters on one tape

At the moments we call events (the head in state A, on a 0, just right of the left counter) the tape reads

0∞ 1 0 [N] A> 0 [M] 1 [R] …

  • N, a base-3 counter: 3-cell digits 100 = 0, 110 = 1, 010 = 2, most significant first, its lowest digit next to the head;
  • M, a binary counter: 4-cell digits 0010 = 0, 1010 = 1, lowest digit first (next to the head);
  • R, a second binary counter after a single 1: 4-cell digits 1000 = 0, 1010 = 1, lowest digit first.

In the words a = 10, b = 110 of the older description, M's digits read as sections a| and aa, which is why the tape looked like sections of a's split by 000.

The rule

One event: N + 1, M + 1, and R + 3 when M wraps

From one event to the next the head runs right over M (and over R only when M wraps), comes back, crawls into N, adds one there and returns. The net effect:

counterchange per event
N+1 in base 3; trailing 2s carry; when N = 2…2 it becomes 0…0 with one digit more (the tape grows 3 cells at the left)
M+1 in binary; trailing 1s carry; from 1…1 it wraps to 0…0 (same width)
Runchanged, except when M wraps: then +3 in binary

So N counts events (mod 3width), M counts them mod 2width, and R gains 3 every 2width of M events: two different bases running side by side. Example from the run of 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB--- (M with 2 digits, R with 8): at steps 12,203, 12,223, 12,243, 12,253, 12,345 the triple (N, M, R) is (5, 0, 92), (6, 1, 92), (7, 2, 92), (8, 3, 92), (9, 0, 95).

Each case of this rule is a theorem in lean/Events.lean, proved for every size: 9 cases for N (lowest digit 0 or 1 after 0, 1 or k+2 twos, or the wrap of 2…2 at the left end), times 6 for M and R (no wrap, carry over i+1 ones, or a wrap of M with i+1 digits combined with the 4 cases of R's two low bits and a carry over j ones). 54 cases (exhaustive by construction of events.py), one theorem each, with its exact step count, e.g. 12i + 12j + 10k + 116 steps for a carry over k twos in N, a wrap of M with i+1 digits and R ending in 10 followed by j ones. When M does not wrap, nothing right of M is read, so these rules hold whatever lies beyond.

Checked

How long the run keeps this shape

In the clean shape R is followed by 1 0 1 and blank tape. This run reaches it at step 533 (N = 1 (1 digit), M = 4 (4 digits), R = 0 (2 digits)) and then 28 consecutive events follow the rule, until the first overflow of R (2 digits).

An overflow of R (its carry running into the cells after it) is not covered by the rules: there the head goes past R and rebuilds the right part; in 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB--- the transition D1 (the only way into F, whose 1 halts) is used in the first 105 steps only at steps 138, 1,008, 3,177, 7,571 (before the clean shape) and 20,756, 53,353, 92,444, 92,861 (at the first three overflows of R). After the first overflows the cells right of R are no longer just 1 0 1: they hold a further structure starting 1 0 1 1 0 …, and M and R take other widths. Counting every event whose left part is N and whose right part starts 0 [M] 1, 5,729 events in the first 3·106 steps follow from the previous one by the rule and 1,022 do not (the wraps of M that touch the undecoded part); at step 3·106: N = 150 (5 digits), M with 1 digit.

The picture

Two words and a separator

Write a for 1 0 and b for 1 1 0. Apart from a few cells at the ends, the tape is a string of a's and b's broken into sections by extra 0s (0 0 0 between two 1s). At its visits to the left end the first section is a b b … b followed by a's and b's, and the later sections are mostly runs of a's. Writing 1 for a and 2 for b, the tape at step 645,244,842 of the first sibling is 1222222222222222 | 111 | 111 | 11111 | 111 | 12 | 11111112 | 111111111 | 11111 | 11111 | 1 | 111 | 1 | 11.

Mechanism

Sweeps and an inner counter

The head moves through runs of words in one state, rewriting each copy the same way, for example A turning 0 1 1 into 1 0 0 or B turning 1 0 into 0 1 (proved in Lean for any number of copies: a_right_01, a_right_011, b_right_10, b_right_11, b_right_110, e_right_10, e_right_11, e_right_110). Its excursions do not usually reach the ends: the turning points far from the left end follow the ruler pattern of a binary counter with digits four cells wide (in a stretch of 17,000 steps from step 28,601 of the first sibling the far turning points are 43, 47, 43, 51, 43, 47, 43, 55, … cells from the left end). The ends are visited rarely, and each visit to the left end finds the whole tape rearranged.

Halting

Where it could halt

The machine halts only when F reads a 1. A closed tape language with windows of six cells (an over-approximation of every configuration reachable from blank tape, found and checked by bbtools.ctl) allows that only with 110001, 110010 from the head on: F on the first 1 of a word 1 1 0 that ends a section. Whether the run ever puts F there is the open question.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.029). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

After 109 steps the tape spans 213 cells (168 left of the start, 41 right), with a density of 1s of 0.427.

Open

What is left

The engine is proved; the halting question now sits entirely in the overflows. No event can halt, since every rule is a run of the machine; a halt can only come between events that the rules do not cover. Open: (1) the overflow of R as a rule: what N, M, R and the cells after R become; (2) the layered layout that follows (the part right of R starting 1 0 1 1 0), which seems to hold further counters; (3) whether the iterated overflows ever produce the halting pattern. The left counter N alone never causes trouble: its wrap at the left end is one of the proved cases.

New view

One visit of a stream

Cut the tape right of the event cell into tokens of two cells: z = 00, a = 10, c = 01. Everything from R on, the blank tape included, is one stream of a and z, and one visit of the head in state B does this to it: the first z becomes a, and every second a before it becomes z.

If the first z is at an odd place the visit comes out in state A: with digits a z = 0 and a a = 1 this is the binary increment of R. If it is at an even place (all digits 1 up to the blank tape) the visit comes out in state C and leaves the digits shifted by one token. So R has no overflow into something else; the rare case is a visit that comes out in C.

Both cases, and M's increment and wrap (three visits of the stream), are proved in Lean for every size from 22 token rows, each of which is a small theorem about one token and the nested visits of the rest. What the c tokens of M do after a visit that comes out in C is in those rows but not yet composed; that is where the question of halting now sits.

Computed
py/laws.py; a direct run
The overflow of R is the even case of the stream law

R has no end: when all its digits up to the blank tape are 1, the first z is at an even place and the visit comes out in C, leaving the digits shifted by one token. In the run of 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB--- this first happens at step 20,645 (stream a18 then blank, left as (z a)9 a). That the event rule fails only there is read off the rules, not a Lean theorem.

Open
After a visit that comes out in C

What the layer of c tokens (M) becomes after a visit of the a/z stream that comes out in C is given row by row in Visit.lean but not composed into a law; the left side is not yet in this form. The machine can halt only there (follows from the event rules, not itself a theorem).
