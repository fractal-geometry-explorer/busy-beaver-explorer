OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state machine halts from blank tape. It belongs to a family of six holdouts with almost the same table (F0 = 0LC, E1 = 0RE). Their tapes are made of the words 10 and 110, grouped in sections split by 000, and they grow slowly to the left. This page lists what has been established, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 22 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The engine is decoded and proved in Lean for every size and every layout. At an event the tape is a base-3 counter N, then a binary counter Z, then a chain of binary words; N and Z get 1 per event, and every word is a counter that, when it wraps, sends 3 to the next word (so a carry is multiplied by 3 at every separator, and an empty word passes 3 times what it got). This chain law covers every layout the run has shown (the plain cascade, the X layer and all later eras are special cases) and is proved in lean/Layers.lean for any number of words of any widths. Inside a layout the whole right side is one number that grows by 1 per event, so an era change happens only when a carry runs off the last word. Using this, a simulator that jumps from one era change to the next and runs each change cell by cell has followed the run through 60 changes of era, 8.0·1029 events (so at least that many steps), with no halt. What an era change hands on (the new widths and values, and how many base-3 digits it takes from N) is the open part; C never runs out of digits: instead the chain keeps gaining words.

The a/z stream and M as a recursive visit law

Everything from R on, blank tape included, is one stream of a = 10 and z = 00. A visit in state B turns the first z into a and every second a before it into z; it comes out in A when the first z is at an odd place (the binary increment of R, for every size: vb_odd, ve_odd) and in C when it is at an even place (vb_even). M + 1 without a wrap (m_inc) and with a wrap, where the stream gets three visits (m_wrap), for every size.

The chain law, for every layout

At an event the right side is 0∞ 1 0 [N] A> 0 [Z] 1 1 [W₁] 0 1 [U₁] 1 0 1 1 [W₂] 0 1 [U₂] … 1 0 1 1 [Wm] 0 1 [Um] 0∞: Z (digits 0010, 1010), each W (0001, 0101) and each U (1000, 1010) binary, lowest digit first, of any width, W and U possibly empty, any number m of pairs. The overflow of R is the even case of the stream law

R has no end: when all its digits up to the blank tape are 1, the first z is at an even place and the visit comes out in C, leaving the digits shifted by one token. In the run of 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB--- this first happens at step 20,645 (stream a18 then blank, left as (z a)9 a). That the event rule fails only there is read off the rules, not a Lean theorem.

No halt through the first changes of era

The simulator runs the machine cell by cell from blank tape; at each full event in a layout it computes from the chain law the last event before a carry would leave the last word, writes that tape and runs on cell by cell through the change of era. 60 changes of era, 8.0·1029 events in all (each at least one step), 20,232,708 steps run cell by cell, no halt; at the end the chain has 17 words, 124 digits in all. Before each jump it checks the law on random events of the era, rebuilt and run cell by cell: 20,803 events, 0 mismatches. What a carry does at a defect

A defect is one erased cell in a separator (after Z 11 read as 01, after a W 01 as 00, after a U 1011 as 1001). When a carry of c > 0 reaches it, the separator is restored and the carry goes on as c − 3. In 3·107 steps 26,504 consecutive pairs of full events follow the law, 3 of them crossing a defect, 0 mismatches; 19 events are not covered (a carry leaves the chain or meets an undecoded defect).

heuristic Evidence only

Growth

Each new 110 at the front costs about 2 to 3 times the steps of the previous one, the signature of a counter; the rest of the tape changes everywhere between visits, so the counter's digits are not simply blocks.

open Open

What a change of era hands on

At the end of an era the words are at or near their largest values; after the change they hold small values, the boundaries between words have moved by a digit, the last word has one digit more, and N has either gained 1 (the change is one event, and two words disappear) or lost 2 to 9 base-3 digits, depending on N mod 9 and more, which become new words. A rule for this hand-over, and whether a change of era can ever halt, are open.

Half-digit shifts

The other kind of defect, a separator missing altogether or a separator after Z read as a, ad, zd or ac, makes the words after it be read again with W and U swapped when a carry meets it. Its law is not decoded.
