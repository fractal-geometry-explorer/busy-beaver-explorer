Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

The tape holds two binary counters, U up and V down. When V runs out the machine rebuilds V from the bottom digits of U; the rebuild is now computed exactly as a map on the counters.

Status as of 24 November 2026.

In short

Decided by an outside Rocq proof: it never halts. Busycoq pull request 58 (Dennis Koshta, 2026-11-05) proves this in Rocq, and we compiled it here with Coq 8.20.1 on busycoq's BB6 branch: its only axiom is functional extensionality from Coq's standard library (Print Assumptions), and its transition table is this machine. The file proves it for the left-right mirror image and transfers it with busycoq's flip lemma. It is their proof, on busycoq's definitions of a machine and of halting, and busycoq's maintainer has not yet reviewed it. Everything else on this page is what this repository found on its own, which by itself leaves the question open.

The question is open here. Lean proves the ticks; the exact macro map finds no halt in the first 109624 steps, and every halt it can produce comes from one walk over the counter's digits running off its top.

Where a halt would come from. Only from a rebuild after V reaches 0; which rebuilds halt is listed for small counters.

The tick rules of the two counters

For each of the headers 0110101, 0110111 the run uses, two theorems: with V odd, L 00000 (00001)^j 00 D>0110101 01 R becomes L 00001 (00000)^j 00 D>0110101 11 R in 10 * j + 38 steps; with V having i + 1 trailing zeros, L 00000 (00001)^j 00 D>0110101 (11)^(i+1) 01 R becomes L 00001 (00000)^j 00 D>0110101 (01)^(i+1) 11 R in 8 * i + 10 * j + 46 steps. That is U + 1 and V − 1 for every number j of carries and i + 1 of borrows and every tape L, R beyond (proved along the symbolic run of tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py).

The macro map and its simulator

At each moment V reaches 0 the tape is the clean configuration 0^inf F U 00 D> H (11)^n 1 0^inf (F the counter's top word), so the state is (H, n, N) with N the up-counter read as one number. py/rebuild.py writes that tape, runs the rebuild (tick runs of its sub-phases jumped by the proved tick rules, the rest cell by cell in C) to the next clean tick configuration (H', n', N', V'), and then adds the V' ticks of the phase by arithmetic: next state (H', n', N' + V'), steps by the closed formula of the tick costs. Tested: its first 4 states and step counts, up to about 1054 steps, equal those of py/counters.py (itself tested against tools/c/tmrun.c).

A faster exact rebuild

py/fastrebuild.py runs the same exact rebuild as rebuild.py (tick runs jumped by the proved tick rules, the rest cell by cell), but its C loop stops only where state D reads 0 between the gap and one of the two headers, instead of at each of the millions of 0s it reads in state D, and it decodes and rewrites the counters with byte slices. Rebuild 6 from blank tape (V of 2,176 digits) took more than 25 minutes and takes 4 s. Tested: the first 7 macro states from blank tape (to about 10991 steps) equal those of the family's own code, state by state.

py/fastrebuild.py run

Four kinds of rebuild, two of them in closed form

Write the counter as its top word (1 or 1000x) and L digits U. A (header 0110101, top 1) reads every digit: new header 0110111, top 10000, all L digits 0, V gains 1 digit, and δ = 2L − U, so V' = 2n+1 − 2L + U. C (header 0110111, top 1) reads the j digits of a walk from the bottom: a first pair 01 or 10 stops it (j = 2); otherwise add 1 to U if its lowest pair is 11 and stop at the first pair whose upper digit is 1 (j = 2k). Then the new header is 0110101, the top 10000 over L − j zero digits, V gains 5j/2 + 1 digits and δ = c + 2L−j − (U >> j), with c depending only on the j digits read (U >> j > 0). B (header 0110111, top 1000x) and D (header 0110101, top 1000x) read every digit and leave N' = 2. Checked against the exact rebuild: A and C for every U < 210 with n − L in {0, 1, 3}; in the run, A twice and C three times (walks of 34, 444 and 8,182 digits), all as stated.

py/fastrebuild.py kinds 10

Where the rebuilds halt: the walk runs off the top

For every U < 2L, L = 3 .. 9, both values of x and n − L in {0, 1, 2, 3} (112 cases): B halts exactly for the U whose walk (the one of C) does not stop below the top of U (the all-ones U aside), when x = 0 or L is even, and never when x = 1 and L is odd; D never halts when n − L ≥ 2, and with n − L = 1 halts exactly where B does when L is even and never when L is odd. A never halts (every U < 210), nor did C for any U < 214 with n = L + 1. (D with n − L = 0 has other halting sets; no D rebuild of the run has n = L.) So the run halts only at a B or D rebuild whose counter has no 1 in the upper place of any pair, after the walk's carry. That is the invariant of the outside proof: busycoq PR 58's walls carry marked 1 bs, a 1 at an odd position of the digits.

heuristic Evidence only

The walks in the run

The kinds follow each other as A, B, C, D, then A or C again (D went to A once and to C once). The two B rebuilds of the run stopped their walk after 12 of 23 and 444 of 619 digits; the D rebuild with n − L = 1 (L = 3,286, even) after 16. A halt needs a walk that runs off the top, which for random digits has probability about 2−L/2, and L grows about threefold per rebuild. A heuristic only, now superseded by the outside proof.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not decided by this repository (the outside Rocq proof of busycoq PR 58 decides it). The kinds A and C are in closed form apart from the constant c of C, and the halting rebuilds are described for small counters; what is missing here is c as a formula, the maps of B and D, and a proof of any of it in Lean.
