Nobody knows, but it comes down to a Collatz-like map. The tape is a wall of 0s that the head eats three cells per sweep; when the wall is used up it is rebuilt about 4/3 as long, and a counter y moves by +4, +6 or −1 depending on the wall's length mod 3. Lean proves the machine never halts unless y ever hits 0. In a million rebuilds y has only climbed, to almost three million.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from B1 (writes 1, moves R): B>1 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.5). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

After 109 steps the tape spans 44,644 cells (32,717 left of the start, 176 right), with a density of 1s of 0.322.

Structure

What the tape is made of

All the action is on the left: the right end of the tape never moves after the first few hundred steps. Look at the tape whenever the head, in state A, stands on the leftmost cell it has ever visited, with nothing grown yet. It is always the same shape, which we call a wall C z y:

From the left: the head on a 0, a 0 10, then a wall of z zeros, then y copies of 10, and a final 0 1. Here z = 5 and y = 4. The real run has walls (0, 0), (1, 4), (5, 10), (12, 9), (17, 13), … at steps 7, 44, 202, 257, 490, …

The basic move

One sweep eats three 0s

Between walls the head sweeps right over a growing block of 10s on the left and back. Each sweep writes two more 10s there and uses up three 0s of the wall (state B runs right, D runs back left, A starts the next sweep):

A>0 (10)^x 0 10 0^(z+3) → A>0 (10)^(x+2) 0 10 0^z in 4x + 11 steps (sweep). The sweep never looks at the 10s on the right. So a wall of z = 3k + r zeros takes k sweeps, 4k² + 7k steps, and leaves r = 0, 1 or 2 zeros and x = 2k grown 10s.

The end of a wall

When the wall is gone it is rebuilt longer

With fewer than three 0s left, the head turns the 2k grown 10s back into 0s, walks to the right end and back, and a new wall begins: the grown part becomes the new wall, about twice the 2k, and the 10s on the right change by a few, depending on the 0s that were left:

The last two need y ≥ 1 (there must be a 10 on the right to use). So one wall to the next is the map

(3k, y) → (4k+1, y+4)   (3k+1, y) → (4k+5, y+6)   (3k+2, y) → (4k+8, y−1)

proved in Lean for every k and y (next_sound), with the exact step count.

The emergent behaviour

A Collatz-like map with a counter

The wall length z is multiplied by about 4/3 at every rebuild, and what happens next depends on z mod 3, that is on the last digit of z in base 3. That is the shape of a Collatz problem: the digits of a number being repeatedly multiplied by 4/3 look random, but nobody can prove anything about them. The counter y is the running score: +4 when the digit is 0, +6 when it is 1, −1 when it is 2. The first walls:

This is the same kind of object as bbchallenge's Antihydra (n → ⌊3n/2⌋, a counter +2 on even, −1 on odd), with 4/3 in place of 3/2 and a much stronger upward drift: on average y gains (4 + 6 − 1)/3 = 3 per step. In 106 steps (Python, py/orbit.py; z then has 124,940 digits) the three digits came 332,673 / 333,716 / 333,611 times and y reached 2,999,377, never dropping below 4 after the first step.

The danger

Where it could halt

The machine halts on F reading a 1. Inside the walls that never happens. It could only happen after the map breaks down: a wall with y = 0 (no 10s left on the right) and z not a multiple of 3. Then the head uses up the final 0 1 and the tape takes another shape; from such a configuration the machine sometimes halts (from C 92 0 after 54,805 steps) and sometimes not (from C 8 0, no halt in 106 steps).

Lean proves (not_halts_of_orbit, orb_exit): if the orbit of (0, 0) never reaches y = 0 with z not divisible by 3, the machine never halts. y can only go down one step at a time, by the digit 2, so it would need a run of about y more 2s than 0s and 1s. If the digits are like dice, the chance of ever getting from height y down to 0 is about 0.335y: after a million steps, about 10−1,400,000. Turning “like dice” into a proof is the open Collatz-type problem.

Heuristic
py/orbit.py, README.md
Why an exit looks hopeless

If z mod 3 behaves like a fair die, y is a random walk with steps +4, +6, −1 (drift +3). From height y it ever reaches 0 with probability about 0.335y; y is 2,999,377 after 106 steps and was never below 4. Every exit would still have to halt from C z 0, which only some do (C 92 0 halts after 54,805 steps; C 8 0 does not in 106).
