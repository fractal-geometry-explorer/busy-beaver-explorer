Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1RC_0LC1RE_1RA0LD_1LB0RD_0RF---_0RA0RF

Nobody knows. This machine keeps a binary counter in the gaps between its blocks of 1s, two 0s for a 0 digit and three for a 1, and counts it down by one every time the head comes back to the right end. Lean now proves that ordinary step for any number of blocks; the open part is what happens in the rare visits where the counter runs out and reloads itself.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in E reading 1. E is entered only from B1 (writes 1, moves R): B>1 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

Macro structure

A counter written in the gaps

Stop the machine every time the head, in state A, reaches the right end. The tape is a row of blocks of 1s, and between two blocks there are two or three 0s (rarely one). Read each gap as a digit: 00 is a 0, 000 is a 1 (and a single 0 is a 2). The digits, lowest on the right, are a binary counter.

Every visit of the right end subtracts one from this counter, the way you would by hand: the lowest digit that is not 0 goes down by one, and every 0 below it becomes a 1. The blocks shift with the digits: the block left of the changed digit gains 3 cells, the block right of it gains 3, the blocks after that lose 1 each, and the last block loses 4. In the run, 36,604 of the first 36,790 visits are exactly this, and Lean proves it.

The head does the subtraction by walking left from the right end. On the way it rewrites every block it crosses into a thinned-out form, 1 0 1 0 1 0, until it meets the first 000. There it turns, and on the way back it fills the blocks in again, with the gaps and lengths shifted by a few cells.

How one visit works

A sweep to the left, a refill to the right

Between two visits of the right end the head makes exactly one trip to the left and one trip back (checked for every visit up to 109 steps). On the way left, states C and D take the 1s two at a time: each pair 11 becomes 01, so a block turns into the thinned-out form 0 1 0 1 …. What happens at the left end of a block depends only on its length and the gap before it:

So the sweep runs left through the trailing odd blocks behind 00 gaps — the trailing 0 digits of the counter — and stops at the first 000, the lowest digit 1. On the way back, state A fills the thinned-out blocks in again, and in doing so the 000 becomes 00 and every 00 it passes becomes 000: binary subtraction of one. Lean proves this whole visit, dec_general, by induction on the number of blocks crossed, for every block length (from 7 cells up; the shortest cases follow other paths).

What makes it hard

Reloads, and counters inside counters

The last block loses 4 cells every other step, and the counter itself runs down. When either runs out, something else happens (115 times in the first 109 steps): the machine creates a new digit further left, a single-0 gap with a new block of 3, sets every digit below it to 1, and goes on counting down from a bigger number. These reloads are themselves counted by the digits further left, so the counter is a nest of counters. Late in the run the block lengths contain runs like 15 31 63 127 255 511 1023 2047, the fingerprints of that nesting, and the leftmost blocks freeze: they never change again.

The machine can only halt if state E reads a 1. An ordinary decrement never does that, so the answer hides in the reload events, which are not yet written as an exact rule. Lean proves the ordinary decrement for any number of blocks; the reloads are the next thing to prove.

What is still missing

The rare visits, and an invariant

Of the 36,790 visits in the first 109 steps, 36,604 are instances of the two decrement theorems. The other 186 are where the interesting things happen: the sweep stops at an even block, or behind short blocks, or the last block is used up, or the sweep reaches the far left. These reload the counter and create the single-0 gaps. A single-0 gap is dangerous: if a sweep ever reached an odd block behind it, the machine would halt. In the run the block after a single 0 is always even when the next gap is 00, and odd when it is 000, which keeps sweeps away from it.

To prove the machine runs forever one needs a description of all the tapes that can occur, closed under every kind of visit, that excludes this pattern. Descriptions that look only at a few neighbouring blocks are not enough: building tapes from the neighbouring pairs or triples seen in the run and running one visit soon produces tapes that halt. The real constraint reaches further along the tape, through the nested reloads.

Macro structure

A record that freezes from the left

The tape is a list of blocks of 1s separated by gaps of two or three 0s (rarely one). At step 5·108 it begins

1^3 0^2 1^6 0^3 1^8 0^3 1^22 0^2 1^6 0^2 1^26 0^3 1^22 0^2 1^3 0^3 1^55 0^2 1^20 0 1^6 0^2 1^15 0^2 1^69 … 1^20260 0^3 1^3545 0^2 1^1257 …

The first seven blocks were already exactly like this at step 6·104, the first thirteen at 4.5·105: the left part freezes, while the blocks on the right keep changing and growing. During a sweep part of the tape is held in a sparse form, (10)^n, which state A fills back into 1s, and states D and F erase blocks as they cross them; Lean proves these three moves for every length.

Only 227 blocks exist at 109 steps. The rule for the active blocks is not known yet; a halt needs state E reading a 1.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.523).

After 109 steps the tape spans 73,592 cells (27 left of the start, 56,453 right), with a density of 1s of 0.996.

Kernel
lean/Shifts.lean (written by bbtools.symtm.prove)
Shift rules

For every length n of the run and any tape around it: L D> 1^n R reaches L 0^n D> R; L F> 1^n R reaches L 0^n F> R; L A> (10)^n R reaches L (11)^n A> R. These are the moves the head makes across the long uniform stretches of the tape.

Kernel
dec_last, lean/Rules.lean (from py/rules.py)
The lowest step of the counter

For every length 2y+2 of the last block and x of the block before it, and any tape further left: L 0 1^x 000 1^(2y+2) A> 0^inf reaches L 0 1^(x+3) 00 1^(2y+2) A> 0^inf in 6y+16 steps (the lowest digit of the counter goes from 1 to 0).

Kernel
lean/Rules.lean (from py/rules.py, written by bbtools.symtm.prove)
The sweep, block by block

For every length: the leftward sweep crosses an odd block behind the gap 00, leaving it in the sparse form 0 1 0 (01)^w (p_pass); it stops and turns right at an odd block behind 000 (p_stop_odd) or at any even block (p_stop_even); at an odd block behind a single 0 it halts (p_halt: state E reads a 1). The pieces of the rightward refill (p_fill_first, p_fill_two, p_fill_mid, p_fill_end) are proved the same way.

Kernel
dec_general, lean/Decrement.lean
The ordinary decrement, for any number of blocks

For any tape L on the left, any a ≥ 1, m ≥ 3 and any list of b1, …, br ≥ 3 (r ≥ 0): L 000 1^(2a+1) 00 1^(2b1+1) … 00 1^(2br+1) 00 1^(2m) A> 0^∞ reaches L 111 00 1^(2a+4) 000 1^(2b1) … 000 1^(2br) 000 1^(2m-4) A> 0^∞: the lowest digit 1 of the counter becomes 0 and the 0s below it become 1s, the blocks change by +3, +3, −1 each and −4. Proved by induction over the list of blocks from the pieces above.

Computed
c/visits.c
One visit is one sweep and one refill

Up to 109 steps (36,752 visits of a new cell at the right end) the rightward pass never turns back before it reaches the end: C never reads a 1 straight after A, and B never reads a 0 straight after A inside the tape.

Computed
py/visits.py
Which visits the theorems cover

Of the 36,790 visits of the right end up to 109 steps (as tmrun ... at A R lists them), 18,360 are instances of dec_last and 18,244 of dec_general. The other 186 are the reload events: 114 sweeps stop at an even block, 48 at an odd block with short blocks behind it, 16 with the last block too short, 8 at the left end. None reaches the halting case.

Heuristic
py/visits.py; searches described in the README
No small local invariant

Reading the tape as tokens (block length up to 8 exactly, longer ones by parity, and the gap after it), the sets of neighbouring pairs and triples seen in the run are not closed: running one visit on words built from them produces new pairs and triples, and repeating this soon reaches words that halt. Closed tape languages from windows of cells, of run-length tokens with parity, and with depth from the end were also tried and allow a halt. So a proof needs an invariant that sees further than a few blocks.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. A halt needs a sweep to reach an odd block behind a single 0 (or the refill to meet 0 1 1). The ordinary decrement never does that; the reload visits would need an exact rule and an invariant closed under all visits.
