Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1RA_0LC0RA_1LF0LD_1LE0RC_1LB1LD_1RB---

Nobody knows. Started on a blank tape, this Turing machine has run for 6·1010 steps without stopping. We now know exactly which pattern on the tape would stop it, and that pattern has never appeared; what is missing is a proof that it never will. This page runs the real machine in your browser, shows the shape of its run, and lists what is known, each result marked with how it was checked.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from C0 (writes 1, moves L): 1 C>0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

What it does

Content that drifts right, and zones of growing pattern

Read the tape as lengths of blocks of 1s separated by single 0s. Almost all blocks have odd length: 1, 3, 5, sometimes 7. Next to the left end stands a zone of the word 1 3 1 5 5 repeated; then a zone of a 15-block word, a zone of a 45-block word (20, 60 and 180 cells: each word three times as long as the one before), and further right long runs of blocks of 3 separated by a single 1 or 5, the runs longer the further right. At 9.3·108 steps:

3 (1 3 1 5 5)^29 1 3 1 5 1 5 (3 3 1 3 1 3 5 5 3 5 5 3 1 3 1)^254 … (3)^8 5 (3)^12 5 (3)^7 1 … (3)^26 5 (3)^31 1

The head goes back and forth across the whole tape. One round trip moves all of this two cells to the right. The right end does not move: it swallows what arrives. The left end makes up for it: every three round trips it adds 4 cells, every six a new word 1 3 1 5 5. So the left zone grows steadily while the rest slides into the right end.

From time to time the head, on its way left, turns round in the middle of the tape, at a defect (next section). It then bounces between the defect and the right end, hundreds to billions of steps, and each bounce moves the defect one block to the left. What it leaves to the right of the defect is new content of the next kind: the word 1 3 1 5 5 three times becomes the 15-block word, that one becomes the 45-block word, and so on. A reset is a defect that travels all the way to the left end and rebuilds the whole left zone; resets came at steps 6,443; 52,596; 65,233; 1,072,500; 4.3·107; 1.1·108; 2.0·108; 9.2·108; 1.9·1010; 6.2·1010, with no regular spacing. Defects can sit inside the stretch of an earlier defect, so the zones are nested, and their number grows without bound.

The sweeps

Two cells at a time

Going right the machine is in state A and does one thing: every 0 it meets moves one cell to the right. Three 0s in a row (the right end) turn it round.

Going left it alternates between states E and D and handles the tape in pairs of cells, E on the right cell, D on the left one:

Example, blocks 5 5 before a left sweep, written with each 0 on the left cell of its pair: 01 11 11 | 01 11 11 becomes 11 10 10 | 11 10 10, the blocks 3 1 3 1 (the first 1s join the block on their left). The next right sweep moves each 0 one cell: the 0s are on left cells again. As long as every block has odd length, every 0 sits on a left cell and only the first two rows are used. A block of even length, or two 0s in a row, puts the following 0s on right cells: that is a defect, and the third row turns the head.

The first two rows are proved for any sequence of pairs (sweep), the rows as single rules, and the sweeps over the zone words for any number of copies (a_w0, d_w0, a_w1, d_w1 and five older rules), all checked by Lean's kernel.

Can it halt?

Only from 1 0 0 0, met from the right

The machine has one halting transition: state F reading a 1. Lean proves that this can only happen in one way: the left sweep (state E) lands on a 0, the two cells to its left are 0 as well, and the cell before them is a 1. Three steps later F reads that 1. So: the machine halts if and only if a left sweep meets 1 0 0 0 with E on the last 0 (pre_of_halts, halts_of_1000, nonhalting_of_no_1000).

How close does the run come? Two 0s in a row inside the tape are rare. They arise when the left sweep lands on the 0 behind a block of length 1 (0 1 0 becomes 0 0 1, then the head bounces), and the next time the sweep comes by they are removed (row 11 00 of the table). In 6.1·1010 steps E stood on the second of two inner 0s 285 times; after step 3.6·107 the cells around were the same every time, 0 1 1 1 0 [0] 1 0. Inside the tape E never had two more 0s to its left.

Copies of real tapes with a cell flipped, removed or added never halted (3,600 tried). But the shape alone is not safe: the six-cell tape 111011 halts after 655 steps, and about 1 in 2,000 random tapes of 30 blocks halts. The real run avoids the halting pattern for a reason that is not yet understood.

Why is it still open? A proof needs a description of all tapes the run can reach that is closed under the machine's steps and excludes 1 0 0 0 in front of E. The tape is nested to unbounded depth, so a finite list of shapes with a few counters does not exist, and four automatic searches for a regular description (automata that remember a window of cells or of blocks) failed: whether a bounce is harmless depends on how the grid of pairs, which is fixed by the far right end, lies relative to the defect, and a window does not see that.

Kernel
e_pair, sweep, lean/Sweep.lean
The regular left sweep, for any tape of pairs

State E on the right cell of a pair, the pair being 01 or 11: after 2 or 4 steps the pair is 11 or 10 and E stands on the right cell of the next pair to the left. By induction the same holds for any list of such pairs, so on blocks of ones of odd length the left sweep is a fixed rewriting and never stops.

Kernel
prehalt, pre_of_halts, nonhalting_of_no_1000, halts_of_1000, lean/Sweep.lean
A halt comes from exactly one pattern

Three steps before any halt the machine is in state E on a 0 with 1 0 0 to its left (E0, B0, C0, then F reads the 1). So if the run from blank tape never shows 1 0 0 E>0, the machine never halts; and from that pattern it does halt.

c/pert.c, c/rnd.c, py/small.py
Perturbed real tapes do not halt; small tapes of blocks do

Tapes of the real run at three moments (3.8·106, 4.5·107, 2.3·108 steps) with one cell flipped, one cell deleted, a 0 or a 1 inserted, two cells flipped, or a 1 cleared next to a 0: 3,600 tapes, none halts within 2·106 steps. Tapes made of blocks of ones separated by single 0s are not safe in general: blocks 3, 2 (111011, state A on the first cell) halt after 655 steps, and of 2,000 random tapes of n blocks of lengths 1 to 5 those that halt within 3·106 steps number 62, 14, 0, 4, 2, 0, 1, 1 for n = 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 20, 30 (odd lengths 1, 3, 5, 7 only: 0, 29, 0, 3, 3, 0, 0, 0). Random bit strings halt often and early (they contain three 0s in a row). So, as for the two sibling machines, tapes of the same shape can halt; a proof has to use more of the real tape than its shape.

Heuristic
py/turns.py, py/micro.py, py/blocks.py
Content drifts right; defects start inner bounces; the tape is nested

Right sweep and regular left sweep together complement the pairs and shift them by one pair, so the content moves two cells right per round trip against a right end that stands still and consumes it. A defect (the left sweep landing on a 0: an even block or two 0s) is an inner left wall: the head bounces between it and the right end, the defect moves left one block per bounce, and what is to its right becomes new content with longer runs of blocks of 3. Defects nest, so at 109 steps the tape is a sequence of zones: (1 3 1 5 5)^a, the 15-block word 254 times, a 45-block word (the three words have 20, 60 and 180 cells), then runs (3)^k separated by a 1 or a 5 with k growing to the right. Composition of the proved rules, checked against traces; not formalised.

Computed
py/far.py, py/rlectl.py, py/realabs.py, py/rlectl2.py, py/realabs2.py
No closed set found (four searches)

(1) The closed-set method of the sibling machines needs a tape zone w^N T u^M tail with a bounded zone; here the number of zones grows, so there is no finite list of nodes. (2) Finite-automata reduction with a DFA that tracks parity and the last k ≤ 6 cells, on either side: the blank start is accepted as possibly halting. (3) A closed tape language whose states are windows of K runs (lengths exact up to a cap, then parity): K = 2, 3, 4 reach a halting abstract configuration, K = 5 exceeds 3·106 abstract configurations. Reason in both: a window forgets what lies beyond it, and windows from the left end get glued to inner ones (the real run visits 4,821 abstract configurations where the closure has 406,469). (4) The same with the far end of the tape, the number of even blocks and a parity added to the state: small sizes reach a halting abstract configuration, larger ones exceed 3·106 configurations; the abstraction cannot tell the long real tape from short block tapes, which do halt.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. What is missing is an invariant that excludes 1 0 0 E>0: two 0s in a row arise inside the tape only when the left sweep lands on the 0 after a block of length 1, and they are removed again by the next bounce unless a third 0 is adjacent. A proof has to control where the grid of pairs (fixed by the right end) stands relative to such places; a regular language with windows does not do it.
