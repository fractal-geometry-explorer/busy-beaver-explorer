1RB0LE_0RC0RA_1RD1RF_1LA0LD_1LD0RF_1RB---

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 13 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Lean proves the sweeps, the ticks of the two common families for chains of any length and blocks of any size, one rebuild for every length of its last block, and that every halt must come from B>0 1 1 (kernel). It also proves no halt in the first 107 steps (native_decide). A C program checks the block form at all 30,932 right-end turns of the first 1010 steps.

What it does. The tape is 1010… cut into blocks (10)n by single extra cells, the separators (1 or 0). A round trip from the right end, a tick, sweeps left over some separators, turns at one, and sweeps back, complementing everything it crosses. Which separator it turns at, and how the block lengths change, depends on the separators and the parities of the blocks; two families of rules cover 30,528 of the 30,932 ticks of the first 1010 steps. The last block grows by one or two each tick, so the right edge grows smoothly.

The rebuilds. 238 of those ticks reach the left end instead. There the head works for a long time and rebuilds the front of the list; the same sequences of lists then recur with more blocks on the right, on larger and larger scales.

Where a halt would come from. Only F reading a 1 halts. Two steps before a halt the machine must be at B>0 1 1 (halt_route): E is entered only from A reading a 1, which leaves a 0 to E's right, so E>1 1 never occurs. In block terms B would have to stand on the last 0 of a block followed by a separator 1; in the run F always reads a 0.

No halt in the first 10,000 steps

The list simulator of the shared library, proved to agree with the machine's semantics, runs 10,000 steps from blank tape without a halt, evaluated by Lean's kernel.

blank_runs_kernel, lean/Blank.lean

Every way to a halt

Two steps before any halt the machine is at B>0 1 1. In particular E>1 1, which would halt (halt_E), is never reached.

halt_route, lean/Route.lean (written by py/route.py)

The sweeps

Moving left, A crosses 1010 in four steps and complements it; moving right, B crosses 10 in two steps and writes 01. Both for any number of copies.

sweep_left, sweep_right, lean/Rules.lean

Two whole ticks

From the right-end turn back to the next one: x | e becomes x . e+2 for every even e (8k+8 steps for e = 2k), and x | o | o' becomes x . o+1 | o'+1 for all odd o, o' ≥ 3 (written in the block notation: lengths of (10)-blocks, | a separator 1, . a 0).

tick_last, tick_oo, lean/Rules.lean

Tick chains of any length

The two tick families, for chains of any length and blocks of any size: x | a . b1 . c1 . … . bk . ck . d . e with all blocks even (tick_chain) and x | p | a . b1 . c1 . … . d . e with p, a odd and the rest even (tick_chain_oo), from one right-end turn to the next, for every list of pairs, every size and every tape x. With tick_last and tick_oo (no pairs) this covers the families x | e (. e . e)k and x | o | o (. e . e)k for every k. The proof splits a tick into five pieces: entering, one pair on the way left, the turn, one pair on the way right, and leaving. Each piece is a symbolic run with unknown tape around it, and the passes over the pairs are inductions on the list.

tick_chain, tick_chain_oo, left_pass, right_pass, lean/Chain.lean (written by py/chain.py)

One rebuild, for every last block

The left-end tick at step 22,986, 3|3|12|15|1.48.94 to 88|6|2|13|21|94, holds with the last block of any even length 2v+4 (8v + 16,500 steps). The rebuild reads the block 48 cell by cell, so that block cannot be made a variable this way.

rebuild_3_3_12_15_1_48, lean/Rebuild.lean (written by py/rebuild.py)

A first run 4 cells longer changes nothing else, for every way of entering it

In the table of 32883 the head can step onto the last cell of the first run of the tape in five states, on a 0 or a 1. For each of these ten entries the outcome for a run of n + 4 cells is the outcome for n cells with one more copy of a 4-cell word: same exit state, same cells left behind. Proved for every bounce with any tape further left, and for every sweep with n = 4a + 5 to 4a + 8, all a. It holds for the three sister machines after renaming the states (and mirroring).

The block form

At every right-end turn of the first 1010 steps (30,932 turns) the tape is (10)n0 s1 (10)n1 … sk (10)nk A>1 1 with single separators si; the program stops if not.

The tick rules

Each tick of the first 1010 steps, run symbolically with the last block lengths as variables, follows a rule valid for all lengths of the same parities: 30,528 ticks are in the families x | e (. e . e)k and x | o | o (. e . e)k (the separator 1 becomes 0, the 0s after it become 1s, the blocks after it change by +1, −1, …, +1, +1), 30,674 follow one of 61 patterns, 238 reach the left end and 20 have a block of length 1 that the templates do not cover.

Growth shape: sqrt/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.389). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.554).

Proposed family: F06, block-list counters

The description grows slowly: 20 to 27 level-2 tokens at every record from 2.6·108 to 1.7·1010 steps, and 11 to 45 runs in the macro simulator (blocks of 4 cells) at the sampled records up to 1017 steps. The machine shares its table with 1RB1RF_1LC0LB_1RE0LD_1LB0RF_0RA0RC_1RE--- (states renamed, some of the four mirrored; related.json), started in another state. On the screen the four runs show the same list of blocks at the same moments. For example near step 6.4·107 all four tapes read blocks of 3, 1,546, 39, 1,753, 117, 1,467 and 997 pairs 01 and then a last block, of 4,405 pairs in 1RB1RF_1LC0LB… and of 4,409 pairs here. The four last blocks differ by 2, 4 and 6 pairs and the records come at slightly different steps. This is an observation at the sampled records: it is not a proof that the runs correspond, and nothing about halting is carried over. For 1RB1RF_1LC0LB… the right end is proved in Lean to be a base-3 counter on the separators of such a list (bb6-1RB1RF_1LC0LB/lean/Counter.lean). Those theorems are about that machine's table with its own state names; they apply here only after the renaming is carried through, which has not been done. A list of blocks with a base-3 counter is F06.

the README, section “Family screen”

The four machines would halt together if the first run never gets short

From the Lean theorems, the four runs stay shifted copies, and the four machines halt together or not at all, provided the first run of the run of 40600 has at least 3 cells every time the head enters it. That is observed (at least 5 cells at each of the 57,701 entries in the first 2·1010 steps, and growing) but not proved: a bounce removes 1 or 2 cells and nothing local bounds the number of bounces between two sweeps. So halting together is not established.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The ordinary ticks of the two common families are proved for every length. Still missing: the other tick patterns (166 of the 30,932 ticks of the first 1010 steps, besides the 238 rebuilds), rules for the left-end rebuilds, and an invariant of the block lists, kept by all rules, that keeps B off 0 1 1. A rebuild reads its blocks cell by cell. It also comes back to the right in a state that depends on the blocks there, so it does not split into the pass pieces of the ordinary ticks. A general rebuild rule would have to follow the counter it runs on the block after the turning point.
