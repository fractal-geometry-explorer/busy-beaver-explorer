What is known about 1RB1RE_1RC0RF_1LD1RA_1LB1LD_1RC0RC_---0LD

1RB1RE_1RC0RF_1LD1RA_1LB1LD_1RC0RC_---0LD

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The tape is a list of numbers that each sweep translates between two codes: runs 1^(3k+2) separated by single 0s when the head is at the right end, runs 1^(3a+1) separated by 0^(3b+2) when it is at the left end. The numbers themselves look irregular. Measured, not proved. Halting needs F on a 0, entered only from B on a 1; F has always found a 1.

The halting configurations

The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (F0); the generated statement covers every configuration.

step_none_iff, lean/Blank.lean

Where a halt can come from

If the machine halts on its third step from a configuration, that configuration is one of: A with 0 under the head, 1 right, 0 two right. So a run can reach a halt only through one of these patterns, and a proof that the machine never halts only has to rule them out. The proof splits on the state and the five cells around the head.

halt_route, lean/Route.lean (written by py/route.py)

No halt in the first 10,000 steps

The list simulator of the shared library, proved to agree with the machine's semantics, runs 10,000 steps from blank tape without a halt, evaluated by Lean's kernel.

blank_runs_kernel, lean/Blank.lean

Shift rules of the sweeps

Lean proves, for every count and every tape around, the shift rule of the leftward sweep: D turns a run of n 1s into n 0s as it crosses it, and so does B with two 1s travelling ahead of it, 3 steps per cell.

shift_l_D_1, shift_l_B_1, lean/Rules.lean

Regular-language deciders tried (F11)

None of these closes: each reaches a halting configuration in its abstraction, which says nothing about whether the machine halts. n-gram CPS (tools/c/ngramcps.c), equal widths on both sides: a halting local configuration is reached with plain up to width 24, history 1 up to width 22, history 2 up to width 13, history 3 up to width 14, LRU history up to width 3 (larger widths ran out of the 150 s or 60 million configuration budget). n-gram CPS with every cell tagged by its position mod p: fails for p = 2 up to width 20, p = 3 up to width 20, p = 4 up to width 20, p = 6 up to width 20. Run-length windows (the K nearest runs of equal macro cells on each side, run lengths kept exactly below T and mod M above): fails for all 72 parameter sets (cells of 1 to 4 bits, K = 2, 4, 6, T = 2, 4, M = 1, 2, 4). Two-automaton closed tape language by SAT (a deterministic automaton reading each side inward, n states each, CaDiCaL): no solution for n = 2..8; n = 9 not finished in 150 s.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: linear/power

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like a power of the step count other than 1/2 (fitted exponent 0.303). Right of the start it grows in proportion to the step count (the head drifts steadily that way) (fitted exponent 0.891).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

Two codes for one list

At the last right extension before 109 steps (step 911,093,030) the tape is 11,717 runs of 1s separated by single 0s, and 11,715 of the runs have length 3k+2 (up to 47 cells). At the last left extension (step 989,746,492) it is 6,128 runs of 1s, 6,115 of length 3a+1, separated by runs of 0s, 6,124 of length 3b+2, e.g. B>0 1^13 0^5 1^4 0^5 1^4 0^5 1^4 0^2 1^12 0^4 1^16 ….

py/observe.py, tools/c/tmrun.c

Never one step from a halt

The machine halts when F reads a 0, and F is entered only from B reading a 1 (B1 → 0RF). In the first 3·106 steps B reads a 1 413,979 times, always with a 1 to its right.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The two codes are measured; the map a sweep makes on the list of numbers is not written down, and the numbers look pseudo-random, so this may be a chaotic machine.
