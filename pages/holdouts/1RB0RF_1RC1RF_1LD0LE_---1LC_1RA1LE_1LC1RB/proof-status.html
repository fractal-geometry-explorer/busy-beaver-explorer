What is known about 1RB0RF_1RC1RF_1LD0LE_---1LC_1RA1LE_1LC1RB

1RB0RF_1RC1RF_1LD0LE_---1LC_1RA1LE_1LC1RB

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

Open, reduced in Lean to a Collatz-like map, probviously non-halting. The tape is always, at the end of a pass, one block of n ones. The pass sends n to f(n), where f depends on the power of two dividing n+2; the machine halts exactly when the orbit of 4 reaches an n with n+2 a power of two. All of this is proved in Lean for every n (halts_iff, f_none_iff), and the orbit of 4 avoids the halting values for 10,000 passes in Lean and 200,000 in exact arithmetic. The low bits of the orbit behave like random bits, and on that model the chance of ever halting is about 2−80,000.

The halting configurations

The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (D0); the generated statement covers every configuration.

No halt in the first 10,000 steps

The list simulator of the shared library, proved to agree with the machine's semantics, runs 10,000 steps from blank tape without a halt, evaluated by Lean's kernel.

blank_runs_kernel, lean/Blank.lean

Blank tape reaches P(4)

After 9 steps the tape is a single block of four 1s with the head, in state B, on the blank cell to its right: P(4) = 0^inf 1^4 B> 0^inf.

blank_P4, lean/Pass.lean

Every pass, for every n

Write X(a, b, c) for 0^inf 1^a E> 0 1^b 0 1^c 0^inf. P(n+1) reaches X(0, n, 2), and from X(0, b, c) the machine halts or reaches P(g(b, c)) with g(0, c) = c+2 (c even) or (3c+9)/2 (c odd), g(2i+2, c) = c+3i+5, g(1, c) = halt, g(2i+3, c) = g(i, c+i+4). So the pass from P(n) ends at P(f(n)), f(n) = g(n−1, 2), or halts. Proved for all n from 17 symtm rules (an odd zigzag, an even zigzag, their ends) and inductions.

pass, xrun, lean/Macro.lean; the rules in lean/Rules.lean

Halts iff the orbit of 4 hits a halting value

The machine halts from blank tape if and only if the orbit of 4 under f reaches none; and f(n) is none exactly when n+2 is a power of two, at least 4.

halts_iff, f_none_iff, lean/Macro.lean

Closed form, and 200,000 passes

Writing n+2 = 2jm with m odd, f(n) = 2jm + (m+2j−1)/2, except m = 1 (halt) and m = 3 with j even and ≥ 2 (f(n) = 9·2j−1 + 3j/2 + 3); checked against g for every n < 200,000 and against direct simulation of passes for n ≤ 3000. Iterating f from 4 for 200,000 passes (exact integers) never halts; n then has 80,223 bits.

holdouts/1RB0RF_1RC1RF_1LD0LE_---1LC_1RA1LE_1LC1RB/py/study.py, c/passmap.c, py/orbit.py --check

The low bits look random

Over the 200,000 passes the counts of j = v2(n+2) follow the geometric law 2−(j+1) (99,772 / 100,000 for j = 0, 50,060 / 50,000 for j = 1, 25,207 / 25,000 for j = 2, …; largest j = 17), and n grows by e0.2780 per pass against e0.2783 predicted for random low bits.

heuristic Evidence only

Only two kinds of pass can lead to a halt

A short argument (not in Lean): f(n)+2 = 2k needs m(2j+1+1) + 2j + 3 = 2k+1 (or the m = 3 exception, which never gives a power of two). Since 2j+1 ≡ −1 modulo 2j+1+1, the powers of two are ±2i there, and 2j+3 is one of these only for j = 2 (9m+7 = 2k+1) and j = 3 (17m+9 = 2k+1). So halting values have about 1.3 preimage-or-self per binary length, among 2L numbers.

holdouts/1RB0RF_1RC1RF_1LD0LE_---1LC_1RA1LE_1LC1RB/py/study.py (lists them for k ≤ 40)

Probviously never halts

The orbit gains about 0.40 bits per pass, so it spends about 2.5 passes per binary length, each with a chance of about 2−L to land on one of those values. From 80,223 bits on, the expected number of halts left is about 2−80,000. No invariant forbidding a halt is known: the residues of the orbit look equidistributed.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Exactly when the orbit 4, 8, 13, 22, 28, … of f (defined and proved in Lean) reaches an n with n+2 a power of two. This is a Collatz-type question; a proof of non-halting would need control of the low bits of the orbit forever.
