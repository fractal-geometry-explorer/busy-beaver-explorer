What is known about 1RB1LD_0RC0LF_1LD1RE_0LA0RB_0RD1RB_0LD---

1RB1LD_0RC0LF_1LD1RE_0LA0RB_0RD1RB_0LD---

Nobody knows whether this six-state machine halts from blank tape. This page records what is established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

The machine is a pair of counters. The head lives near the start cell and makes round trips: one to the left, where it adds one to a binary counter written in 4-cell digits, and one to the right, where it updates a second, more complicated representation of a counter. The left counter is understood exactly (its three moves are proved in Lean and checked over 8 million visits); the right one is not.

Status as of 12 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. No halt in the first 1010 steps (C simulator). The whole machine is a carry system of eight local rules on the tape, each proved in Lean. It halts exactly when state B comes to stand in front of 0 1 1 1, and the only rule that can bring it there (R1) was used 424 times in the first 1010 steps, each time in front of a 0. The left half is a proved binary counter and blocks of 3-cell digits on the right are proved to count in binary, but the right half as a whole has not been turned into numbers, so the question has not become arithmetic.

The three moves of the left counter

For every n and any tape around them: increment L 1000 (1010)^n 1 D>0 R reaches L 1010 1 (0001)^n E>0 R (n trailing one-digits become zero-digits, the zero-digit before them becomes a one); overflow without prefix 0^inf (1010)^n 1 D>0 R reaches 0^inf 1 0 1 (0001)^n 0 D> R; overflow with the prefix 10 0^inf 1 0 (1010)^n 1 D>0 R reaches 0^inf 1 0 1 0 0 (0100)^n 0 C> R. Each is a theorem about the machine's step function with an exact step count.

left_increment, left_overflow_even, left_overflow_odd, lean/Rules.lean (generated by py/gen_rules.py)

The machine as a carry system: eight local rules

Write > for the head in state B about to read the next cell, and < for the head in state D on the cell before the mark. Between two such moments the machine does exactly one of: R1 >011 → 011>; R2 >0100 → 011>0; R3 >0101 → 0100>; R4 >00 → 0<1; R5 0>1 → <00; L1 10< → <10; L2 00< → 1>0; L3 1< → 0>. Each is a Lean theorem about the machine's step function, with its number of steps (3, 5, 4, 2, 2, 2, 2, 1), for every tape around the cells shown. The cases cover everything B or D can read, except B on a 1 that has a 1 on its left (the halt, next claim).

r1, r2, r3, r4, r5_0, r5_1, l1_0, l1_1, l2, l3, lean/Carry.lean (generated by py/gen_carry.py)

The halting pattern

From B about to read 0 1 1 1 the machine halts four steps later, whatever else is on the tape. Reading the table backwards (an argument on paper, not in Lean), every halt comes about this way: F is entered only from B on a 1, and a B that reads a 1 with a 1 on its left was entered from E on a 1, E from C on a 1, C from B on a 0. In the carry system: the halt is rule R1 applied in front of a 1.

halt_pattern, lean/Carry.lean

Blocks of 3-cell digits count in binary

For every k and any tape around: after a 1, the block (010)^(k+1) 00 hit by B becomes (000)^(k+1) 01, with D back on its first cell, in 10k + 12 steps. Read 010 as the digit one and 000 as zero, lowest digit first: k+1 ones followed by a zero become k+1 zeros followed by a one. This is the carry of the right half; the left half's carry (4-cell digits) is the claim on the three moves of the left counter.

carry, lean/Carry.lean

The left half is a binary counter of round trips

Every time D reads the 0 at cell 1 with a 1 at cell 0 (the head about to visit the left half), the cells from the left end to cell 0 read (10)? w 1 with w a word of 4-cell digits 1010 (one) and 1000 (zero), least significant digit next to the centre. In the first 109 steps this happens 8,388,630 times and every time the number w is one more than the time before, except 45 growth moves, all of the two kinds above: 10+all ones (b digits) becomes all ones with b+1 digits and no prefix, which overflows at the next visit to 10 + b+1 zeros.

c/leftchk.c | py/leftdec.py (see Reproduce)

The left end grows at 2k and 3·2k−1 round trips

Counting the visits above as round trips T = 1, 2, 3, ..., the left end of the tape grows (by two cells) exactly when T is 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, ..., 2k, 3·2k−1, ... up to T = 12,582,912 (all 46 growths in the first 109 steps). A round trip costs 135 to 225 steps on average, so the left end grows like the logarithm of the step count.

R1, the only way to a halt, is rare

R1 is the entry E1 of the table (E reading a 1). In the first 1010 steps it is used 424 times (5 times in the first 103 steps, 18 in 104, 46 in 105, 90 in 106, 148 in 107, 228 in 108, 306 in 109), first at step 77, and each time the cell after the 011 is a 0 (the run does not halt). So a halt can only happen at these rare moments.

The right half: a chain of counter segments

Right of the centre the tape is a chain of segments, each a binary counter with 3-cell digits (one of 100, 010, 001 for a one, depending on the segment, and 000 for a zero), least significant digit nearest the centre, separated by short markers such as 101 and 010 100. The lowest segment changes on every round trip; a segment that overflows carries into the next one. At each growth of the left end the lowest segments are re-laid (one segment gains a digit and changes its phase). The right end grows at irregular values of T (974, 1277, 1405, 1903, ..., 8,360,447, 14,168,192), not at powers of 2. The rule for the re-laying has not been found.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. In the carry system the question reads: does B ever come to stand in front of 0 1 1 1, that is, is rule R1 ever applied in front of a 1? Answering it needs a description of the right half as numbers (which digits sit where when R1 fires), and that has not been found. The community tracker says this machine equals Beaver Math Olympiad problem 10, 'a seven-rule carry system' with rules proved in Rocq; we have not seen that statement, so we cannot say how its seven rules group our eight, nor what arithmetic form it gives the question.
