Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LF_0RC0RB_1LC1LD_1RE0RD_---0RA_1LA0LF

Nobody knows yet. This machine keeps almost all of its tape as a plain 1010... pattern that its head flips back and forth without changing anything; what matters are a dozen small defects, particles, that a little counter at the left end pushes around one at a time, like carries. Lean proves the machine can only halt in a rare event that, so far, has only happened at the far right wall of the tape, four times, early on.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in E reading 0. E is entered only from D0 (writes 1, moves R): D>0 0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.516). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

After 109 steps the tape spans 65,134 cells (49,805 left of the start, 56 right), with a density of 1s of 0.525.

Structure

A sea with particles

Stop the machine at almost any moment and the tape is 1010101010... nearly everywhere. Call a junction a place where two equal cells touch (00 or 11). Junctions are rare and come in small clusters: the particles. Between them lie long stretches of the alternating sea.

The trick of this machine: when the head crosses the sea, in either direction, it complements every cell (0 becomes 1, 1 becomes 0). Moving left, F turns 1s into 0s and on a 0 writes a 1 and passes to A, which turns the next 1 into a 0 and passes back to F. Moving right, a six-step zig-zag of C, D, E, A, B does the same four cells at a time. Complementing an alternating pattern gives an alternating pattern, and it does not move the junctions. So the head can cross any amount of sea without changing anything that matters; the whole state of the machine is the list of particles and the distances between them.

At the left end sits a small gadget (0 0 1 0 0 1 0 1 1 1 0), at the right end a wall of 1s (1^6 0 1^4). In between, after 109 steps, 11 to 15 particles; the seas between them are thousands of cells long.

The basic move

The gadget counts to four, then sends the head deep

The head returns to the left end four times per cycle. Three of those trips are short (22, 34 and 22 steps): they only flip the gadget through its states, like the low bits of a counter. The fourth trip is deep: the head runs right across the sea until a particle turns it back. Every return to the left end adds two cells, so the tape grows by 8 cells per cycle on the left.

When the first particle is P = 0 0 1 0 1 1, one cycle does exactly this (Lean, cycleP, for all x and y):

0^inf F> 0010010111 0 01 (0101)^(x+4) 001011 (1010)^y  R
   -- 40x + 330 steps -->
0^inf F> 0010010111 0 01 (0101)^(x+3) 001011 (1010)^(y+3)  R

The gadget moves 8 cells left, P moves 12 cells left, so the sea between them shrinks by 4 cells per cycle and the sea behind P grows by 12. The rest of the tape, R, is never looked at. After x cycles P reaches the gadget, they collide, new particles come out, and the deep trips go further.

The cast

The particles

Sampled once per cycle, the particles keep their shape for long stretches and each moves a fixed number of cells per cycle when the deep trip reaches it:

gadget0 0 1 0 0 1 0 1 1 1 08 left (the tape grows)

Tstretches of the second sea 1110 1110 ...8 left

The far particles are only reached when the near ones have been used up, the way a carry reaches the high digits of a counter only rarely. That is why the tape grows like the square root of the step count: each cycle costs a trip across a tape that keeps getting longer.

The question

Where it could halt

The machine stops when E reads a 0. E is entered only from D reading a 0; D is entered either from C reading a 1 (C writes 1, moves left, and then E would read that very 1: no halt) or from D reading a 1. So two steps before a halt, D must be erasing a block of 1s to its right (Lean, halt_needs_drun), and the halt comes if that block ends in 0 0.

Such a D-run starts when C, moving left, meets two 1s side by side. In the sea and among the particles that never happens. In the first 109 steps there were exactly four D-runs, at steps 130, 309, 2,306,898 and 2,380,763: each time a trip had carried all the way to the right end and D erased the wall block 1^6; the block of 1s after it saved the machine. Since step 8.4·106 no trip has come near the wall.

So the question is whether some future carry reaches the wall and finds it in a fatal shape. Nobody knows.

A twin

The same table, twice on the list

1RB0RA_1LC0RA_0LD0LC_1RD1RE_1LF0LE_---0LB is this machine seen in a mirror, with the states renamed, started in this machine's state F. Its first step writes a 1 and lands in this machine's state A, so it runs this machine from a tape with one extra 1. That 1 ends up at the end of the wall and stays there: at every one of the 32,539 left-end records of the first 109 steps the two tapes agree except for that 1, and the twin is always 56 steps ahead.

Where a halt can come from

If the machine halts on its third step from a configuration, that configuration is one of: D with 1 under the head, 0 right, 0 two right. So a run can reach a halt only through one of these patterns, and a proof that the machine never halts only has to rule them out. The proof splits on the state and the five cells around the head.
