Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1RE_1LC0RA_---1LD_1LA0LF_0LB0LC_1RB0LB

Nobody knows. The tape is a list of pairs of numbers, each written as a block of ones followed by a stretch of 01s; every round trip the two numbers of a pair change roles and the list is rewritten. This page shows the encoding, the proved rules and what a halt would need.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in C reading 0. C is entered only from B0 (writes 1, moves L): 0 B>0; E1 (writes 0, moves L): 0 E>1. So a halt is the moment one of these patterns appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The run at a glance

The diagram stacks the tape at many moments. Sampling at each new cell shows the shape of the run as the visited region grows; sampling evenly in time shows where the head spends its time. The records list every step at which the head reaches a cell no earlier step reached, with the side, the width so far and the whole tape: the first place to look for a repeating macro structure.

The structure

A list of pairs (a, b)

The head sweeps from end to end; a visit adds 3 or 5 cells at each end. After a rightward sweep the tape is a sequence of pieces 1^a 0 (01)^b: a block of a ones, a 0, and b copies of 01 (98.7% of the tape is covered this way). The leftward sweep turns each stretch (01)^b into a block of 2b ones (C, rule left_C) and blocks of ones into stretches of 01 or 10: the two numbers of a pair trade their encodings every round trip. The rightward sweep shifts the 01 stretches by one cell (A and B). In most pieces a − b is 1 or −1, but it ranges from −13 to 25; the list as a whole evolves like a cellular automaton with no known rule.

Halting

What a halt would need

C0 is the only halting transition and C is entered only from B on a 0 (writing 1, moving left). B is entered from A0 and F0, both writing a 1 and moving right, so C then reads that 1; and from E0 and F1, both writing 0 and moving left. So a halt needs 0 0 E>0 or 0 0 F>1: E or F turning left onto two 0s. At the right end the head always arrives in A or B after a 1, which is safe. In the first 109 steps C is entered 157,760,038 times and reads 1 every time; no halt in 1010 steps.

Where a halt can come from

If the machine halts on its third step from a configuration, that configuration is one of: E with 0 two left, 0 left, 0 under the head; F with 0 two left, 0 left, 1 under the head. So a run can reach a halt only through one of these patterns, and a proof that the machine never halts only has to rule them out. The proof splits on the state and the five cells around the head.

At every 31st visit to the right end in the first 3·108 steps, 98.7% of the tape is covered by pieces 1^a 0 (01)^b with a at least 2. The difference a − b is usually 1 (8,496 pieces), −1 (4,122), 2 or 3, but ranges from −13 to 25.

Computed
direct simulation
The halting state is never close

C0 is the only halting transition and C is entered only by B0 (1LC). When B comes from A0 or F0 (both 1RB) the cell C reads is the 1 just written, so a halt needs B to come from E0 or F1 (0LB) onto two 0s: 0 0 E>0 or 0 0 F>1. In the first 109 steps C is entered 157,760,038 times and reads 1 every time.

Computed
sweeps of 13 November 2026 (family F11); the tagged and run-length deciders are not in the repository yet
Regular-language deciders tried (F11)

None of these closes: each reaches a halting configuration in its abstraction, which says nothing about whether the machine halts. n-gram CPS (tools/c/ngramcps.c), equal widths on both sides: a halting local configuration is reached with plain up to width 24, history 1 up to width 24, history 2 up to width 24, history 3 up to width 24, LRU history up to width 24 (larger widths ran out of the 150 s or 60 million configuration budget). n-gram CPS with every cell tagged by its position mod p: fails for p = 2 up to width 20, p = 3 up to width 20, p = 4 up to width 20, p = 6 up to width 20. n-gram CPS where each side also carries the state of a small automaton that has read that side from the far end (every automaton with up to 3 states on both sides, or 4 states on one side), with position mod 1..4 and widths 4, 6, 8: no closed set without a halt in all 120 parameter sets. Run-length windows (the K nearest runs of equal macro cells on each side, run lengths kept exactly below T and mod M above): fails for all 72 parameter sets (cells of 1 to 4 bits, K = 2, 4, 6, T = 2, 4, M = 1, 2, 4). Two-automaton closed tape language by SAT (a deterministic automaton reading each side inward, n states each, CaDiCaL): no solution for n = 2..8; n = 9 not finished in 150 s.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The pieces are crossed by proved rules, but how a round trip rewrites the list of pairs (a, b) is not understood; a proof needs an invariant of the list that keeps two 0s away from where E or F turn left.
