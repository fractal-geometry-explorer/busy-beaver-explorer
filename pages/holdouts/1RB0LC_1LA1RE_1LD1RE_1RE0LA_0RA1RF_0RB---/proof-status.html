OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 13 November 2026.

In short

Open; analysis only begun. Sampled when the head is in C at the left end, the tape is mostly X(n, t) = C> 000 (100)^n T or Y(n, t) = C> (100)^n T, with a tail bit t (T = 1 or 101). X(n) goes to X((3n+5)/2) when n is odd and to Y((3n+6)/2) when n is even. Y(n) with n even goes to X((3n+4)/2). These single-number rules match every applicable event of the run from blank tape up to 4·1010 steps. Y(n) with n odd instead starts a nested phase with a second number y ≈ 3n/2, which ends when its successor is even and nests deeper otherwise. Those rules, and with them the halting condition, are further work. No halt was found from small X/Y starts.

Third pass. No halt in 1018 steps (macro simulation); no pushdown certificate with a DFA of at most 6 states on either side.

The single-number rules, against the run from blank tape

X(n, t) → X((3n+5)/2, t') for odd n and → Y((3n+6)/2, t') for even n, with t' = t xor [n mod 4 ∈ {0, 1}]; Y(n, t) → X((3n+4)/2, t xor [n mod 4 = 0]) for even n. The 21 X/Y-events of the blank run up to 4·1010 steps that follow an X-event or an even Y-event are exactly as predicted (from X(5) at step 184 to X(75,654) at step 30,906,054,124). The one odd Y-event, Y(33,621) at step 6.1·109, went through the nested phase C> 000 (10)^2 (010)^50433 1, C> (100)^3 (010)^50432 01 and came back as X(75,654) = X(3·50,432/2 + 6).

No halt from small single-number starts

Direct runs of 108 steps from X(n, t) and Y(n, t) for n < 40 and both tails: none halts. Several of them go through nested phases two or three levels deep, e.g. from Y(3) and Y(7).

Nested phases in closed form for 3 residues of 4

From Y(n, t) with n odd: n = 4i+1 goes to X(9i+9, t xor [i odd]) after 5 left-end moments, n = 8i+7 to X(27i+36, t xor [i odd]) after 8; n = 8i+3 nests deeper (9 to 13 moments, e.g. Y(3) → X(22), Y(27) → Y(119)). Every odd n < 400, both tails, returns to the single-number form without halting.

Rounds between end events, by remainder class

Sample the run whenever the head is in state A reading 1 with only 0s to its left. Read rightwards from the head, the tape is then A U^m B: one long periodic block between two short context words. For each class (A, U, B, m mod 4) the next sample is simulated for six values of m and extended as an affine map of m (with a cubic step count). Along the direct run from blank tape to 3·109 steps (23 events) the class map predicts the next event in all 13 rounds it applies to. This is an extrapolation checked by computation, not a proof.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The nested phase is the 3/2 map run on a second number until a parity test ends it; n = 8i+3 needs the next level, so the complete map is a recursion on the 2-adic digits of n. No synthetic nested phase with n < 400 halts.
