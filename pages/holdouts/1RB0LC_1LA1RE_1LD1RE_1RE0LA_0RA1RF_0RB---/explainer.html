Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LC_1LA1RE_1LD1RE_1RE0LA_0RA1RF_0RB---

Not known yet. Most of the time the machine runs a simple Collatz-like map, multiplying a number n by about 3/2 at each pass. When a parity test comes out the wrong way, it opens a nested phase with a second number, which can nest again. How it could halt, if it ever does, is hidden in those nested phases.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from E1 (writes 1, moves R): E>1 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

What it does

A 3/2 map with nested phases

When the head is in state C at the left end, the tape is usually X(n) = 000 (100)^n T or Y(n) = (100)^n T, with a short tail T = 1 or 101 that flips according to n mod 4.

fromn oddn even
X(n)X((3n+5)/2)Y((3n+6)/2)
Y(n)nested phaseX((3n+4)/2)

From blank tape: X(5), X(10), Y(18), X(29), X(46), Y(72), X(110), Y(168), X(254), …, X(22,412), Y(33,621). Y(33,621) has an odd count, so a second number, 50,433 copies of 010, appears; it is followed by 50,432 copies, which is even, and the phase ends at X(75,654) at step 3.1·1010. When the second number is odd as well, the phase nests deeper; small starts show two or three levels.

New

The nested phases, level by level

When the single-number map lands on Y(n) with n odd, a second number appears and is run through the same 3/2 rule until a parity test ends the phase. The first two levels are now exact:

nnext single-number eventmoments at the left end
4i+1X(9i+9)5
8i+7X(27i+36)8
8i+3deeper: X(22) from Y(3), Y(119) from Y(27), …9 to 13

The factors 9/4 and 27/8 are (3/2)² and (3/2)³: each level applies the 3/2 rule once more, and how many levels a phase takes depends on how many factors of 2 the numbers contain. Every nested phase from n < 400 comes back without halting.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.066). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.501).

After 109 steps the tape spans 42,324 cells (48 left of the start, 30,497 right), with a density of 1s of 0.502.

The single-number rules, against the run from blank tape

X(n, t) → X((3n+5)/2, t') for odd n and → Y((3n+6)/2, t') for even n, with t' = t xor [n mod 4 ∈ {0, 1}]; Y(n, t) → X((3n+4)/2, t xor [n mod 4 = 0]) for even n. The 21 X/Y-events of the blank run up to 4·1010 steps that follow an X-event or an even Y-event are exactly as predicted (from X(5) at step 184 to X(75,654) at step 30,906,054,124). The one odd Y-event, Y(33,621) at step 6.1·109, went through the nested phase C> 000 (10)^2 (010)^50433 1, C> (100)^3 (010)^50432 01 and came back as X(75,654) = X(3·50,432/2 + 6).

Nested phases in closed form for 3 residues of 4

From Y(n, t) with n odd: n = 4i+1 goes to X(9i+9, t xor [i odd]) after 5 left-end moments, n = 8i+7 to X(27i+36, t xor [i odd]) after 8; n = 8i+3 nests deeper (9 to 13 moments, e.g. Y(3) → X(22), Y(27) → Y(119)). Every odd n < 400, both tails, returns to the single-number form without halting.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The nested phase is the 3/2 map run on a second number until a parity test ends it; n = 8i+3 needs the next level, so the complete map is a recursion on the 2-adic digits of n. No synthetic nested phase with n < 400 halts.
