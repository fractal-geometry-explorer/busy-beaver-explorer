1RB0RC_1LC0LD_1RA0LB_1LE0RA_1RD1LF_0LC---

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The machine is a particle system on a background of 10s. Lean proves the motion of the moving particle Q exactly (for every size of the tape around it) and that the run from blank tape reaches that picture; the collisions of Q with the other particles are only observed. The C simulator finds no halt in 1010 steps.

Q bounces: exact rules for every size

With Q0 a c R = 1 A>1 0 (10)^a 0 (10)^c R and Q1 a c R = 1 A>1 0 (10)^a 1 1 0 (10)^c R (blank tape on the left, any tape R on the right): Q0 a (c+2) R reaches Q1 (a+2) c R in 4a+14 steps and Q1 a (c+1) R reaches Q0 (a+2) (c+1) R in 4a+10 steps, for all a, c and R. The head never looks at R.

bounce0, bounce1, lean/Macro.lean (from bounce0_cells, bounce1_cells, lean/Rules.lean)

Q travels one pair per two bounces

Q0 a (c+1+2k) R reaches Q0 (a+4k) (c+1) R in exactly 8ak + 16k² + 16k steps: the reflector moves k pairs to the right while the tape grows 4k cells on the left.

travel, tcost_eq, lean/Macro.lean

Collisions and the growth in bursts

When Q reaches a stationary particle, the two exchange places or merge and a new reflector is sent on; when a Q reaches the wall the right end grows three cells per bounce for a while (bursts at steps 1,366, 96,934, 473,188, 1,276,984 and 1.2×109 in the first 3×109 steps, the right end at 14, 80, 262, 562, 922 and then growing again). The collision rules are observed in simulation, not proved, so the long-run order of events is open.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. A proof would need the collision rules of Q with P, with the wall and with the left end, and an invariant on the lattices they build; the only halting transition (F reading 1) cannot occur in the proved bounce rules, which use only states A to D.
