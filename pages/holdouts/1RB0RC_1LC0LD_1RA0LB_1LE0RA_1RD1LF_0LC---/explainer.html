Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0RC_1LC0LD_1RA0LB_1LE0RA_1RD1LF_0LC---

Nobody knows. Started on a blank tape, this machine builds a tape of 10 10 10 ... carrying a few kinds of particles. The head bounces off one of them, a moving reflector, and pushes it to the right; the others stand still and pile up in lattices. Lean proves exactly how the reflector moves; what happens when particles meet is only observed. It has run 1010 steps without stopping.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from E1 (writes 1, moves L): 1 E>1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.546). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

After 109 steps the tape spans 61,189 cells (50,664 left of the start, 922 right), with a density of 1s of 0.498.

Structure

What the tape is made of

Almost all of the tape is the pattern 1 0 1 0 1 0 .... A sweep of the head shifts this background by one cell (in state A, A>1 0 becomes 0 1 A>; in states B and C the same happens leftwards), so the head can cross any amount of it at a steady pace. What makes the machine interesting are the places where the pattern is broken, the particles:

  • Q, the reflector: a single extra 0 (short form) or an extra 1 1 0 (long form). The head cannot cross it: it bounces back to the left end.
  • P = 0 1 1 0 0, a stationary particle: the head crosses it, and after the round trip it is back in place.
  • the wall (1 0 0 1 1 0 0)^m at the right end, which grows in bursts;
  • the left end 1 1 0 (or (10)^3 1 1 0), which grows by two cells per bounce and from time to time emits a new Q.
Exact rule

One bounce, and how far Q travels

Look at the tape when the head, in state A, stands on the second cell of the tape. With Q in its short or long form the tape is

Q0 a c R = 1 A>1 0 (10)^a 0 (10)^c R
Q1 a c R = 1 A>1 0 (10)^a 1 1 0 (10)^c R

(blank tape on the left, any tape R on the right). One trip of the head to Q and back gives

Q0 a (c+2) R  →  Q1 (a+2) c R        in 4a + 14 steps
Q1 a (c+1) R  →  Q0 (a+2) (c+1) R    in 4a + 10 steps

so every two bounces Q moves one pair to the right and the tape grows four cells on the left. Iterated,

Q0 a (c+1+2k) R  →  Q0 (a+4k) (c+1) R   in 8ak + 16k² + 16k steps.

The head never looks at R, so these hold whatever lies beyond Q; all three are proved in Lean for every a, c, k and R. The trip to Q gets longer as the left part grows, so a reflector that has to cross n pairs takes about 16n² steps: this is where the square-root growth comes from. The same rules hold, with the same proofs, for the sister machine 1RB0RC_1LC0LD_1RA0LB_1LE0RA_1RA1LF_0LC---, which differs only in what E does on a 0.

The long run

Particles over a billion steps

Each row below is the tape at one left turn of the head (every 40th, up to step 109; time goes down, the left end is the diagonal edge). Grey is visited background, black marks a broken pattern (two equal neighbouring cells). Diagonal lines are reflectors Q moving right; vertical lines are stationary particles P; the black block on the right is the wall.

The run is a sequence of epochs. A reflector is sent off from the left end and travels right; when it meets a P the two interact and the reflector goes on, leaves a particle behind, or is absorbed (then a new one starts at the left end); when one reaches the wall, the right end grows three cells per bounce for a while. The stationary particles end up in regular lattices: at step 1010 the tape reads, in particle form (n stands for (10)^n),

3 1^2 0 1513 P 40809 0 12470 P 733 1^2 0 4183 P 7557 P 430 P 1385 P 2985 P 1385 P ... P 555 P 899 P 555 P ...
  ... P 185 P 297 P 185 P ... P 59 P 99 P 59 P 99 ... (1 0 0 1 1 0 0)^m

with 293 particles P, most of them in lattices whose gaps alternate in pairs: from the right, (3, 11) 52 times, (9, 41) 18 times, (59, 99) 37 times, (185, 297) 27 times, (555, 899) 6 times, (1385, 2985) 3 times. Each lattice is built in one epoch, and each is more widely spaced than the one before. The rules of these collisions are seen in the simulation; they are not proved.

Halting

Where it could halt

The only halting transition is F reading 1, and F is entered only from E reading 1. A bounce off the reflector uses only states A to D (E and F never occur in the proved bounce rules), so the machine cannot halt while a reflector travels. A halt would have to come from a collision: a reflector meeting a particle, the wall or the left end in a configuration that has not happened in 1010 steps. Ruling that out needs the full table of collisions and an invariant on the lattices they build.

Computed: no halt in 1010 steps, the particle form of the tape. Open: the collision rules, and halting.

Heuristic
py/snap.c, py/particles_img.py
Collisions and the growth in bursts

When Q reaches a stationary particle, the two exchange places or merge and a new reflector is sent on; when a Q reaches the wall the right end grows three cells per bounce for a while (bursts at steps 1,366, 96,934, 473,188, 1,276,984 and 1.2×109 in the first 3×109 steps, the right end at 14, 80, 262, 562, 922 and then growing again). The collision rules are observed in simulation, not proved, so the long-run order of events is open.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. A proof would need the collision rules of Q with P, with the wall and with the left end, and an invariant on the lattices they build; the only halting transition (F reading 1) cannot occur in the proved bounce rules, which use only states A to D.
