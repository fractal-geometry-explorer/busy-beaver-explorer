The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from C0 (writes 1, moves L): 0 C>0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The argument in five layers

From a tape to a small counter

The question is whether this machine, started on a blank tape, ever halts. Nobody knows yet, but the run can be described exactly by smaller and smaller systems, each one proved in Lean to halt exactly when the one below it halts. This section is the map; each later section explains one step on it, with something to try.

  1. The tape

    Cells of 0 and 1, a head in one of six states. This is the machine itself (the simulator above).

    read in pairs at each turn at the right end Kernel

  2. A word

    A string of letters, one per pair of cells: 11 is 1, 01 is 0. About half as long as the tape.

    one round trip of the head = one XOR sweep Kernel

  3. The XOR sweep

    A four-line rule that turns one word into the next. No states, no head: just the word.

    the long block at the right end matters only through its length mod 3 Kernel

  4. The zone orbit

    A short word, the zone, plus one number, the length of the block. This is what Lean runs, and what your browser runs below: to 1.8·1015 machine steps in a second or two.

    a reset splits the zone in two Computed

  5. Two zones

    An old zone frozen at the left end, behind a stranded block, and a new zone that has to get through the block before anything can happen there.

The panel shows one moment of the real run at the first four layers at once. Pick a turn (the head coming back to the right end of the tape) and read it down: the cells, the letters those cells spell, the same letters split into zone, block and head, and the two numbers the zone orbit keeps.

Layer 1 to 2 · Proved

Reading the tape as a word

Watch the simulator for a while and one moment keeps coming back: the head arrives at the right end of the tape, in state C, with blank tape to its right. Call this a turn. At every turn the tape, read two cells at a time from the right end, is made of the pairs 11 and 01 only. So it can be written as a word: 11 is the letter 1 and 01 the letter 0. The head sits on the first cell of the last pair, which is always 11, so a word always ends in 1.

Why should anyone believe this pairing is the right way to look? Because Lean proves it. lean/Sweep.lean defines a macro step on words: the left pass (states D and its partners crossing the pairs to the left), the turn, and the right pass back, including the rare passes that run off the left end. mstep_sound proves, for every word, that the machine does exactly what the macro step says, step count included. From that, halts_iff (lean/Reduce.lean) says: the machine halts from blank tape if and only if the orbit of words from the blank tape reaches a halting macro step. From here on the tape is gone; only words are left. Kernel

An earlier check ran that orbit of words by compiled evaluation through the first 4·108 machine steps (blank_runs_orbit, lean/OrbitRun.lean). native_decide The zone orbit below goes much further.

Layer 2 to 3 · Proved

One round trip is one XOR sweep

The macro step is long, but on the words that occur at turns it does something very simple. mstep_ok (lean/Bits.lean) proves that one round trip of the head is one XOR sweep of the word: Kernel

  1. find the rightmost 00 in the word;
  2. flip the letter just left of it;
  3. replace every letter right of the 00 by itself XOR its right-hand neighbour;
  4. append a 1 (the new pair 11 the head leaves at the right end).

The machine takes exactly 4·d + 12 steps for it, where d is the number of letters right of the 00. In machine terms: the left pass crosses the pairs until it finds two 01 in a row, turns there, and the way back rewrites every pair it crosses.

Two outcomes are not covered. If the only 00 is the first two letters of the word, there is nothing left of it to flip: the pass meets the left end of the tape and the machine halts (mstep_halt). If there is no 00 at all, the pass runs off the left end and the machine does something else for a while (a reset); Lean then falls back on the full macro step. So a halt needs a sweep that gets all the way to the left end of the word.

Try it. Load a turn of the real run, or type any word ending in 1, and step through the four stages. The last line runs the real machine on that word and compares.

One more property drives everything below, and the XOR row shows it: a letter changes only through itself and its right neighbour. Whatever lies left of the 00 is not touched at all (swr_append_ok), and a sweep that runs through a stretch without 00 changes that stretch to its XOR differences and passes on only its last letter (swr_split, xd_append). Information moves left by at most one letter per sweep. Kernel

Layer 3 to 4 · Proved

The block forgets its length

At the right end of every word sits a long, regular stretch: …110110110, followed by the head’s 1. On the tape this is the block of words 111110 that the simulator shows growing (each letter is two cells, so 110 is 11 11 01, the six cells 111101, which is 111110 read from another starting point). It grows by one letter per sweep, and it is most of the tape: after 3·107 sweeps it has 29,990,528 letters, against a few hundred or thousand for everything else.

Every sweep crosses the whole block, because the block has no 00. Crossing it replaces each letter by its XOR with the next. For the pattern 110 repeated, the XOR differences are 011 repeated: the same pattern, moved by one place. So after a sweep the block is again a block, one letter longer, and all the zone on its left ever sees of it is one letter, the leftmost one. That letter is fixed by the length of the block mod 3.

Lean’s zone_step (lean/Xor.lean) says exactly this for every length: a sweep of «zone, block of length n + 1» is the sweep of «zone, one letter pat(n)», with the block put back in front. Kernel The panel sweeps one zone next to four blocks of consecutive lengths; the zones that come out agree whenever the lengths agree mod 3.

Layer 4 · Proved and run

The zone orbit

Drop the block and keep its length. What remains is the zone orbit: a state is a number n (the block’s length) and a short word Z (the zone). One step sweeps Z with the one letter pat(n) on its right and adds 1 to n; a step costs 4·(n + d) + 12 machine steps. When a sweep finds no 00 in the zone, the orbit hands that one step to the real macro step and splits the block off again afterwards.

halts_iff_zone (lean/Zone.lean) proves: the machine halts from blank tape if and only if the zone orbit halts. Kernel This is an exact reduction, not a model. And because a step of the zone orbit costs time proportional to the zone, not the tape, it can be run very far: compiled evaluation in Lean runs it for 3·107 steps, which covers the first 1.7·1015 machine steps, with no halt (zone_runs, lean/ZoneRun.lean). native_decide

The panel runs the same orbit in your browser, written separately in JavaScript. It agrees with the real machine at every turn of the first 3,000, and its state at step 3·107 (machine step 1.80·1015, block of 29,990,528 letters) is letter for letter the one Lean computes; tools/tests/test_tools.py checks both. Deeper sweeps are rarer and rarer, like the higher digits of a counter, which is the staircase in the depth plot. Layer 5 · Computed

Two zones and a stranded block

Why does the left end go quiet? At a reset the zone is rebuilt from the right end of the tape, and the letters the old zone had collected stay behind. From then on the word reads, from the left end: the old zone, a long stranded block (the pattern 110 again, with no 00), the new zone, the growing block and the head. The bar above the depth plot shows these parts, measured live from the orbit.

By the lemmas of the XOR sweep, the old zone moves only when a sweep gets through the whole new zone (swr_append_ok). Such a sweep then crosses the stranded block, which shifts its pattern by one place and passes one letter on to the old zone, just as the growing block does (swr_split). The new zone’s irregular edge eats into the stranded block from the right, a little with every such sweep, so for a long run of these sweeps (a good part of the block’s length, but not all of it: a twin simulation finds the disturbance spreading about two cells per pass) the old zone moves exactly as it would on its own, next to an endless block. Kernel (the lemmas) Computed (the argument that combines them, checked against the real orbit at every such sweep so far).

At zone step 3·107 the parts are: old zone 50 letters, stranded block 9,357, new zone 79. The old zone alone is a tiny system with one tick per sweep that gets through: run alone, the old zone has no reset and no halt in 9,357 ticks, more than the block can shield it for (c/z1.c in the folder of 1RB0RB_1LC1RA_1LF0LD_0LE1LD_1LA1LC_---1LA). In 3·1010 sweeps after zone step 3·107 only 23 sweeps got through the new zone, at the same sweep numbers for 1RB0RB_1LC1RA_1LF0LD_0LE1LD_1LA1LC_---1LA, 1RB0RB_1LC1RA_1LF0LD_0LE1LD_1RA1LC_---1LA and 1RB1LD_1RC0RC_1LD1RB_1LF0LE_0LA1LE_---1LB, whose new zones are the same. Computed

The layers below the zone orbit are all proved. The two-zone picture is not yet a proof, and nothing here decides the machine: a halt needs a sweep through the new zone, the stranded block and the old zone, all the way to the left end. Sweeps that get through the new zone came about 7 times per factor of 10 in the number of sweeps; if that goes on, the next sweep that could reach the left end is something like 101200 sweeps or more away. Heuristic Deciding the machine needs a proof about the zone orbit itself: that the new zone never lets a sweep through often enough, or that the old zone never halts whatever it is fed.

Layer 6 · Proved and computed

A period at a time

The cells nearest the block are periodic, and a cell is back to its bit after a list of events or after two copies of it. So the events that reach a cell during the first 3·2d sweeps can be built cell by cell, one or two passes per cell, without running the sweeps one by one. The window doubles at about two cells in five while the lists grow by about 7% per cell. Lean proves the method (wfold_run) and runs it by compiled evaluation: nothing leaves the word for 3·263 sweeps, so no halt in the first 1.5·1039 steps. Kernel Native The C engine goes to 3·277 sweeps. Computed

Two things grow with the logarithm of the time, plotted below. Behind a front the stream is disturbed and loses about a fifth of its events per cell; past the front only background is left, and it crosses the stranded block without loss. Every event that has reached the old zone is background, so the old zone has so far moved exactly as alone. The front moves about 3.3 cells per doubling of the window, the background events into the old zone come about 1.35 per doubling. At those speeds the front reaches the old zone at d ≈ 2,900, after about 3,800 background events, fewer than the 12,059 the old zone alone needs to let a sweep through. Heuristic

Hover a point for its value. A window of 3·277 sweeps is about 4.5·1023 sweeps; the speeds are fitted over d = 30 to the last window and are measurements, not theorems. A bound on the front speed would turn them into lower bounds on the time to a halt; deciding the machine would still need what happens once the front arrives.

At a turn at the right end, read in pairs, the tape is a binary word. When it has a 00, one macro step is exactly one sweep: the rightmost 00 is found, the letter left of it is flipped, every letter right of it becomes the XOR of itself and its right neighbour, and 11 is appended (4d + 12 steps, d the depth of the 00). Two zones

Since the last arrival the tape holds a new zone, a stranded block of 9,357 letters and the old zone at the left end. By the Lean lemmas the old zone moves only when a sweep gets through the new zone, and for as long as the block lasts it then moves exactly as it would alone: run alone it first lets a sweep through, a reset, at its tick 12,059. The disturbance from the new zone spreads about two cells per pass, not one, so the block shields the old zone for only about 5,100 passes, not 9,357 (extrapolated from the first 156 cells), and what the old zone does once it is reached is open. Kernel
swr_feed_ok, swr_feed_halt, swr_feed_reset, cell_bit, zone_period, never_of_quiet, lean/Cascade.lean; never_halts_of_quiet, lean/ZoneCascade.lean
One sweep is one event in a cascade of one-bit cells

Keep the word as a row of one-bit cells, the first next to the block of words. A sweep is an event that enters at the first cell and travels away from the block: P c (it arrives carrying the letter c) or F (it stopped just before this cell and flips it). Every cell applies the same small table to its own bit, and the event either dies inside the word, which gives the new word, or leaves the last cell, as F a halt and as P a reset. What a cell's bit does depends on the event only, so the first m cells of the zone orbit are periodic with a period dividing 3·2m, and if nothing leaves the word during one period of the whole word, the machine never halts.

Kernel
wfold_run, window_quiet, window_of_check, lean/Window.lean
The cascade a period at a time

A cell is back to its bit after a list of events or after two copies of it, so the events that enter cell m during the first 3·2d sweeps can be built cell by cell (one or two passes each, d the number of cells that needed two). Run through the rest of the word, they give every event that leaves it in that window.
