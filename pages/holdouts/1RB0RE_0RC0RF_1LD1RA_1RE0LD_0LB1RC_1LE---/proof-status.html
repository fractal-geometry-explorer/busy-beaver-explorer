OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 13 November 2026.

In short

The question is open, but the run is now understood up to its rebuilds. Between rebuilds the machine is a binary counter that applies one fixed operation to a list of blocks once per tick, and the list is the binary expansion of the time it has left (checked against the machine, not proved). Rebuilds are still simulated cell by cell. With this the run passes 986 rebuilds, more than 1031,896 steps, without a halt (before: three rebuilds, 3·1010 steps).

It can halt from tapes of its own shape. A single block of 3k cells that meets the counter exactly at the start of a round halts (k = 3 to 9, direct simulation). Whether the run from blank tape ever produces such a coincidence is the open question.

Between rebuilds: a binary counter that applies one list operation per tick

At the moments the head is in C on a 0 just right of a 1, the tape is a list of blocks of 1s separated by single 0s (the interior), a last block l, and to the right of the head a zone of single 1s. Call it regular if the first block is a multiple of 3, the other interior blocks are 2 mod 3, l is 1 mod 4, and the gaps of the zone are 0 mod 4 (the first, counted from the head) and 3 mod 4 (the others). From one such moment to the next (a sub-round) a regular configuration changes in two independent ways. The interior takes one step W: (..., x, 3a + 2) becomes (..., x + 3, 2, ..., 2) with a blocks of 2. The last block and the zone are a binary counter: with segments s1 = g1, si = gi + 1, a last block l ≥ 5 becomes a new first segment l − 1 and l = 1; with l = 1 the n leading segments equal to 4 are removed, the next segment loses 4 cells (blank tape supplies them if there is none) and l = 4n + 5. So l plus the segments is 4m + 1 for 2m sub-rounds (one round, what earlier notes called the fast counter), then m grows by 1. Checked against the machine one sub-round at a time: of the 300,000 sub-rounds after step 2,918 (12.6 million steps) the 299,954 regular ones all follow this; the other 46 are the transients of the first two rebuilds. Observed and checked, not proved.

The list is the binary expansion of the time it has left

With digits ai = (bi − 2)/3, W removes the last digit a, adds 1 to the digit before it and appends a zeros; the sum of all digits plus their number never changes. A tail (..., q, a) becomes (..., q + a + 1) after exactly 2a steps, so an interior with digits a1, ..., ak is a single block after exactly T = Σi 2ai + ... + ak + k − i steps of W: the positions of the 1s in the binary expansion of T are the partial sums of the list read from the right. (This is arithmetic on the rule W, not on the machine.) Checked on the machine: the model predicts single blocks at sub-rounds 1,616 and 261,907 of that run and both are there; W steps and counter steps taken many at once agree with the machine in 28 test jumps; and after the third rebuild the lists at seven ends of rounds, up to step 2.8·1010, are those of a direct run of the machine (3 2^25 14 2 8 2 14 | 85 at step 22,558,329,286, ..., 3 2^12 20 2^6 14 2 8 2 14 | 109 at step 28,107,393,382).

Tapes of the same shape that halt

A rebuild starts when the interior is a single block. The tape 13k 0 14k−3 with the head in C on the 0 right of it, a single block of 3k cells meeting the counter exactly at the start of round k − 1, halts for every k from 3 to 9 (after 311, 659, 1,343, 2,699, 5,399, 10,787 and 21,551 steps; direct simulation). So no property of the shape alone can show that the machine never halts: it depends on which single blocks and counter values the run from blank tape produces.

986 rebuilds without a halt

A run that takes the regular sub-rounds by arithmetic (any number at once, with the two rules above) and gives everything else to the machine itself, cell by cell: the transient after each single block, and the sub-rounds where the counter reaches an irregular gap of the zone. It passes 986 rebuilds; by then the counter has made more than 2105,958 sub-rounds, so the machine more than 1031,896 steps, and the single block has 317,877 cells. A halt could only be found by the direct part and none was. The arithmetic part rests on the two rules above for all sizes, which are checked, not proved.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. Between rebuilds the machine cannot halt if the model is right; a rebuild's transient can (tapes of the same shape do). What the transient does to the block and the counter has no rule yet, so the question is whether the single blocks and counter values of this run ever meet in a halting way. The halting family above needs the single block to arrive exactly when a round of the counter starts, a coincidence of two binary numbers that the run has avoided so far.
