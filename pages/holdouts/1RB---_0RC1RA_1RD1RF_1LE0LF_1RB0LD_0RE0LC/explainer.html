The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in A reading 1. A is entered only from B1 (writes 1, moves R): B>1 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The run at a glance

Space-time diagram and records

The diagram stacks the tape at many moments. Sampling at each new cell shows the shape of the run as the visited region grows; sampling evenly in time shows where the head spends its time. The records list every step at which the head reaches a cell no earlier step reached, with the side, the width so far and the whole tape: the first place to look for a repeating macro structure.

What it does

A counter with four-valued digits

Read the tape two cells at a time, from the right end. Most of it is the same eight-cell word, 01011111, repeated. Between the repeats sit a few special groups, the digits, each with four possible values a, b, c, d.

Every pass of the head changes the digit nearest the right end to its next value: a, b, c, d, a, ... On two of those four steps (d to a, and a to b) the head carries on to the next digit and steps it too. So each digit moves half as often as its right neighbour, exactly like the digits of a binary counter, and the stretches of background between the digits slowly grow.

Ten times so far a carry ran off the top of the counter and the head went to the far left end, rebuilding the tape into a larger arrangement (after the tenth, a row of two-digit counters). Those rebuilds are the part nobody has described yet, and the reason the machine is still open.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.522).

After 109 steps the tape spans 52,296 cells (37 left of the start, 38,610 right), with a density of 1s of 0.721.

Their tables differ, and no renaming of the states turns one into the other. While they run, the states pair up as A–A, B–B, C–C, D–D, E–A, F–E, F–F (this machine first), which is not one to one. Transitions not used in these steps: this machine never uses A1 and the other never uses F1. Lean proves (lean/Twin.lean here, lock_or_break, not_halts_of_noBreak) that the two runs have the same tape, the same head position and paired states up to the first break, a configuration where the paired states act differently, and that neither machine halts if no break ever comes. A halt of either machine is itself a break, and the halting transitions of the two do not correspond, so the two are not known to halt together.
