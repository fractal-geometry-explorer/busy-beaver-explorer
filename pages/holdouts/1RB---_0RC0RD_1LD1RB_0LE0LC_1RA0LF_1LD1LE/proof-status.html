Open here; an outside proof awaits reviewA Rocq proof that it halts is in an open busycoq pull request, not merged and not compiled here. This page's own ledger treats halting as open.

Nobody knows whether this six-state machine halts from blank tape. Its tape has just two parts: a solid block of 1s and a stretch of alternating 1 0 1 0 …. On every trip to the left end the machine carries the solid block across the alternating stretch, so the two swap sides; on the way back their sizes change. The sizes jump around without an obvious pattern, while the total width creeps up.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Lean proves no halt in the first 107 steps. The C simulator shows the two-part tape at every one of the 1,139 side switches after step 104, up to 109 steps, and the block carried across intact all 569 times. How the trip back sets the new sizes is not yet known.

Two parts

At each of the 1,139 moments after step 104 (up to 109) at which the head extends the tape on the side opposite to its last extension, the tape is a solid block and an alternating stretch: at the right end 1^m followed by 0 1 0 1 …, at the left end 0 1 0 1 … 0 0 1^m.

The block is carried across

In all 569 trips from the right end to the left end, the solid block arrives at the right end with exactly the length m it had at the left (e.g. m = 3,056 at steps 978,096,466 and 980,177,263), and the trip takes 2·m·r steps to within a few per cent, r the length of the alternating stretch.

How it could halt

The halting transition is A reading 1. A is entered only from E reading 0 (moving right), and E's right neighbour is a 1 only when E was entered from F reading 1. So a halt is exactly 0 F>1: F on a 1 with a 0 to its left.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The two-part shape and the carrying of the block hold in every case seen; the map from one block length to the next, and why it never produces F on a 1 next to a 0, are open.
