The machine

6 states, two symbols, 2 halting transitions

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in E reading 1. E is entered only from D0 (writes 1, moves L): 1 D>0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from E0 (writes 1, moves L): 1 E>0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The run at a glance

Space-time diagram and records

The diagram stacks the tape at many moments. Sampling at each new cell shows the shape of the run as the visited region grows; sampling evenly in time shows where the head spends its time. The records list every step at which the head reaches a cell no earlier step reached, with the side, the width so far and the whole tape: the first place to look for a repeating macro structure.

Macro structure

Two binary counters on one tape

Run the machine for a while and the tape sorts itself into two numbers written in binary. On the far left, after a single 1 that acts as a sentinel, is a counter U whose digits are blocks of 5 cells: 00000 is a 0 and 00001 a 1, most significant digit on the left. Then come a short gap 00, the head in state E, and a fixed header such as 0110101. Right of the header is a second counter V whose digits are pairs, least significant first: 01 is a 1 and 11 a 0.

The tick configuration is 1 U 00 E>0110101 V. Every tick adds 1 to U and takes 1 from V. Most of the time nothing else happens.

The rule

One tick: U + 1, V − 1

The head walks left through the trailing 1-digits of U, turning them into 0-digits (the carries), writes a 1-digit, then walks right to V and turns its trailing 0-pairs into 1-pairs and its lowest 1-pair into a 0-pair (the borrows). Written with j carries and i + 1 borrows:

beforeaftersteps
L 00000 (00001)^j 00 E>0110101 01 RL 00001 (00000)^j 00 E>0110101 11 R10 * j + 26
L 00000 (00001)^j 00 E>0110101 (11)^(i+1) 01 RL 00001 (00000)^j 00 E>0110101 (01)^(i+1) 11 R4 * i + 10 * j + 30

These two shapes are proved in Lean for every j and i (lean/Rules.lean), for every header the run has used (0110101, 0110111). Because the cost of a tick depends only on the numbers of carries and borrows, a long run of ticks has an exact cost: the sum of the trailing zeros of the numbers U+1, ..., U+k and V−k+1, ..., V, which has a closed form. The accelerated simulator uses it to skip billions of ticks at once.

When V runs out

The rebuild as a function of the counter

When V reaches 0 the tape is clean, 0^inf 1 U 00 E>0110101 (11)^n 1 0^inf, and the machine's state is (n, N): the length of V and the up-counter read as one number with its sentinel.

The rebuild reads the lowest j digits of N and leaves N' = N >> j, with a new V = 2n+c − δ. The simplest case is an even N: it reads one digit, the counter is halved, and V gets 3 more digits, V' = 2n+3 − 4. So the next counter is 2n+3 + N/2 − 4, a halving map. The rebuild in one formula

Try the rebuild on any counter

The whole rebuild can be written as a short recursion on the counter N. It was read off computed rebuilds and agrees with the exact rebuild on every case tried; it is not proved. Type a counter in binary (its top digit is the sentinel) and see which digits the walk reads, what is subtracted, and the next counter.



The rule: an even N loses one digit and δ = 4. For an odd N skip the pairs 11 above the lowest digit (k of them) and look at the next pair, lower digit first. (1,0) or (0,1): stop, with δ = 1 + 25k+5(22k+4 − 1) or 4 + 25k+5(22k+3 − 1). (0,0): drop those digits, subtract 1 from the rest and go on with that number; its δ gets (22k+3 − 1) 2c2+2+5k added, c2 the number of V digits the rest of the walk adds. The next counter is 2n+c + (N >> j) − δ with c = (5j + 1)/2.






  Where it could stop
  
Halting needs a walk across the whole counter


Ticks never halt, so a halt can only come from a rebuild. The rebuild reads the up-counter from the bottom, digit by digit, by a simple rule: an even counter is read for one digit; otherwise the machine runs through pairs of digits, passing ones, then zeros, and stops at the first 1 it meets in an even position. As long as it stops before the top two digits, the rebuild does not halt (checked for every counter below 215 and along the run; not proved).


Each rebuild also puts a new block 1 0...0 on top of the counter, longer than what it removed at the bottom. So the counter is a growing stack of such blocks, and a halt needs one single walk to slip through all of them. In the run the walk never gets within 88 digits of the top. That makes a halt look very unlikely, but it is a matter of chance-like coincidences, not of a rule we can prove: the question is open.


Two sibling machines were proved non-halting by others with a ‘wall invariant’: a property of the tape at the moments V = 0 that is kept from one such moment to the next. Their rebuild converts the whole counter each time. This machine's rebuild nibbles at the bottom and leaves debris, and no property of that kind has been found for it.

11 rebuild rules
For 11 residue classes of the up-counter N modulo 2j (j in [1, 3, 5], headers 0110101, 0110111) the rebuild after V = 0 is proved for every length n of V and every tape L left of the digits it reads: L dj-1..d0 00 E> H (11)^(n+k) 1 0^inf becomes L 00 E> H' w (01)^(n+e) 1 0^inf, that is: the up-counter loses its lowest j digits (N becomes N >> j) and the new down-counter is 2n+k+c − δ with c and δ fixed by the class. For example, header 0110101 and N = 0 (mod 21): new header 0110101, 3 more digits, δ = 4. The classes were found by sampling; each theorem is then proved along the symbolic run of tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py.

tick, phase, phase_to_zero, lean/Phase.lean (written by holdouts/1RB1LC_0RC0RB_1LD0LA_1LE---_1LF1RA_1LA1LC/py/gen_phase.py)
A whole run of ticks, for any counters
Three statements about the tick configuration (up-counter U, gap, header, down-counter V, anything beyond), for counters given as arbitrary lists of binary digits rather than one pattern at a time. tick: if U is not all ones and V is not zero, the machine reaches the same configuration with U + 1 and V − 1. phase: for every k up to the value of V for which U + k still fits in U's digits, it reaches the configuration with U + k and V − k, the widths unchanged, without reading anything beyond the two counters. phase_to_zero: in particular, if U + V fits, V is counted down to 0 and U has gained exactly V. This is the step ‘then add the V ticks by arithmetic’ of the macro map, now a theorem; the macro simulator itself is still trusted code.

The macro map and its simulator
At each moment V reaches 0 the tape is the clean configuration 0^inf F U 00 E> H (11)^n 1 0^inf (F the counter's top word), so the state is (H, n, N) with N the up-counter read as one number. py/rebuild.py writes that tape, runs the rebuild (tick runs of its sub-phases jumped by the proved tick rules, the rest cell by cell in C) to the next clean tick configuration (H', n', N', V'), and then adds the V' ticks of the phase by arithmetic: next state (H', n', N' + V'), steps by the closed formula of the tick costs. Tested: its first 80 states and step counts, up to about 10182 steps, equal those of py/counters.py (itself tested against tools/c/tmrun.c).

py/walk.py check 15, random 300 120
The stopping rule of the rebuild: a walk over the digits of the counter
With header 0110101 the rebuild reads the digits b0, b1, ... of the up-counter N from the bottom. If b0 = 0 it stops after that one digit. Otherwise it goes on in a state Q over the pairs (b1,b2), (b3,b4), ...: in Q a pair (1,1) stays in Q, (0,0) switches to a state R, and (0,1) or (1,0) stops; in R a pair ending in 0 stays in R and a pair ending in 1 stops. So Q runs through ones, R runs through zeros and stops at the first 1 in an even position. If the walk stops after j digits (j is odd) and at least two digits of N remain above, the rebuild does not halt: the new counter is N with its j low digits removed, the header stays 0110101, and V gets (5j + 1)/2 more digits, V' = 2n + (5j+1)/2 − δ with δ depending only on the j digits read. Checked against the exact rebuild, not proved: all 64,516 such rebuilds with N < 215 (two values of n each), 300 random 120-digit counters, and every rebuild of the macro run below; no exception.

Where a halt can come from
Given the walk, a halt from header 0110101 needs a rebuild whose walk runs through the counter up to its top two digits. For N < 212 the 376 rebuilds of that kind were computed: none halts directly; 252 give header 0110101 again and 124 (the all-ones-like counters) give header 0110111 with the tiny counter N = 2, and rebuilds from header 0110111 often do halt. So, for the counters checked, the machine halts only if some rebuild's walk crosses the whole counter.

holdouts/1RB1LD_1RC0RB_1LA1RC_1LE0LA_1LF---_1LC---/py/map.py (check 13, random 2000 400, blank 300)
The whole rebuild as one short recursion on the counter
The rebuild at a counter N (header 0110101, V = 0 with n digits) is given by a function W(N) = (j, δ): the next macro state is (n + c, 2n+c + (N >> j) − δ) with c = (5j + 1)/2. W is a recursion on N alone. N even: j = 1, δ = 4. N odd: skip the k pairs 11 above the lowest digit and look at the next pair. Pair (1,0), lower digit first: j = 2k + 3, δ = 1 + 25k+5(22k+4 − 1). Pair (0,1): j = 2k + 3, δ = 4 + 25k+5(22k+3 − 1). Pair (0,0): drop the 2k + 2 lowest digits, subtract 1 from what is left and apply W to that, giving (j2, δ2); then j = j2 + 2k + 2 and δ = δ2 + (22k+3 − 1) 2c2+2+5k, c2 = (5j2 + 1)/2. So the states Q and R of the walk are ‘no borrow’ and ‘a borrow is pending in the rest of the counter’. The recursion was read off computed rebuilds and then compared with the exact rebuild (the machine run cell by cell, tick runs jumped by the proved tick rules): all 127 classes of low digits with j ≤ 13, each with two values of n; 2,000 random 400-digit counters (longest j = 21); and the first 300 rebuilds of the run from blank tape. No difference. It is not proved.

Why the proofs of the two siblings do not carry over
The same exact macro simulation run on the sibling 1RB1LE_1RC0RB_1LD1RC_1LA1LE_1LF0LA_1LC--- (proved non-halting in busycoq PR #57) shows a different regime: there every rebuild reads the counter up to its top, and V grows by a factor of about 3.5 each time (54, 192, 528, 1,851 digits after the first four rebuilds). The wall invariant of that proof describes a counter that is converted as a whole at every wall. Here a rebuild reads a few low digits (half the time one digit), the counter is a long list of isolated 1s that are consumed far later, and reading up to the top is exactly the event that has to be excluded.

holdouts/1RB1LD_1RC0RB_1LA1RC_1LE0LA_1LF---_1LC---/py/map.py (run 100000000)
How long the walks get
In those 100 million rebuilds j = 1 occurs 48.1% of the time and j ≡ 3 (mod 4), which puts two 1s an odd distance apart at the top of the counter, 35.4% of the time. Long walks are rare but not exponentially rare: j ≥ 65 in 46,700 rebuilds, j ≥ 129 in 6,114, j ≥ 257 in 691, j ≥ 513 in 62, j ≥ 1025 in 5; each doubling of j costs a factor of about 8 to 10. The longest walk read 1,877 digits when the counter had 74 million; after the first thousand rebuilds no walk read more than 0.23% of the counter. Above the irregular low digits the counter is a list of isolated 1s, and by the walk's rule a walk that has left the low digits stops at the first isolated 1 in an even position; so it cannot pass two isolated 1s an odd distance apart, and every rebuild with j ≡ 3 (mod 4) puts the new top 1 an odd distance (7j + 1)/2 above the old one. This is why a halt looks very unlikely, and also why no simple invariant is known: the low digits, where the next j is decided, are what earlier subtractions of δ left behind.

py/walk.py run
What the counter looks like, and why a halt looks unlikely
Each rebuild that reads j digits puts a new block 1 0...0 with (7j − 1)/2 zeros on top of the counter and removes j digits at the bottom, so the counter grows by about 2.5 j + 1 digits per rebuild: a stack of blocks 1 0a above a short stretch of irregular digits (what is left of old blocks after the subtractions of δ). The walk passes a run of ones in state Q and a run of zeros in state R, and R stops at the first 1 in an even position. To cross the whole stack, every block boundary met would have to sit at an odd position; the positions depend on j modulo 4 of the rebuilds that made the blocks, which behave irregularly. A random model gives a chance that shrinks geometrically with the number of blocks (already about 15 ones are never reached). This is a heuristic, not a proof: it is the kind of machine the community tracker calls a lottery.

    
Open
Does it halt from blank tape?
Not known. The two siblings 1RB1LC_0RC0RB_1LD0LA_1LE---_1LF1RA_1LA1LC and 1RB1LE_1RC0RB_1LD1RC_1LA1LE_1LF0LA_1LC--- have non-halting proofs in Coq in open pull requests of ccz181078/busycoq (#58 and #57; read on 12 November, not rebuilt here: Coq is not installed in this container). Their method, in plain terms: call a ‘wall’ the tape at a moment when V is 0. The proof gives a property of walls (enough spare room, and a marker: a 1 digit at a prescribed kind of position in the counter) and shows that every wall with the property runs to another wall with the property. It works there because the rebuild of those machines converts the whole counter in one go and loses only a bounded amount each time, so the marker can be followed from wall to wall. Carrying it over to this machine was tried in round 6 and did not succeed. Here a rebuild removes only the few digits the walk reads and leaves the rest of the counter in place, with the remains of earlier subtractions underneath; a later rebuild can erase several of the ‘good’ block boundaries at once (about three were seen) and creates a new one only when j = 3 modulo 4. No quantity was found that every rebuild keeps or improves, so no wall property is known that is both preserved and excludes the halting walk. Also not proved: the walk itself for all N (Lean has the 11 classes with j ≤ 5 and the two lemmas above; a generated proof of one infinite family, counters ending in 1 02k+1 1 0, did pass Lean once but took 14 minutes to check and is not included).
