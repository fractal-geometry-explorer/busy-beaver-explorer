Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0RA_1RC0RC_1LD0LE_1RA1LC_1LC0LF_0LC---

Nobody knows whether it halts. Started on a blank tape, this machine runs a clock: a block of 1s at the left end grows by two cells per tick, and tick k applies one map P to the word beside the block 2k times. The clock is proved in Lean for every tick whose 2k trips all return; an exact accelerated run follows it through 28 phases past 5.792·10176 steps.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from E1 (writes 0, moves L): 1 E>1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The clock

A block that grows by two, each tick twice as long

Watch the tape each time the head, in state C, sits at the left end. It always reads C>0 1^L 0 W: a block of L ones, then a word W of short blocks. Within a phase L grows by 2 per visit and each visit takes about twice as long as the one before. From the real run:

Inside one tick the machine runs a recursion. The head sweeps the block into 0s, makes one trip into the word W (a P-step: it rewrites the front of W and comes back), and then performs ticks 0, 1, …, k−1 on a new small block, each one eating two of the 0s it left; finally it merges. So tick k contains one P-step plus ticks 0 … k−1: 1 + 1 + 2 + … + 2k−1 = 2k P-steps in all. The word sees nothing but P, applied 2k times.

This is proved in Lean for every k and every word (tick in lean/Clock.lean): if 2k P-steps of total cost E take W to W', then 0 0 D> 1^(3+2k) 0 W reaches D> 1^(3+2k+2) 0 W' in exactly E + 12·2k − 2 steps.

The word

What P does to the word

From the phase start at step 273,305, one P-step per line (blocks of 1s):

1  3 1 1 3 3 9 1 1 1 …
2  3 3 1 1 3 9 1 1 1 …
4  3 3 3 1 1 9 1 1 1 …
7  3 1 1 1 1 1 1 9 1 1 1 …
8  3 3 3 3 1 7 1 1 1 …
15 3 1 1 1 1 1 1 1 7 1 1 1 …
16 3 3 3 3 9 1 1 1 1 1 1 3 3 …

A short binary counter (digits 1 1 and 3) runs in front; each time it wraps, the long block behind it changes (9 → 7 → …, then rebuilt). The word holds clocks of its own: later in the run blocks 1^17, 1^29, … appear, and a single P-step can contain whole ticks of such an inner clock. This nesting makes the run harder to accelerate: the exact simulator, which also does the inner ticks at once, gets to about 1023 steps in ten minutes.

Phases

A phase ends when a trip does not come back

The tick needs every one of its 2k trips to come back to the block. When one does not (the front of the word has run out), the clock breaks: the head goes on to the left over the half-built recursion, the block is dissolved into the word, and a new phase starts from a short block. Another view

Rounds, and a proved sibling

An outside Coq proof (busycoq pull request 48, not merged, not checked here) shows that the sibling 1RB0RA_1RC0LC_0LD1RF_1RA1LE_1LD0LB_0RE--- never halts. It looks at the machine once per round: when A stands on a new blank cell at the right end, the whole tape, read from the head outwards, is a word over three tokens, V = 1100, K = 00, Q = 10. During a round the head enters the first token a few times; each token passes on a number of entries to the next one, doubling or halving it; the entries that reach the far end are counted there in binary, and those digits are the clock of this page.

This machine has rounds of the same form (exact accelerated run, 20 rounds to step 6.28·1063):

But its word map is different. Counting the entries into each token in rounds run directly: on the leading Vs they go 2, 7, 17, 37, 77, … (n → 2n + 3), a K halves them, and some tokens multiply them by about 3/2, which the sibling never does. The head of the word grows and its separators alternate between K and Q from round to round, where the sibling's head is constant. So the sibling's proof is a plan, not a proof, for this machine: its rule table and its invariant would have to be found again.

Halting

What halting would take

The only halting transition is F on 1. F is entered only from E on 1 (0LF), and E only from C on 1 (0LE): the machine halts exactly when C reads a 1 with two 1s to its left, 1 1 C>1.

A normal tick never halts: the Lean theorem says it reaches its end. So a halt can only come at the end of a phase, in the trip that does not come back or in what follows it before the next visit of the left end. The accelerated run simulates exactly those parts cell by cell, and none of the 43 phase ends halted. Whether a later one does depends on the word the earlier phases built, which looks irregular: the question is open.

tick, tick_left_end, lean/Clock.lean
The clock, for every k

tick: for every k, every word W and all tapes L, if W reaches W' by 2k P-steps (each one an excursion L 0^3 1 B> W → L D> 1^3 0 W') of total cost E, then L 0 0 D> 1^(3+2k) 0 W reaches L D> 1^(3+2k+2) 0 W' in exactly E + 12·2k − 2 steps. Strong induction on k: the tick is the sweep, one P-step, ticks 0 … k−1, and the merge. tick_left_end: the same at the left end of the tape.

Heuristic
the accelerated run
What the word does

After a leading block 3 the word is a short binary counter in the digits 1 1 / 3, followed by a long block (1^9, 1^7, …) that shrinks by 2 each time the short counter wraps — a second clock inside the word. The longer blocks 1^17, 1^29 … are such second-level clocks; a P-step can contain whole ticks of them (the accelerator does those at once too).

Heuristic
py/calls.py, py/rounds.py; README
The round is a transducer with a call count, but not the sibling's

In directly simulated rounds 1 to 5 the head enters the leading V tokens 2, 7, 17, 37, 77, 157, 317, 637 times (n → 2n + 3); further in, K halves the count and some tokens turn n into about 3n/2. The sibling's rules only double, halve or keep, its word head is constant, and here the head grows and its separators alternate between K and Q with period 2. So the invariant of PR 48 does not carry over as it is; the transducer, its automaton and the potentials would have to be found for this machine.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

A normal tick cannot halt (the clock theorem). Halting needs a P-step that does not come back — that happens only at the end of a phase — followed by C on a 1 with two 1s to its left (1 1 C>1). Open: what P does over 2k trips (a counter in the word), what a phase end builds, and whether one ever reaches the halting configuration.
