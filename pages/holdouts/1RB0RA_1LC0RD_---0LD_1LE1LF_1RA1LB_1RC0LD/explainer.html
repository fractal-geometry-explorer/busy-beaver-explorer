Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0RA_1LC0RD_---0LD_1LE1LF_1RA1LB_1RC0LD

Nobody knows. Three quarters of the tape is one repeated word that the head crosses without effort; the last quarter is a binary word that changes every round trip like a cellular automaton. This page shows both and what a halt would need.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in C reading 0. C is entered only from B0 (writes 1, moves L): 0 B>0; F0 (writes 1, moves R): F>0 0. So a halt is the moment one of these patterns appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The run at a glance

Space-time diagram and records

The diagram stacks the tape at many moments. Sampling at each new cell shows the shape of the run as the visited region grows; sampling evenly in time shows where the head spends its time. The records list every step at which the head reaches a cell no earlier step reached, with the side, the width so far and the whole tape: the first place to look for a repeating macro structure.

The structure

A uniform block and a binary zone

The head sweeps from end to end. A visit to the right end adds 3 cells; a visit to the left end adds 10 to 64. At every visit to the left end the tape starts with the word 001 repeated, and this block is a steady 77% of the tape (4,281 of 5,560 cells at step 2·107, 40,812 of 52,972 at 1.8·109). Lean proves how the head enters at the left end and crosses the block: 0^inf D> (001)^(n+2) becomes 0^inf 1 0 (110)^(n+1) 1 0 D> in 5n+10 steps, and how the leftward sweep turns it back.

The remaining 23%, on the right, is a binary word: single 1s separated by one or three 0s, 01 and 0001 (after a rightward sweep, blocks 011 and 0111). Every round trip rewrites it, and the block boundary moves so that the proportions stay fixed. The rewriting looks like a one-dimensional cellular automaton; no rule for it is known.

Halting

What a halt would need

C0 is the only halting transition, and C is entered only from B on a 0 (writing 1, moving left). B is entered from A0 (1RB) and from E1 (1LB). After A0, the cell C reads is the 1 A has just written, so that path never halts. A halt needs E reading a 1 with two 0s to its left: 0 0 E>1. In the first 109 steps C is entered 100,516,612 times and reads 1 every time; no halt in 1010 steps.

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.5). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.509).

After 109 steps the tape spans 39,585 cells (26,606 left of the start, 2,388 right), with a density of 1s of 0.488.

Where a halt can come from

If the machine halts on its third step from a configuration, that configuration is one of: E with 0 two left, 0 left, 1 under the head. So a run can reach a halt only through one of these patterns, and a proof that the machine never halts only has to rule them out. The proof splits on the state and the five cells around the head.

The halting state is never close

C0 is the only halting transition and C is entered only by B0 (1LC). When B comes from A (A0 = 1RB) the cell C reads is the 1 A just wrote, so a halt needs B to come from E1 (1LB) onto two 0s: the pattern 0 0 E>1. In the first 109 steps C is entered 100,516,612 times and reads 1 every time.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The uniform block is harmless (its sweeps are proved); everything depends on the binary zone, whose rewriting has no known description, and on the interface between the two.
