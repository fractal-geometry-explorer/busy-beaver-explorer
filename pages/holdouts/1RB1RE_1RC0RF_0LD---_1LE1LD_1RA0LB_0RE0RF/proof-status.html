What is known about 1RB1RE_1RC0RF_0LD---_1LE1LD_1RA0LB_0RE0RF

1RB1RE_1RC0RF_0LD---_1LE1LD_1RA0LB_0RE0RF

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 22 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. This machine is the table of DeepMind's busycoq PR 93 (a halting proof for 1RB1RD_1LC0RE_1LD1LC_1RA0LB_0RD0RF_0RD---) with that table's halt moved: it goes on where PR 93's machine halts, and it halts only at a three-step detour when a 1 sits next to it. Lean proves 44 of PR 93's macro rules for it; PR 93's engine runs it 7,889,000 fast steps, to a tape of about 22764 cells, without a halt (trusted computation). Every unbatched step of that engine through 30.4 million fast steps falls into nine shapes, and only one comes near the halt: the event where PR 93's machine halts, one blank cell short every time.

Lean proves no halt in the first 107 steps (the first 10,000 by the kernel alone), 8 shift rules of the sweeps, the detour, the 44 engine rules, and that a halt needs the head to meet 0 0 0 1 moving right.

8 shift rules of the sweeps

For every n and every tape around: A crosses 110101010 rightward writing 001111111, D crosses it leftward writing 111011101 (eight proved rules over its rotations). Each is a theorem steps (s·n) … = some … found and proved by tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py.

lean/Sweeps.lean (sweep_rA_110101010, sweep_rB_110101010, sweep_lD_110101010_1, sweep_rA_101010110, …)

PR 93's table with its halt moved, and the detour

Mirrored, with the states renamed (A→C, B→D, C→E, D→A, E→B), this machine is the source table T3 = 1RB1RA_1LC0RD_1LD1LB_1RA0LE_0LB0LF_0LB--- of DeepMind's busycoq PR 93 (which proves that 1RB1RD_1LC0RE_1LD1LC_1RA0LB_0RD0RF_0RD--- halts), started in the same state C, with two changes: T3's halt F1 becomes T3's own E1 move (0LF), and T3's one step at D reading 0 becomes a three-step detour. In this machine's orientation the detour is B reading 0: with 0 next to it, three steps end exactly where PR 93's one step ends (detour); with 1 next to it, C reads that 1 and the machine halts (detour_halt). That is its only way to halt.

lean/Detour.lean

44 macro rules of PR 93's engine hold for this machine

PR 93's 21 basic macro rules, its 5 rules at the blank end, its 8 rules for the DefWord words and its two fast right sweeps, each stated in this machine's orientation for every count and every tape around (some split into the cases count = 0 and count ≥ 1). Their runs never meet the detour's halting case, so the engine's fast steps never hide a halt: a halt can only happen in one of its raw single steps. Generated by py/pr93rules.py with tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py; py/rulecheck.py checks the engine's shift scans the same way.

lean/PR93Rules.lean

The only way into the halt

The four steps before any halt are forced: C reads 1, so B read 0 with a 1 beyond; B came from A0 (E1 would leave a 0 under C); A came from E0; and E came from A1 or F0, moving right (D0 writes a 1 beside it). So a halt needs E, moving right, to read 0 0 0 1.

Where a halt can happen (closed tape language, 5-cell windows)

The closure search of bbtools.ctl with windows of 5 cells is closed but allows halts; the halting abstract configurations constrain the neighbourhood of a halt: a halt (C reading 1) needs 11100 or 11111 to the left; any right side starting with 1. A Python check, not a Lean certificate (the Lean library only has the right-edge and no-halt corollaries, which do not apply).

python3 -m bbtools.ctl 1RB1RE_1RC0RF_0LD---_1LE1LD_1RA0LB_0RE0RF 5

No halt in 7,889,000 fast steps of PR 93's engine (tape about 22764 cells)

PR 93's engine, extracted to OCaml with only its source table changed (T3 with F1 := 0LF), runs this machine's mirror from blank tape, and engine/watch tests at every fast step whether the next steps reach the halting pattern. None does in the first 7,889,000 fast steps, at the end of which the tape holds about 22764 cells (so about 101663 or more steps). At fast step 2,452,241 (tape about 21400 cells) the run reaches the configuration where PR 93's machine halts (F reading 1); this machine goes on there. The engine agrees with direct simulation through fast step 1,331 (3·1011 steps; in the first 3.6·109 of them all 453,682,544 steps of B reading 0 have 0 next to them). The extracted engine is not a proof for this table: PR 93's soundness proof is for T3.

Every unbatched step of PR 93's engine through 30,442,000 fast steps: nine shapes, none near a halt but one

engine/rawlog.ml logs every raw step of the engine from blank tape. Through fast step 30,442,000 there are 65 raw stretches of nine shapes, and the run from the 30.4M checkpoint to 47,000,000 fast steps shows no other shape. Every D step reading 0 in them comes right after B1 = 0RD, which leaves a 0 beside it, so it cannot halt. The only step on the leftward route to the halt is C<0 in the event where PR 93's own machine halts (its F1, 9 times to 30.4M); there the cell after the two 0s is always a 1, so the route stops one 0 short. No halt in 47,000,000 fast steps.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: power/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.506). Right of the start it grows like a power of the step count other than 1/2 (fitted exponent 0.15).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

Macro structure: bouncer with a sorted list of blocks 1^(6j+1)

Left of the start: (110 10 10 10) repeated, a √t bouncer (proved shift rules). Right of it, on the slow side (394 cells after 109 steps), a list of blocks of 1s separated by 00 whose settled lengths are 6j + 1, sorted, with 6j + 5 blocks at group boundaries, e.g. at step 945,073,674: 41 : 43 : 43 : 43 : 43 : 43 : 43 : 43 : 43 : 43 : 7 : 1 : 47 : 49 : 49 : 11 : 1 : 53 : 55 : 59 : 5 : 1 : 1 : 3 : 1 (run lengths, : = 00).

No halt in the first 8.29×1013 steps of an accelerated run

Observed, not proved: the accelerated simulator rsim.py ran 8.29×1013 steps from blank tape without reaching a halting transition. Its tape, state and head position were equal to those of a plain C simulator at 6 checkpoints, the last at 1.01×1010 steps. Of these steps, the part after the last checkpoint rests on the simulator alone, including 7,944 trip-level jumps: a jump applies many identical round trips at once after seeing the same tape shape recur with constant count changes, which is validated against direct simulation but not proved.

Does it halt from blank tape?
