Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0RA_1LC0LE_1RA1LD_0LB1LF_1LD0RC_1LB---

Nobody knows yet. This machine builds a row of counters on its tape. The front counter grows by a factor of about 4/3 each round, and whether the count is divisible by 3 decides what happens next, just like in the Collatz problem. It has been followed for 101000 steps without stopping.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from D1 (writes 1, moves L): 1 D>1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.604). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

After 109 steps the tape spans 46,692 cells (39,843 left of the start, 35 right), with a density of 1s of 0.386.

Structure

What the tape is made of

Read the tape in words of three cells: 110 (drawn in the accent colour) and 100 (grey). Whenever the head is back at the left end, in state C, the tape is: a short start 0 1 0, a counter of 110s, a marker 1111, and then a row of levels. A level is a run of 110s, a separator 11100 (orange) and a run of 100s. The first level is the front; write it (x, y).

At step 58,429, for instance, the counter is 2 and the levels are (0, 72), (12, 26), (10, 3), (2, 1), (2, 0), (2, 0), followed by 11 and blank tape on the right.

The basic move

One sweep

The head runs right through the counter and the front level, turns the first three 100s after the separator into four 110s, and runs back. The separator moves right by three words, and the tape grows by three cells at the left end. Everything behind the front is untouched.

Exactly: (x, y + 3) → (x + 4, y) in 8p + 24x + 75 steps. A phase is ⌊y/3⌋ sweeps in a row.

The emergent behaviour

A counter that multiplies by 4/3

When the 100s of the front run out, the remainder r = y mod 3 decides what happens.

rnew frontnext levelcounter
0(0, x)(z + 2, w − 2)p + 2
2(0, x + 2)(z + 2, w − 2)p + 2
1a different regime: secondary bouncers rebuild the counter into new levels, and the run comes back with p = 0

Since a phase of ⌊y/3⌋ sweeps adds 4 to x each time, a front that starts empty ends with x = 4⌊y/3⌋, so the new front is

y ↦ 4y/3 if y ≡ 0,   y ↦ 4(y − 2)/3 + 2 if y ≡ 2 (mod 3).

From step 58,429 the front goes 72 → 96 → 128 → 170 → 226, and 226 ≡ 1 ends the clean run. Later: 734 → 978 → 1304 → 1738 ≡ 1. Each reset also ticks the next level, two words at a time; when that level's 100s run out it is reset in turn and ticks the one after it: a mixed-radix counter whose digits get longer every time they are reset. Because y grows geometrically and a phase costs about y² steps, the tape grows like the square root of time.

The question

Where it could halt

The only halting transition is F reading 1. F is entered only from D reading 1, so the machine halts exactly when D reads a 1 that has a 1 on its left: C at 1 1 C>1 or E at 1 1 E>0. The sweeps and the clean phase ends never do that (they are proved for all values). What is not yet written as a rule is the y ≡ 1 regime and the reset of the levels far to the right; those are where a halt would have to come from.

Heuristic
README
A Collatz-like counter

The residue of y mod 3 at the end of each phase looks irregular; y ≡ 1 starts a secondary bouncer that rebuilds the left part, and the run returns to the clean form with p = 0. Levels further right are reset in turn, each a bit longer than before.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. It halts exactly when D reads a 1 with a 1 to its left (C at 1 1 C>1 or E at 1 1 E>0); the proved rules never do that, but the y ≡ 1 regime and the deep resets are not closed rules yet.
