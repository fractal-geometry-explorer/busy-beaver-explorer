1RB0RA_0RC0LB_1LD1RF_1LE1RA_1RD1RB_1LA---

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 13 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The run is a regular round structure (the total length of the blocks doubles each round, a round takes about four times as long as the one before); one round step is proved in Lean for all counts, the rounds are observed, not proved, and nothing found so far says whether the halting transition (F reading 1) can ever fire.

Other proofs and outside reports

Community label

In the community's BB(6) tracker the machine's class is Bell-like. These are labels read off the run by others, not results.

The block cycle, for all counts

With the head in C at the right end of b^(n+1) 1 a^(m+1) 1 c 1 a^(k+1) 0000 c (a = 11010, b = 10000, c = 10), the machine turns the last b into an a and lengthens the last run of a by one in 50k + 50m + 287 steps, without reading the tape left of the block: the bouncer's elementary move.

No closed tape language from windows up to 7

bbtools.ctl with windows of K = 2 to 6 cells closes but allows F to read a 1 (6 to 192 abstract halting configurations); with K = 7 it finds no closed set within its round limit. The n-gram CPS decider (widths 3 to 7, with history and LRU augmentations) also fails.

tools/py/bbtools/ctl.py

A round map for the whole block list, checked against the direct run

Sample the run whenever the head is in state B reading 0 with only 0s to its left (about once or twice a round). The tape is cut into runs of repeated words and short literals; the next sample is computed from simulations in which every long run is short, one run at a time is lengthened, and two further instances check that the new counts are affine in the old ones. Along the direct run from blank tape to 3·109 steps (18 samples) this map predicts all 17 next samples exactly. An extrapolation checked by computation, not a proof.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.062). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.487).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

Rounds: the new block is the sum of the old ones

At the start of each round the tape is a small gadget at the left end, then blocks b^n1 c 00 b^n2 c 00 ... b^nk. During the round the bouncer works from the right, turning each block into a's one b per pair of sweeps while a tail of a's at the right end grows by one per b; at the end of the round a pass from the left turns everything back into b's and the tail becomes a new block. So the new block is (up to a few cells) the sum of all the blocks, the total doubles: 3, 21, 61, 152, 341, 720, 1491, 3038, 6138, 12339, 24743, 49564, 99212. In some rounds (2·106, 2.6·109 steps) the new block absorbs the previous largest one instead of standing next to it. Observed in the first 6.5·1011 steps, not proved.

py/rounds.py

Why the left side grows like a logarithm

The head reaches the far left only once per round, where the gadget (a small counter) gains about two cells; rounds take four times longer each, so the left reach grows like log4 of the step count. The right side grows by 5 cells every two sweeps: a bouncer, square-root growth.

tools/c/tokrun.c

40 more samples past the direct run, no halt

Iterated from the last sample of the direct run: 40 samples without a halt; the list then has about 50 blocks and the largest holds a count of 69 bits (the direct run reaches 14 bits). The blocks keep the shape (10100)^n 1 000 and roughly double. The run was stopped by a time limit after round 40, not by an event of the machine; the classes change every round (a block is added), so each round costs new simulations. An earlier run with a fixed threshold stopped after 33 samples at a class whose simulations disagreed, when two blocks of about 20 words stood next to the left-end gadget; with the threshold raised for such rounds the iteration passes that point.

Does it halt?

Not known. The machine halts only when F reads a 1; F is entered (from C reading a 1) about 2% of all steps and in the first 2·108 steps (4,004,892 visits) it always had 0101 to its left. Whether a round can ever end in a configuration where it reads a 1 depends on the left-end gadget and on how blocks merge, which are not understood yet.
