Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1LF_1LC1RD_1RA1LA_0RA0RE_0RC---_1RF0LC

Nobody knows. This machine moves a block of N copies of 110 across a junction two in, three out, so N grows by 3/2 each step, while a wall on the left keeps a tower of smaller counters driven by the parity of N. No halting wall has been found.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in E reading 1. E is entered only from D1 (writes 0, moves R): D>1 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

What it does

A block moved two in, three out

Write b for the three cells 110. At the start of every step the tape is W A> 0 bN 1: a short wall W on the left, then one long block of N copies of b. The head builds a junction 111 and sweeps: each sweep runs right to the end of the tape and back, takes two copies of b from the block on the left of the junction and adds three to the block on its right. When the left block is used up, the right block has about 3N/2 copies, and the next step starts. The tape grows on the right at each sweep (the square-root side); the wall on the left changes only once per step and grows slowly (the logarithmic side).

The parity of N decides how the step ends. The wall is a stack of counters:

wall WN evenN odd
11, 3N/2 + 71^4 b 1, (3N+9)/2
1^(3j+1) b 1 (j odd)1^(3j+4), 3N/2 + 71 b^(j+1) 1^3 b 1, (3N+9)/2
1^(3j+1) (j even)1, 3N/2 + 7 + j1^(3j+4) b 1, (3N+9)/2
1 b^k 1^3 b 11 b^k 1^6, 3N/2 + 7k even: 1 b^(3k/2+2) 1^3 b 1; k odd: 1^4 b 1 b^((3k-1)/2) 1^3 b 1
1 b^k 1^6k even: 1, 3N/2 + 3k/2 + 9; k odd: 1^4 b 1, 3N/2 + (3k+13)/2k even: 1^4 b 1; k odd: 1 (k is added into N)

So a run of alternating parities lengthens the wall 1^(3j+1); an odd step at the wrong moment turns it into a number k in the wall, and that number is itself multiplied by 3/2 at later odd steps, possibly spawning a further level. Two even steps fold the wall back into N. From the run: N = 468, 709, 1068, 1609, 2418, 3634, 5462, … with walls 1, 1, 1^4 b 1, 1^7, 1^10 b 1, 1^13, 1, …

Lean

The step map, for every wall at once

There are infinitely many walls, and the end of a step looks different for each. What they share: the head crosses the middle of the long block of b's only whole, in one of three ways:

statedirectionbeforeafter
C→0 C> (110)^k(110)^k 0 C>
A→0110 A> (110)^k(111)^k 0110 A>
F←(111)^k F> 1F> 1 (110)^k

So the parts of a step before and after the transfer loop can be computed by a small simulator that steps cell by cell near the wall and near the right end (keeping some copies of b on each side as working room) and jumps over the copies between by a shift rule. It never looks at how many copies it jumps. Lean proves that whatever this simulator answers is what the machine does (lean/Zone.lean, generic; lean/Reduction.lean, this machine), and chains it with the transfer loop into a computable step map stepMap (W, N) = (W', N'), halt, or stuck (no answer).

The theorem halts_iff: if the map never answers stuck along the orbit that starts at step 10,206 of the run (11101101111101 A> 0 b^48 1), the machine halts from blank tape exactly when that orbit reaches a halting step. The kernel checks the first 40 steps; the same simulator in Python has run 40,000 steps (always answering) and agrees with plain simulation for 4,000.

What is still missing for a statement with no hypothesis is a proof that the simulator never gets stuck; and for a decision, a description of all reachable walls.

Halting

No way to halt has been seen

The machine halts only when E reads a 1, which needs the head in A on a 0 followed by 111. In the 300,000 steps of the map from the run, and in every step from the 100 walls reachable with arbitrary parities, this never happens. Unlike the Antihydra-type cryptids, no halting condition on the map is known; but the reachable walls form a tower of counters with no proved description, so non-halting is not proved either.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.047). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.494).

After 109 steps the tape spans 44,753 cells (21 left of the start, 32,221 right), with a density of 1s of 0.724.

Kernel
sweep, lean/Rules.lean; one_sweep, sweeps, lean/Sweeps.lean
The transfer loop

At a junction L 1 b^(x+2) 111 F> b^(y+1) 1 (b = 110, any tape L, blank on the right) one sweep of 18y + 50 steps gives L 1 b^x 111 F> b^(y+4) 1; by induction t sweeps move 2t copies out of the left block and 3t into the right one.

Kernel
first, lean/Rules.lean
The start of a step

From the wall 1, 1 A> 0 b^(n+6) 1 reaches the first junction in 5n + 66 steps.

Kernel
step_next, step_halt, lean/Reduction.lean; arun_sound, arun_halt, lean/Zone.lean
The step map for every wall

A step from W A> 0 b^N 1 has three parts: the head crosses the block and builds the junction L 1 b^x 111 F> b^(y+1) 1 with x = N − const; the transfer loop makes x mod 2, y + 3⌊x/2⌋ of it; the end of the step works on the wall and leaves W' A> 0 b^N' 1. The first and third parts are computed by a simulator that steps cell by cell near the wall and near the right end and crosses the middle of the long block only whole, by three shift rules; it never looks at the number of copies it crosses. Lean proves that whatever it answers is what the machine does: if stepMap (W, N) = (W', N') the machine goes from the first step start to the second, and if it answers halt the machine halts in that step. For every wall W and every N for which the simulator answers; it may answer stuck (N too small for the working room (about two copies of b per wall cell), a meeting with the block that the three rules do not cover, or out of fuel), which has not been seen along the orbit.

Reduction to the orbit

The run from blank tape reaches the step start 11101101111101 A> 0 b^48 1 after 10,206 steps. Let orbit be the orbit of the step map from there. If the map never answers stuck along the orbit, the machine halts from blank tape if and only if the orbit reaches a halting step. Without any hypothesis: a halting step in the orbit gives a halt, and an orbit that goes on for ever gives a run that never halts.

Heuristic
Why it may never halt

The numbers in the wall grow like N only along runs of odd N and are absorbed into N by two even steps in a row, so the wall stays short; a halting configuration would need a wall shape the map has not produced in any explored branch. This is evidence, not an argument.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. By the reduction it halts iff the orbit of the step map reaches a halting step (given that the map keeps answering). No halting step is known at all; a proof of non-halting would need a description of every reachable wall (a tower of 3/2-counters) and of the steps on it.
