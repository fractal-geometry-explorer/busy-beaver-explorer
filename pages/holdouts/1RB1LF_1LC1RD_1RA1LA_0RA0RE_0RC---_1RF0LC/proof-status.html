What is known about 1RB1LF_1LC1RD_1RA1LA_0RA0RE_0RC---_1RF0LC

1RB1LF_1LC1RD_1RA1LA_0RA0RE_0RC---_1RF0LC

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 13 November 2026.

In short

The question is open, and reduced to an orbit. A step takes W A> 0 b^N 1 (b = 110) to W' A> 0 b^N' 1 with N' = (3N + c)/2, where the new wall W' and c are computed from the wall W and the parity of N. Lean proves the transfer loop behind the 3/2, the step map for every wall on which its simulator answers, and that, as long as it answers along the orbit, the machine halts from blank tape exactly when the orbit reaches a halting step. The map has been iterated for 40,000 steps without a halt (the plain-simulation map for 300,000), and no wall with a halting step has been found in a search of the wall graph: no halting mechanism is known for this machine, but no invariant excluding one is proved.

The transfer loop

At a junction L 1 b^(x+2) 111 F> b^(y+1) 1 (b = 110, any tape L, blank on the right) one sweep of 18y + 50 steps gives L 1 b^x 111 F> b^(y+4) 1; by induction t sweeps move 2t copies out of the left block and 3t into the right one.

sweep, lean/Rules.lean; one_sweep, sweeps, lean/Sweeps.lean

The start of a step

From the wall 1, 1 A> 0 b^(n+6) 1 reaches the first junction in 5n + 66 steps.

first, lean/Rules.lean

The step map for every wall

A step from W A> 0 b^N 1 has three parts: the head crosses the block and builds the junction L 1 b^x 111 F> b^(y+1) 1 with x = N − const; the transfer loop makes x mod 2, y + 3⌊x/2⌋ of it; the end of the step works on the wall and leaves W' A> 0 b^N' 1. The first and third parts are computed by a simulator that steps cell by cell near the wall and near the right end and crosses the middle of the long block only whole, by three shift rules; it never looks at the number of copies it crosses. Lean proves that whatever it answers is what the machine does: if stepMap (W, N) = (W', N') the machine goes from the first step start to the second, and if it answers halt the machine halts in that step. For every wall W and every N for which the simulator answers; it may answer stuck (N too small for the working room (about two copies of b per wall cell), a meeting with the block that the three rules do not cover, or out of fuel), which has not been seen along the orbit.

step_next, step_halt, lean/Reduction.lean; arun_sound, arun_halt, lean/Zone.lean

Reduction to the orbit

The run from blank tape reaches the step start 11101101111101 A> 0 b^48 1 after 10,206 steps. Let orbit be the orbit of the step map from there. If the map never answers stuck along the orbit, the machine halts from blank tape if and only if the orbit reaches a halting step. Without any hypothesis: a halting step in the orbit gives a halt, and an orbit that goes on for ever gives a run that never halts.

The wall is a stack of counters (observed)

Every wall in the first 100,000 steps of the orbit is 1 X_1 … X_n with items K(k) = b^k 1^3 b 1 (an open counter) and K'(k) = b^k 1^6 (a closed one), at most 7 items. A step sends the parity of N into the wall from the right. An open counter on top closes when the parity is even; when it is odd it becomes about 3k/2 and passes the parity of k to the items below it. A closed counter is dissolved and its copies of b go back to the block, about 3k/2 of them, after it has passed a parity down; an empty closed counter hit by an odd parity gets a new open counter on top. This recursive rule (step in py/stackmodel.py) gives the same (W', N') as the step map that Lean proves, py/zonemap.py, on all 100,000 orbit steps (N then has 58,503 bits) and on 2,563 random walls of up to five items with either parity. It was fitted by hand and is not proved. It has no halting rule, and it does not describe what happens when a count would go below zero or when an empty closed counter lies under other items.

python3 py/stackmodel.py orbit 100000, python3 py/stackmodel.py random 1500

Rounds between end events, by remainder class

Sample the run whenever the head is in state B reading 1 with only 0s to its right. Read leftwards from the head, the tape is then A U^m B: one long periodic block between two short context words. For each class (A, U, B, m modulo 1 or 2 (the smallest modulus that works for the context)) the next sample is simulated for six values of m and extended as an affine map of m (with a cubic step count). Along the direct run from blank tape to 3·109 steps (39 events) the class map predicts the next event in all 26 rounds it applies to. This is an extrapolation checked by computation, not a proof.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.047). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.494).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

Why it may never halt

The numbers in the wall grow like N only along runs of odd N and are absorbed into N by two even steps in a row, so the wall stays short; a halting configuration would need a wall shape the map has not produced in any explored branch. This is evidence, not an argument.

273,141 rounds of the class map without a halt

Iterated with exact integers from the event at step 29,839 of the run (A = (empty), U = 110, m = 79, B = 18 0110 13): 273,141 rounds without meeting a halting class; m then has 79,895 bits (about 1048,102 steps of the machine). 226 distinct contexts occur, the longest of 4,246 cells. The iteration stops there, at a context too large for the six simulations (a second long block has formed); this is a limit of the tool, not an event of the machine.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. By the reduction it halts iff the orbit of the step map reaches a halting step (given that the map keeps answering). No halting step is known at all. In the observed stack model a halt could only come from a counter running below zero, which needs the counts in the wall to overtake N; a proof of non-halting would need the stack rules proved for every wall and a bound that keeps the wall below N.
