OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The head shuttles out from the right end over the background word 1110111110, which it rewrites in a second phase and back (proved in Lean). It turns back at a defect or passes it depending on the defect's shape, and the excursions follow the ruler sequence of a binary counter whose bits are the defects. At the left end sits a unary counter (0111)^n: n grows by one at each of the rare events at that end (17 events between 3929 and 2.27·109 steps, n from 6 to 22). Between the ends, domains of another pattern grow at the right end and dissolve into gliders that travel left.

Where a halt can come from

If the machine halts on its third step from a configuration, that configuration is one of: C with 0 under the head, 1 right, 0 two right; F with 1 under the head, 1 right, 0 two right. So a run can reach a halt only through one of these patterns, and a proof that the machine never halts only has to rule them out. The proof splits on the state and the five cells around the head.

Excursions follow a binary ruler sequence

Between consecutive visits of the head to the right end in state B, near 2·108 steps, the head turns back after about 900, 15,600, 900, 22,900, 900, 15,600, 900, 28,500, … cells: the ruler sequence of a binary counter, each defect passing the head on every other visit.

Regular-language deciders tried (F11)

None of these closes: each reaches a halting configuration in its abstraction, which says nothing about whether the machine halts. n-gram CPS (tools/c/ngramcps.c), equal widths on both sides: a halting local configuration is reached with plain up to width 24, history 1 up to width 24, history 2 up to width 22, history 3 up to width 24, LRU history up to width 7 (larger widths ran out of the 150 s or 60 million configuration budget). n-gram CPS with every cell tagged by its position mod p: fails for p = 2 up to width 20, p = 3 up to width 16, p = 4 up to width 20, p = 6 up to width 16. n-gram CPS where each side also carries the state of a small automaton that has read that side from the far end (every automaton with up to 3 states on both sides, or 4 states on one side), with position mod 1..4 and widths 4, 6, 8: no closed set without a halt in all 120 parameter sets. Run-length windows (the K nearest runs of equal macro cells on each side, run lengths kept exactly below T and mod M above): fails for all 72 parameter sets (cells of 1 to 4 bits, K = 2, 4, 6, T = 2, 4, M = 1, 2, 4). Two-automaton closed tape language by SAT (a deterministic automaton reading each side inward, n states each, CaDiCaL): no solution for n = 2..9; n = 10 not finished in 150 s.

heuristic Evidence only

Domains and gliders

In the defect picture, regions of a different pattern (black wedges) grow at the right end; when one stops growing, it is eaten from its left edge and sends gliders to the left; gliders slow down near the left end. The intervals between counter events grow irregularly (ratios 1.4 to 4).

the picture pages/holdouts/1RB0LC_1RC1RA_0RD1LE_1RE1RF_1LC0RB_---1RD/defects.png

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.049). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.495).
