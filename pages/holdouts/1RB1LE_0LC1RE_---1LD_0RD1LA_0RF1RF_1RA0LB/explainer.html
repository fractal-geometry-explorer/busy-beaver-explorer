A ladder that eats its own past, and then falls off the edge

1RB1LE_0LC1RE_---1LD_0RD1LA_0RF1RF_1RA0LB

Decided: it haltsProved in this repository, checked by Lean. The ledger below says what each part rests on.

It halts, after about 1.27·10758 steps. The machine keeps a ladder of counters at the right end of its tape. Each counter is invisible to everything left of it, so the far left of the tape runs the same program in extreme slow motion. There the ladder's tail eats the remains of an earlier ladder; when they are used up, the head steps off the left end of the tape and the machine stops.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in C reading 0. C is entered only from B0 (writes 0, moves L): 0 B>0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

Reading the tape

Pairs, digits, and a front

Stop the machine each time its head, in state A, stands on the blank cell right of everything it has written (a visit). Read the tape from the head leftwards two cells at a time: X is the pair 0 1, Y is 1 1; nothing else occurs. Write the lengths of the runs of Y between the X's as digits, so 2 1 4 4 is Y Y X Y X Y Y Y Y X Y Y Y Y. Each visit makes the word one pair longer.

At every second visit the word has the same build:

front   4^n1 s1   4^n2 s2   ...   4^nk sk   tail

The front is 2 1 or 5, in turn. A level is a rung of n blocks 4 (on the tape: 0111111111) followed by a separator s, one of eight short words. The tail is what is left over, down to the blank left end. Going from the front 2 1 the head makes what we call an H-hit on the rest of the word, from 5 a V-hit; then a short visit of 8 steps turns the front into the other one.

The mechanism

Every level is a digit with 16 positions

The separator and the kind of the next hit give a level 16 possible positions. A hit always moves it to the next one:

So the ladder is an odometer: the first level turns with every hit of the front, the second with 6 of every 16 hits of the first, and so on. This table is proved in Lean for every rung length and every surrounding tape (lean/Levels.lean).

Why it can be simulated

Levels are transparent

A hit on the part of the tape left of some border is always the same short list of calls: the head crosses the border in a certain state and later comes back in a certain state (for a V-hit: in C, back in E; for an H-hit five calls). A level looks at what is to its left only through these calls. And it passes on H-hits and V-hits in turn, exactly as the front does.

So the tape left of a level behaves exactly as if the front stood directly before it. It only gets its hits more rarely. That makes an exact shortcut possible: keep each level as two numbers (position and rung length), computed with exact integer arithmetic, and run the real machine, cell by cell, only on the front followed by the tail. Step counts stay exact too, because a hit costs 112 n + c or 56 n + c steps inside a level with n blocks. py/accel.py does this, and lean/Ladder.lean does it again as a function proved correct; up to step 1011 its visit numbers and step counts are those of the plain simulator.

The long run

Resets, fuel, and 887 levels

A hit that reaches the blank left end of the tape comes back in an unusual state, and that flattens the ladder: one sweep turns every block 4 into 0 3. It happened at visits 3, 11 and 25 and, with a ladder of six levels, at visit 3,006 (step 27,167,343). After a reset a new ladder grows at the right end, and the flattened blocks are fuel for its tail. In a cycle of 12 hits,

the tail uses up two fuel blocks and leaves three new levels behind. But every new level makes the hits on the tail 8/3 times rarer. The fuel left by the reset of visit 3,006 is about 590 blocks, enough for 887 levels: the tail needs about (8/3)887 ≈ 10378 visits to eat its way to the left end, and a visit by then takes about 10379 steps.

The end

Off the edge

The machine can only halt in state C on a 0, and C is only entered when B reads a 0 and steps left. Inside the tape every 0 stands alone, so this needs B at the blank left end.

When the fuel is gone, the tail is 0 3 1 0 3 1 0 5 with blank tape behind it, and an H-hit is due. Its first three calls come back as usual. In the fourth the head runs through the tail in state C, turns the last pairs over, and B arrives on the blank cell left of the tape: one step left, C reads a 0, and the machine halts. How sure is this?

What the result rests on

The halt is a theorem checked by the proof assistant Lean (halts in lean/Halt.lean). It has three parts:

  • The rules, for every size (checked by Lean's kernel). A hit on one level is proved for every rung length; from that, by induction, any number of hits on a ladder with any number of levels does what the table says and hands the right number of hits to the tape left of it. The step counts are part of the statements.
  • A program that is proved correct (kernel). The shortcut simulator of the section above is written again as a function inside Lean, and Lean proves: whatever number this function returns is the number of steps after which the machine really halts. The function may choose badly which levels to treat as numbers; then it fails, but it cannot return a wrong answer.
  • Running it (compiled evaluation). Lean runs the plain machine for the first 27,172,911 steps, to just after the last reset, and then runs the function: 3,539 simulated hits later it returns the 759-digit number. These two evaluations are done by Lean's compiled code, not by its small kernel; this is the usual, slightly weaker, level of trust for long computations (native_decide).

Before the theorem there was the same result from two independent programs (py/accel.py and the re-computation in verify/), compared with plain simulation at more than 12,000 checkpoints; they give the same step.

Kernel
lean/Odo.lean, lean/Hits.lean (from py/hits_lean.py), lean/Ladder.lean
Levels are transparent: any list of levels, any number of hits

A call on a tape T is a run that enters T in one state and leaves it in another, whatever lies to the right; a V-hit is one call, an H-hit five. hit0 … hit15 (from the stretch theorems): a hit on a level with j + 1 blocks at each of the 16 positions is such a hit, takes 112(j+1) + c or 56(j+1) + c steps inside the level, leaves the level at the next position, and at the six passing positions makes exactly one hit on the rest. level_run: y hits in a row on one level, with closed forms for position, block count, hits passed on and steps (16 hits are one turn: 4 blocks more, 6 hits passed on). stack_run: by induction on the list of levels, y hits on a whole ladder act level by level as the table says and pass the remaining hits to the tape below. front_run: the front and its two visits per hit. haltUp_sound, frontHalt_sound: a hit below the levels that halts (in its first call, or in the fourth call of an H-hit) is a halt of the machine, with the steps made in every level before it.

Kernel
lean/Ladder.lean
The accelerated run is sound

crun_sound, hrun_sound: a simulator for one call on a finite tape (blank beyond), correct for every tape to the right of the call. opAdv_sound (a jump of y hits of which none or one reaches the simulated cells), opPeel_sound, opUnpeel_sound (cells read as a level, and back), opFinal_sound (the hit that halts) and loop_sound: if the run is at the configuration of a state after its step count, and loop returns N from that state, then the machine reaches a halting configuration after exactly N steps from blank tape. Which levels to read and how far to jump is chosen by code that needs no proof: a wrong choice can only make loop fail.

Independent re-computation of the halt

A second implementation of the level odometer, written separately (its own cell simulator, its own measured step constants, the deepest level always simulated cell by cell), gives the same 887 levels, the same tail, the same last visit and the same 759-digit halting step. Direct simulation agrees with the accelerated run at 12,169 checkpoints in 440 windows spread over the whole run (visit number, step count and whole tape), and about 80 truncated endgames run directly to the halt stop at exactly the predicted step (the longest after 4,568,217,938,175 steps). Not observed directly: anything between step 1.2×1012 and the halt with more than about 14 levels on the tape; that part is covered by the Lean theorem above.

lean/Levels.lean (from py/levels_lean.py)
The level rules, for every block count

A level 4n s is the cells s (0111111111)n. For each of its 16 positions and each stretch of a hit between two crossings of the level's borders, the machine's run is proved for every n ≥ 1 and every tape left and right of the level (63 theorems). A 64th and 65th theorem, last_visit_run and last_visit_halts, run the last visit on the word with all 887 levels removed (2 1 0 3 1 0 3 1 0 5, blank to the left): it halts after 278 steps. The generator checks that the stretches chain as the table says: the states in which the head leaves and comes back, that an absorbing level never touches the tape to its left, and the level after the hit.
