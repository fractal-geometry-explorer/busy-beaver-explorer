Decided: it haltsProved in this repository, checked by Lean and by Coq. The ledger below says what each part rests on.

This six-state, two-symbol Turing machine, one of the holdouts of the BB(6) search, halts when started on a blank tape, after about 1.27·10758 steps. The exact step is known, and the halt is a Lean theorem: the rules of the machine's ladder of counters are proved for every size and every number of counters with Lean's kernel, and the long run itself is evaluated inside Lean by a function that is proved correct (compiled evaluation, native_decide).

Status as of 16 November 2026.

In short

The machine halts (Lean theorem halts; two steps of it by compiled evaluation). At the visits of the right end the tape is a short front, a ladder of levels (a rung of n blocks 0111111111 and one of eight separator words) and a tail at the left end. Every level is a digit with 16 positions: a hit from the right moves it to the next position, at ten positions the hit is absorbed, at six it is passed on to the left. What lies left of a level therefore lives exactly as it would right behind the front, only 8/3 times slower. After the reset of step 27,167,343, which turned the old ladder into fuel, the tail eats two fuel blocks and leaves three new levels in every 12 hits. With 887 levels the fuel is used up, the next hit runs into the blank left end, and state C reads a 0: the halt, at a step with 759 digits.

The machine halts (Coq, on busycoq's definitions)

halt : halts tm c0, with busycoq's TM, halts and c0. The 16 level hits, the 9 halting hits and the two fronts are proved for every block count by busycoq's symbolic execution; stack_run (any number of hits on any ladder) and loop_ok (a shortcut run that keeps the levels as numbers and simulates only the tail cells, sound by construction) are ordinary proofs. The run from the blank tape, loop 10000 true s0 = true, is one evaluation checked by Coq's kernel (about 30 seconds; it makes no plain run of 27 million steps first and batches up to 64 tail hits per pass over the levels). Print Assumptions halt: closed under the global context. Coq 8.18.0, about 2 minutes for the file; too little fuel makes the check fail.

Levels are transparent: any list of levels, any number of hits

A call on a tape T is a run that enters T in one state and leaves it in another, whatever lies to the right; a V-hit is one call, an H-hit five. hit0 … hit15 (from the stretch theorems): a hit on a level with j + 1 blocks at each of the 16 positions is such a hit, takes 112(j+1) + c or 56(j+1) + c steps inside the level, leaves the level at the next position, and at the six passing positions makes exactly one hit on the rest. level_run: y hits in a row on one level, with closed forms for position, block count, hits passed on and steps (16 hits are one turn: 4 blocks more, 6 hits passed on). stack_run: by induction on the list of levels, y hits on a whole ladder act level by level as the table says and pass the remaining hits to the tape below. front_run: the front and its two visits per hit. haltUp_sound, frontHalt_sound: a hit below the levels that halts (in its first call, or in the fourth call of an H-hit) is a halt of the machine, with the steps made in every level before it.

The accelerated run is sound

crun_sound, hrun_sound: a simulator for one call on a finite tape (blank beyond), correct for every tape to the right of the call. opAdv_sound (a jump of y hits of which none or one reaches the simulated cells), opPeel_sound, opUnpeel_sound (cells read as a level, and back), opFinal_sound (the hit that halts) and loop_sound: if the run is at the configuration of a state after its step count, and loop returns N from that state, then the machine reaches a halting configuration after exactly N steps from blank tape. Which levels to read and how far to jump is chosen by code that needs no proof: a wrong choice can only make loop fail.

The level rules, for every block count

A level 4n s is the cells s (0111111111)n. For each of its 16 positions and each stretch of a hit between two crossings of the level's borders, the machine's run is proved for every n ≥ 1 and every tape left and right of the level (63 theorems). A 64th and 65th theorem, last_visit_run and last_visit_halts, run the last visit on the word with all 887 levels removed (2 1 0 3 1 0 3 1 0 5, blank to the left): it halts after 278 steps. The generator checks that the stretches chain as the table says: the states in which the head leaves and comes back, that an absorbing level never touches the tape to its left, and the level after the hit.

Independent re-computation of the halt

A second implementation of the level odometer, written separately (its own cell simulator, its own measured step constants, the deepest level always simulated cell by cell), gives the same 887 levels, the same tail, the same last visit and the same 759-digit halting step. Direct simulation agrees with the accelerated run at 12,169 checkpoints in 440 windows spread over the whole run (visit number, step count and whole tape), and about 80 truncated endgames run directly to the halt stop at exactly the predicted step (the longest after 4,568,217,938,175 steps). Not observed directly: anything between step 1.2×1012 and the halt with more than about 14 levels on the tape; that part is covered by the Lean theorem above.
