Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LB_0RC0LE_1RD0RF_1LB1RB_0LA0RE_1RE---

Decided: it haltsProved in this repository, checked by Lean and by Coq. The ledger below says what each part rests on.

Yes, it halts, and Lean has checked the proof. Behind two counters ticking together this machine is a small rewriting system: blocks 000/100 that act as binary digits, blocks 1010 that double the visits passing through, and a block 1011 that stops the machine. For 161,210 rounds the trap never forms; in round 161,211 it does. Nobody could watch that happen: it takes about 1024,266 steps.

Five levels

From six states to one computation

"Does this machine halt?" is answered here by walking down five levels of description. Each level forgets detail the one above it keeps, and each step down is a Lean theorem that carries the answer back up: a halt found at a lower level is a halt of the level above. At the bottom there is no machine left, only one computation, and running that computation is the last link of the proof (the bottom line).

Level 1 to level 2

Rounds and visits

Call everything from some cell to the right a stream. A visit: the head stands on a 0 (the lead cell) with a 0 on its left and a stream s on its right, in state C (form V) or in state F (form W); it comes back in state A two cells further left, both 0s restored and s rewritten. lean/Visit.lean proves, for every stream and every tape to the left, what a visit does to a stream according to how the stream begins. The theorems were produced by running the transition table symbolically on every short beginning of a stream (py/visit2.py), and each proof is an exact count of the machine's own steps between the nested visits:

v(s) is what one visit makes of s. The generator also proves V_1110 (1110 s becomes 1010 v(v(s))) and V_1111_halts; the law does not use them.

"Then V" means: the machine makes a visit of its own, in form V, to the stream behind the block, whose lead cell is the last cell of the block. In this machine the two forms are not the same. Entered in F, a visit obeys the rules for 0, 100, 1010 and 1011, but on 1100 s it writes 1010 and visits s once (W_1100), and on 1101 and 111 it has no rule of this shape at all. A visit in form W is only ever made as the first of the two nested visits behind a doubler 1010. If a nested visit halts, so does the visit around it (V_100_halts_1, V_1010_halts_1, V_1010_halts_2, ...).

Rounds. From blank tape the machine reaches 0∞ A> 1 0 0 1 0∞ after 6 steps (start). From a tape 0∞ A> 1 0 0 S it takes 8 steps to stand on the first of these cells in state C, with 1 0 S as its stream, and a visit then brings it to 0∞ A> 1 0 0 S', two cells further left, where S' is what the visit made of 1 0 S (round); if that visit halts, the machine halts (round_halts). So the whole run is a sequence of rounds, and each round is one visit with visits nested inside it.

Level 2 to level 3

The same rules without the machine

Leave out the states, the step counts and the two forms, and what remains is a rewriting system on words. In VisitLaw.lean it is two inductive relations on streams with no machine in them: AV s s', "one visit rewrites s to s'", and AH s, "a visit to s halts".

the stream beginsone visit makesrule
0 s1 sAV.z
100 r000, and one visit to rAV.h
110 r000, and one visit to rAV.g
1010 r1010, and two visits to r, if r does not start with 11AV.l
1011 rthe visit haltsAH.x

A visit also halts if one of its nested visits halts (AH.h, AH.g, AH.l1, AH.l2). So a block 000 or 100 is a binary digit: it flips on every visit and passes every second visit on. A block 1010 is a doubler: it passes every visit on twice. A block 1011 is the trap.

The guard. The doubler rule carries the side condition "the stream behind does not start with 11" (Guard), because the first of its two nested visits is made in form W. In this machine form W has no rule for 110, so the doubler rule can only be turned into visits of the machine when the stream behind the doubler does not start with 11. Lean proves that after two visits a stream never starts with 11 (av2_guard), and the checker below tests the condition every time it uses the rule.

From the rules to the machine. Law lists what a machine's two forms must satisfy, one field per rule, and Law.av, Law.ah turn every abstract visit into a visit of the machine in form V (and in form W when the guard holds). lean/Law.lean fills in the fields with the theorems of the table above (law); everything is checked by the kernel.

Level 3 to level 4

Counting visits instead of making them

After k rounds the word begins with about k/2 doublers, so about 2k/2 visits reach the blocks behind them, and round 161,211 could never be replayed visit by visit. But all the visits that reach one block are alike, so it is enough to know how many there are. VisitLaw.lean proves what n visits in a row do (VS n s s', for every n):

  • 000 r under n visits ends as the digit n mod 2, and ⌊n/2⌋ visits reach r (law_Z);
  • 100 r ends as the digit (n + 1) mod 2, and ⌈n/2⌉ visits reach r (law_H); the same for 110 r when n ≥ 1 (law_G);
  • 1010 r stays 1010, and 2n visits reach r (vs_L, with the guard on r);
  • a 0 where a block should start takes the first visit to become a 1 (law_0);
  • and the same laws for "one of the first n visits halts" (hvs_Z, hvs_H, hvs_G, hvs_L, hvs_X).

The pass. The checker (scan in VisitCheck.lean) carries the number of visits through the word block by block, in one pass, using these laws: through a doubler n visits become 2n; through a digit n becomes n/2, rounded down or up depending on the digit, and the digit is set to the parity of n; when the count reaches 0 the rest of the word is copied unchanged. The counts are huge but simple: each is a power of two minus a small number, so the checker keeps a count as a pair e, c standing for 2e − c, and doubling is e + 1, halving is e − 1. scan_sound proves that what the pass returns is what 2e − c visits of the rewriting system make; hscan_sound proves the same for its search for a halting visit (sound, not complete: when it is unsure it answers no).

Level 4 to level 5

161,210 rounds, then the trap

What is left is a program. round s is one pass of scan over the word 1 0 s with a single visit (e = c = 0); iter k s makes k rounds and fails if a round fails, as it does when a positive count meets 1011; and check k s makes k rounds and then asks hscan whether the next round has a halting visit. halts_of_check_blank (lean/Law.lean, kernel, with the three standard axioms) says: if check k [true] = true for some k, the machine halts from blank tape. The word [true] is the word 1 after the 6 steps of start.

So the proof needs the value of one expression, check 161210 [true]. That is about 1011 list operations, too many for the kernel, so Lean compiles the checker to native code and evaluates it (native_decide). The run is cut into ten segments, each "k rounds take the word after a rounds to the word after b rounds", with the words at the boundaries (up to a million cells) written into the proof as numbers (VisitW*.lean); a kernel-checked lemma, iter_add, chains the segments, and an eleventh evaluation, last, is the halting round:

How many steps Computed

Between 6.3 × 1024,266 and 1.08 × 1024,267 steps from blank tape to the halt, that is 190.3·280605 to 323.1·280605. A number with 24,267 digits (or 24,268): large, but an ordinary power, not a power tower.

Every visit costs a fixed number of the machine's own steps, apart from its nested visits: 4 or 8 steps for 0 → 1, 6 or 8 for 100, 10 for 110, 22 or 24 for 1010, entered in C or F (the step numbers in the proofs of lean/Visit.lean, the ones the panel on rounds and visits checks against the machine). So the step count is the number of visits of each kind times these costs. c/steps.c in holdouts/1RB0LB_0RC0LE_1RD0RF_1LB1RB_0LA1RB_1RE---/ repeats the checker's pass for all 161,210 rounds and adds up the counts per rule: in the completed rounds there are 5.9519·280605 visits each of the kinds 0 → 1, 100 and 1010 (the largest single count is 280605, in round 161,210: the count in round r peaks at 2r/2), and at most 2.1164·280605 more of each in the halting round, which stops part of the way through. The lower bound takes the completed rounds at the cheaper cost, the upper bound adds the whole halting round at the dearer cost.

The program's word after 161,210 rounds agrees cell for cell with the word in VisitW161210.lean, which the Lean segments check; the sums are kept as a mantissa and an exponent in floating point (the bounds are widened by 0.1% for that). Lean's theorems say "after some number of steps" and do not state the numbers. Nothing in these paragraphs is a Lean theorem.

Before the proof

Two counters and an engine

Before the visit law was found, the run was described as two counters ticking together. That description is kept here: it is how the run looks at the level of the machine, and parts of it are Lean theorems of their own (lean/Rules.lean, lean/Events.lean).

Two counters and an engine

Most of the time the tape is L · engine · R. Each visit of the head to the engine is an event, and every event adds 1 to both counters. In the reference machine of the family (1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_0RD1RF_0RC---), L is written in blocks of four cells, 0101 for a 1 and 0001 for a 0, lowest digit next to the engine, and R keeps one cell per digit, three cells apart (the cells 9, 12, 15, … right of the head), lowest digit nearest the engine. This machine is a relative in different clothes: its left counter has 4-cell digits x011, its left-end extensions come after exactly 3·2k turns of the busiest cell, each count twice, and at its left-end visits the tape is that of the reference family: at step 14,008,454 its 183-cell tape agrees with that of 1RB0LB_0RC0LF_1LD1RE_0LA0RB_0RD0RF_0LA--- at step 9,617,068 in all cells but the first two.

The left side is clockwork

When L overflows, the carry runs to the left end of the tape, the tape grows by two cells there, and L starts again. The lowest digit of L is the cell where the head turns most often, and the number of its turns between two consecutive left-end extensions follows a strict pattern, without a single exception in the first 1.5·109 steps (ymax.c):

48 96 96 192 192 384 384 … 3·221 3·221 3·222

So the periods of the left side are powers of two in events, each length twice (the counter gains one digit every two periods). The time per event is not constant, because a carry on the right costs more when it crosses junk, so the steps between left-end extensions grow by factors between about 1.35 and 2.05 instead of exactly 2.

A counter with junk between its digits

The cells that change between events are the digits of the counters: each digit changes about half as often as the one before (chg.c counts, over a whole period, how often each cell changes from one event to the next). On the right, between some digits of R, there are stretches of fixed cells (0101010101 00 1 00 1 00 1 and the like) that the carries cross without changing. They are written when a carry runs into the end of the tape, at irregular event counts, and their layout is what makes the run irregular. How they are produced is not decoded yet.

The dangerous moments

The machine halts only in state F reading a 1, and F is entered from C1 (0RF) only. After C1, used about 2.9·107 times in 109 steps, the head has read a 0 every time (F0 is used exactly as often). Unlike the other members this machine has no rare transition: every transition is used more than 107 times in 109 steps. Halting would need that ordinary step to find a 1. In every run short enough to simulate directly this has not happened. The proof on this page shows that it does happen, in round 161,211.

The increments, proved for every size

This machine runs the reference's two counters with other words: the left digits are 0110 (0) and 0111 (1), the engine blocks are 011001 and 001010000, and the right counter with its junk units is the reference's. lean/Events.lean proves each kind of visit to the engine for every size. In the first 108 steps 95.5% of the 879,588 visits are instances of these theorems.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.075). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.067).

After 109 steps the tape spans 248 cells (86 left of the start, 149 right), with a density of 1s of 0.395.

Kernel
V_0, V_100, V_110, V_1010, V_1011_halts, W_*, round, round_halts, start, lean/Visit.lean (generated by py/visit2.py)
The visit law, in two forms

A visit (the head on a 0 with a 0 on its left and a stream s on its right, in state C or F; it returns in state A two cells further left) rewrites the stream by 0 s → 1 s, 100 s → 000 v(s), 110 s → 000 v(s), 1010 s → 1010 v(v(s)), and halts the machine on 1011 s; for every stream and every left tape. Form W obeys the rules for 0, 100, 1010, 1011; it has no such rule for 110 (W_1100: 1100 s → 1010 v(s)). The run is a sequence of rounds 0^inf A> 1 0 0 S → 0^inf A> 1 0 0 v(1 0 S), starting after 6 steps with S = 1 0^inf.

Kernel
AV, AH, Law.av, Law.ah, av2_guard, law_Z, law_H, law_G, vs_L, hvs_*, VisitLaw.lean
The abstract visit law and its count laws

The four rules and the halting pattern as inductive relations on streams, with no machine in them; the 1010 rule is guarded (the stream behind must not start with 11), and after two visits a stream never starts with 11. Under n visits, 000 r and 100 r are binary digits that pass every second visit on, and 1010 r passes 2n visits on; likewise for "one of the first n visits halts".

Kernel
scan_sound, hscan_sound, round_sound, VisitCheck.lean
A checker for the visit law, proved correct

scan carries a count of visits, kept as the pair e, c for 2e − c, through a finite word in one pass; hscan looks for a halting visit. Both are proved against the abstract system.

Kernel
law, halts_of_check_blank, lean/Law.lean; halts_of_check, VisitCheck.lean
If the checker accepts, the machine halts

This machine satisfies the law, so for every k: if the checker runs k rounds from the word 1 and then finds a halting visit in the next round, the machine halts from blank tape. Axioms: propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

Computed
c/steps.c in holdouts/1RB0LB_0RC0LE_1RD0RF_1LB1RB_0LA1RB_1RE---/
Between 6.3 × 1024,266 and 1.08 × 1024,267 steps

Computed, not proved: the machine halts after between 6.3 × 1024,266 and 1.08 × 1024,267 steps (190.3·280605 to 323.1·280605). Every visit costs a fixed number of steps of its own: 4 or 8 steps for 0 → 1, 6 or 8 for 100, 10 for 110, 22 or 24 for 1010 (entered in C or F). A C program repeats the checker's pass for all 161,210 rounds and adds up the visits per rule (5.9519·280605 of each main kind in the completed rounds, at most 2.1164·280605 more in the halting round). Its final word agrees cell for cell with the word the Lean segments check; the sums are in floating point, and the bounds are widened by 0.1% for that.
