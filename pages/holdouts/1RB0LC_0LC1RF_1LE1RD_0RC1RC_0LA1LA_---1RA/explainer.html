A 6-state Busy Beaver machine · Status as of 9 November 2026.

Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LC_0LC1RF_1LE1RD_0RC1RC_0LA1LA_---1RA

Probably never. The tape holds two numbers side by side, a and b. Most passes multiply a by 3/2 and move b a little. Now and then the machine subtracts the smaller number from the larger one, as in Euclid's algorithm, and swaps them. It stops only if that subtraction leaves exactly b = 1 at the wrong moment, a coincidence that gets rarer as the numbers grow.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from B1 (writes 1, moves R): B>1 0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

What it does

Two numbers side by side

Every so often the head is in state A at the left end of the tape, and the tape is then a short word followed by two blocks of 1s, of lengths a and b, separated by one 0. The word is either 011011 (call this U(a, b)) or 1110 (V(a, b)).

One pass from such a moment to the next does one of two things. If b is odd, or a is odd and b is large, the machine multiplies a by about 3/2 (or 3) and moves b by a few units. If both are even, it subtracts the smaller number from the larger one and triples what is left on the left, swapping the two when b was the smaller: a step of Euclid's algorithm mixed with a Collatz step. The main cases, from U (V is the same with slightly different constants):

From blank tape: U(4, 3) at step 118, then V(4, 14), V(20, 7), V(40, 2), U(10, 37), …, U(100, 7), U(158, 2), V(10, 155), …, U(74,180, 24,362) at step 8.7 billion. Both numbers keep growing, and the visited tape grows like the square root of the step count.

Can it stop?

Only at b = 1

The machine stops exactly when it reaches U(a, 1) or V(a, 1) with a divisible by 4. The rule that lowers b by 5 makes it even, so b = 1 can only come out of a subtraction that leaves exactly 1. That needs b to land on one of a handful of values next to a: a coincidence with a chance of about 2/a.

Starting the map from random pairs of size S gives halting frequencies close to 2/S (2% at S = 100, 0.25% at 1,000, 0.021% at 10,000). Along the actual run a grows exponentially, so the total chance is dominated by the first terms. About 3·10−5 was left when direct simulation stopped, and about 10−96,600 is left after the 1,000,000 passes that were iterated.

What is checked

The map is proved; what is left is arithmetic

  • Every pass is proved for even a, the only kind the run meets: Lean's kernel checks that from every U(a, b) or V(a, b) with a even the machine reaches the event the map predicts, in exactly the predicted number of steps, unless b = 1 and 4 divides a. There are 32 cases, each a few sweeps. The repeated part of a sweep (four 1s of a turned into six on the left, or both blocks shortened by two) is proved once and iterated by induction.
  • Non-halting is reduced to the map. Lean proves that if the map's orbit from U(4, 3) never reaches b = 1 with 4 dividing a, the machine never halts. It then evaluates that orbit: 3,000 passes in the kernel (more than 10500 steps) and 105 compiled (1010000 steps), none of them halting.
  • What is missing is a reason why b = 1 never comes up. That looks as hard as any Collatz question.
First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.057). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.492).

After 109 steps the tape spans 31,855 cells (69 left of the start, 23,551 right), with a density of 1s of 1.0.

Kernel
next_sound, next_isSome, lean/Map.lean; lean/Cases.lean, generated by py/gen_map.py and py/gen_mapdef.py
The pass map for every even a

Events: Ev .U a b = A> 011011 1^a 0 1^b and Ev .V a b = A> 1110 1^a 0 1^b. next F a b gives the next event and the exact number of steps to it; next_sound proves that the machine goes there. next_isSome: it is defined at every event with a even, a ≥ 2 and b ≥ 1 except U/V(a, 1) with a = 0 mod 4. The cases: a = 2 mod 4 with b odd (to o((3a+10)/2, b)); a = 0 mod 4 with b = 3, 5 or b ≥ 7 odd; a, b even with b ≥ a+4, b ≤ a−2, b = a or b = a+2. Each is a family with a generic part and a few small values, 32 case theorems in all, built from symbolic-simulator segments and four loops proved by induction: a round of the b-odd phase (four 1s of a become six) for each residue of a mod 4, and a round of the Euclid phase (both blocks lose 11) for each of the two orders. Axioms: propext and Quot.sound only.

The pass map against direct simulation, including odd a

Sample the run when the head is in A at the left end. From step 118 the tape is U(a, b) = A> 011011 1^a 0 1^b or V(a, b) = A> 1110 1^a 0 1^b. The next such configuration is given by an explicit map with cases on a mod 4, the parity of b, and b compared with a (written out in py/emap.py): for example U(a, b) with a = 0 mod 4 and odd b ≥ 7 goes to U((3a+16)/2, b−5), and with a and b even and b ≤ a−2 to V(3b+4, a−b−1). The map was checked against direct simulation for every start with a < 60 and b < 70 (7,980 starts the map covers), 300 random starts with 15 ≤ a < 2,000, and 84 starts with small odd a and large b. The cases with odd a, which the run never reaches, are checked this way only; the even-a cases are now proved (above).

Heuristic
py/emap.py mc 1000 20000
Probviously non-halting: the chance of a halt is about 2/a

Reaching b = 1 needs a subtraction step that leaves exactly 1, so b has to land on one of a few values next to a (b = a−2, a+4, a−7, a−13), and then a has to be divisible by 4. Iterating the map from 20,000 random starts at each scale (100,000 at S = 10⁴) S (a in [S, 2S), b in [0, 2S)) gives halting frequencies 2.0·10−2, 2.5·10−3 and 2.1·10−4 for S = 102, 103 and 104, so the frequency is about 2/S. Since a grows exponentially, this sum is dominated by its first terms. From the last simulated event (a = 74,180) the estimate is about 3·10−5, and after the 106 passes iterated it is about 10−96,600.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known, but probably not. A proof would need to show that the subtraction steps never leave exactly b = 1 with a = 0 mod 4 along this orbit: a question about the long-run residues of a 3/2-Collatz-like orbit, which is out of reach.
