Does this machine ever halt?

1RB---_1LC0RF_1LD0LC_0RE0LF_0RD1LA_1RB0LE

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows. The head bounces between a block that grows at the left end and a wall of exactly 1,573 cells at the right end that it rewrites every sweep like a counter; the counter would need something like 348 sweeps to fill the wall, and what happens then is the question. It is proved to share that question with 1RB0RA_0LC0RF_0LB1RD_1LE---_1RA0LF_1LE0RC: one halts exactly when the other does.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in A reading 1. A is entered only from E1 (writes 1, moves L): 1 E>1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

Macro structure

A counter inside a fixed wall

The left part of the tape is the word 01101 repeated, about one more copy per sweep. The right part is a wall that has had exactly 1,573 cells since 2·107 steps: the right end of the tape never moves.

The wall is different after every sweep, but not at random. Comparing one wall with the next, the changes always start at the same cell, 44, and usually stay within a hundred cells; only now and then do they reach further, and each new depth (25 cells further each time) is reached about three times later than the previous one. That is how the carries of a counter behave: digits about 25 cells wide, in a base near 3.

About 48 such digits are left before the carries reach the end of the wall, which would take something like 348 sweeps. Whether the machine then halts or makes room is the open question; decoding the digits would make it exact.

One question, two machines

Two tables that are one machine

Two of the 608 holdouts are one halting question. Call 1RB---_1LC0RF_1LD0LC_0RE0LF_0RD1LA_1RB0LE (this page) the first machine and 1RB0RA_0LC0RF_0LB1RD_1LE---_1RA0LF_1LE0RC (its page) the second machine. It is proved, in Lean and again in Coq, that the first halts from blank tape if and only if the second does. That does not say whether either one halts: it says that the list of open machines is one shorter than it looks. The next five sections show why, and let you check each piece in the browser.

It starts with the tables. Point at (or tap) any cell of one table and the matching cell of the other lights up: the same symbol is written, the direction is reversed, and the states are renamed A→D, B→E, C→A, D→B, E→C, F→F. So the second machine is the first one watched in a mirror, with different names on the states. The one real difference is where it starts: the second machine's A is the first machine's C.

Renaming the second table's states back and exchanging left and right gives the first table exactly, the halting cell included (the green table). The machines differ only in the state they start in: C instead of A.

A different start state usually means a different run, and the related-machines screen found no common configuration in the first 107 steps. But it compared the runs step for step. The next section compares them event for event.

The two runs

One run, watched in a mirror

Run both machines from blank tape. After 2 steps of the first and 49 of the second, the two tapes are the same when the second is read in a mirror, except that the second has one more word, 10001101, at the far right end. From then on each step of one is a step of the other, with one exception. When the head moves right from q, the rightmost cell visited so far, it crosses the end of the tape. The first machine does that in 5 steps; the second has to cross the extra word too and takes 49. Call one step of both, or one crossing, a model step. Where they part

Crossing the far end: 5 steps against 49

Here is the one place where the two runs come apart for a moment, drawn in the first machine's frame (the second machine's run is this, mirrored). Both lanes start from the same configuration of the real run, just before its third crossing: state B on the 1 at q (green outline). The lower lane has k extra words after q (blue). The slider moves both lanes together; the upper one takes 5 steps, the lower one 44k + 5.

Whatever k is, the head walks right through the words, turns at the blank end and walks back, and both lanes end in state D one cell left of q with 1 0 1 written from q on: q has moved two cells right and the extra words moved with it. Lean proves this for every k (far_B, 44k + 5 steps, in lean/FarEnd.lean); Coq needs only k = 0 and k = 1 (far0, far1, each checked by running the machine).

The invariant

Four cells that settle it

Could the head ever leave q in some other state, or on a 0? Then the first machine would step onto a blank cell while the second steps onto the extra word, and the runs would part. The proof rules this out by looking only at the last four cells of the tape, q−3 to q, the window, and forgetting everything to their left. A card below is one abstract configuration: the four cells, and the state and head position when the head is in the window. When the head is further left (card out) even the state is forgotten, and the head may come back in any state that a move to the right can produce: B, D, E or F.

From the start there are exactly 28 of them, and none moves right from q except in state B on a 1 (green cards: the crossing). Click a card to see where one step can lead (blue): always to another card. That closure is the whole invariant; Coq checks it with one vm_compute (check_S0).

The window works because the left end of the tape cannot reach it unseen. When the head leaves the window to the left, the window's last three cells always read 1 0 1 (the two out cards), and whatever the counter does far to the left, the head can only come back through the window's first cell. The window is a deliberately coarse picture, an over-approximation: it allows more than the machine does, so if even the coarse picture never leaves q the wrong way, the real run cannot. The Lean proof uses a slightly finer window with 32 configurations (lean/RightEnd.lean, closed by decide); the Coq proof was written on its own and lands on 28.

The proof

Checked in Coq, step by step

The Coq proof is one file of 371 lines, coq/Eqv_TailWord.v, built on the definitions of busycoq, the Coq library in which the bbchallenge results are checked. It is meant for busycoq itself, in the style of its Eqv_WordPairs.v. Its steps, from the machines to the theorem:

  1. The mirrorEvery transition of the second table is the first's with the direction reversed and the states renamed by g. tm'_some, tm'_none; 12 cases.
  2. One model for both runsA configuration Cfg keeps the window apart from the rest of the tape. to_config false reads it as a configuration of the first machine; to_config true as the mirrored configuration of the second, with W8 = 10001101 after q.
  3. The model stepf is one step of both machines, or the crossing at the far end. far0 (5 steps) and far1 (49 steps) are run by busycoq's execute. f_spec: each model step is at least one step of whichever machine you read it as, and a model halt is a halt of both.
  4. The invariantP c: the window of c is one of the 28 cards of S0. check_S0 (one vm_compute) shows the list is closed under the abstract step and that no card leaves q except B on a 1; anext_spec shows the abstract step covers the real one.
  5. The startinit0, init1: from blank tape the first machine reaches the model's start in 2 steps, the second in 49.
  6. The theorembusycoq's halts_iff turns the model into halts (machine b) c0 <-> iter_halts f cfg0 for both b, so TailWord.eqv : halts tm c0 <-> halts tm' c0.

Family screen, 13 November 2026

Which family it belongs to

The 22 machines of family F09 (“bouncers over lists of counters, not yet classified”) were screened in one way. The machine runs from blank tape for 2·1010 steps; each time the tape reaches a new cell after a threshold, the tape is compressed twice: first repeats of one word, then repeats of groups of words. If the tape is a few growing blocks and a bounded rest, the number of level-2 tokens stays bounded while the tape grows. Everything in this section is observed in one run. None of it is proved.

Proposed family: F06, block-list counters (a counter in a wall that is rebuilt longer at each overflow, with a growing block as its clock).

During the present epoch the description of the tape does not grow: 146 to 176 level-2 tokens at the four left-end records from 2.6·108 to 1.6·1010 steps, and in the macro simulator (blocks of 5 cells) exactly 314 runs at each of 80 sampled left-end records from 3·107 to 1016 steps. Only the block (11010)^n grows.

The wall is a counter with digits of 25 cells. After its first 44 cells, each of the first 40 digits has the form x110 x110 x011 0011 0x11 0x11 0 at every one of 2,819 sweeps (steps 2·107 to 3·108): five free cells per digit. The first digit returns to the same contents every 8 sweeps, the first two digits together every 64 sweeps and the first three every 512 sweeps, taking 8, 64 and 512 different values (py/digits.py). The deepest digit changed by a sweep is digit 2 on 1,336 sweeps, digit 3 on 782, digit 4 on 326, digit 5 on 124, and so on down to digit 11 once.

The counter has overflowed before. At the left-end record at step 1,428,412 the tape was (11010)^244 followed by about 240 cells that end in 16 eight-cell words. No new cell was added at the left end for the next three million steps, and at the next left-end record (step 4,487,735) the block was gone, a 25-cell word 0101100110111001100110111 stood 45 times in a row (new digits, all equal) and the tape ended in 30 eight-cell words. So the machine is a counter that is rebuilt longer when it overflows, with the block as its clock, and the next overflow is far beyond simulation. That is the shape of the counter families (F06), not of a bounded engine (F01): the wall is bounded only within an epoch.

Next step. Decode the five cells of a digit into a carry rule (a transducer on digits), prove it with symtm, and work out the rebuild; then the question is whether the rebuild can go wrong for some length of the block.

One halting question for two machines (the shifted runs)

1RB0RA_0LC0RF_0LB1RD_1LE---_1RA0LF_1LE0RC is the table of 1RB---_1LC0RF_1LD0LC_0RE0LF_0RD1LA_1RB0LE mirrored, with the states renamed, started in state C. After 2 and 49 steps the two runs are at the same configuration, except that the second has the word 10001101 once more at the right end. Two tapes that differ only by k such words just right of a cell q, with blank tape beyond q in the first, make the same moves until the head moves right from q. In state B on a 1 the head crosses the k words and comes back with the same cells, for every k (far_B); q then moves two cells to the right. With the renaming and the mirror proved as well: if in the run of 1RB---_1LC0RF_1LD0LC_0RE0LF_0RD1LA_1RB0LE the head moves right from the rightmost cell visited so far only in state B on a 1 (from step 2 on), then it halts from blank tape if and only if 1RB0RA_0LC0RF_0LB1RD_1LE---_1RA0LF_1LE0RC does (two_machines). The condition is proved (next claim).

The condition is proved: the right end grows only through far_B

Good30773 is a fact about the last four cells of the tape, q−3 to q, and not about the rebuilds of the counter. Keep only those four cells and the state, and forget everything left of them: with the head inside the window, the state, the cells left of the head in the window and the cells from the head to q; with the head further left, the four cells only, and let the head come back in any state that a move right can produce (B, D, E or F). Starting from step 2 this over-approximation has exactly 32 abstract configurations (30 with the head in the window, 2 outside it; whenever the head is left of the window the last three cells read 101). None of them moves right from q except in state B on a 1, and the set is closed under a step (closed, by decide). So the condition holds for every number of steps (good30773), and 1RB---_1LC0RF_1LD0LC_0RE0LF_0RD1LA_1RB0LE halts from blank tape if and only if 1RB0RA_0LC0RF_0LB1RD_1LE---_1RA0LF_1LE0RC does (two_machines'), with no condition.
