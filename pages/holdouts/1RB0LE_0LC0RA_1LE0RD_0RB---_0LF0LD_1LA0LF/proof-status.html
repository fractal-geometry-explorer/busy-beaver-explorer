What is known about 1RB0LE_0LC0RA_1LE0RD_0RB---_0LF0LD_1LA0LF

1RB0LE_0LC0RA_1LE0RD_0RB---_0LF0LD_1LA0LF

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state machine halts from blank tape. Its tape is single 1s separated by gaps of one, two or three 0s, and it grows to the left. At its visits to the left end a prefix made of the word 0 0 1 grows by one word each time while the time between visits roughly doubles, then shrinks back: the behavior of a counter. This page lists what has been established, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 22 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Its events are decoded: a binary counter at the left end (3-cell digits) gains 1 per event and a 2-bit counter M right of a fixed 46-cell word gains 1; this is proved in Lean for every carry length. When M wraps, a slower counter further right changes (not decoded), and now and then the fixed words themselves are rebuilt.

New, 14 November 2026. The whole run is now one rule: the tape is a binary counter with doublers (words 1010, 000, 100), and each round sends one defect through it. A tape with one defect cannot halt; a halt needs two defects to meet. In 100,000 rounds there was no halt, and in the 4,500 rounds followed word by word every defect disappeared long before the next one started. Whether that is always so is the open question.

New, 22 November 2026. Lean now proves that a tape with at most one defect cannot halt, so a halt needs a defect that survives its round. A new engine followed the defect word by word through 500,000 rounds (more than 2944,371 steps): every defect died about halfway into the tape. The top of the counter is now understood exactly: its layers are the binary expansions predicted by the conserved value, 1/15 − c·2−k/2/127. The defect only ever eats the irregular bottom layer; whether it can cross all of it in one round is the open question.

Later on 22 November 2026. Lean now also proves that the four visit functions are runs of the real transition table, and the round law. So the machine never halts from blank tape if every round's defect dies in its round; that one statement is all that is left.

Evening, 22 November 2026. A correction and a sharper statement. The hypothesis of the Lean theorem of the afternoon is false as stated: counted from blank tape, the defect of round 2 survives, so that theorem was vacuous. Lean now proves the corrected reduction: the machine reaches round 9 at step 5,848, and from there it never halts if each round's defect needs at most one visit of A, a number computed by a short recursion over the round's starting tape. In the run the defect needs 3 or 4 visits and gets 2k/2. Whether that margin holds in every round is the open question.

The event rule (non-wrapping events)

From L 0 0 0 (1 0 0)^k ENGINE A> 0 PREFIX [M] R (ENGINE = 00101001010100, PREFIX the 46-cell word 1001010100100101010010001001000100100010101010, M two 4-cell digits 1000 = 0 / 1010 = 1, low first) the machine reaches the same shape with the left counter + 1 (1 0 0 (0 0 0)^k) and M + 1, for M = 0, 1, 2, every k and every tape L, R (8k + 608 or 8k + 616 steps); also when the k ones reach the left end of the tape (the counter gains a digit). Nothing right of M is read.

Token rules and the conserved value

Taking the four visit functions A, E, F, P as the definition, every token rule (the counter rules for L, O, I, the rules of the travelling defect, and the re-readings at the blank end) is a computation of them, and none uses the halting case E(1 1 …). Every visit of A derived by these rules adds exactly 1 to the value v of the tape. That the machine's visits are these four functions is lean/Link.lean. That the rules apply to every tape with at most one defect is lean/Total.lean.

sound, conserve, lean/Tokens.lean

A tape with at most one defect cannot halt

The token rules are deterministic, and on every tape of words with at most one defect any number of visits of A in a row are derived by them, every tape in between again having at most one defect. At the bit level each of these visits is a computation of the four functions, none of which is the halting case E(1 1 …). So within a round the machine can halt only on a tape with two defects, which needs the defect of an earlier round to survive its round. This replaces the hand argument of the 14 November notes.

det, total, total_bits, lean/Total.lean

The four visit functions are the machine

Every computation of the visit functions A, E, F, P is a run of the transition table: a visit of X on the half tape from the head leftwards ends with the head just right of it in state B, for any tape to the right, which it never reads. One round takes 0 S (blank to the right, state A) to 0 1 0 A(S) the same way round, starting from blank tape with S = []. This replaces the check by c/zvis.cpp.

link, round, lean/Link.lean

If every defect dies, it never halts

If in every round from blank tape the visit of A leaves a tape without defect, the machine never halts from blank tape. Correction (evening): that hypothesis is false, because the defect of round 2 survives (old_hyp_false, lean/Fuel.lean), so this theorem says nothing. It is replaced by nonhalt_of_need, which starts at round 9.

nonhalt_of_clean, lean/Link.lean

The fuel a defect needs

nx W, ne W, n0 W: a recursion over the words of a clean tape W (nx (O W) = 2 nx W + 1, nx (L W) = ⌈nx W / 2⌉, nx (I W) = ne W + 1, …). That many visits of A always leave x W without a defect, and the same for 1 W and the tapes that Eh and E0 leave. So whether a round's defect dies is decided by the round's starting tape alone.

fuel, dies, lean/Fuel.lean

The old reduction was vacuous

From blank tape, S2 = x L O I and A(x L O I) = L I x I: the defect of round 2 survives, so the hypothesis of nonhalt_of_clean is false.

old_hyp_false, lean/Fuel.lean

The corrected reduction

The machine reaches the start of round 9, 0 x a9 with a9 = L L L L O L L L I L I L I L O O I O I I, at step 5,848 (list simulator evaluated by the kernel). If for the round tape x W of every round from 9 on nx W ≤ 1, the machine never halts from blank tape. Round 9 itself satisfies it.

The run as rounds of one function

On the mirrored tape, a visit of A (the head at a cell in state A until it first steps left of it) is a function on the half tape, and four such functions (A, E, F, P) close up. From [A] 0 0 S the machine returns to [A] 0 0 S' one cell further out with S' = 1 0 A(S). Derived by local simulation and checked against direct simulation of the machine.

No halt in 100,000 rounds

The visit engine, with visit counts kept as sums of powers of two, ran 100,000 rounds without a halt; the counts reach 187,512 bits. Only 7 window rules of A and E are ever used.

c/zvis.cpp

Every defect followed for 500,000 rounds

A new engine runs the token rules with each carry kept as a run-length coded bit string, the digit end of the tape as one big number, and the carries left after the defect has died walked together; it gives the same tape as py/tokz.py after every round (compared token for token at rounds 3,000, 8,000 and 30,000) about a thousand times faster. In each of the 500,000 rounds the defect died at 1 I O → O L inside the tape, long before the round ended (from round 13 on), and no two defects ever met. The largest carry of the last round has 944,371 bits, so the run covers more than 2944,371 (about 10284,283) steps of the machine. The visit engine's earlier record was 100,000 rounds.

The defect over the long run

From round 100 on the defect gets into the digit end Z in 30.8% of the rounds, a little less often right after rounds in which it did (27% after three in a row). There it eats 4.85 words on average, at most 60 (round 475,184), with a tail falling by about 0.81 per word, as random digits would give. Z loses 2.11 words per round and gains 4; the largest average loss over 100 rounds in a row is 2.88, over 1,000 rounds 2.33, over 10,000 rounds 2.19. From round 1,000 on |Z|/k stays between 1.73 and 1.89, and the margin of the lemma, |Z| − (k + 2 log2 k + 20), is at least 699.

Four layers of the digit end

Read lowest digit first, the digit end Z of the tape is four layers (round 50,000; round 8,000 is alike). From the top: 24,996 digits of period 4 (the expansion of 1/15), 24,997 of period 28 (1/15 minus a multiple of 1/127), 29,782 digits that are the same in round 50,056 (frozen: once laid down they no longer change), and 13,402 irregular digits at the bottom. The regular layers are what the first two terms of the value predict; the defect only ever eats the irregular one, about 0.27 k digits.

The margin

A round tape is Lm x U, so the defect gets 2m visits (m = k/2) and dies if nx U ≤ 2m. In every round from 13 to 3,000 the defect needs 3 or 4 visits (26 in round 12, with m = 6, the smallest margin), and 3 at rounds 20,000, 50,000 and 300,000.

python3 py/fuel.py 3000; py/fuel.py --dump on dumps of c/toksim.cpp

Fuel against height

The defect's path is fixed by the words ahead of it; its fuel only decides when it stops. At every one of the 329,061 defect steps of rounds 6 to 1,500, fuel·2−b (b the doubler height of the defect's word) is at least 1 minus twice the sum of 2−b over the steps before, with exact fractions. So a defect can only run out after crossing words of small height, which before the death point never happens (all heights there are at least m).

python3 py/potential.py 1500

Heights never drop over two rounds

With hk(p) the doubler height at token index p at the start of round k: hk+2(p) ≥ hk(p) for every p up to where round k's defect died, in every round from 40 to 3,000, and the same for 19,991 random tapes Lm x W. One pass moves every height it crosses by exactly 1. Not proved; if it is a theorem, what is left is only how deep into Z the defect dies, and a rough count asks just that Z keeps a fixed fraction of the tape.

heuristic Evidence only

A counter

A word per visit and twice the time per word is the signature of a binary counter whose digits are the gaps; the drops of k are its carries reaching further into the tape.

A counter with doublers and one travelling defect

In the words L = 1010, O = 000, I = 100 the function A is a binary counter (O, I the digits) in which every carry crossing an L becomes two. Each round adds one defect (a 10, 1 or 0 that does not fit the words), which travels through the counter, rewrites it, and disappears where it turns 1 I O into O L. A tape with at most one defect cannot halt (now proved in Lean, lean/Total.lean); a halt needs two defects side by side. The token rules reproduce the exact tape cell for cell (rounds 5 to 24), and in every round from 13 to 4,500 the defect disappeared about 40% of the way into the tape, long before the round ended.

py/tokz.py; README

A conserved value

a value of the tape (each digit 1 counts 2-b, b = doublers minus digits before it, with corrections for the defect) grows by exactly 1 with every visit of A, with or without a defect: proved in Lean for the token rules (lean/Tokens.lean), and checked with exact fractions on every round. At the start of each round it is 1/15 up to about 2-k/2: the far end of the tape is the binary expansion of 1/15, I O O O repeated k/8 times.

py/tokv.py; README

What the missing lemma really asks

Followed over rounds 100 to 7,500, the defect gets into the digit-only end Z of the tape in only 32% of the rounds, and whether it does looks like an independent coin each round. When it gets in, it eats 4.8 words on average, about what random digits give (it crosses O and I I and stops at I O). Z gains 4 words per round and loses 2.15, so it stays near 1.85 k words, well above the k + 2 log2 k + 20 the proof needs. So the lemma is not about a bias in the digits; it is about how rarely the defect gets past the part rich in doublers. One bad round can cost any amount, so this is a statement about pseudo-random digits, like Antihydra's. Under the random-digit model a defect survives a round with chance about 2-0.4k, below 2-40,000 after round 100,000.

open Open

The rule

How the gaps encode a number, and what a visit does to it, are not known.

The wrap of M and beyond

When M wraps, a slower counter right of M (4-cell words 0101/0100 and others) changes; how, and how the PREFIX is rebuilt, is not decoded. A halt (D reading 1) can only come in those steps, never inside a non-wrapping event.

Does every defect disappear in time?

If the defect of every round disappears before the round ends, the machine never halts. Where it disappears depends on the digits of the counter, which look irregular; no proof.

The one missing lemma

With the conserved value, the defect of a round must disappear if the tape ends in a few periods I O O O and every digit before them is reached by many visits (b larger than a few times log2 k). Measured, b is at least 2m - 5 in the part rich in doublers; a proof of a lower bound is missing. It would show that the machine never halts. It follows if the digit-only end Z keeps at least k + 2 log2 k + 20 words, which needs the defect to reach Z in fewer than about half of the rounds; measured, a third. 22 November 2026: the digit end has four layers (see "Four layers of the digit end"), and only the bottom one, about 0.27 k irregular digits, is ever eaten. The three regular layers above it are about 1.6 k digits, so the lemma would follow from two statements: that the layers are always as observed (they come from the first two terms of the value), and that no defect crosses the whole irregular layer in one round. The second is still a statement about pseudo-random digits. Neither is proved. Evening: crossing the irregular layer would not save a defect: it would still carry about 21.5 k visits and die in the layers above. What a proof needs is that the doubler heights in front of the death point never collapse (see "Heights never drop over two rounds").

Every round's defect needs at most one visit

nx W ≤ 1 for the round tape x W of every round from 9 on. By nonhalt_of_need this is all that is left; it is equivalent to every defect dying, now as a property of each round's starting tape.
