This six-state, two-symbol Turing machine was one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. An outside Rocq proof (busycoq pull request 86, compiled here) now shows that it never halts when started on a blank tape. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

At the end of each round the tape is a leader 11100, a list of digits of six cells each, a fixed stretch, and a tail of single 1s that grows by one per round. Within an epoch each round changes one or two digits next to a walker that moves left, and the round takes exactly twice as long as the one before plus a constant that depends only on the change. When the walker reaches the front the list is rebuilt longer; the rebuilds are not understood.

It is their proof, on busycoq's definitions of a machine and of halting, and busycoq's maintainer has not yet reviewed it. Everything else on this page is what this repository found on its own, which by itself leaves the question open.

Before the outside proof, this repository's own work left it open. No halt in direct simulation to 3.54·1011 steps. The rounds inside an epoch follow an exact law (checked on every round of two epochs, and two rounds predicted before the simulator got there); a halt could only come from a rebuild, whose rule is not known.

The walker law: every round of epochs 2 and 3

Write the six-cell units 1 x y 1 0 0 as digits by xy: 00 = 1, 01 = 0, 10 = 2, 11 = 3. In an epoch a walker moves left through the digits. Phase 1 (building 2s): a 0 becomes 1 (c = +16), then that 1 becomes 2 and the walker moves left (c = +4); if the walker finds a 1 that was already there, it becomes 3 (c = +18) and phase 2 starts. Phase 2 (a carry): the digit left of the 3 goes 0 → 1 and the 3 becomes 0 (c = +14), then that 1 becomes 3 and the carry moves left (c = −10); at the front the leader takes the place of the digit (11100 → 10100, c = +14) and the next round is a rebuild. When the phase-1 walker reaches the front (no digit left), the leader also becomes 10100 with the digits unchanged (c = +16, seen once, in epoch 3), and the next round is a rebuild. Each round lasts T′ = 2T + c. From the start of epoch 2 (step 40,509,722, T = 2,926) the law gives all 20 rounds of the epoch to the step (the last ends at 6,201,936,302); from step 12,398,695,221 it gives every round of epoch 3 seen, including 96,327,513,809 and 180,338,374,341, which were predicted before the simulator reached them.

The rebuilds, and the question

Decided outside: busycoq pull request 86 proves in Rocq that it never halts (compiled here; see the verdict). From this repository's own work alone: rebuilds at about 2.3·104, 4.05·107 1.2·1010 and 3.48·1011 steps turn the tail of single 1s and the fixed stretch into a new, longer list of digits (12, 13, then 14 digits) and a new fixed stretch. Their rule, which decides the next epoch, is not known; nor whether the machine halts.
