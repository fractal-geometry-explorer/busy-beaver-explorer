1RB1RF_1LC1LB_1RD1LD_1RE0RA_---0LB_0LF0RC

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 14 November 2026.

In short

The question is open, and probably out of reach. The tape is two growing blocks followed by a short list of counters. The first counter loses 1 every few epochs; when it runs out, the machine either halts (the halting configurations are proved in Lean) or turns one of its blocks into a new, much larger counter. Which of the two happens is decided by remainders of numbers produced by a Collatz-like map. The first run-out, at step 2.995·1023, did not halt; the second is due after about 10^(1011) steps, far beyond exact simulation.

Other proofs and outside reports

Community label

In the community's BB(6) tracker the machine's class is Chaotic Bell Class 2. These are labels read off the run by others, not results.

A marker in front of the last counter halts

For all n, p, k the configurations with run lists 3 2^n 3^p 5 3^k 1 and 3 2^n 3^(p+1) 1 3^k 1 halt: the marker walks through the last counter, one run per two visits, and the machine stops at the right end of the tape (E reads a 0 past the last run).

The first counter ran out at step 2.995·1023, without a halt

The counter that starts at 22 (step 6,183) reaches 0 at step 2.995·1023. No marker is in front at that moment: the block of 103,352,228,706 runs of 3 becomes a new counter, the old last counter (4) is absorbed, and the tail becomes 3^131245843096 s 3^103352228710 1. The new first counter has lost 143 by 10159 steps and 199 by 10210 (about one per factor of ten in steps), so it runs out after about 10^(1011) steps.

The run-list rules

At the left-end visits in state D the tape is 3 2^n 3^p FRONT 3^X s 3^k1 s 3^k2 1 (run lengths; separators s = 0, 2, 4). Three local rules (proved in Lean) and a table of eleven events at the separators (learned from the plain machine by affine fits on small instances and checked on far ones) give a simulator that agrees with the plain machine at every visit to 3·109 steps and with the accelerated macro run, tape and step count, at all 3,172 visits up to 3.4·10158 steps. To leading order the number of pairs K at the start of an epoch follows K' = 3K/4 + X or 3K/4.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: fixed/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.462). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

A run-out can halt, and does in a minority of cases

Started from the configuration of step 1.1·1027 with the first counter replaced by J, the run-list simulator halts for J = 0, 3, 6 and goes on (with two or three new counters of 15 to 28 digits) for J = 1, 2, 4, 5 and 7 to 29. With the number of pairs varied as well (J = 2, 5, 9, twenty values each) 7 of 60 runs halt. So the structure does not exclude a halt: it is one outcome of a run-out, chosen by remainders. If each run-out halted independently with a probability bounded away from 0, the machine would halt eventually, but the next run-out is about 10^(1011) steps away.

py/runlist.py exhaust 0 30; README

No halt in the first 6.44×102890 steps of an accelerated run

Observed, not proved: the accelerated simulator rsim.py ran 6.44×102890 steps from blank tape without reaching a halting transition. Its tape, state and head position were equal to those of a plain C simulator at 6 checkpoints, the last at 2.56×1010 steps. Of these steps, the part after the last checkpoint rests on the simulator alone, including 10,834 trip-level jumps: a jump applies many identical round trips at once after seeing the same tape shape recur with constant count changes, which is validated against direct simulation but not proved.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. A proof of non-halting would have to show that no run-out ever lets a marker through; a proof of halting needs the remainders of 1011-digit numbers produced by a Collatz-like map with no known shortcut.
