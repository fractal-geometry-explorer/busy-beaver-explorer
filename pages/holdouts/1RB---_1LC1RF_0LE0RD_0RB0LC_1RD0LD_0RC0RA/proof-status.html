What is known about 1RB---_1LC1RF_0LE0RD_0RB0LC_1RD0LD_0RC0RA

1RB---_1LC1RF_0LE0RD_0RB0LC_1RD0LD_0RC0RA

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

The head works at one place, cell 110, in state D (an event). Each event adds 1 to a count that the tape holds twice: in binary on the left, where it is split into a low, a middle and a high counter written with different digit words, and in binary on the right, in 3-cell digits. The event rules are proved in Lean for every size. The layout changes at each visit of the head to the left end (the boundary between the low and middle counters moves by one digit), and the right counter collects junk words between its segments.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The events at the engine follow seven rules proved in Lean for all sizes (and 121 rarer ones, also proved), which cover 99.4% of the 45 million events of the first 3·109 steps; none of them halts (each ends at the next event). Halting needs B to meet 1 1 1 (from the table), which only the sweeps after a left-end visit and the overflows of the right counter's low segment come near (25 times in 3·109 steps). After step 3·109 the engine moves to cell 111 and a new layout begins, not decoded.

The halt from the halting context

From B> 1 1 1 the machine reaches A on a 1 in two steps, and halts on the next (step_none_iff). B followed by 1 enters F, F on a 1 enters A, and A is entered no other way after the first step, so every halt passes through this context. Lean (halt_ctx) proves only that from this context the machine halts; that every halt passes through it is read off the table above, not proved in Lean.

halt_ctx, lean/Rules.lean

Shift rules

Eleven shift rules over the words of the tape (for example B> (100)^n becomes (110)^n B> in 5n steps, (10)^n E> 1 becomes E> 1 (01)^n in 4n steps), proved for every n.

The counter rules, for every size

Seven event rules (t_inc_low, t_inc_R, t_inc_both, t_carry_alt, t_carry_both, t_carryH_alt, t_carryH_both): from an event, the next event, with the exact step count, for any number of carried digits in each counter and any number of spacers (up to five size variables per rule).

Near misses are rare and come at the left end

In the first 1010 steps B has 1 1 ahead of it 239 times and 0 1 0/1 1 0-type contexts otherwise; it never has 1 1 1. The near misses come right after the head turns at the left end, when B, sweeping right, meets the first word of the middle of the tape.

c/ctxcount.c, c/forbid.c

The left end: a run of 10 lengthened by two

At every new cell on the left, the tape starts D>0 0 0 (10)^n or E>0 0 (10)^n; after an early phase where n grew by one per visit while a block of 0s was filled in, n has grown by exactly 2 per visit, from 31 (step 90,908,236) to 49 (step 32,039,589,166).

Coverage: 99.4% of the events

In the first 3·109 steps there are 45,121,817 events (D on cell 110); the seven counter rules match 44,107,018 of them (97.8%), all 128 rules all but 256,981. 256,975 of those are one shape: the low segment of the right counter (6 digits) wrapping into the junk word that separates it from the next segment, once every 64 right increments in the epoch from step 1.83·109.

Growth shape: exp/exp

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.049). Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. Halting needs B to meet 1 1 1 (from the table), which could only happen at a visit to the left end; whether the irregular middle of the tape ever puts a third 1 there is the open question. A proof would need the exact rule of the middle counter, which is not yet found.
