What is known about 1RB1LE_0LC0LB_1RD1LC_1RD1RA_1RF0LA_---1RE

1RB1LE_0LC0LB_1RD1LC_1RD1RA_1RF0LA_---1RE

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape. Its run is a sequence of epochs; each epoch is a Collatz-like countdown, and the way it ends decides whether the machine halts, runs away forever, or starts a bigger epoch.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open, and is now a question about a 3/2 map. In configurations X(k, m) = A>0 0 (10)^k 1^m 0 (blank on both sides) the machine sends k to about 3k/2 and m down by 2 or 3 according to the parity of k. When m runs out the machine halts, runs right forever on blank tape, or starts a new epoch with m ≈ 3k.

Where the run is. Exactly computed: epochs start with m = 6, 41, 16,169 and then a 1,139-digit number. The fourth epoch would take about 101138 macro steps, far out of reach, so the outcome cannot be computed. With fair-coin parities each epoch ends in a halt with probability 1/5, in a run to infinity with probability 3/10 and in a new epoch with probability 1/2: overall the machine halts with probability 2/5 (Racheline's estimate on the bbchallenge wiki, recovered here exactly).

The macro rules

For X(k, m) = 0^∞ A>0 0 (10)^k 1^m 0 0^∞ and k ≥ 1: for m ≥ 4, or m = 3 and k even, k = 2j goes to X(3j + 3, m − 2) and k = 2j + 1 to X(3j + 5, m − 3); m = 3 and k = 2j + 1 goes to X(3j + 4, 1); m = 2: k even gives X(2, 3k + 5), k odd halts; m = 1: k odd gives X(2, 3k + 3), k even leaves D running right on blank tape forever (D reads 0, writes 1, moves right, stays in D); m = 0: k even gives X(2, 3k), k odd runs right forever. Checked against direct simulation for every 1 ≤ k < 40 and m < 14. Inside a step the head makes k/2 passes, each eating two 10 pairs at the right and pushing a run of 0s at the left out by 6; the 0s are rewritten as the new (10)^k' on the way back.

The run, epoch by epoch

From blank tape the run reaches X(1, 1) after 24 steps. Iterating the macro rules exactly (Python big integers): epochs X(2, m) start with m = 6 (step 68), 41 (step 698), 16,169 (step 78,482,217), and the third epoch ends after 6,456 macro steps at m = 2 with k even (1,138 digits), so a fourth epoch starts with m = 3k + 5, a 1,139-digit number. The first epochs agree with the C simulator's records (k = 2, 6, 12, 21, 35, …; m = 16,169 at step 78,482,217).

Halts with probability 2/5

With y = k + 6 the rule is y ↦ ⌈3y/2⌉, m −2 for y even and −3 for y odd. If parities are fair coins, the countdown lands on m = 3, 2, 1 with probabilities 2/5, 2/5, 1/5, and the end rules give: new epoch 1/2, halt 1/5, run away 3/10. So the run halts with probability 2/5 and becomes a translated cycler (D running right) with probability 3/5, after astronomically many steps either way.
