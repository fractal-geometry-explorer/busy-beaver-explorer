Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RF0LE_1RA---

Nobody knows. Started on a blank tape, this Turing machine counts one binary number down and another one up, again and again, in phases that get longer and longer. This page shows the real machine in your browser, explains the two counters, and says exactly where a halt could come from.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from E0 (writes 1, moves R): E>0 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The family

Four machines, one engine

Four holdouts share the first four states of this machine, 1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB, and differ only in the last two: 1a 1RB0RF_0RB---, 1b 1RB0RF_1RC---, 1c 1RF0LE_1RA---, 1d 1RF1RC_1RA---. States A to D do almost all of the work; E and F only decide what happens when the head runs into the right part of the tape. This page is about machine 1c; the other three work the same way with different constants.

In 1a and 1b the gaps on the right have lengths 2, 5, 8, 11, ... (3d + 2); in 1c and 1d they have lengths 4, 9, 14, ... (5d + 4). 1a and 1b even agree step for step as long as F only ever reads a 0 followed by another 0, which is what happens in every run we made.

The tape

Blocks on the left, gaps on the right

Stop the simulator at a moment when the head is in state A on a 0 with a block of 1s to its right. The tape then looks like

0^∞ 1^(2s) 0 1^(2v1+1) 0 1^(2v2+1) ... A>0 1^m 0^(5q1+4) 1 0^(5q2+4) 1 ... 0^∞

Left of the head: blocks of an odd number of 1s separated by single 0s (and one even block at the far left). Right of the head: single 1s separated by gaps whose lengths are 4, 9, 14, .... The odd blocks and the gaps are the digits of two numbers.

One pass

How a block of 1s is used up

From such a moment to the next (a pass) the head eats the block 1^m two cells at a time: states A, B and C carry one 1 at a time to the left part, then D runs right and writes into the first gap. What happens depends on m:

  • m even: two 1s move to the left, the first gap gets one cell shorter;
  • m odd: D runs right over all the gaps of the smallest size, (0^4 1); those gaps are filled with 1s, and the first larger gap is cut;
  • m = 2 or 3: the block merges with the blocks on its left and the head walks back to the 0 left of them.

These pass rules are proved in Lean for every value of the block lengths and every surrounding tape (lean/Rules.lean).

Two counters

One number counts down, another counts up

Read the tape at the start of a pass as two binary numbers. Left of the head, going outward, every pair of cells is one bit of E, lowest bit first: 1 1 is a 0 and 1 0 is a 1, down to an even block, the floor. Right of the head, after the top block, every group of 5 cells is one bit of N: 0^5 is a 0 and 0^4 1 is a 1. (So an odd block 1^(2v+1) is v zero bits and a one bit, and a gap is a run of zero bits closed by a one bit.)

With top block 1^3 (mode 3) one pair of passes subtracts 1 from E and adds 1 to N; with 1^5 (mode 5) two pairs subtract 1 from E and add 2 to N. A pair costs a constant plus 4 steps per trailing zero of E plus 10 per trailing one of N, exactly the work of borrowing and carrying.

This is now proved in Lean for every E and N (lean/Counter.lean: run3, run5), and Lean also proves that the run from blank tape reaches this counter after 256 steps (lean/Init.lean).

Phases

When the countdown ends

When E reaches 0 the floor 1^(2s) is popped into the top block; the next pass pushes s single 1s, which is E ≈ 2s − 1, all ones, and the mode switches between 3 and 5. N is left on the right, but the pass that cut it leaves a gap of the wrong size, a defect, in front of it.

Defects are worked off in a cascade of short sub-phases: a gap one cell too long collapses into a block 1^2; a block 1^2 followed by a gap makes the next gap one too short; a gap one too short followed by a single 1 is cut and hands its defect to the next gap. Sometimes N is swallowed into the left part entirely and restarts at 0. Then a clean countdown runs again. Since the floor gains 2 cells each time E passes a power of two, the floor is about twice the bit length of the largest E so far, and phases grow fast.

The accelerator py/jump2.py simulates the cascades directly and jumps over every clean countdown with the Lean-proved rule. It was not run on this machine in round 2; the first-round accelerator py/accel.py reached 9.70·10171 steps (see the heuristic claim).

Halting

How it could stop

The machine halts only when F reads a 1. From a pass start that happens exactly when an odd pass, after the gaps of the smallest size, meets a gap of length one followed by a block of two or more 1s (pass_odd_halt, proved). In the language above that needs two defects at once: a gap cut down to length one, directly in front of the 1^2 left by another defect. In every transition seen so far a single defect was worked off without harm.

During a countdown there are no defects at all, so a halt can only come inside a transition cascade. The defect monitor of c/ps.c was not run on this machine in round 2 (it was run on 1a and 1b, where there was never more than one defect at a time).

Why it is open

What is missing

The counter is proved; the transitions are not. A transition is a cascade whose course depends on the low bits of E and N and on where earlier defects sit, and no closed formula for the configuration after it is known, so neither an invariant (“there is never more than one defect”) nor a reduction to a number-theoretic map is available yet. The runs make the one-defect invariant plausible but do not prove it.

Because the phases grow so fast, simulation can only ever check a few dozen transitions; whatever decides this machine has to be an argument about all transitions at once.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.047).

After 109 steps the tape spans 181 cells (9 left of the start, 166 right), with a density of 1s of 0.354.

The pass rules

Six rules on symbolic tapes, for all values of their parameters and any surrounding tape: a pass on an even top block (L 1 A>0 1^(2k+4) 0^(g+4) 1 R), on a block of 2, on an odd block crossing j small gaps, on a block of 3, a gap of length one closing, and pass_odd_halt: an odd pass whose D-run meets a gap one cell too short reaches F reading a 1, the only halting transition. Proofs by rfl steps and crossing lemmas, written by tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py.

lean/Counter.lean, generated by holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/py/gen_counter.py
The counter, proved for every size

Write E in p bits on the left (from the head outward, a 0 bit is 1 1 and a 1 bit is 1 0, above a floor that starts with a 1) and N in n bits on the right (a 0 bit is 0^5, a 1 bit is 0^4 1, followed by any tape X). In mode 3 (top block 1^3) the machine takes (E, N) to (E − k, N + k) for every k ≤ E with N + k < 2n, in at least 26·k steps (run3); in mode 5 (top block 1^5) it takes (E, N) to (E − k, N + 2k) in at least 56·k steps (run5). Proofs: three pass-pair rules written by tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py, the binary decrement and increment on bit lists, and induction on k.

The family's accelerated simulator py/accel.py (with c/acc.c, in the folder of 1a) runs the machine directly until a shape repeats 12 times with E → E − 1 (or E − 2k) and F → F + 1, fits the step formula a + b·ν(E) + g·ν(F+1) exactly, checks the first min(N, 215) cycles of the jump against direct simulation token for token, and then jumps N cycles. It made 73 jumps (E up to 571 bits) with no failed check and no halt. That the formula holds for the whole jump is a pattern, not a proof.

Heuristic
holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/py/jump2.py (log of the tapes at each counter start)
The phase transition

When E reaches 0 the floor block 1^(2s) under it is popped into the top block, the next pass pushes s single 1s, and a new countdown starts with E = 2s − 1 (s one bits; floor 1^2) in the other mode (3 and 5 alternate); N stays on the right, but the cut leaves a gap of irregular size in front of it. Irregular gaps then act in a cascade of short sub-phases: a gap one too long (for 1a/1b; the analogous residue mod 5 for 1c/1d) collapses into a block 1^2, a gap one too short followed by a single 1 is cut down and passes its defect on to the next gap; sometimes the whole of N is pulled to the left and N restarts at 0. The floor grows by 2 whenever E passes a power of two, so it is about twice the bit length of the largest E so far. Seen at every transition of the runs above; not proved, and no closed formula for the new (E, N) as a function of the old one is known.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. By pass_odd_halt the machine halts exactly when an odd pass, after the gaps of the smallest size, meets a gap of length one followed by a block of two or more 1s. In the counter language this needs two defects to meet: an irregular gap cut down to length one directly in front of a 1^2 block left by another defect. During a countdown there are no defects (proved: the countdown is run3/run5), so it can only happen inside a transition cascade. In the runs above there was never more than one defect at a time; no invariant proving that is known, and the transitions have not been reduced to a number-theoretic map.
