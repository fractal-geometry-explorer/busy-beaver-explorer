Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1LD_1LC0RC_0RF1RA_0LE1LA_1RC0LA_---0RB

Nobody knows. But what it does is understood exactly: it keeps a few huge blocks of 1s and cuts them into quarters, glues them back and grows them by half, over and over. This page lets you watch each of its rules on the real machine and follow its run for tens of thousands of rounds; Lean proves it does not halt in its first 10130 steps.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from C0 (writes 0, moves R): C>0 0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The idea

A few blocks of 1s, and five things that can happen to them

After its first few steps this machine keeps its tape very tidy: a handful of solid blocks of 1s, with one to four 0s between them. Some blocks are enormous, and the machine spends almost all of its time sweeping across them. But at certain moments the head comes to rest in the same position: in state D, on a single 0, with a block of 1s on each side. Call such a moment an anchor.

Write m for the number of 1s just left of the head (blue) and c for the number just right of it (green). What the machine does between one anchor and the next depends only on m, on how many 0s lie to the left of that block, and on whether anything lies further left. There are five cases, and each one is simple arithmetic on the block lengths:

eventwhenwhat happens
splitm is even, at least 4, and has two or more 0s on its leftA quarter of the block, ⌊m/4⌋, goes to the far side of the gap. Three quarters go to the right block. The remainder, 0 or 2, stays.
oddm is odd, at least 3, and there is more tape further leftThe block loses 2, the 0 on its left turns into a 1 (so the gap shrinks by one, and the block joins its neighbour when the gap closes), and the right block gains 3.
foldm is odd, at least 3, and nothing is further leftThe odd round repeats by itself until one 1 is left: every 2 ones become 3 on the right. The right block gains 3(m−1)/2. This is the only place where the tape grows by a fixed fraction.
shiftm is even and has a single 0 on its leftThe block is too close to its neighbour to be split. The head steps over it, and the block (minus one) is now on the right.
zero, one, twom is 0, 1 or 2: what a split leaves behindThe few remaining 1s are handed across the gap. In most of these cases the head then runs to the right behind a marker 0 1, the carrier, which passes whole blocks and turns into three 1s at the first pair of 0s or at the end of the tape. A new block of 3 starts there.

None of this is a guess. Each rule is a theorem checked by Lean for every block length and every surrounding tape, and together they give an exact, fast model of the machine: instead of simulating billions of single steps, apply one rule per anchor. The next two sections let you try that.

Rules used so far

Things to notice. Splits and the small cases only move 1s around; an odd round adds 2 and a fold adds half of a block. After +30,000 the tape holds more than 10130 ones. Reaching that tape takes the real machine more steps than that, which is how Lean proves that the machine does not halt in its first 10130 steps.

Emergent behaviour

Cut into quarters, glue back together, grow by half

Seen from far away, the run repeats one pattern at ever larger sizes.

  1. One big block. After a fold the tape is a single 1, the head, and one block: 1 D> 0 1N.
  2. Cutting. The block moves to the left of the head and is split: a quarter stays on the left, three quarters go right. The three quarters come back to the head and are split again, and again, leaving a row of pieces, each about three quarters of the one before. The cutting stops when a remainder or a parity does not fit.
  3. Gluing. Odd rounds close the gaps between the pieces, and shifts carry the head back to the left.
  4. Growing. When an odd block reaches the left end of the tape it is folded: every 2 ones become 3.

At event 29 the tape is 1 D> 0 133. Two events later it is 132 D> 0 16, and the split cuts 32 into 8 (left) and 24 (right, making 30). The 30 then moves back to the left of the head piece by piece, the 8 is split into 2 and 6, the pieces are glued together by odd rounds, and two folds later (event 61) the tape is 1 D> 0 181.

The sizes N of these single blocks are 33, 81, 150, 366, 639, 963, 2229, 3357, 5697, 22527, … Each is larger than the last by a factor that is often close to 3/2, but not always, because how many folds and odd rounds happen in between depends on the remainders of the block lengths modulo 4 and on their parities. That is what makes the machine Collatz-like: its future is decided by the low digits of numbers that keep being multiplied by 3/4 and 3/2, and nobody knows how to predict those.

The sister machine 1RB1LC_1LC0RD_0LE1LA_0RF1RA_1LA0LA_---0RB does the same thing with a gap of three 0s for a split instead of two.

Halting

What a halt would need

The machine stops only if it reads a 0 in state F. The rules above cover every situation the run has met so far, and none of them halts. But they do not cover every tape one could write down. No rule is given when

  • the right block is too short for a small case (fewer than 3 ones for zero, fewer than 2 for one and two),
  • the carrier meets a single 1 standing alone after a single 0, or lands directly on a 1.

Small tapes of these kinds often do halt. For example 15 0 D> 0 (nothing on the right at all) halts after 3 steps, and 15 0 D> 0 13 0 1 (the carrier meets a lone 1) halts after 9 steps. So the machine is not a provable non-halter by its rules alone: it halts if and only if the run ever reaches one of these uncovered tapes and the plain machine then stops.

In the run from blank tape that has not happened in the first 2,999,957 events. Whether it ever does is the open question.

Lean

What is proved, and what is not

  • Every rule, for every block length and every surrounding tape (Lean kernel): c_split, c_odd, c_fold, c_shift, c_zero, c_one, c_two, c_oneE, c_oneO, c_pass and the carrier’s arrival arr_sem, in lean/Events.lean, from single rounds in lean/EventRules.lean.
  • The rules are the machine (kernel): F_sem says that the map F (a rule at an anchor it covers, one plain machine step anywhere else) follows the real machine exactly, and halts_iff says the machine halts from blank tape if and only if repeating F from the blank tape reaches a halting configuration.
  • If the rules never run out, the machine never halts (kernel): events_forever.
  • No halt in the first 10130 steps (kernel, 30,000 events evaluated inside Lean): no_halt_kernel in lean/Far.lean.
  • No halt in the first 1013179 steps (compiled evaluation, 2,999,957 events): no_halt_native.
  • This page’s JavaScript is a transcription of the Lean definitions. explainer/test.js checks it against the plain machine: every example of the rule viewer, and the first 457 events of the run (4·108 machine steps).

Not proved: that the rules never run out. Nothing here decides whether the machine halts.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.398). Right of the start it grows like a power of the step count other than 1/2 (fitted exponent 0.121).

After 109 steps the tape spans 12,322 cells (8,350 left of the start, 169 right), with a density of 1s of 1.0.

Kernel
halts_iff, events_forever, lean/Events.lean
Halting is a question about the event map

The machine halts from blank tape if and only if the orbit of the blank tape under the map F (a rule at every anchor that has one, one machine step otherwise) reaches a halting configuration. If the rules alone can be applied for ever from the first anchor, the machine never halts.

Kernel
fold, fold_full, lean/Fold.lean; rules in lean/FoldRules.lean (py/fold_rules.py)
The fold, for every size

For every n, every M ≥ 1 and every tape R to the right: 0^inf A> 0 1^(2n) 0 0 1^M R reaches 0^inf A> 0 0 0 1^(M+3n) R, and then 0^inf A> 0 1 1 0 1^(M+3n) R: a left-end block of 2n ones is merged into its neighbour as 3n. Three symbolic rules (fold_step, 12n + 23 steps per zig-zag; fold_last; fold_open) and an induction over n.

Where a halt can happen (closed tape language, 5-cell windows)

The closure search of bbtools.ctl with windows of 5 cells is closed but allows halts; the halting abstract configurations constrain the neighbourhood of a halt: a halt (F reading 0) needs 00000 or 00110 to the left and 01001, 0111x, 01110 or 01111 on the right (8 abstract configurations). A Python check, not a Lean certificate (the Lean library only has the right-edge and no-halt corollaries, which do not apply).

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. A halt needs the event orbit to reach an anchor that no rule covers (a right block that is too short, or the carrier meeting a lone 1) and the machine to stop from there. That has not happened in the first 2,999,957 events.
