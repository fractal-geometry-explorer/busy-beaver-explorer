Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0RA_1LC0RE_0LD0LB_1LA1LF_1RA0LF_1LC---

Nobody knows yet. This machine counts in base 3, least significant digit at the left end. Its tape is a row of long runs of alternating cells; each pair of neighbouring runs is a digit, valued by the run lengths mod 4, and every trip of the head from the left end and back adds one. The increment is proved; what happens when a shrinking run runs out is not.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from D1 (writes 1, moves L): 1 D>1; E1 (writes 0, moves L): 1 E>1. So a halt is the moment one of these patterns appears, with the head on the cell after >.

statereads 0reads 1
A1 R B0 R A
B1 L C0 R E
C0 L D0 L B
D1 L A1 L F
E1 R A0 L F
F1 L Chalt

Tape as runs

Runs of alternating cells

Cut the tape into maximal runs of alternating cells, 0101… or 1010…. Two neighbouring runs meet at a doubled cell, 00 or 11, so the list of run lengths says everything (the first run starts with 0). At the start of a trip (A at the left end) the runs are, at step 10,000,025,550:

82476 16507 | 82426 3897 | 36473 2653 | 33442 423 | 75 75 | 18 9 | 13 184

Name them r0 r1 | x1 y1 | x2 y2 | …: digit 0 is (r0, r1), digit k is (xk, yk).

Macro behaviour

A counter in base 3

A digit's value is read from its two run lengths mod 4:

valuedigit 0 (r0, r1) mod 4digit k (x, y) mod 4
0(0, 3)(2, 3)
1(2, 2)(0, 2)
2(3, 1)(1, 1)

(After digit k changes, xk can be one cell longer than this reading, until digit k−1 next goes from 0 to 1 and takes that cell.) Each trip adds one, and the runs change by a few cells:

digitchangeruns
00 → 1r0 + 2, r1 + 3, x1 − 1
01 → 2r0 + 5, r1 − 1
02 → 0, carryr0 + 1, r1 + 2
k2 → 0, carryx − 2, y + 2
k1 → 2x + 2, y − 1
k0 → 1x − 1, y + 3, next x − 1

Going right, A flips every cell it passes. A carry is a bounce: B meets the 00 at the start of xk, runs back over yk−1, turns, and goes on through digit k. Where the carry stops the head turns and goes home, B and C carrying the pattern one cell along; the left end grows by 4 cells per trip.

All of this is proved in Lean, for all run lengths and for every carry depth at once (an induction over the digits the carry passes): trip_stop1, trip_stop0, inc0, inc1 in lean/Counter.lean.

Why it is not a proof yet

Runs that run out

Over one cycle of digit k (3k+1 trips) xk loses 4 cells and yk gains 4, so every xk eventually runs out. Then the next carry does not bounce through: the head shuttles between the end of yk−1 and the right end of the tape, moving yk−1 two cells at a time to the far right (a transfer; these are the long pauses of the run and the reason the right side grows). Afterwards the digits past it are paired anew and counting goes on. For example at step 39,282 the runs 187 97 2 31 25 7 7 become 186 43 27 5 13 177. New digits are made at the right end from short runs.

In the first 3·1012 steps 99.86% of the 726,270 trips are the proved kinds; the rest come with transfers or the right end, which are only observed.

Halting

What a halt would need

The only halting transition is F on a 1. F comes from E (on a cell B has just made 0, so F reads that 0) or from D, and D only from C on a 0. So the machine halts exactly when D reads a 1 whose left neighbour is also a 1. In the proved trips that never happens. In the first 3·1012 steps D read a 1 about six million times; the alternating stretch ending at D's cell was 2 cells long 158 times and 3 cells long 17 times (a halt needs 1), in the cases we looked at near the right end or in a transfer.

So a halt, if any, would come from a transfer or from the right end. The exact simulator finds none in 1014 steps.

The pieces of a trip

Eight rules for all run lengths and every surrounding tape: digit 0 going from 0 to 1 and from 1 to 2 (whole trips), the carry leaving digit 0, the carry passing a digit at 2 (a bounce), the carry stopping at a digit at 1 or at 0, and going home over a passed digit and over digit 0, with exact step counts.

Kernel
carry_chain, home_chain, trip_stop1, trip_stop0, lean/Counter.lean
The increment, for every carry depth

By induction over the list of digits a carry passes: from the start of a trip with digit 0 at 2, any number of digits at 2, then a digit at 1 (or at 0), the machine reaches the start of the next trip with the exact tape and step count stated. With inc0 and inc1 this is every kind of trip while the runs involved are long enough (r0 ≥ 7, r1 ≥ 5, a passed digit x ≥ 9, y ≥ 5, the stopping digit x ≥ 4, y ≥ 6 or x ≥ 6, y ≥ 7).

99.86% of trips are the proved kinds

Of the 726,270 trips in the first 3·1012 steps, 725,254 have one of the four proved shapes with the runs long enough. Of the other 1,016, the carry reaches the right end in 98, a run is too short in 269, and a digit has residues outside the table in 648 (and one is the first trip). They come, as far as we have looked, with transfers (a run xk has run out and the run yk−1 is shuttled, two cells at a time, to the right end of the tape, after which the digits past it are paired anew) or with the right end, where new digits are made from short runs.
