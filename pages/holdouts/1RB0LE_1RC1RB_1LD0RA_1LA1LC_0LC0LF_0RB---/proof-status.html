This six-state, two-symbol Turing machine, one of the holdouts of the BB(6) search, halts when started on a blank tape. The halt is a Coq theorem on busycoq's definitions. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 19 November 2026.

In short

It halts, proved in Coq (theorem halt : halts tm c0 on busycoq's definitions, no axioms). With its states renamed, its table is 1RB1RA_1LC0RD_1LD1LB_1RA0LE_0LB0LF_0RA--- started in state D, one transition away from the table DeepMind proved halting in busycoq PR 93. DeepMind's fast engine, changed for that transition as for 1RB0LC_1RC1RA_1LD0RE_1LA1LD_0RA0RF_0LD--- (the same table started in B), reaches a halting configuration from state D after 688,228 engine steps, on a tape of about 26326 cells.

What it does. Before the halt the run is a cascade of halving runs: each round turns a run of n equal blocks into about n/2 blocks one level up. In the first 109 steps D read a 0 about 21,000 times, always one cell short of a halt. That description comes from simulation; the halt does not depend on it.

The sweeps' shift rules

Moving right, A (with 1 0 behind it) and C (with 1 behind it) turn each pair 11 into 01, 6 steps a pair: a run of 2n ones becomes (01)n. Moving left, C (facing a 0) and D (facing a 1) turn each 01 back into 11, 2 steps a pair. B runs over 1s. Each holds for every n and every surrounding tape.

b_run, a_halve, c_halve, c_double, d_double, lean/Rules.lean

The shape of a halt

From 1 1 1 D> 0 the machine reaches F on a 1 in 3 steps and halts on the next. Conversely (by reading the table, not formalized) F is entered only from E on a 1, E only from A on a 1, and A has a 1 to its left only when it came from D on a 0, so every halt comes this way.

halt_shape, halt_after_shape, lean/Rules.lean

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.069). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.504).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

Rounds and the halving law

Sample the run when the head is in A at the left end of the tape (rounds; consecutive samples about 1.25 times further apart). At every sample the tape is a list of blocks 1k 00, almost all with k = 6j+1 and grouped in long runs of equal blocks, lowest level at the right. Between consecutive rounds a run of n blocks of level j becomes a run of ⌊n/2⌋−1 to ⌈n/2⌉+1 blocks of level j+1 (checked for every run of at least 10 blocks after the first 3·106 steps, to 1.7·109), and a new run of level-0 blocks 100 appears, alternately about 0.65 and 1.9 times the previous one. A few odd blocks (k = 5, 11, 17, ...) sit between the runs.

c/samples.c, py/rounds.py

Within a round: an expanding bouncer

After the left-end sample the head runs right to a point well inside the tape and then zig-zags in a window whose left edge creeps left and right edge creeps right (by a few cells per bounce, using the halving and doubling rules above), until the window reaches the left end: the next sample. This is where the square-root growth on the right comes from.

No halt in the first 1.90×1014 steps of an accelerated run

Observed, not proved: the accelerated simulator rsim.py ran 1.90×1014 steps from blank tape without reaching a halting transition. Its tape, state and head position were equal to those of a plain C simulator at 6 checkpoints, the last at 1.00×1010 steps. Of these steps, the part after the last checkpoint rests on the simulator alone, including 6,642 trip-level jumps: a jump applies many identical round trips at once after seeing the same tape shape recur with constant count changes, which is validated against direct simulation but not proved.
