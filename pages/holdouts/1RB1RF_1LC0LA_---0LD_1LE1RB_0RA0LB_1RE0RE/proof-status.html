What is known about 1RB1RF_1LC0LA_---0LD_1LE1RB_0RA0LB_1RE0RE

1RB1RF_1LC0LA_---0LD_1LE1RB_0RA0LB_1RE0RE

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Read in blocks of 6 cells, the tape is a periodic word with a few phase defects; the head moves them, and the largest block grows by a factor near 4/3 per phase, which suggests a Collatz-like map on the block lengths. The 50 sweep rules used in the first 109 steps are proved in Lean; the accelerated run reaches 9·1011 steps without a halt. The large-scale map has not been found.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.024). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.455).

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The map from one phase to the next (the block lengths between the defects) has not been written down; if it is Collatz-like, as the growth factor near 4/3 suggests, halting would depend on residues of an exponentially growing orbit.
