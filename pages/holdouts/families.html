Families of the undecided holdouts

When the families were drawn up (21 November 2026), 522 of the 608 holdouts were undecided here. They are grouped so that one proof technique could settle a whole family at once: machines whose tables differ in at most one transition (up to renaming the states and mirroring the tape) share most of their macro rules, and table sets with the same mechanism were joined. The families are ranked by how many machines one argument would settle, weighed by how likely it is to work. The ranking is a judgement, not a result.

Each machine below shows its standing today, so a family shrinks on this page as its machines are decided. The same families filter the table of the holdout map; see also the latest results and the machines closest to being solved.

Recommended next attacks

  1. Finish the two PR 60 halts (2 machines). All other proofs in busycoq PRs 43 to 68 were compiled on 21 November and their 32 machines are decided; PR 60's two proofs need about 555 core-hours of native_compute jobs, which needs a large machine (sample jobs pass here).
  2. R01, the F05 core (45 machines). The largest family by far, with its whole macro structure already proved in Lean on all 40 core tables. One invariant on the drift settles all of them. The equivalence step is done (the 40 are 23 questions); the invariant itself looks like a Collatz-type statement, so the odds are low.
  3. R02, clock and word (15 machines on six tables). The clock lemma is in Lean for six; what is left is a finite-state question about one word map per machine, which an automaton search on the word alone can answer far more cheaply than the whole-tape search that failed.
  4. R04 and R11, base-4 and ternary countdown counters (22 machines). Halt hunting with exact odometer jumps; this kind of family (F06) produced half of the decisions of the week of 13 November, all halting, and short-timer tapes of the same shape are proved to halt.
  5. R05, cellular-automaton sweeps (10 machines). New idea: for the two running-parity machines, a Lean proof that each sweep is one row of Pascal's triangle mod 2 turns halting into a statement about binary digits (Lucas' theorem).

Next in line: R03 (one table core, a shared library as for F05) and R06 (the PR 93 engine retargeted).

How the families were made

  • Table distance. For every pair of the 608 machines, regroup.py counts the transitions that differ between the two tables, minimised over the 720 renamings of the states and the mirror image. Distance 0 is the same table started elsewhere (298 pairs), distance 1 one changed transition (582 pairs). Joining machines at distance 1 or less gives the table sets. Machines in one table set share most of their macro rules, so a Lean library written for one usually proves on the others (as Family.lean did for all 40 of F05).
  • Mechanism. Table sets with the same mechanism, read from each machine's own description and the community tracker's class, were joined into one family, and machines with the same mechanism on unrelated tables were added by a pattern on their descriptions (the rules are in regroup.py).
  • Ranking. By the number of machines one argument would settle, weighed by how likely the argument is to work, judged from what is already proved. These are judgements, not results.
  • Machines that fit none of the families keep their 13 November family (listed at the end), and Collatz-like machines and cryptids form one parked family.
