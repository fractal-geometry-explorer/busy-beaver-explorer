What is known about 1RB0RD_0LC0LF_1RD1LB_0RE---_1RA0LC_0LA1LF

1RB0RD_0LC0LF_1RD1LB_0RE---_1RA0LC_0LA1LF

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The head sweeps between the ends over a background of 100 blocks, and the tape grows by 3 cells every few sweeps. A handful of short defects ride on the background, change phase each sweep and drift left at different speeds. Lean proves the shift rules over the background and the two halting patterns; the run has not halted in 1010 steps, but the defects' interactions are not decoded.

Shift rules, for every length

Going right: A turns (100)^n into (001)^n and (1010)^n into (0101)^n; D turns (001)^n into (010)^n and (0101)^n into (1010)^n; E turns (0101)^n into (1010)^n; each for every n and every tape around.

The two ways to halt

The only halt is D reading a 1, and D is entered only from A1 = 0RD and C0 = 1RD. So A reading 1 1, or C reading 0 1, halts at once, whatever the tape around.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.054). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.483).

Does it halt?

Not known. What is left is how the defects behave when they meet each other or the left end, and whether any meeting puts A on 11 or C on 01.
