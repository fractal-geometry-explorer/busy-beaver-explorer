What is known about 1RB0LA_1LC1RF_---1LD_1RE0LA_1RA0RB_1RE0RF

1RB0LA_1LC1RF_---1LD_1RE0LA_1RA0RB_1RE0RF

Nobody knows whether this six-state machine halts from blank tape. It behaves like a slow unary counter: the tape starts with a run of copies of 110, and on every return to the left end that run is exactly one copy longer. Behind the run sits a second run of 110 and a short, disordered stretch at the right end, and that stretch is where the open question lives.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Lean proves no halt in the first 107 steps. The C simulator shows, up to 109 steps, 671 returns to the left end, at each of which the counter p has grown by exactly 1 (from 2 at step 52 to 672 at step 997,776,132). What the right end does is not understood.

The counter

Every time up to 109 steps that the head is at the left end in A on A>0 0, the tape is 0 0 (110)^p followed by the rest. There are 671 such times; p is 2 at step 52 and 672 at step 997,776,132, and it goes up by exactly 1 each time.

py/counter.py on a tmrun … at A L log

Cost of a round

The number of steps between returns, divided by p², is 10.06–10.1 for the last five rounds. Summing 10·p² over rounds gives t ≈ 3.4·p³, i.e. p ≈ 670 at 109 steps, as seen.

py/counter.py

What follows the counter

Right after (110)^p comes 0 0 0 when p ≡ 0 or 2 (mod 3) and 0 1 1 when p ≡ 1 (mod 3), at all 601 returns after step 106. Then comes a second run (110)^q (q = 629 for the last returns, about 0.9·p), then 20–30 disordered cells, e.g. 0^4 (110)^3 0^8 110 0^2 10 0 110 0^2 1 at step 997,776,132.

Width

From one return to the next the width grows by 6 cells in 556 of 670 rounds (other values 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 10, 14); it is 3,943 at step 997,776,132.

same log

Not every run of 1s is even

Odd runs of 1s (lengths 1, 3, 5) occur far from the head, in every state and on both sides (a scan of every 13th step up to step 400,000), and the disordered end at the 667 returns to the left end takes 658 different shapes of 3 to 50 cells. So the even-run property holds only for the runs E reaches; an invariant has to say where the odd runs can be.

plain simulation

How near it comes to halting

In the first 109 steps E reads a 1 123,762,963 times with exactly two 1s from the head (E>1 1 0) and 429,985 times with four (E>1 1 1 1 0); never with one, which is what a halt needs. So the disordered right end never puts E on a lone 1 either: E's runs of 1s are always of even length.

No closed tape language found

Closed-language searches all fail: windows of up to 8 cells grown as needed (also with flags for run parity, odd runs seen, position mod 3) leave thousands of halting abstract configurations (9,071 at 8 cells with run parity). The shortest abstract path to a halt (45 steps, 8 cells) goes wrong when the tape first outgrows the window: from C>1 1 1 1 0 0 1 1, 9 cells, the window forgets where blank tape starts. DFAs learned from the run (k-tails with folding) with the least NFA on the other side fail too.

How it could halt

The halting transition is C reading 0. C is entered only from B reading 0 (which writes 1 and moves left), and B is entered from A on 0 (which leaves a 1 to B's left) or from E on 1 (which leaves a 0). So a halt is exactly E>1 0: E on a 1 with a 0 to its right. It has not happened in 109 steps.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The counter steps by 1 at every one of the 670 rounds seen, and E never meets a lone 1 in 109 steps (its runs of 1s are always even); why, and why the disordered right end keeps it so, are open.
