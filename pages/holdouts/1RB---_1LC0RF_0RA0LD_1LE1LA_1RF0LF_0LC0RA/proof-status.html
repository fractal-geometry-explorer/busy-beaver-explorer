1RB---_1LC0RF_0RA0LD_1LE1LA_1RF0LF_0LC0RA

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state machine halts from blank tape. It belongs to a family of six holdouts with almost the same table (a relabelled relative (its F plays E's part)). Their tapes are made of the words 10 and 110, grouped in sections split by 000, and they grow slowly to the left. This page lists what has been established, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The engine is decoded and proved in Lean for every size: from one event to the next, a base-3 counter N gets 1, a binary counter M gets 1, and when M wraps a binary counter R gets 3. No event can halt; a halt can only come from what was called an overflow of R. That is now located exactly: everything from R on is one endless stream of two tokens with a recursive visit law (Lean, every size), and the event rule stops when a visit of it comes out in state C instead of E. What the machine does after such a visit is proved row by row but not yet composed into a law.

The event rule, for every size

From an event 0^∞ 1 0 [N] A> 0 [M] 1 [R] (N base 3 in 3-cell digits 100/110/010, M and R binary in 4-cell digits 0010/1010 and 1000/1010, low digit first) the machine reaches the next event with N + 1, M + 1 and, when M wraps, R + 3; 54 theorems, one per case (54 cases, exhaustive by construction of events.py, not by a Lean theorem; carries over k twos in N, i ones in M, j ones in R; the wrap of N at the left end), each with its exact step count and any tape beyond.

Token rows of the visit law

Vis q x r x': the head enters the stream x from the left in state q and first leaves it to the left in state r, leaving x'. With tokens of two cells right of the event cell, 30 rows occur in 3·107 steps; each is a Lean theorem, conditional on the nested visits of the rest of the stream and valid for every stream.

The a/z stream and M as a recursive visit law

Everything from R on, blank tape included, is one stream of a = 10 and z = 00. A visit in state B turns the first z into a and every second a before it into z; it comes out in E when the first z is at an odd place (the binary increment of R, for every size: vb_odd, ve_odd) and in C when it is at an even place (vb_even). M + 1 without a wrap (m_inc) and with a wrap, where the stream gets three visits (m_wrap), for every size.

Where it can halt

A closed tape language with windows of 6 cells (closed, found and checked by bbtools.ctl, not yet in Lean) allows a halt only with A reading a 1 and 110001 from the head on: the first 1 of a word 110 followed by a section break 000 1.

The run follows the rule

The run reaches the clean shape (R followed by 1 0 1 and blank tape) at step 118,003 (N = 35 (5 digits), M = 2 (2 digits), R = 0 (12 digits)); 5,556 (to step 3·106) consecutive events then follow the rule, until R = 4,095 = 212 − 1 at step 3·106, about to overflow. Counting all events of the form N, 0 [M] 1, anything: 47,902 of the first 3·106 steps follow from the previous one by the rule, 3,503 do not (wraps of M that reach the undecoded part).

The overflow of R is the even case of the stream law

R has no end: when all its digits up to the blank tape are 1, the first z is at an even place and the visit comes out in C, leaving the digits shifted by one token. In the run of 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB--- this first happens at step 20,645 (stream a18 then blank, left as (z a)9 a). That the event rule fails only there is read off the rules, not a Lean theorem.

Growth

Each new 110 at the front costs about 2 to 3 times the steps of the previous one, the signature of a counter; the rest of the tape changes everywhere between visits, so the counter's digits are not simply blocks.

After a visit that comes out in C

What the layer of c tokens (M) becomes after a visit of the a/z stream that comes out in C is given row by row in Visit.lean but not composed into a law; the left side is not yet in this form. The machine can halt only there (follows from the event rules, not itself a theorem).
