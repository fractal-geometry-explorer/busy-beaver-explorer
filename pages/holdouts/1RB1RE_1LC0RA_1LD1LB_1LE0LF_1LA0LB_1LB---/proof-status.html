1RB1RE_1LC0RA_1LD1LB_1LE0LF_1LA0LB_1LB---

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. At a right turn the tape is a word over 100, 10 and 10000 (at a left turn: blocks of 5 and 7 ones and the group 2 6 9). All of it except a zone at the left end slides 9 cells to the right per round trip; the right end consumes the last run of 100s, two per round trip. The left zone acts like a counter: usually a few hundred cells, it sometimes reaches thousands (carries). The sweep rules are proved in Lean.

Lean proves no halt in the first 107 steps (10,000 by the kernel alone); the C simulator finds none in 109.

How it works

The tape is a conveyor belt. At a right turn it is a word over 100, 10 and the marker 10000. Each round trip, everything except a zone at the left end slides 9 cells to the right, unchanged. The zone at the left end is an engine that writes new words onto the belt; it behaves like a counter, usually a few hundred cells wide but sometimes rewriting thousands (a long carry).

At the right end the machine eats the belt: the last run of 100s shrinks by two words per round trip, and when it is used up the next marker and run take its place. A halt needs 1 D>1, which never happened in 109 steps.

The mirrored table of 1RB0RC_1LC1LA, a different start

Mirroring left and right and renaming the states A, B, C, D, E, F of this machine to B, C, D, E, A, F gives the transition table of 1RB0RC_1LC1LA_1RD0LB_1RE1RC_1RA0RF_1RC--- exactly (proved in lean/Twin.lean: the renaming commutes with every step, and halts_iff says this machine halts from blank tape exactly when that table halts from the other start). So this machine started on blank tape runs like the mirror image of that one started in state B instead of A. The shared table does not make the halting questions the same. The two machines start from different configurations, and their runs from blank tape never reach a common configuration (checked for the first 200,000 steps, up to the renaming). So one could halt and the other not; each is its own open question.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The right-end reader looks like a simple finite process on the runs it eats; the left engine is a counter-like zone whose output is not understood. A proof would describe both and show that the reader never meets a word that halts.
