What is known about 1RB1LC_1LA1RB_1LD0LA_1RE1RD_---1RF_1LB0RF

1RB1LC_1LA1RB_1LD0LA_1RE1RD_---1RF_1LB0RF

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

The left part of the tape is a binary counter, written as a composition of a number s into parts; each tick of it adds 1 to a binary number N written on the right. Every single rule of this counter is proved in Lean; an accelerated simulator built from them jumps whole rounds and reproduces the direct run exactly.

Status as of 9 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Lean proves no halt in the first 107 steps (native_decide), that a halt can only happen at the right edge of the tape, and every single rule of the counter (kernel). An accelerated simulator built from those rules reproduces all 44 overflow configurations of a direct 1011-step run exactly and then follows the run, without a halt, to about 1.8·1083 steps.

What the machine computes. The left part of the tape is a binary counter written as a composition of s into parts (gaps 1 1 0^(5a-2)); every tick of it adds 1 to a binary number N written on the right. A full count of a level-k counter adds 2k+1 to N in exactly 84·2k − 5 + 4(s2(N) − s2(N+2k+1)) steps. When N runs out of digits on a tick of even counter value, N is replaced by 2b+2+24 and the counter loses a digit (proved in Lean); on an odd counter value the tape is rebuilt (not decoded, simulated directly).

Why it is still open. The rebuilds at odd counter values are the only part not reduced to arithmetic. Five have been followed (at 3.1·106, 2.3·1023 steps, and so on); the one at about 1.8·1083 steps starts a structure nested inside the tape that did not finish in 2·1010 direct steps.

The halting configurations

The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (E0); the generated statement covers every configuration.

step_none_iff, lean/Blank.lean

A halt can only happen at the right edge

A closed tape language from windows of three cells covers every configuration of the run from blank tape; in all of its abstract configurations where E can read a 0, the tape right of the head is blank. So the machine halts exactly when E steps onto the blank region right of everything written: D must run over a solid block of 1s to the end of the tape.

halt_at_edge, halts_iff_edge, lean/Edge.lean, lean/EdgeCert.lean

The counter rules

Thirteen rules with symbolic lengths, each proved for every value of its variables and every tape in its contexts: the start of a round X(k, W) → Z(k+2, 0, W) in 5k+22 steps; the tick Z(s, v, N) → Z(s, v+1, N+1) for every number t of trailing parts 1 and every carry length c, in 20 + 18t + 4c steps; the end of a round in 13k+29+4c steps; and the small restructure (W full on an even tick: N becomes 2b+2+24, joined with a following word if there is one) in its three cases.

The exact length of a round

Summing the tick costs, a round from X(k, N, b) without overflow takes 84·2k − 5 + 4(s2(N) − s2(N + 2k+1)) steps (s2: binary digit sum). Checked against direct simulation on 2,070 configurations with b ≤ 11: result and step count exact.

The big restructure, and the question

What a tick on an odd counter value does to a full word is not decoded: it depends only on the counter value and not on the width (checked for s ≤ 6 at three widths), and it writes words with single 0s between them and configurations with gaps of 5a+1 cells. The one at 1083.26 builds a second counter inside the tape, to the right of a 0^21 gap, and was still running after 2·1010 direct steps. Whether the machine halts is open.
