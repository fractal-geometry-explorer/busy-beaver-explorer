Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1LC_1LA1RB_1LD0LA_1RE1RD_---1RF_1LB0RF

Nobody knows. Started on a blank tape, this Turing machine has run for 1011 steps without stopping, and a simulator built from rules proved in Lean follows it, still without a halt, to about 1083 steps. Underneath, a binary counter adds one to a binary number at every tick. This page runs the real machine in your browser, explains the counter, and lists what is known.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in E reading 0. E is entered only from D0 (writes 1, moves R): D>0 0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The records list every step at which the head reaches a cell no earlier step reached, with the side, the width so far and the whole tape: the first place to look for a repeating macro structure.

Macro structure

Two binary numbers

Run the simulator and stop it whenever the head, in state C, stands at the left end of the tape reading a 0 that is followed by 1 0. Every such moment, after a short start-up, looks like this:

C>0 1 0 1^(5k+3) 0 W

a block of 5k+3 ones, then a word W made of blocks of an odd number of 1s separated by single 0s. Read W in pairs of cells: every pair starts with a 1, and the second cell of the pair is a binary digit. So W writes a number N in binary, least significant digit first. Call this configuration X(k, N, b), where b is the number of digits.

The rule

The counter: compositions that count in binary

Between two X moments, stop the machine each time the head, in state F, reads a 0 that has a 0 to its left. The tape is then

1 1 0^(5a1-2)  1 1 0^(5a2-2)  ...  1 1 0^(5am-2)  F>0  W

a row of gaps whose lengths a1, ..., am add up to a fixed number s. Such a list is a composition of s, and the machine steps through all of them in the order of a binary counter: write a 1 for every cut between two parts, and the cuts form an (s−1)-digit binary number v. For s = 5:

partsvN
(5)000033
(4, 1)000134
(3, 2)001035
(3, 1, 1)001136
(2, 3)010037
………
(1, 1, 1, 1, 1)111148

One tick turns (…, a+2, 1, …, 1) with t trailing parts 1 into (…, a+1, t+1): that is v + 1, and t is the carry of that addition. At the same time the machine adds 1 to N, the number written in W. The tick takes exactly 20 + 18t + 4c steps, where c is the carry of N + 1. After (1, …, 1) the round ends: the gaps melt back into one block 1^(5k+8), and the tape is X(k+1, N+1).

Lean proves each of these moves for every a, t, c and every tape around them (lean/Rules.lean). Adding up the costs gives the exact length of a round from X(k, N, b): 84·2k − 5 + 4(s2(N) − s2(N+2k+1)) steps, where s2 counts binary 1s; checked against direct simulation on 2,070 configurations.

Overflows

When N runs out of digits

An overflow is a tick at which N = 2b − 1, all digits 1. What happens depends on whether the counter value v is even or odd.

  • v even: a small restructure. W becomes 2b+2 + 24 (three digits longer) and the counter loses its lowest digit: v becomes v/2 + 1 on s − 1 parts, after 103 + 4b + 18·t steps. If another word follows W after a single 0, the two are joined into one word. Lean proves all of this (small_*). In the language of the X moments it is the old formula X(k, N, b) → X(k, 2b+2+24+(M−1)/2, b+3).
  • v odd: a big restructure. The tape is rebuilt. The result depends only on v (the number of 1s at the top of N only lengthens the new word), and it can contain several words separated by single 0s and gaps of 5a+1 cells instead of 5a−2, which belong to another kind of counter. This part is not decoded; the simulator below runs it directly.

A halt needs the head, in state E, to reach the blank tape at the right end (Lean proves this is the only way). Ticks and small restructures never do that: they are proved step for step. So a halt could only come out of a big restructure.

Why it is open

Following the run to 1083 steps

With the rules above, a whole round, or any number of ticks up to the next overflow, is one arithmetic jump. Starting from X(6, 215, 8) at step 5,452 (proved in Lean), the jumps reproduce every one of the 44 X moments of the direct 1011-step run, with exact step counts. Then they run on:

No halt anywhere on the way. This is a computation, not a proof: the rules are proved one move at a time, but the sums that join them are Python, and the two earlier big restructures were simulated directly.

The big restructure at 1083.26 is different from the earlier ones. After a million steps the tape holds a frozen copy of most of the old counter, then a gap of 21 cells, then a fresh configuration C>0 1 0 1^8 0 W running its own counter on a word of about 270 digits, as if the gap were the end of the tape. It had not come back after 2·1010 steps. Settling the machine needs this nested structure decoded: when it ends, what it leaves, and whether one of them can let E reach the right edge.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.032). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

After 109 steps the tape spans 254 cells (238 left of the start, 10 right), with a density of 1s of 0.457.

A closed tape language from windows of three cells covers every configuration of the run from blank tape; in all of its abstract configurations where E can read a 0, the tape right of the head is blank. Open
The big restructure, and the question

What a tick on an odd counter value does to a full word is not decoded: it depends only on the counter value and not on the width (checked for s ≤ 6 at three widths), and it writes words with single 0s between them and configurations with gaps of 5a+1 cells. The one at 1083.26 builds a second counter inside the tape, to the right of a 0^21 gap, and was still running after 2·1010 direct steps. Whether the machine halts is open.
