The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from C1 (writes 1, moves L): 0 C>1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The run at a glance

Space-time diagram and records

The diagram stacks the tape at many moments. Sampling at each new cell shows the shape of the run as the visited region grows; sampling evenly in time shows where the head spends its time. The records list every step at which the head reaches a cell no earlier step reached, with the side, the width so far and the whole tape: the first place to look for a repeating macro structure.

Macro structure

Two binary counters on one tape

Run the machine for a while and the tape sorts itself into two numbers written in binary. On the far left, after a single 1 that acts as a sentinel, is a counter U whose digits are blocks of 3 cells: 000 is a 0 and 100 a 1, most significant digit on the left. Then come a short gap 0, the head in state D, and a fixed header such as 01101010101. Right of the header is a second counter V whose digits are pairs, least significant first: 01 is a 1 and 11 a 0.

The tick configuration is 1 U 0 D>01101010101 V. Every tick adds 1 to U and takes 1 from V. Most of the time nothing else happens.

This machine was listed as a single, but it runs the same scheme with 3-cell digits on the left (000 for 0, 100 for 1), a one-cell gap and a longer header; its ticks are slower (a constant of 146 steps) and its resets come much more often.
The rule

One tick: U + 1, V − 1

The head walks left through the trailing 1-digits of U, turning them into 0-digits (the carries), writes a 1-digit, then walks right to V and turns its trailing 0-pairs into 1-pairs and its lowest 1-pair into a 0-pair (the borrows). Written with j carries and i + 1 borrows:

beforeaftersteps
L 000 (100)^j 0 D>01101010101 01 RL 100 (000)^j 0 D>01101010101 11 R6 * j + 146
L 000 (100)^j 0 D>01101010101 (11)^(i+1) 01 RL 100 (000)^j 0 D>01101010101 (01)^(i+1) 11 R16 * i + 6 * j + 162

These two shapes are proved in Lean for every j and i (lean/Rules.lean), for every header the run has used (01101010101, 01101010111, 01101011101, 01101011111). Because the cost of a tick depends only on the numbers of carries and borrows, a long run of ticks has an exact cost: the sum of the trailing zeros of the numbers U+1, ..., U+k and V−k+1, ..., V, which has a closed form. The accelerated simulator uses it to skip billions of ticks at once.

When V runs out

The rebuild as a function of the counter

When V reaches 0 the tape is clean, 0^inf F U G S>H (11)^n 1 0^inf, and the machine's state is (H, n, N): header, length of V, and the up-counter read as one number together with its top word F. The rebuild then runs to the next clean tick configuration (H', n', N', V'), and the phase adds V' to the counter: the next state is (H', n', N' + V').

Here the digits are 3 cells (000 = 0, 100 = 1), the gap is a single 0 and there is no top word. The rebuild runs through tick-like sub-phases with longer headers before it reaches the next clean tick configuration.

The macro map in one formula

Try a macro step on any counter

The copy and eat steps can be written out completely. The formulas were fitted to computed rebuilds and agree with the exact rebuild on every case tried; they are not proved. Type a counter in binary and choose the two flags of the header; the page shows what the step reads, what is subtracted, and the next state.



Copy (a = 0): N gets one more 1 on top, n grows by 1, a becomes 1. Eat (a = 1): s low digits are removed and the next counter is 2n' + (N >> s) − δ, n' = n + 3s/2 + 1. If the lowest digit equals b the step reads two digits. Otherwise it walks over pairs of digits: in mode Q it skips pairs 11, stops at a mixed pair, and at 00 changes to mode R; in mode R it skips 00, passes pairs whose upper digit is 0, and stops at the first pair whose upper digit is 1. The flag b is a borrow pending on the counter; mode R is mode Q with a borrow pending.






  Where it could stop
  
Halting, reduced to the rebuild map


Ticks never halt, so a halt can only come from a rebuild. There are two kinds. A copy step keeps the counter and adds a 1 on top. An eat step reads the counter from the bottom by a simple walk over pairs of digits (passing ones, then zeros, stopping at the first 1 in the right kind of position), removes what it read and puts a long new block on top. In every case computed, an eat step that stops before the top digits does not halt.


So a halt would need one walk to cross the whole, ever-growing counter. That looks very unlikely, but nobody has a rule that forbids it: two sibling machines were settled by others with a property kept from one V = 0 moment to the next, and no such property has been found here. The question is open.

The tick rules of the two counters
For each of the headers 01101010101, 01101010111, 01101011101, 01101011111 the run uses, two theorems: with V odd, L 000 (100)^j 0 D>01101010101 01 R becomes L 100 (000)^j 0 D>01101010101 11 R in 6 * j + 146 steps; with V having i + 1 trailing zeros, L 000 (100)^j 0 D>01101010101 (11)^(i+1) 01 R becomes L 100 (000)^j 0 D>01101010101 (01)^(i+1) 11 R in 16 * i + 6 * j + 162 steps. That is U + 1 and V − 1 for every number j of carries and i + 1 of borrows and every tape L, R beyond (proved along the symbolic run of tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py).

tick, phase, phase_to_zero, lean/Phase.lean (written by holdouts/1RB1LC_0RC0RB_1LD0LA_1LE---_1LF1RA_1LA1LC/py/gen_phase.py)
A whole run of ticks, for any counters
Three statements about the tick configuration (up-counter U, gap, header, down-counter V, anything beyond), for counters given as arbitrary lists of binary digits rather than one pattern at a time. tick: if U is not all ones and V is not zero, the machine reaches the same configuration with U + 1 and V − 1. phase: for every k up to the value of V for which U + k still fits in U's digits, it reaches the configuration with U + k and V − k, the widths unchanged, without reading anything beyond the two counters. phase_to_zero: in particular, if U + V fits, V is counted down to 0 and U has gained exactly V. This is the step ‘then add the V ticks by arithmetic’ of the macro map, now a theorem; the macro simulator itself is still trusted code.

The macro map and its simulator
At each moment V reaches 0 the tape is the clean configuration 0^inf F U 0 D> H (11)^n 1 0^inf (F the counter's top word), so the state is (H, n, N) with N the up-counter read as one number. py/rebuild.py writes that tape, runs the rebuild (tick runs of its sub-phases jumped by the proved tick rules, the rest cell by cell in C) to the next clean tick configuration (H', n', N', V'), and then adds the V' ticks of the phase by arithmetic: next state (H', n', N' + V'), steps by the closed formula of the tick costs. py/map.py check 100 40, py/map.py small 9
The shape of the macro map: copy steps and eat steps
At a V = 0 moment the header is 0110101 a 1 b 1 with two flags a, b. Fitted to computed rebuilds and then checked, not proved: if a = 0 the rebuild is a ‘copy’: the counter is kept and gets one more 1 on top (new counter N + 2n+1, n grows by 1), and a becomes 1. If a = 1 it is an ‘eat’: an even number s of low digits of N is removed, n grows by 3s/2 + 1 and the new counter is 2n' + (N >> s) − δ with 0 < δ < 23s+8; the flags may come out either way. The number s is given by the same kind of walk as for the sibling 1RB1LD_1RC0RB_1LA1RC_1LE0LA_1LF---_1LC---: with b = 0 an even N gives s = 2 and an odd N starts the walk in state Q; with b = 1 an odd N gives s = 2 and an even N starts it in state R; Q passes pairs 11, switches to R on 00 and stops on 01 or 10; R passes pairs ending in 0 and stops on a pair ending in 1. Checked against the exact rebuild: 100 random 40-digit counters with all four headers and three values of n (600 copies, 600 eats), and every 9-digit counter (1,530 copies, 1,344 eats; the all-ones counter is left out of the copy rule; 192 eats have a walk that reaches the top three digits, one of which halts); no exception. δ as a function of the digits read was not written down.

    
Computed
holdouts/1RB1LE_1RC0RB_1RD1LF_1LA0LC_1LD0LA_---0RE/py/closed.py (check 12, blank 280)
The macro map in closed form
The copy and eat steps written out completely, as a function of the macro state (a, b, n, N). Copy (a = 0): (1, b, n + 1, N + 2n+1). Eat (a = 1): (a', b', n', 2n' + (N >> s) − δ) with n' = n + 3s/2 + 1, where s, a', b' and δ come from one pass over the low digits b0, b1, ... of N. If b0 = b: s = 2, δ = 27 − b1 (b = 0) or 11 (b = 1). Otherwise the pass walks over the pairs (b1, b2), (b3, b4), ... in mode Q (b = 0) or R (b = 1), with k the number of pairs skipped since the last term. Q: (1,1) is skipped; (0,0) gives the term 25k+9 − 48·8k − 10 and switches to R; (0,1) stops with last term (22k+3 − 1) 23k+6 + 23k+4 + 1; (1,0) stops with (22k+4 − 1) 23k+6 + 23k+4 + 1 − e. R: (0,0) is skipped; (1,0) gives the term 25k+4 + 78·8k − 10 and stays in R; (0,1) stops with 25k+5 + 78·8k + 1 − e; (1,1) stops with 25k+4 + 78·8k + 1. Here e is the digit after the stopping pair, δ is the sum of the terms, each shifted left by 3 places per pair read after it, a' is the upper digit of the stopping pair in Q and the lower one in R, and b' = a' xor e. So the flag b is a borrow pending on the counter, and R is Q with a borrow pending. The formulas were fitted to computed rebuilds and then compared with the exact rebuild: all 252 classes of low digits with s ≤ 12 (b = 0 and 1), each with two values of n, and the first 279 macro steps of the run from blank tape (189 eats, 90 copies). No difference. They are not proved.

Heuristic
holdouts/1RB1LE_1RC0RB_1RD1LF_1LA0LC_1LD0LA_---0RE/py/closed.py (run 100000000)
How long the walks get
Of the 67 million eat steps of that run about half read s = 2 digits. Long walks are rare: s ≥ 64 in 11,488 eat steps, s ≥ 128 in 652, s ≥ 256 in 40, s ≥ 512 in 6; each doubling of s costs a factor of about 16. The longest walk read 750 digits when the counter had 29 million; after the first 3,000 macro steps no walk read more than 0.17% of the counter. This is a power-law tail, thinner than that of the sibling 1RB1LD_1RC0RB_1LA1RC_1LE0LA_1LF---_1LC--- (a factor of 8 to 10 per doubling there), so a walk across the whole counter looks very unlikely, but no bound on the walk is proved.

    
Heuristic
py/rebuild_stats.py 1RB1LE_1RC0R... run
What the rebuild map looks like in the run
The 293 rebuilds of a 90-second macro run end in all four headers; 96 are copies and the others eat s = 2, 4, 6, ... digits (about half of them 2). So this machine behaves like its sibling 1RB1LD_1RC0RB_1LA1RC_1LE0LA_1LF---_1LC--- with an extra copy step: a counter that gains long blocks on top and loses a few digits at the bottom by a walk that would have to cross the whole counter for anything unusual (such as a halt) to happen. The same chance argument suggests it never halts; it is a heuristic.

    
Open
Does it halt from blank tape?
Not known. The two siblings 1RB1LC_0RC0RB_1LD0LA_1LE---_1LF1RA_1LA1LC and 1RB1LE_1RC0RB_1LD1RC_1LA1LE_1LF0LA_1LC--- have non-halting proofs in Coq in open pull requests of ccz181078/busycoq (#58 and #57; read on 12 November, not rebuilt here: Coq is not installed in this container). Their method, in plain terms: call a ‘wall’ the tape at a moment when V is 0. The proof gives a property of walls (enough spare room, and a marker: a 1 digit at a prescribed kind of position in the counter) and shows that every wall with the property runs to another wall with the property. It works there because the rebuild of those machines converts the whole counter in one go and loses only a bounded amount each time, so the marker can be followed from wall to wall. For this machine no such property was found (round 6): only the copy step converts the whole counter; the eat steps remove a few low digits by the walk and leave the rest, as in 1RB1LD_1RC0RB_1LA1RC_1LE0LA_1LF---_1LC---, and no quantity is known that every step keeps and that stops the walk before the top. No rebuild rule is proved in Lean for this machine. A known reduction, not rebuilt here: busycoq's Eqv_v3.v (entry TM411) proves in Coq that this machine halts if and only if 1RB1LE_1RC0RB_1RD0LF_1LA0LC_1LD0LA_---1RB halts.
