What is known about 1RB0LC_1RC1RA_1LD0RE_1LA1LD_0RA0RF_0LD---

1RB0LC_1RC1RA_1LD0RE_1LA1LD_0RA0RF_0LD---

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 12 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The tape holds a counter (100)^n and a list of runs of 1s of lengths 7, 13, 19, … (6k+1). The run goes in epochs: in each, the number of runs of every length is halved and they move up one length, half the counter becomes new runs of 7, and the new counter is about 5/2 times the old number of runs of 7; so everything grows by 5/4 every two epochs, and the step count by 25/16. These rules hold only up to small corrections (a run more or less, odd runs between the groups), which is why the community calls this a chaotic Bell. The machine halts exactly when state B reads 0 1 1 1 (B0 C1 E1 put F on a 1; the path is proved in Lean). An exact accelerated simulator, validated against direct simulation, finds no halt in 7.9×1014 steps. Its F-event differs from the one of the other three machines (it writes 1 1 1 1 and leaves B, they write 1 0 0 0 and leave A), so its tapes are not theirs, but the structure and the growth law are the same.

9 shift rules

For every n, the head crosses n copies of a short word in a fixed number of steps per copy and leaves another word behind (for example the head crossing (100)^n or (1)^n; these are the sweeps over the counter and the runs). Generated and proved by bbtools.symtm, checked by Lean's kernel.

The local paths and the halting path

Outside the sweeps, the machine does a handful of local things, each proved for every tape around it: A on a 1 bounces (bounce_on_1, bounce_on_0_0, bounce_on_0_1); B reading 0 1 1 0 gives 1 1 1 1 with B on the right (8 steps: B0 C1 E1 F0 D0 A0 B1 A0) (fevent, the F-event, twice per round trip in the second half of an epoch); and B reading 0 1 1 1 halts: B0 C1 E1 put F on a 1 (fevent_halt). By the table this is the only way to halt: F is entered only by E1, E only by C1, and C by B0 (moving right) or A1 (moving left; then C1 puts E on the 0 that A has just written, and E0 goes on).

The exact accelerated simulator agrees with direct simulation

bell_wsim.py keeps the tape as repeated words, crosses a repeated word in one move (an exact shift rule found on the fly), and at the left turns looks for a stretch of round trips after which the tape has the same shape with counts changed by constants; it then applies many such stretches at once, with the exact step count. Its tape, state and step count were compared with a plain C simulator at 660 tapes up to 2.08×1010 steps: all equal.

The shared orbit

Its F-event differs from the one of the other three machines (it writes 1 1 1 1 and leaves B, they write 1 0 0 0 and leave A), so its tapes are not theirs, but the structure and the growth law are the same. The reason is in the tables: a4 does with E, F what a2 does with C, E on the bounce of A, a3 replaces the step C0 of a2 by three steps with the same result, and all three turn B> 0 1 1 0 into 1 0 0 0 A>. They could part only where B reads 0 1 0 (a2 and a4 differ there: b_010), 0 1 1 1 (a2 and a4 halt, a3 goes on) or 0 0 1 (a3 halts); in 109 steps of a2 and a4, B never reads 0 1 0 and reads 0 0 only at the blank right end.

Neighbours and other starts

The community tracker calls it “Chaotic Bell with a neighbour that halts”. heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.509). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.11).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

The epoch law

Read off the recorded lists, not proved: with n the counter and c7, c13, … the numbers of runs, one epoch gives c7′ ≈ n/2, c6j+7′ ≈ c6j+1/2 (the half, or one or two less) and n′ ≈ (5/2)·c7 (within 10−4 at 1014 steps). Hence the two interleaved geometric series in the table of counts, the factor 5 per +12 in length, growth by 5/4 every two epochs and step counts growing by 25/16. Between the groups sit a few odd runs that change from epoch to epoch without a visible rule; they are the chaotic part, and the halting pattern can only arise there.

data/far_1e14.json

Where B reads 0 1 1

In the first 109 steps of a2 state B reads 0 1 1 20,972 times; in 20,941 of them the cells around are 111101[0]110101, in 29 001101[0]110101, and twice (steps 104 and 1,264) something else; the next cell is always 0. a1 has 20,998 such events with the windows 111100[0]110101 and 001100[0]110101. So after the first 1,300 steps the F-events seen are all of two harmless kinds; whether that holds for ever is the open question.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. It halts exactly if B ever reads 0 1 1 1. A proof of non-halting needs an invariant for the odd runs between the groups, which no closed rule describes so far; the simulator slows down beyond 1015 steps because those runs pile up at the right end.
