1RB0LF_1RC0RA_1LD0RB_0LE0LC_0LA0LC_0LB---

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Lean proves the shift rules of the sweeps over the (10) sea and the only halting shape (1 1 E> 0). In 109 steps E read a 0 30,301 times, never with 1 1 to its left. The rounds follow a regular pattern, but the reshuffle at the end of a round is not worked out.

The sweeps' shift rules

Rightward, A turns (01)^n into (10)^n, 2 steps a pair, and A or B with a 0 behind them cross the 0001/1000 markers; leftward, C crosses (01)^n and D crosses (10)^n unchanged, 2 steps a pair.

a_run, b_run, a_run4, c_run, d_run, lean/Rules.lean

The shape of a halt

F on a 1 halts; F is entered only from A on a 1 (0LF), and A comes from B on a 1 (moving right, which leaves a 0 where F lands) or from E on a 0 (moving left). So it halts iff E reads a 0 with 1 1 to its left: 1 1 E> 0 reaches F on a 1 in 2 steps (the converse by reading the table, not formalized).

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.027). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.477).

Rounds

Sampled with C on a 1 at the right end (once a sweep), the tape is 1 100 100 (10)^x0 1000 (10)^x1 100 (10)^x2 100 ... (10)^X 1000 (10)^Y plus a short end. During a round X drops by 2 and Y grows by 3 each sweep (from step 7.25·108 to 9.74·108: X 8477 → 2877, Y 18063 → 26463). When X runs out the small runs on the left change (e.g. 52,4,293,497,17 → 40,16,290,500,15 → 56,4,277,512,2) and a new round starts with a larger X: rounds began near steps 4.59·107, 1.01·108, 2.28·108, 5.11·108 with X = 5546, 8649, 12310, 18877.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. Halting needs 1 1 E> 0, which the sweeps never produce; it would have to come from the reshuffle at the end of a round, whose rule (how the small runs change) has not been found.
