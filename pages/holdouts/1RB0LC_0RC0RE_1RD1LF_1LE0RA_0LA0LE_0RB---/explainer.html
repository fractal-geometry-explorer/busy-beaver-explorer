The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from C1 (writes 1, moves L): 1 C>1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The run at a glance

Space-time diagram and records

The diagram stacks the tape at many moments. Sampling at each new cell shows the shape of the run as the visited region grows; sampling evenly in time shows where the head spends its time. The records list every step at which the head reaches a cell no earlier step reached, with the side, the width so far and the whole tape: the first place to look for a repeating macro structure.

Macro structure

A base-5 counter of markers

Stop the machine each time the head steps onto a new cell at the growing end. The tape is then always

1 (0010)b0 m1 (0010)b1 … mk (0010)bk 000

(mirrored in this machine: it grows to the left) long blocks of the word 0010 separated by markers, each in one of six phases: 1 (0), 0 (0′), 0011 (1), 011 (2), 11 (3), 00111 (4).

Between two such moments the head makes one sweep: a wave runs from the end toward the start, passing every marker in phase 4, and turns at the first marker that is not; that marker goes up one phase, and on the way back the phase-4 markers are reset to phase 0. The phases are the digits of a counter in base 5 that counts the sweeps. When every marker is in phase 4 the wave runs to the start of the tape and the counter gains a digit (a new marker at the end).

The blocks around a marker change with its phase (phase 0 takes two words from the block before it, phases 0′ and 3 one), so the markers drift toward the start, the last one fastest. When a marker has used up the block before it, it merges with the marker there: a few special sweeps, after which the counter goes on. Lean proves the regular sweeps for all block lengths.

Run the counter. The tape between two sweeps, written as its first block b0 and the markers with the block after each. Each step is one sweep of the machine by the two regular rules; the run stops at a merge, where a marker has used up the block before it and the machine does something special.








  Open question
  
Where it could halt


The only halting transition is F1, and F is entered only at some merges (and, in two of the sixteen machines, on a detour that does what the others do in one step). Every entry into F so far has read a 0. Every merge of two markers with long blocks around them runs, symbolically and for all block lengths, without halting. In this machine even random short tapes of the same shape do not halt, so no halting configuration reachable in this shape is known at all.
The counter is deterministic, and the block lengths it makes look very regular (many are close to multiples of powers of 5), but nothing here decides whether such a coincidence ever comes.


Sixteen holdouts run this counter, in five distinct runs; this machine is in class I. The family is described in full on the page of 1RB0LC_0RC0RB_1LD0RE_0LE0LF_1LA0LB_0LC---.

The shape of the run


Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.557). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).



After 109 steps the tape spans 78,481 cells (61,887 left of the start, 52 right), with a density of 1s of 0.269.
