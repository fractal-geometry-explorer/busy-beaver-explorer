1RB---_1LC0LD_0RF0LD_0LE0RA_0LC1RA_1RC1RD

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 22 November 2026.

In short

Open, and probably never halting. From step 18 the run is the orbit of an explicit map on (tail, n). The tail is 1, Xk, Yk or Zk, and n is multiplied by 3/2 or 9/8 per pass. The map is proved in Lean's kernel for every tail and every n, except Zk with n < 12k+12, the only place where a halt can come from. That region is now mapped in Lean as well: from Zk with n < 12k+12 the machine halts in the pass exactly when k is odd and n = 12k+9, and otherwise reaches a next E-event with an explicit tail, which is in general a new word (a block followed by a short end word). Lean also checks the orbit: the first 3,000 passes from blank tape stay in the proved region (kernel; no halt in the first 10450 steps), and so do the first 105 (native_decide; 1016000 steps). The closest approach to the bad region was at pass 28 (n = 3,989, k = 5), still 55 times above the bound. The exits of the region are closed for k < 20 (native): each of the 2,502 starts halts (7 do) or reaches a tail of the map again within 12 passes, so halting is exactly the orbit of the extended map up to region starts with k ≥ 20. A non-halting proof would need a bound on k in terms of n along the whole orbit, which is a Collatz-type question.

The E-pass map, for all n

With an E-event E> 0^6 (10)^n T (Ev n T): next T n gives the next E-event and the exact number of steps to it, for every tail T (1, Xk, Yk, Zk) and every n, except Zk with k = 0 or n < 12k+12. next_sound proves that the machine goes there in exactly that many steps. It rests on 83 case theorems (tail 1 by parity of n; X and Y by n mod 8, with a generic k and a few small k; Z by the parities of k and n), each a chain of symbolic-simulator segments and loops (three rounds of the 9/8 phase, or one round of the 3/2 phase) proved by induction. Axioms: propext and Quot.sound only.

next_sound, lean/Map.lean; lean/Cases.lean, generated by py/gen_map.py and py/gen_mapdef.py

The region Zk, n < 12k+12: halts in the pass iff k is odd and n = 12k+9

For every k and n with n < 12k+12, and for Z0 with any n: zregion_halt proves that Ev n (.Z k) halts in its pass when k is odd and n = 12k+9 (the run ends at (10)^(27j+28) A> 1), and zregion_ev that every other start reaches an E-event EvT n' T = E> 0^6 (10)^n' T. Inside the region the whole pass is the 9/8 phase of the Z tail (nine rounds use 24 of n and 2 of k), so n' = (9n+c)/8 and T is the rest of the tail block, about k − n/12, followed by a new end word; for n ≡ 7 (mod 8) the rest is itself multiplied by 9/8. 190 families (by n mod 24, the rest of k, and the boundary near n ≈ 12k), each a lemma zw_<case> with n', T and the exact step count, generated by py/gen_zreg.py and proved like the map (symbolic segments and loops iterated by induction). zregion_two: the start n = 2 never halts (Zk goes to Zk+1 at n = 2). Axioms: propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

The E-pass map against direct simulation

With an E-event E> 0^6 (10)^n T (n maximal): the tail T is always 1, Xk = 000 (10)^k 1, Yk = 11 (01)^k or Zk = 000 (10)^k 0001. The next E-event has n' = (3n+c)/2 for tails 1 and Zk, and n' = (9n+c)/8 for Xk and Yk. The new tail and c depend on the tail and on n mod 2 or n mod 8, and the index k changes by −2 to +1 or becomes 3k/2+3 or (3k+1)/2 (table in py/emap.py). The X and Y rules hold for all n ≥ 0, and Zk needs n ≥ 12k+12. Checked against direct simulation on a grid (every tail with k < 25 at 120 values of n each: 9,120 starts, no mismatch) and against the 200 rules measured independently by direct simulation. (Now proved in Lean, above.)

106 passes inside the region where the map applies: the margins

Iterating the map from (1, 0) with exact big integers: 106 passes, all covered by the map, so no halt. n then has 80,583 digits; the index k never exceeded 2,972. Of the 38,053 Z-events the tightest is still n/(12k+12) = 55 (pass 28, n = 3,989, k = 5); after pass 70 the ratio is above 106, and in each later block of 2.5·105 passes it is above 1020000. The share of Z-events with k ≥ K is 23% for K = 10, 0.8% for K = 100 and 0.02% for K = 1000 (8 events), none for K = 3000. A longer run, 7.4·106 passes so far of a planned 107 (n then has 594,529 digits; 283,259 Z-events), gives the same picture: the largest index is k = 22,853, at a Z-event between passes 1.4·106 and 1.5·106, when n had about 112,000 digits.

The exits are not a small family of words

Following the 4,320 region starts with 14 ≤ k < 30 by direct simulation: 4,286 reach a tail of the map within 16 passes, 18 halt, 16 are the n = 2 cycle. On the way they meet 57 shapes of tail words (with the exponents left out), such as 11 (01)^a 0^b 1, 000 (10)^a 000 (10)^b 1 and (0001010)^a 101, and the list keeps growing with k. An end word after a tail is not passive: Xk, Yk or Zk followed by a word such as 0001 gives the map's next event with that word still after it in only 26, 9 and 0 of 240 starts. So the exits cannot be closed by a map on lists of blocks with fixed end words; the table above closes them for small k instead.

Rounds between end events, by remainder class

Sample the run whenever the head is in state E reading 1 with only 0s to its left. Read rightwards from the head, the tape is then A U^m B: one long periodic block between two short context words. For each class (A, U, B, m modulo 2, 8 or 24 (the smallest modulus that works for the context)) the next sample is simulated for six values of m and extended as an affine map of m (with a cubic step count). Along the direct run from blank tape to 3·109 steps (33 events) the class map predicts the next event in all 22 rounds it applies to. This is an extrapolation checked by computation, not a proof.

Probviously non-halting: the index would have to catch up with n

A halt can only start from Zk with n < 12k+12. In a phase of X/Y tails, k moves by −2 to +1 and is multiplied by 3/2 at 1 pass in 8, and it starts again from 0 at every phase. Along the orbit the fraction of Z-events with k ≥ K falls like K−1.1 to K−1.7 (0.6% for K = 100), while n grows by a factor of about 9/8 per pass. So the chance that some phase reaches k ≈ n/12 is about (n/12)−1.2 per phase: about 10−5 at the end of direct simulation, and far smaller after that, where n has thousands of digits.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known, but very probably not. Unlike Antihydra-type machines, the bad region is not reached by a fair random walk: k would have to grow as fast as n. No simple invariant k ≤ f(n) is in sight: within a 9/8 phase the residues n mod 8 that drive k are themselves a (9n+c)/8 orbit, and since (9n+c)/8 is a bijection on residues, any finite sequence of residues occurs for some n. So a bound on k is a Collatz-type statement about this orbit. Of the 3·105 starts (1, n) with n < 3·105, only n = 8, 11, 13, 15 reach the uncovered region within 200 passes. Halting is now an exact statement about the orbit of the extended map (halts_iff_blank), as long as the orbit never meets a region start with k ≥ 20; beyond that the exits are new tail words that do not form a small family.
