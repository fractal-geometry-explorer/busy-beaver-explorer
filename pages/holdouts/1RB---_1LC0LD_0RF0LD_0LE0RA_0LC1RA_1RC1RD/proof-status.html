What is known about 1RB---_1LC0LD_0RF0LD_0LE0RA_0LC1RA_1RC1RD

1RB---_1LC0LD_0RF0LD_0LE0RA_0LC1RA_1RC1RD

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 13 November 2026.

In short

Open, and probably never halting. From step 18 the run is the orbit of an explicit map on (tail, n). The tail is 1, Xk, Yk or Zk, and n is multiplied by 3/2 or 9/8 per pass. The map is proved in Lean's kernel for every tail and every n, except Zk with n < 12k+12, the only place where a halt can come from. Lean also checks the orbit: the first 3,000 passes from blank tape stay in the proved region (kernel; no halt in the first 10450 steps), and so do the first 105 (native_decide; 1016000 steps). The closest approach to the bad region was at pass 28 (n = 3,989, k = 5), still 55 times above the bound. A non-halting proof would need a bound on k in terms of n along the whole orbit, which is a Collatz-type question.

The E-pass map, for all n

With an E-event E> 0^6 (10)^n T (Ev n T): next T n gives the next E-event and the exact number of steps to it, for every tail T (1, Xk, Yk, Zk) and every n, except Zk with k = 0 or n < 12k+12. next_sound proves that the machine goes there in exactly that many steps. It rests on 83 case theorems (tail 1 by parity of n; X and Y by n mod 8, with a generic k and a few small k; Z by the parities of k and n), each a chain of symbolic-simulator segments and loops (three rounds of the 9/8 phase, or one round of the 3/2 phase) proved by induction. Axioms: propext and Quot.sound only.

The E-pass map against direct simulation

With an E-event E> 0^6 (10)^n T (n maximal): the tail T is always 1, Xk = 000 (10)^k 1, Yk = 11 (01)^k or Zk = 000 (10)^k 0001. The next E-event has n' = (3n+c)/2 for tails 1 and Zk, and n' = (9n+c)/8 for Xk and Yk. The new tail and c depend on the tail and on n mod 2 or n mod 8, and the index k changes by −2 to +1 or becomes 3k/2+3 or (3k+1)/2 (table in py/emap.py). The X and Y rules hold for all n ≥ 0, and Zk needs n ≥ 12k+12. Checked against direct simulation on a grid (every tail with k < 25 at 120 values of n each: 9,120 starts, no mismatch) and against the 200 rules measured independently by direct simulation. (Now proved in Lean, above.)

Rounds between end events, by remainder class

Sample the run whenever the head is in state E reading 1 with only 0s to its left. Read rightwards from the head, the tape is then A U^m B: one long periodic block between two short context words. For each class (A, U, B, m modulo 2, 8 or 24 (the smallest modulus that works for the context)) the next sample is simulated for six values of m and extended as an affine map of m (with a cubic step count). Along the direct run from blank tape to 3·109 steps (33 events) the class map predicts the next event in all 22 rounds it applies to. This is an extrapolation checked by computation, not a proof.

Probviously non-halting: the index would have to catch up with n

A halt can only start from Zk with n < 12k+12. In a phase of X/Y tails, k moves by −2 to +1 and is multiplied by 3/2 at 1 pass in 8, and it starts again from 0 at every phase. Along the orbit the fraction of Z-events with k ≥ K falls like K−1.1 to K−1.7 (0.6% for K = 100), while n grows by a factor of about 9/8 per pass. So the chance that some phase reaches k ≈ n/12 is about (n/12)−1.2 per phase: about 10−5 at the end of direct simulation, and far smaller after that, where n has thousands of digits.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known, but very probably not. Unlike Antihydra-type machines, the bad region is not reached by a fair random walk: k would have to grow as fast as n. No simple invariant k ≤ f(n) is in sight: within a 9/8 phase the residues n mod 8 that drive k are themselves a (9n+c)/8 orbit, and since (9n+c)/8 is a bijection on residues, any finite sequence of residues occurs for some n. So a bound on k is a Collatz-type statement about this orbit. Of the 3·105 starts (1, n) with n < 3·105, only n = 8, 11, 13, 15 reach the uncovered region within 200 passes.
