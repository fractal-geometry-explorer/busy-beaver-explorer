What is known about 1RB0LA_0RC0RF_0RD0RA_1LD0LE_0LA1LE_---1LB

1RB0LA_0RC0RF_0RD0RA_1LD0LE_0LA1LE_---1LB

Nobody knows whether this six-state machine halts from blank tape. It is a close relative of 1RB0LA_0RC---_0RD0RF_1LD0LE_0LA1LE_0RA0LA: the tape is blocks of 1s separated by single 0s, and each round turns the first block into 0 1 pairs that come back as gliders, each moving a hole 3 cells left. Rounds from a first block of even length are step for step the same as in that machine and are proved in Lean; rounds from an odd block differ, and that is also where a halt could happen.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Lean proves the whole round from an even first block and no halt in the first 107 steps. The machine halts exactly when F reads a 0; F is entered only from B reading a 1, and on the tapes seen that happens when a sweep that has passed an odd block meets a block of a single 1 (C>1 0 1 0 runs into F on a 0). In 109 steps no round after step 2,136 started with a block shorter than 12 cells.

Where the two machines part

The sweep crossing a hole after an odd stretch, C>1 0 1 1 → 0 1 0 C>1, is the same in both machines; on C>1 0 1 0 (a block of one 1 after the hole) this machine reaches F on a 0 and halts, where the twin carries on.

Every round starts on single holes

At the 660 times up to step 109 that the head is at the left end in state E on a 0, the tape is blocks of 1s separated by single 0s (except at steps 7 and 204): 2 blocks 593 times, 3 blocks 53, 4 blocks 10, 5 blocks 2. The shortest block at a round start is 3 (step 1,605); after step 2,136 it is never below 12.

Why the width grows like t1/3

As in the twin: about x/4 gliders each cross the second block and come back, so a round costs about 1.5·x·a ~ w2 steps, and it adds 3 ones (x = 4K), 4 (x = 4K+2) or 4 to 28 (odd x). So w3 ~ t.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The even rounds are proved; odd first blocks make the sweep pass through holes, and the machine would halt if such a sweep met a block of a single 1. A proof needs the odd rounds as rules and an invariant that keeps blocks long.
