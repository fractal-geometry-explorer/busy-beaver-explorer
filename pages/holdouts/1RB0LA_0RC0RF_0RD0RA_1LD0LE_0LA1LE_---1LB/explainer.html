A 6-state Busy Beaver machine · Status as of 8 November 2026.

Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LA_0RC0RF_0RD0RA_1LD0LE_0LA1LE_---1LB

Nobody knows. Like its twin 1RB0LA_0RC---…, this machine keeps a nearly solid block of 1s with one or two holes, and each round moves a hole: the first block becomes 0 1 pairs, and every two pairs come back as a glider that knocks the hole 3 cells left. Rounds cost about the square of the width and add a few cells, so the width grows like the cube root of the time.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from B1 (writes 0, moves R): B>1 0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

What it does

Same rounds as its twin

Stop the machine whenever the head is at the left end in state E. The tape is then a few blocks of 1s separated by single 0s: at step 997,741,851 it is E>0 1^3127 0 1^726, blocks [3127, 726].

A round does what it does in 1RB0LA_0RC---_0RD0RF_1LD0LE_0LA1LE_0RA0LA. The sweep turns the first block's pairs 1 1 into 0 1 (here with states B, C, A instead of B, C, F, A). A first block of even length ends the sweep with a glider, two 0s that move right through the next block at one cell per 5 steps. Each glider reaches the next hole, fills it, and leaves a hole 3 cells further left; E walks back, two more pairs make the next glider, and so on. The round from [16, 20] is identical, step for step, in both machines and ends at [24, 15] after 715 steps.

Lean proves the whole round for every even first block: [4K, a, b, …] becomes [a+K, 3K+3+b, …] and [4K+2, a, b, …] becomes [a+K, 3K+6+b, …]. All 329 even rounds of the first 109 steps match.

The hard case

Odd blocks, and a halt on a block of one

When the first block is odd, the sweep runs through the hole into the next block (C>1 0 1 1 becomes 0 1 0 C>1), leaving a mark behind. Here the two machines differ: in this one, if the stretch after the hole is a single 1 (C>1 0 1 0), the next steps put F on a 0, which is the halting transition. So the machine halts if a sweep coming out of an odd block ever meets a block of length 1.

Blocks at round starts are long: after step 2,136, none is shorter than 12 cells in 109 steps. Whether short blocks can appear in the middle of a round, where the gliders rework the tape, is part of the open question.

Growth law

Why the width grows like t1/3

Each glider crosses the second block and E walks back over it, so a round from blocks [x, a] costs about 1.5·x·a steps, of the order of the width squared, and adds only a few ones (3 to 28). A constant gain per w2 steps gives w3 ~ t. After 109 steps the tape is 3,867 cells wide.

py/rounds.py
Why the width grows like t1/3

As in the twin: about x/4 gliders each cross the second block and come back, so a round costs about 1.5·x·a ~ w2 steps, and it adds 3 ones (x = 4K), 4 (x = 4K+2) or 4 to 28 (odd x). So w3 ~ t.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The even rounds are proved; odd first blocks make the sweep pass through holes, and the machine would halt if such a sweep met a block of a single 1. A proof needs the odd rounds as rules and an invariant that keeps blocks long.
