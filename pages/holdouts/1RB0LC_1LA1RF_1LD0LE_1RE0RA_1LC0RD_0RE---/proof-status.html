OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 23 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The tape is a list of blocks (10)^n cut by single 1s, after a reservoir of the word 1010101 at the left end. In a drain each round trip moves one pair of the last block into the reservoir; when it is down to one pair a rebuild starts, in which a reflector Q walks left and triples the part of the list it passes. Every separator and every reservoir word is a mover, and Lean proves that the head crosses any run of movers unchanged, so every bounce is a lemma except the few cells where the head turns. On these lemmas an exact simulator runs 1.5×1018 steps without a halt. Every halt would be the head in D, moving right, on 1 0 1 1, inside one of those collisions. Every kind of collision seen is now a Lean lemma for every value of its gaps, and none halts; nothing yet shows that no other kind ever happens.

Crossing pairs

lean/Rules.lean

The reflector's walk

With the head at the right end in state E and blank beyond, Q0 c a = L (01)^c 0 (01)^a E> and Q1 c a = L (01)^c 1 (01)^a E>: one bounce takes Q0 (c+1) (a+1) to Q1 c (a+3) in 4a+14 steps and Q1 (c+1) (a+1) to Q0 c (a+3) in 4a+10 steps, and k double bounces take Q0 (c+2k) (a+1) to Q0 c (a+1+4k) in 8ak+16k²+16k steps, for every c, a, k and every tape L. Q moves two cells left per bounce, the right end two cells right.

bounce0, bounce1, bounce2, walk, lean/Walk.lean (cell rules lean/WalkRules.lean, written by py/gen_walk.py)

Movers: the head crosses them unchanged

At a right-end turn a mover is the word 0 0 1 1 0 followed by g pairs 01; the list's separators, the reservoir's words (g = 1) and the separators Q has passed are all movers. For any list of gaps of at least one pair, the left sweep (state E on a 0) crosses all the movers and comes out on their left in state E on a 0, and the right sweep crosses them again and comes out in state D with every mover four cells further right (after a one-pair gap it runs in state F for a while, which changes only the first cell it writes). Hence any bounce, whatever happens left of the movers, as long as the head sent in from the right in state E on a 0 comes back in state D or F without looking right, keeps the gaps between movers and shortens the last one by a pair; with Q on the left this gives Q's walk with movers in closed form.

left_all, right_gen, bounce_gen, bounce0m, bounce1m, walkm, lean/Movers.lean (cell rules lean/MoverRules.lean, written by py/gen_movers.py)

Every collision seen, for all values of its gaps

Up to 1.6×1018 steps the exact simulator runs 46 families of collisions cell by cell at the ends of the movers (plus 43 whole-tape runs made when no mover lies between the two places where the head turns). py/gen_collisions.py turns the first run of each family into a symbolic configuration, every run of pairs a variable and everything the run does not read a context, and proves it with bbtools.symtm: 20 distinct lemmas, each for every value of its gaps. Two of them first appear after 1015 steps, so new shapes still turn up. At the right end a collision sweeps back over any list of swept movers 1 (10)^(g+2) and a last one 1 (10)^1: zlist and zend take any such list. None of these can halt. One family (once, crossing about 85 movers stored as cells) and the whole-tape runs are not generalised.

lean/Collisions.lean, lean/ZList.lean, py/gen_collisions.py

A right sweep that ends on one-pair gaps

When a swept block of movers ends with k+1 movers of one-pair gap, the head crosses them in 7(k+1) steps and comes out in state F, leaving k movers at rest with one-pair gaps: for any k, entered in D or in F, after any block crossed by right_gen.

Where a halt could come from

From the table: B is entered only from A reading 0, moving right and writing a 1, so B reading 0 sends the head back onto that 1; F is entered only as A> 0 1 x, and it halts when x = 1. A reading 0 is entered only from D reading 1 (from B it lands on the 1 just written), and D reading 1 with A then reading 0 only from E (C writes the 1 that A would read). So every halt is D> 1 0 1 1 with D moving right: a block of one pair between two extra 1s. In 3×109 steps F is entered 63,295,957 times, always on a 0, in 22 different 17-cell windows.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: linear/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows in proportion to the step count (the head drifts steadily that way) (fitted exponent 1.01). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.462).

How a rebuild goes

Observed at every bounce of the rebuilds at 1.4×108 and 1.79×109 steps: a separator of the list (001100 in that phase) that Q passes becomes a mover 11, carried four cells right per bounce, so each block Q passes comes out as 3n (n even) or 3n−4 and 3n+4 alternately (n odd); (11 43) becomes (29 133), then (83 403). The walk ends when the first mover reaches the right end: Q is left behind as a scar 00 or 11 and a new Q starts near the right end. A later Q turns a 00 scar into a mover and stops at a 11 scar. Then the head returns to the left end and a new drain begins.

The collisions seen so far

A halt can only come from a collision run cell by cell: inside the mover lemmas F always reads the first cell of a gap or the 0 that starts the next mover. To 1013 steps there are 57 different collision runs, all made of a few pieces: the left sweep crossing a gap or a mover, the right sweep passing a mover (E1 D1 A0 B1 F0) or turning at one (E1 D1 A0 B0, D1 A1), and the turns at Q and at the two ends. In all of them F reads a 0. The closest call is at the right end: on a swept mover with a gap of one pair the head reads E1 D1 A0 B1, and F reads the blank only because that mover is the last one; a one-pair gap anywhere earlier in that list would halt. In every list seen the earlier gaps are at least three pairs.

README, section Movers

A rebuild triples gaps, gap by gap

On a stack of single cells, runs of pairs and blocks of movers (exact against direct simulation to 1.1×1011 steps), a rebuild copies the gaps of one block into the next by a three-state transducer: an even gap g gives 3g+4, odd gaps give 3g+8 and 3g by turns. When the fuel runs out in the middle of a gap the transfer starts again on the rest of it or nests one level deeper, and an inner level that has copied its last gap un-nests; from one block the next copy starts by the same rule as a nesting. To 1.88×1012 steps all 16,270 events checked are as predicted; 5 level changes at the reservoir and 2 boundary cases are not covered. The stretch is the kernel lemma walkm; the rest is not composed into a theorem. A finite abstraction of the stack does not close.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The machine halts only when F reads a 1, and that happens only as D> 1 0 1 1 (see below). Every bounce is a proved mover lemma except the cells where the head turns, and every collision seen to 1.6×1018 steps at the ends of the movers is a lemma for all values of its gaps, none halting; an exact simulator finds no halt to 1.5×1018. But new shapes still turn up (two after 1015 steps), and no invariant yet rules out one that halts. No regular invariant was found: CTL by SAT with up to 16×16 and 20×12 states, NGramCPS (widths 2 to 14, history, LRU), RepWL and a CPS over runs of alternating cells all fail, each because a window forgets how far away the left end of the tape is.
