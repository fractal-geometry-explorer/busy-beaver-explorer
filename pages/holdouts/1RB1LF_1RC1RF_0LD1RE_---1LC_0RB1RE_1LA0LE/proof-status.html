What is known about 1RB1LF_1RC1RF_0LD1RE_---1LC_0RB1RE_1LA0LE

1RB1LF_1RC1RF_0LD1RE_---1LC_0RB1RE_1LA0LE

Open: a cryptidIts halting is thought to come down to a Collatz-like question that no known method decides (the community's label, not a result).

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape. Its right end is a pool of 1s that pays for a doubling block of pairs. The complete map from one right-end visit to the next is proved in Lean: for an even pool it is a doubling map, u ↦ 2u modulo v, whose modulus v grows by one at every doubling that does not wrap. The machine can halt only when a doubling lands exactly on v at one of the special moduli 2k+1 − k.

Status as of 17 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. At every right-end visit in state B the tape is T(c, a, b) = [1^2 0 (10)^c] 1^a 0 (10)^b B>0. With an even pool a, and u = b + 2, v = a/2 + b + 3, the next visit is T(c, a − 2b − 2, 2b + 2) when 2u < v (u doubles, v grows by one); when 2u > v a carry replaces u by 2u − v; when 2u = v the pool becomes odd: T(c, 2v − 3, 0). An odd pool is doubled down until it cannot pay, then leaves a bracket behind or swallows the one that is there. The only halting visits are T(none, 1, b), reached exactly from odd pools A = 2k+2 − 2k − 3 without a bracket. Both layers are theorems checked by Lean's kernel: the machine halts iff this right-end map reaches T(none, 1, b), and, one level up, iff the doubling map in (u, v), odd pools and brackets included, reaches an odd pool A = 2k+2 − 2k − 3 without a bracket.

How far it has been followed. The exact map (c/fast.c) was run for 1011 operations: 50,000,005,600 right-end visits, of the order of 1031 machine steps, 35 odd pools, 18 of them without a bracket, none of them a halting one (the closest: 989 against 1005). A later halt needs an exact hit at a modulus beyond 237; heuristically that has probability about 10−11. Halting configurations of the same form exist (T(none, 12, 19) halts after five visits), so there is no parity or congruence argument; the map is a chaotic doubling map and no proof is in sight.

Other proofs and outside reports

Independently formalized here and in DeepMind cryptids

Both this repository and DeepMind's cryptids repository prove in Lean that the machine halts from blank tape if and only if an explicit map on a few numbers reaches its halting case. The two proofs were written separately, with different variables and file layouts: theirs is M_1RB1LF_1RC1RF_0LD1RE_HHH1LC_0RB1RE_1LA0LE.lean in google-deepmind/cryptids (commit 3647718, 12 November 2026), and it was not compiled here.

Community label

In the community's BB(6) tracker the machine's class is Cubic Mess. Its 3 November 2026 edition lists the machine as a cryptid: its halting is thought to come down to a Collatz-like question that no known method decides. These are labels read off the run by others, not results.

It halts iff the right-end map reaches T(none, 1, b)

The map F on nodes (the visits T(c, a, b), and the fresh sweep that a pool of one starts at w = 1) is proved move by move on the tape: rounds, the end of a sweep, carries, the even and odd events, the bracket an odd pool leaves behind, the merge of a pool of three into the bracket, each with its short cases. Then: the machine halts from blank tape iff the orbit of T(none, 2, 0) (step 7) under F reaches a visit T(none, 1, b). Evaluated: the first 100 nodes (kernel) and 104 (native_decide) do not halt.

halts_iff, halts_iff_T1, F_sem, orbit_kernel, orbit_native, lean/Map.lean

It halts iff the doubling map reaches a halting pool

A layer above F, by arithmetic alone. The map op works on even states (c, u, v) and odd pools (c, A, b). Even: 2u < v gives (2u, v + 1), 2u > v gives (2u − v, v), 2u = v gives the odd pool (c, 2v − 3, 0). Odd, A = 2i + 3: for b < i the pair block doubles, (A − 2b − 2, 2b + 2); for b = i the pool comes down to 1, which halts without a bracket and merges with a bracket c into (c + 2b + 5, c + 2b + 7); for b = i + r a pool without bracket leaves the bracket A + 1 behind and restarts at (r − 2, r) (r ≥ 3), or goes to (A + 3, A + 5) (r = 2) or the odd pool 2A + 5 (r = 1); with a bracket c it merges into (r − 1, c + i + b + 6) (r ≥ 2) or the odd pool 2c + 2A + 5 (r = 1). Proved: every node of F is a state of op, every move of F is one or more operations, and the machine halts from blank tape iff op from (none, 2, 4) (the visit at step 7) halts, iff its orbit reaches an odd pool without bracket A = 2k+2 − 2k − 3. Such a pool comes from an event 2u = v without bracket at v = 2k+1 − k, or from a merge that ends as the tail runs out (the pool 2c + 2A + 5). The third kind of odd pool without bracket, an odd pool that overflows by one (2A + 5), is always 2j+3 − 1 and never halts, so these two are the only ways to halt. The first 104 operations (kernel) and 107 (native_decide) do not halt.

The building blocks of the map, for all parameters

Each move of the map between two visits is a Lean theorem about every value of its counts and every tape in its context: the start of a sweep, a round (two 1s of the pool and one pair of the tail become four 1s of the middle block), the end of a sweep (the middle block becomes the doubled pair block), the carry (the middle block becomes the pool), the two events where pool and tail run out together (one makes the pool odd), the birth of a bracket and its merge. lean/Map.lean assembles them into the map F. Fifteen more rules in the forms the assembly uses (pools of one, two and three, the short tails, the bracket and merge cases) are generated the same way.

start, round, round_last, round_w1, final, carry, event_odd, event_even, bracket, merge, unwind, lean/Rules.lean (generated by py/rules.py)

Even pools: doubling modulo a growing modulus

For even a, with u = b + 2 and v = a/2 + b + 3: 2u < v gives a visit with (2u, v + 1); 2u > v is a carry to (2u − v, v); 2u = v gives T(c, 2v − 3, 0). Inside a sweep the state is unchanged by a round and moved by one operation at a carry. Now a theorem (it was checked against step for 7,080 configurations).

ev_sweep, ev_end, lean/Doubling.lean; py/model.py check

Which odd pools halt

An odd pool without bracket T(none, A, 0) doubles its pair block (0, 2, 6, 14, …) and comes down to exactly 1 iff A = 2k+2 − 2k − 3 (1, 3, 9, 23, 53, 115, …); for a pool born at 2u = v (A = 2v − 3) that means v = 2k+1 − k. Now a theorem; it was checked against step for all odd A < 400.

Its mirror partner: halting together is not decided by the tables

Mirrored, the table of 1RB1RF_1LC1LF_0RE1LD_0LB1LD_---1RC_1RA0RD differs from this one only in the direction of A0, and its pass starts are the mirror images of this machine's right-end visits without a bracket. The two maps agree while the pool is even and part at odd pools, where this machine leaves a bracket. Lean shows tapes on which one machine halts while the other rejoins its own run from blank tape, so the two halt together only if their particular runs from blank tape say so.

heuristic Evidence only

A later halt is very unlikely

The map spends about two operations at each modulus v and each hits 2u = v with chance about 1/v, so the target v = 2k+1 − k is hit with chance about 2−k; the targets still ahead start at k = 36, about 10−11 in total. The modulus is not monotone (merges and new brackets move it), so this is an estimate.

Why no congruence helps

With D = v − u, an event at modulus v needs D = v/2i on arrival with 2i dividing v, so every event after the first has 4 | v, and a target v = 2k+1 − k needs i ≤ ν2(k). Backwards a state (D, v) has two predecessors, one or none according to the parities of D and v, one on average: a critical branching process, so the states that reach a target thin out like 1/v without any residue class being excluded.

Does an event ever fall on a target modulus?

Halting configurations of the form T exist and are reachable by the map (T(none, 12, 19) halts), and events with v ≡ 0 mod 4 leave one target in four possible, so no congruence excludes a halt. Deciding the machine means deciding a chaotic doubling map with a moving modulus.
