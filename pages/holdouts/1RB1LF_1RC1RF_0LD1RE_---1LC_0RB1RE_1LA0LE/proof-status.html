What is known about 1RB1LF_1RC1RF_0LD1RE_---1LC_0RB1RE_1LA0LE

1RB1LF_1RC1RF_0LD1RE_---1LC_0RB1RE_1LA0LE

Open: a cryptidIts halting is thought to come down to a Collatz-like question that no known method decides (the community's label, not a result).

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape. Its right end is a doubling bouncer: a block of 10 pairs doubles at every sweep, paid for from a pool of 1s, and the tape is rewritten each time the pool runs out.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. At every right-end visit in state B the tape is [1^2 0 (10)^c] 1^a 0 (10)^b B>0. While a ≥ 2b + 3 the next visit is (a − 2b − 2, 2b + 2); otherwise the pool overflows and the tape is rewritten. Of all such configurations only the ones without the bracket and with a = 1 halt.

Why it has not halted. The doubling step keeps the parity of a, and a is even most of the time: in the first 109 steps there are 1,453 such visits and only 48 have a odd, in 7 short odd phases (pools 5, 61, 165, 165, 989, 1,853, 189 at their start). An odd phase that starts at (A, 0) halts only if A − 1 is one of the sparse values 2k+2 − 2k − 4, so the danger shrinks as the tape grows; a halt is possible but not expected.

heuristic Evidence only

A halt needs an exact hit

Doubling from (A, 0) gives a = A − (2k+2 − 2k − 4) after k steps, so an odd phase of this kind halts only if A is 3, 9, 23, 53, 115, 241, 495, 1005, …, about log2 A values out of A. Odd phases are rare and their pools grow with the tape, so the chance that some future phase hits shrinks; nothing rules it out.

The overflow rule

The exceptional steps (a < 2b + 3) are not yet written as a formula; a complete macro machine would turn the parity bookkeeping into a proof strategy or find the halt.
