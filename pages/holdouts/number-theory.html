Which Collatz-like holdouts could be solved?

For each of these 40 machines Lean already proves "the machine halts if and only if this map does something". What is left is a question about the map: will an orbit ever land on a sparse set, or a counter ever hit zero? This page ranks the 40 by how likely a proof is and shows what was tried. The ranking is a judgement, not a result. For the 39 below the top one, the open-problems page writes out the exact question each reduces to and the known problem it resembles.

None is decided. One machine stands out, and two smaller results are new:

  • computed 1RB1LF_1LC1RD_1RA1LA_0RA0RE_0RC---_1RF0LC is the best candidate. Its wall changes only through the parity of N, so a closed set of walls would prove non-halting with no number theory. Under every parity sequence, the stack of counters never goes negative (29,195 walls to 30,000 cells). What is left is a 2-adic coincidence that the reachable walls get at most 10% of the way towards.
  • kernel Three machines iterate one function, f(n) = 3n − 10·2k + 3k + 14. Lean now proves the closed form for every n in all three (lean/Closed.lean).
  • computed The subtract-and-multiply maps have no residue invariant modulo any 2a3b up to 7,776.

The ranking

  • A a route with no number theory may exist (1)
  • B a simple invariant (parity, residue) is plausible (2)
  • C Collatz-type; a Lean step that sharpens the question is open or just done (14)
  • D Collatz-type; known methods shown not to work (10)
  • E Antihydra type, or not fully reduced (5)
  • F probably halts, out of reach of computation (8)

The best candidate: a stack of counters under free parities

For 1RB1LF_1LC1RD_1RA1LA_0RA0RE_0RC---_1RF0LC, each step starts at W A> 0 bN 1 (b = 110), and the next wall W′ depends only on W and N mod 2. Every wall seen is 1 followed by counters: open K(k) = bk 13 b 1 or closed K′(k) = bk 16. A step sends the parity into the top counter. An open counter closes on an even parity and grows by about 3/2 on an odd one. When it grows, it passes its own parity down and pays for the counter below. Choose the parities yourself below and try to drive a count below zero. That would need the counter above to stay odd for about as many steps as its own value.

Three machines, one function

With hk = 5·2k − k − 6 (3, 12, 31, 70, 149, …), set f(n) = 3n − 10·2k + 3k + 14 for hk < n < hk+1, and let f halt at n = hk. Then 1RB0LD_1LC0RA_1RA1LB_1LA1LE_1RF0LC_---0RE is the orbit of 4, 1RB1LC_1RC0LD_1LA0RB_1LB1LE_1RF0LA_---0RE the orbit of 10 (in its coordinate N = 2n + 2), and 1RB1RE_1LC0RA_1RD0LB_1LB1RC_1LF0RD_---0LE the orbit of 1. Each machine halts if and only if its orbit lands on some hk. The three orbits share no value in their first 20,000 terms.
