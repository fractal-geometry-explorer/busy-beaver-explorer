What is known about 1RB1LC_1LA---_1LD0LA_1RE0LE_0RF1RE_1LB1RD

1RB1LC_1LA---_1LD0LA_1RE0LE_0RF1RE_1LB1RD

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The right end of the tape is ... 1 0 1^P 0 (10)^Q 1^5. Lean proves the round (P, Q) → (P − 2, Q + 2) for every P ≥ 7, even Q ≥ 2 and every tape to the left, and k rounds by induction. When the ones are used up the phase end walks into the rest of the tape, which is an irregular mixture of short words that grows with every phase, and starts the next phase: often with P = 9, sometimes with P' = 2P + 11, and then again and again (a chain 37, 85, 181, ..., 3,145,717 of 16 doublings). 156 phases, more than 3·1013 steps, have been followed exactly without a halt. What decides the next phase: a phase end either reads at most a few dozen cells next to the block and gives P′ = 2P + c (the doublings), or crosses the whole tape left of the block and restarts with P′ in {9, 29, 41, 65, 169}; the long ends rewrite the left tape, and nothing regular has been found in what they leave.

156 phases without a halt

py/phases.py runs the exact accelerated simulator py/tsim.py and skips the rounds of each phase by the Lean rule (only for even Q; the few phases with odd Q, where the rule does not apply and the run does something else, are simulated cell by cell). In 156 phases there is no halt; the largest phase has P = 3,145,717 and alone costs about 3.5·1013 steps.

python3 py/phases.py 156 (about 10 minutes)

Doubling chains

The phase starts have P = 9 in 69 of the 156 phases; otherwise P' = 2P + 11 follows a phase that ended next to the right words, giving chains such as 37, 85, 181, 373, ..., 3,145,717 (16 doublings), after which P drops back to a small value.

py/phases.py

What decides doubling or reset: how far the phase end reads

For each of the first 36 phases the tape after the rounds was copied and the phase end run on it alone, recording how far left of the block the head goes. There are two kinds. Short ends read at most 56 cells and give P′ = 2P + c, with c set by those cells: the context 1110 11111 0 1010 (14 cells) gives c = 11 and leaves the same 14 cells again, which is why the doublings 37, 85, 181, ... repeat; other contexts give c = 27, 15, 19, 5, −11, 31, 19. Long ends cross the whole tape left of the block and run into the blank tape beyond (24 of the 36), and then P′ is 9, 29, 41, 65 or 169, whatever P was. So a phase doubles exactly when the few cells next to the block allow a short end; the long ends rewrite the whole left tape and are where its irregular words come from.

Why it is hard

What the next phase does depends on the cells next to the block (a short end, a doubling or near-doubling) or on the whole tape left of it (a long end, which crosses all of it and restarts with a small P). The long ends rewrite the left tape each time; no counter or regular structure has been found in what they leave.
