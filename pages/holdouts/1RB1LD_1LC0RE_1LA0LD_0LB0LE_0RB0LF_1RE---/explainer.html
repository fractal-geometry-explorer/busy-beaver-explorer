Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1LD_1LC0RE_1LA0LD_0LB0LE_0RB0LF_1RE---

Nobody knows. This machine runs a countdown on a list of blocks. Each block is a run of the word 1000, and the short words between blocks act like the digits of a base-3 counter, passing borrows to the left. A block at the right end grows every tick and later becomes a new block. This page runs the real machine in your browser and explains the countdown. The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from E1 (writes 0, moves L): 1 E>1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.511).

After 109 steps the tape spans 96,734 cells (131 left of the start, 73,502 right), with a density of 1s of 0.37.

The pieces

Blocks, separators and a timer

Look at the tape when the head turns at the right end, in state E. Reading from the left, it is a short prefix and then a list of blocks (1000)^x1 s1 (1000)^x2 s2 … (1000)^xk. Then come the marker 10100, the head, and the block (1110)^y followed by 111111.

Most separators s are one of three words: c = 101010, 10 and 00. The last block, xk, is the timer. The block (1110)^y at the right end only grows. It is the same kind of block, written as 1110 because it lies to the right of the head.

A tick is three round trips of the head to the right end, which add 4 to y. What else changes depends on the separator in front of the timer:

separator before the timerafter the tickLean
cbecomes 10; timer unchangedtick_c
10becomes 00; timer −1tick_10
00timer −1, then a borrow runs lefttick_00

The borrow moves left one block at a time. At a 00 it takes one unit from the block on the left and keeps going (pass_00). The 00 it passed comes back as c. At a c it stops and the c becomes 10 (stop_c). At a 10 it stops and the 10 becomes 00 (stop_10). The head then sweeps back to the right end, rewriting each block it passes (sweep, arrive).

The emergent behaviour

A base-3 countdown

Put each block's length and the separator on its left into one number: N = 3x + e, where e = 2, 0, 1 for c, 10, 00. Then every move above is one rule. A borrow lowers N by 2, and it moves on to the next block on the left exactly when N mod 3 goes from 1 to 2.

Each tick sends one borrow into the timer. The timer passes every third borrow to its left neighbour, that block passes every third of those, and so on. Block i, counted back from the timer, loses about (2/3)·3-i units per tick, while y grows by 4. Here are 16 ticks from step 120,317,220. The timer runs 116 → 106, its separator cycles c → 10 → 00, and the block on its left loses one unit every three ticks:

The whole run

Walls, overflows and an exact map

Read the tape left of the marker 10100 from the head outwards, so that the timer comes first. A block is then 0001, and the separators c, 10, 00 are 010101, 01, 00. Each tick is a rewrite at the separator after the timer: a separator starting with 01 loses a 0101 (c → 10 → 00), and 00 becomes c and passes the borrow on. The three other separators 1 → 01101 → 00101 → 1 form a wall: every three borrows they take two units from the block before and one from the block after, and pass nothing on. A tick costs 30y + 5j/2 + 210 + 13m + (0, 12 or 6) steps.

The events are the overflows. If the block next to the timer empties (O1) or the timer itself empties (O2), the growing block becomes the new timer with length y+2, y restarts at 8, and a wall is left behind. If every separator is 00, the borrow passes every block and the far end (O3), and each block loses one. These formulas hold at every overflow the run reaches. Events at the far end are computed by running the machine on a copy of the tape with every block cut to 40 copies. Each is computed with two different cuts, and the two results must agree.

Against the real machine, this map reproduces all 127,803 tick boundaries of the first 1011 steps exactly, including the step counts. Run on its own, it reaches 1.6·1018 steps without a halting event.

The countdown rule matches the run

Write N = 3x + e for each block after the first, where x is its length and e is 2, 0 or 1 when the separator on its left is c, 10 or 00. Each tick sends a borrow into the timer. An exact map reproduces the run

Read the tape left of the marker 10100 from the head outwards. The run is then a map on (y, word): the ticks as rewrites of cells, and events (the timer or the block next to it running out, a borrow at the far end), which are computed by running the machine on a copy with every block cut to 40 copies and lifted back. Each event is computed with two cuts, which must agree. In the first 1011 steps the map reproduces all 127,803 tick boundaries of the machine exactly: y, the word and the step count. 185 of these moves are events.

The overflow formulas

Three kinds of overflow follow exact formulas at every instance the map meets. O1, the block next to the timer empties: (y; x1, x2, …) → (8; y+2, x1−1, x2−1, …), 15 times. O2, the timer empties: (y; 0, x1, …) → (8; y+2, x1−1, …), 27 times. O3, every separator is 00 and a borrow passes the far end: every block loses one and y+2 is the new timer, 35 times. O1 and O2 leave the separators 10110 and 1 behind. These form a wall that passes no borrow until it wears away.

Heuristic
holdouts/1RB1LD_1LC0RE_1LA0LD_0LB0LE_0RB0LF_1RE---/py/farend.py
The far end cannot halt while the last block lasts

A borrow arriving at the far end halts the machine when the far end (the left end of the tape) reads 111 or one of 23 other words of up to 7 cells. The 119 far ends seen in 1011 steps are closed under borrow arrivals, and none of them halts. This assumes that a far end behaves the same behind any block of 10 or more copies.

Heuristic
Why it is hard

Block i, counted back from the timer, loses about (2/3)·3-i per tick, and walls hold borrows back, so the far end is reached exponentially rarely. Halting is only possible at an event, once the far end has changed shape.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The open part is what the list and its walls look like once the last blocks have worn away, and whether a far end like 111 can then appear.
