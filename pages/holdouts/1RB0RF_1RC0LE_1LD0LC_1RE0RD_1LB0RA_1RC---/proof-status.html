What is known about 1RB0RF_1RC0LE_1LD0LC_1RE0RD_1LB0RA_1RC---

1RB0RF_1RC0LE_1LD0LC_1RE0RD_1LB0RA_1RC---

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 13 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. When the head steps off the left end the tape is 1 000 (10)^3 010 (01)^5 (10)^N followed by an irregular part made mostly of runs of 01 and 10 with single extra 0s or 1s between them. The sea (10)^N gains 3 copies per left turn (N = 3,696 after 1,241 left turns, 109 steps). The irregular part is not a bounded zone: it has 861 cells at 2.7·106 steps, 4,092 at 6.4·107 and 15,560 at 109. So the closed-set method (a bounded engine between two periodic runs) does not apply as it stands.

Lean proves (kernel) that a halt can only come from state A on a 1 with a 1 to its right, and that the machine never halts if that never appears. The C simulator sees no halt in 5·1010 steps. Unlike its group mate 1RB1RA_0LC0RA…, this machine is fragile: of 3,000 copies of its own tapes with a small change, 260 halt (83 of 600 with one cell flipped).

Other proofs and outside reports

Community label

In the community's BB(6) tracker the machine's class is Unknown. These are labels read off the run by others, not results.

How it works

At the left end the tape has a sea (10)^N, N growing by 3 per left turn; right of it an irregular part of runs of 01 and 10 that the head zigzags through and rewrites. The irregular part grows too (15,560 cells at 109 steps).

A halt needs state A on a 1 with a 1 to its right (proved in Lean). The run never shows it, but tapes one flipped cell away from the real ones halt in 14% of trials, so only the exact structure of the real tapes can explain the non-halting, and that structure is not yet known.

Where a halt can come from

If the machine halts on its third step from a configuration, that configuration is one of: E with 1 under the head, 1 right, 1 two right. So a run can reach a halt only through one of these patterns, and a proof that the machine never halts only has to rule them out. The proof splits on the state and the five cells around the head.

The sweep rules

Shift rules of the zigzags, for every repeat count and any tape around: going left C carries 10 and turns each 10 into 01, and each 101 into 001; going right D turns each 010101 into 110100; going left B, carrying 01, turns each 1011010 into 0100101.

l_sea, l_101, r_word, l_word, lean/Rules.lean (generated by py/rules.py)

A halt comes from exactly one pattern

One step before any halt the machine is in state A on a 1 with a 1 to its right. If the run from blank tape never shows A>1 1 the machine never halts; and from that pattern it does halt.

holdouts/triage.json

Sea and irregular part, both growing

At the left-end events the tape is 1 000 (10)^3 010 (01)^5 (10)^N and an irregular rest. Left turn 31 (3.8·105 steps): N = 66, rest 375 cells. Turn 151 (1.7·107): N = 426, rest 2,275. Turn 601 (2.6·108): N = 1,776, rest 8,433. Turn 1,241 (109): N = 3,696, rest 15,560. So N grows by 3 per left turn and the rest by about 12.5 cells per left turn. (The earlier figure of a middle zone of about 470 cells was one moment, 2.9·106 steps.) In the rest, runs (01)^k between single 0s mostly have k divisible by 3.

The halt needs A>1 1

The only halting transition F1 is reached only from A>1 1. In the first 109 steps A reads a 1 only 388,832 times (far less than the other machines of the group), never with a 1 on its right; in the first 3·107 steps nearly all of these are in the middle of the tape.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The irregular part is where the work and any halt happen; it grows without bound and its rules are not known beyond the four sweep rules. Since neighbouring tapes halt, the next step is to find what the real tapes have in common (for example the run lengths divisible by 3).
