OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Both ends of the tape grow logarithmically. The left end is a long run of 10 after a few 0s (24 copies at step 556,283,168, 25 at step 1,678,517,170). The right end is a run of 110 (15 copies at step 1.8·109). In between lies a word of short digits (10, 110, 1110) in no fixed order that changes at each pass, like the digits of a counter, and decides when the head next reaches the left end. It is the same kind of machine as 1RB---_1LC1RF_0LE0RD_0RB0LC_1RD0LD_0RC0RA. No exact rule for the middle has been found.

Status as of 14 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The machine can halt only from 1 C> 1 1 (both directions proved in Lean); in 20,000,000,000 steps that never happens. In the first 109 steps C had 1 C> 1 0 about 2.9·107 times and 0 C> 1 1 about 8.8·107 times, but never 1 C> 1 1: two of the three cells match all the time, the third never.

New, 14 November 2026. The whole run is now one rule: the tape is a binary counter with doublers (words 1010, 000, 100), and each round sends one defect through it. A tape with one defect cannot halt; a halt needs two defects to meet. In 100,000 rounds there was no halt, and in the 4,500 rounds followed word by word every defect disappeared long before the next one started. Whether that is always so is the open question.

The halting context

From 1 C> 1 1 the machine reaches its halting transition (proved step by step, then step_none_iff). The halting transition is F reading a 1. F is entered from A reading a 1 (moving left) or E reading a 0 (moving right). A is entered only from C reading a 0, which writes a 0 to A's left, so the first way is impossible. E is entered from D reading a 0 (moving left, and then F reads the 0 D wrote) or a 1 (moving right), so the halt needs D> 1 0 1; D is entered only from C reading a 1 and moving left, which gives 1 C> 1 1. Lean proves the steps from each context to the halt (halt_ctx, halt_ctx_A, halt_ctx_E). The argument backwards is checked in Lean too, by enumerating the cells the head reads: a configuration halts three steps later if and only if it is 1 C> 1 1 (halt_iff_3; one and two steps before a halt: halt_pred_1, halt_pred_2), so a halting run from blank passes through it (halts_blank_pred_3).

A counter with doublers and one travelling defect

In the words L = 1010, O = 000, I = 100 the function A is a binary counter (O, I the digits) in which every carry crossing an L becomes two. Each round adds one defect (a 10, 1 or 0 that does not fit the words), which travels through the counter, rewrites it, and disappears where it turns 1 I O into O L. Worked out by hand from the four functions: a tape with one defect cannot halt; a halt needs two defects side by side. The token rules reproduce the exact tape cell for cell (rounds 5 to 24), and in every round from 13 to 4,500 the defect disappeared about 40% of the way into the tape, long before the round ended.

A conserved value

For the twin 1RB0LE_0LC0RA_1LE0RD_0RB---_0LF0LD_1LA0LF, whose round words this machine carries: a value of the tape (each digit 1 counts 2-b, b = doublers minus digits before it, with corrections for the defect) grows by exactly 1 with every visit of A, with or without a defect: worked out by hand, checked with exact fractions on every round. At the start of each round it is 1/15 up to about 2-k/2: the far end of the tape is the binary expansion of 1/15, I O O O repeated k/8 times.

open Open

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. Halting needs C on a 1 with 1s on both sides, which the words of the tape have never offered; whether they never will depends on the counter in the middle, whose rule is not found.

Does every defect disappear in time?

If the defect of every round disappears before the round ends, the machine never halts. Where it disappears depends on the digits of the counter, which look irregular; no proof.

The one missing lemma

With the conserved value, the defect of a round must disappear if the tape ends in a few periods I O O O and every digit before them is reached by many visits (b larger than a few times log2 k). Measured, b is at least 2m - 5 in the part rich in doublers; a proof of a lower bound is missing. It would show that the machine never halts.
