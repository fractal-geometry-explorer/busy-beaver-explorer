Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LD_1RC1RE_1LA0LD_1LB1LC_0RA1RF_1RA---

Nobody knows. This machine builds an odometer out of blocks of 1 0 1 0 …: every sweep of the head carries a signal to the right that skips blocks of odd length and shortens the block after the first even one, like the carry of a binary counter. We have an exact description of every sweep, and ran it far beyond direct simulation, but the far end of the tape keeps a few small blocks from its first moments, and whether they ever line up into the one pattern that halts is unknown.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from E1 (writes 1, moves R): E>1 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.477). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

After 109 steps the tape spans 81,747 cells (60,778 left of the start, 56 right), with a density of 1s of 0.551.

Structure

What the tape is made of

Look at the tape each time the head, in state D, reaches a new cell on the left: it is a list of items, each a block of alternating cells (1 0)^n followed by one separator cell, 0 or 1:

D> 0 (10)^n0 s0 (10)^n1 s1 … (10)^nk sk with s0 = 0 and sk = 1

Write the list as pairs (n, s). After the first few hundred steps there are 10 to 35 items, most with separator 0; the first items are long (billions of pairs after 1010 sweeps), the last few are short leftovers from the start of the run.

Mechanism

One sweep is a carry

The head runs right, turning each block it crosses into solid 1s, and back left, turning them back. What it changes is decided by a signal it carries, set by parities. Leaving item 0 the signal is X (act) if n0 is even and Y (pass) if it is odd. A Y crossing an item with separator 0 leaves it alone and becomes X if that item's length is even, Y if odd. An X shortens the item it reaches by one pair and the sweep ends. Every sweep also adds 3 pairs to item 0, which flips its parity.

Read the parities bi = ni mod 2 as binary digits: the sweep skips the run of 1-digits at the front and shortens the item just after the first 0-digit, exactly where the carry of a binary counter would stop. So item k is touched about once every 2k sweeps. Items with separator 1 (fresh ones) are handled slightly differently: a Y passing one sets its separator to 0, taking a pair from the next item if its length was even.

The step count of a sweep that acts on item d is exactly 12 + 3d + 3·Σj<d(2nj+1), plus 2 or 4 for each separator-1 item crossed.

The arithmetic

When an item runs out

sweepwhenwhat happens
resetX reaches an empty item (0, 0)everything before it shifts one place right with separator 1, a new item (2, 0) appears in front, the item before the empty one gains 1, the empty one disappears
mergeX reaches an empty (0, 1) inside the list(p, 0) (0, 1) (m, s) becomes (p + m, s)
end decrementX reaches an empty (0, 1) at the end(p, 0) (0, 1) becomes (p − 1, 1): the tape's right end moves left
end resetY reaches the last item (m, 1)like a reset of the whole list; the last item becomes (m + 2, 1), or (m + 1, 1) (1, 1) if m is even

So the list behaves like an odometer whose digits have unbounded size: the front item counts sweeps, an exhausted item pushes the front item into the list, and the list grows by one item now and then (about logarithmically).

The question

Where it could halt

The halting transition is F reading 1. It is reached when a Y signal meets an even item with separator 1 whose successor is an empty item with separator 1 followed by a 1 (halt_case). More generally, any sweep outside the rules above is suspect; the item simulator stops on such a sweep, and none has occurred in 5·1010 sweeps. Separator-1 items created by resets are odd and are turned into separator-0 items by the next passes, so small separator-1 items mostly live at the far end: leftovers like (175, 1) (69, 1) (7, 1) (1, 1) that the carry reaches only once every 230 sweeps or so, and that the end resets keep feeding with new (1, 1) items. Whether they ever form the halting pattern is the open question.

Status

What is proved

In Lean (kernel), for all block lengths: the sweeps that act on items 1, 2 and 3 and the reset at item 1 (lean/Rules.lean), and that the halting pattern halts (lean/Halt.lean). The general sweep for any depth, and an invariant of the item lists that would exclude the halting pattern forever, are not proved; the item model is a trusted computation checked against direct simulation.

halt_case, lean/Halt.lean
Where a halt comes from

A sweep whose signal passes an odd item 0 and meets an even item with separator 1, followed by an empty item with separator 1 and a further 1, halts (F reads a 1).

The item model is exact

Heuristic
A counter that slowly eats its tail

Item k is decremented about once every 2k sweeps, so the small items left at the far end from the first few hundred steps change only every 230 sweeps or so. A halt needs a specific small pattern there; nothing seen so far rules it out or makes it likely.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. A proof of non-halting would need an invariant of the item lists that is closed under the sweep rules and excludes the unmodelled sweeps, and a Lean proof of the general sweep (any depth).
