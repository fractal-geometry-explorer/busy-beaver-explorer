What is known about 1RB0RC_1RC1LB_1LD1RA_1LE0LB_---1LF_0LF0LC

1RB0RC_1RC1LB_1LD1RA_1LE0LB_---1LF_0LF0LC

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open, but it is reduced to the left end. Lean proves from a closed tape language (windows of 4 cells) that any halt from blank tape happens with blank tape to the left of the head. By hand and in simulation, the only way there is F reaching the leftmost written cell, which it has never done in 1010 steps (it came to the second cell 4 times and read a 1 each time).

The list simulator of the shared library, proved to agree with the machine's semantics, runs 10,000 steps from blank tape without a halt, evaluated by Lean's kernel.

Every halt is at the left edge

In the run from blank tape, any configuration where the machine halts is in state E reading a 0 with only blank cells to the left of the head. Proved from a closed tape language with windows of 4 cells (472 abstract configurations), whose only halting configurations have a left state that accepts the blank stream alone; halt_left_of is the left-hand mirror of TM.CTL.halt_tail_blank, proved in this folder.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.026). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.516).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

Only F on the leftmost cell can halt (by hand)

An argument by hand, not checked by machine: A is entered only by moving right, so it never stands on the leftmost cell; the 0-writers that do (D on a 1, F) step off it at once, so the leftmost cell holds 1 while the head is to its right. With the Lean result, a halt needs E on a 0 beyond that cell; the one way there is F reading the leftmost 1 (F1 = 0LC), after which C, D and E step onto blank cells and E halts. F on the second cell reading a 0 leads to the same.

the transition table

Layers that grow about 2.8 times per epoch

At the left-end records, with t = 1110 and u = 111100, the tape is a short left prefix and then layers of periodic words, the outer one with period u t^15 u t and an inner one with period u t u t^2 u u t^4. Their repeat counts at the records from step 225,725 on: 2, 5, 15, 40, 114 and 6, 19, 56, 159, 446; width 597, 1533, 4173, 11509, 31981 (ratio about 2.76); the time between records grows about 7.7 times.

a Python run of bbtools.machine.records

Proposed family: F01, periodic blocks driven by a bounded engine

With repeats of single words only the tape looks irregular (33,389 level-1 tokens for 186,485 cells at 1.6·1010 steps), but the words repeat in groups: with groups counted once the description is 24 to 72 level-2 tokens at every record from 2.6·108 to 1.6·1010 steps. For example at the left-end record at step 5,128,930,040 the whole tape is 0 1^9 0 (0111)^25 [100 1^3 0 1^4 0 (0111)^16]^316 100 1^3 0 1^4 0 (0111)^11 100 1^3 0 1^4 0 (0111)^4 1 [0 (0111)^3 100 1^4 0 (0111)^5 100 1^3 0 1^4]^1245 00 111: two layers and about twenty short pieces. A few periodic blocks of long period (76 and 52 cells in the example; 28 cells for a third layer seen at the right-end records) and a bounded rest is the F01 shape. The README's layers that grow about 2.8 times per epoch are these blocks.

Does it halt?

Not known. What is left is whether the few cells at the left end ever let F reach the leftmost cell (or the second cell on a 0).
