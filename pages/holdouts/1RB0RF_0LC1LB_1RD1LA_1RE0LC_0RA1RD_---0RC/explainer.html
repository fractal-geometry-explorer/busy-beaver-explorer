The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from A1 (writes 0, moves R): A>1 0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The run at a glance

Space-time diagram and records

The diagram stacks the tape at many moments. Sampling at each new cell shows the shape of the run as the visited region grows; sampling evenly in time shows where the head spends its time. The records list every step at which the head reaches a cell no earlier step reached, with the side, the width so far and the whole tape: the first place to look for a repeating macro structure.

What the tape looks like

Levels of 9-, 15-, 21-cell words and a staircase

At step 4·109, left to right: 2,498 copies of 100, a run of 662 nine-cell words, a run of 15-cell words cut by the head (32 left of it, 1,592 right of it), a run of 95 21-cell words, then a staircase of blocks (111)m 011011(01)j with m growing from 3 to 22 along the tape.

The level-k word has length 6k + 3 and two forms: dense 16k+100 and sparse 011(01)3k. This is the same picture as in 1RB0RF_0LC---_1LD1LC_1RE0LA_1RA1RD_0RD0RF, though the tables differ.

The rule

Going right makes words dense, going left makes them sparse

Lean proves that D going right turns sparse words of levels 1 to 3 into dense ones, E does the same for levels 1 and 2 from another rotation, and C going left turns dense level-1 words back into sparse ones (level 2 was checked by the symbolic tool only). So the head is a bouncer that flips words as it passes; the moving boundaries between levels are not derived.

Can it stop?

Only on A> 1 0

Only F reading 0 stops the machine, and F is reached only from A reading 1 and stepping right, so it stops exactly when A stands on 1 0. That does not happen inside a run; it could happen at a boundary or in the staircase.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.517). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.112).

After 109 steps the tape spans 30,226 cells (22,819 left of the start, 495 right), with a density of 1s of 0.64.
