What is known about 1RB0LC_1LA0RD_1RA0LA_1RE1RD_1RA1RF_1LC---

1RB0LC_1LA0RD_1RA0LA_1RE1RD_1RA1RF_1LC---

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 13 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. At each new cell on the right the tape is 2 3^16 5 3 5 3^a1 5 3^a2 ... 5 3^am TAIL (lengths of runs of 1s), and the counts ai follow a closed-form law Pn → Qn → Rn → Pn+1. The law is checked against direct simulation on every event to 109 steps and against an exact accelerated simulator all the way to P14 (step 2.5×1018), where the oldest segment runs out as the law says. The same exact run then passes the collapse without halting and goes on to 2.28×1020 steps in a second regime (end counter 81), as simulations of shortened tapes had forecast. Those forecasts put the next collapse around 1046 steps. No rule for the collapses is known, so halting is undecided.

No halt in the first 2.28×1020 steps, through the first collapse

The macro simulator (blocks of 4 cells, crossing long runs of equal blocks in one go; exact, and it agrees with the direct simulator at 2×109 steps) ran 55 minutes from blank tape to step 227,980,806,246,614,777,399 without a halt. Every right-end event up to P14 lies on the law, and P14, at step 2,515,258,871,431,259,069, is exactly the predicted list: the oldest segment is down to 0 and the end counter is 10. The run then leaves the law, as predicted, passes the collapse without halting, and the next new cell on the right comes at step 16,989,897,072,297,794,501 with the end counter at 81; 4 such events are recorded after the collapse.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: exp/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.514). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.042).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

After the collapse: the next one (forecast)

The law only looks at a segment's distance from the right end, so the right end of P13 was simulated beforehand from a shortened tape with 3, 4 or 5 kept segments. That forecast said the run would pass the collapse at P14 without halting and that the end counter would jump from 10 to 81; the full exact run has now done exactly that (previous claim). The same shortened runs say that in the second regime the oldest segment (321 blocks) loses about 9 per cycle, so the next collapse would be some 36 generations later, near 1046 steps; for this machine the end counter (−2 per cycle) runs out at about the same time, so the outcome of that second collapse is unclear. The second regime has no law yet, and nothing is known about that second collapse.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. Halting needs the head in state D on a 0 with 1 1 to its right (D0 → 1RE, E1 → 1RF, F1 halts), which the law never produces. The first collapse did not produce it either (exact run). Whether a later collapse can needs a rule for the collapses, which is not known yet.
