Open: a cryptidIts halting is thought to come down to a Collatz-like question that no known method decides (the community's label, not a result).

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape. Its run is an odometer with three wheels; it can only halt at the moment the slowest wheel turns over, and only if the fastest one is at one exact position then.

Status as of 16 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Every time the head reaches a new cell on the right in state B the tape is S(c, a, b) = (10)^c 1^a 0 (10)^b 1 B>0, and a three-line rule (checked against direct simulation) gives the next one: the right block doubles while the pool can pay, a carry turns a pair of the left block into pool, and when the left block is empty the pool becomes the new left block (a reset). With u = b + 1 and v = a/2 + b + 2 this is exactly a doubling map, u ↦ 2u modulo v, whose modulus grows by one at every operation; c counts the wrap-arounds left before the next reset. The machine halts iff a reset happens with 2u = v exactly, and this equivalence is now a theorem checked by Lean's kernel, for the exact map and from blank tape, with nothing about the run assumed.

Why a halt is unlikely. The exact map run for 1011 operations (5·1010 visits, far beyond 1030 machine steps) makes 54 resets, none halting; the left block now has tens of billions of pairs. At a reset 2u − v is spread over about v/2 values and one of them halts; the moduli grow geometrically from reset to reset, so the chance of any later halt is about 10−10. That same growth makes resets logarithmically rare (reset k needs about 1.47k operations), and the doubling map has no known shortcut or invariant, so neither a much longer run nor a proof is in sight.

Other proofs and outside reports

Independently formalized here and in DeepMind cryptids

Both this repository and DeepMind's cryptids repository prove in Lean that the machine halts from blank tape if and only if an explicit map on a few numbers reaches its halting case. The two proofs were written separately, with different variables and file layouts: theirs is M_1RB1RA_0LC0RE_1LF1LD_1LA0LA_1LC1RB_HHH1LC.lean in google-deepmind/cryptids (commit 3647718, 12 November 2026), and it was not compiled here.

Community label

In the community's BB(6) tracker the machine's class is Cubic Mess. Its 3 November 2026 edition lists the machine as a cryptid: its halting is thought to come down to a Collatz-like question that no known method decides. These are labels read off the run by others, not results.

The building blocks of the odometer, for all parameters

Each move between two visits is a Lean theorem about every value of its counts and every tape in its context: the start of a sweep, a round (two 1s of the pool and one pair of the right block become four 1s of the middle block), the end of a sweep (the doubled block), the carry (a pair of the left block and the middle block become the new pool), the reset (the middle block becomes the new left block), and the boundary cases: a pool of two, a carry with two, one or no pairs of the right block left (the last is the emptying carry), a reset with one pair left.

The odometer is a doubling map

With u = b + 1 and v = a/2 + b + 2: 2u < v is a visit with (2u, v + 1); 2u > v is a carry to (2u − v, v + 1) that uses one unit of c, or with c = 0 a reset to c = 2(v − u − 1), u = 2u − v, v = u + 3; 2u = v is a carry to (1, v + 2), or with c = 0 the halt. Proved for every visit (F_op), and evaluated: no halt in the first 104 operations (kernel) and 107 (native_decide). v + c = 5 + visits + resets + emptying carries.

The odometer rule

For S(c, a, b) = 0^∞ (10)^c 1^a 0 (10)^b 1 B>0 0^∞ with a even and a ≥ 2, the next right-end visit in state B is: if 2b < a, S(c, a − 2b, 2b + 1) (the right block doubles, eating 1s); if 2b ≥ a and c ≥ 1, apply the rule to (c − 1, 2a + 4, max(b − a/2 − 1, 0)) (a carry: one 10 of the left block becomes 1s); if 2b ≥ a and c = 0, halt when b = a/2 and otherwise apply the rule to (a, 4, b − a/2 − 1) (a reset: the 1s become the new left block). Checked against direct simulation for all c < 6, even 2 ≤ a < 30, b < 34 (2,856 configurations), and on every such visit in the first 3·106 steps of the run, which first reaches S(0, 4, 1) at step 43.

heuristic Evidence only

A later halt has probability about 10−10

At a reset 2u − v is one of about v/2 values, one of which halts. The moduli at resets grow by a factor between 1 and 2 (about 1.47 on average), so the chances form a geometric series: about 6/v after a reset at v, 10−10 after reset 54. The same growth is why resets are logarithmically rare: reset k needs about 1.47k operations.

Why no invariant is in sight

Every lap starts at u = v − 3, so a lap is a function of its modulus; its pattern of wrap-arounds is the binary expansion of a real number within 1/v of u/v, and an exact hit is a long run of zeros in that expansion, to the exact integer. Backwards, a state has exactly one predecessor when v is even, and two or none (u even or odd) when v is odd: the backward tree of the halting states is a critical branching process, which is where the 1/v density comes from. Every class of (u, v, c) modulo 8 contains states that halt, so no invariant modulo a power of 2 can rule the halt out.

Does a reset ever land on 2u = v (b = a/2)?

No congruence excludes it: the halting state has predecessors under the map for every even v. Deciding the machine means deciding a chaotic doubling map with a moving modulus.
