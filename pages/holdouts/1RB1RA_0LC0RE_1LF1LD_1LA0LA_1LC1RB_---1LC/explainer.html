An odometer that might stop on one exact number

1RB1RA_0LC0RE_1LF1LD_1LA0LA_1LC1RB_---1LC

Open: a cryptidIts halting is thought to come down to a Collatz-like question that no known method decides (the community's label, not a result).

Nobody knows whether it halts. The tape is an odometer with three wheels: a fast one that doubles, a middle one that ticks down, and a slow one that resets. In numbers it doubles a counter u modulo a modulus v that grows by one each time. The machine can only halt at a reset, and only if the doubling lands exactly on v at that moment. In 54 resets that has never happened, and the target keeps getting smaller. Below, each level of that description has its own interactive picture, and at the end the whole question is one five-line definition.

Four levels

From six states to three numbers

The question "does this machine halt?" can be asked at four levels of description. Each level throws away detail the one above it keeps, and each step down is a theorem: the answer is the same at every level. The page walks down the ladder: the six-state machine on its tape, the tape at the moments it reaches new ground, the three numbers that describe those moments, and the long laps those numbers make. Level 1 to level 2

The tape at its visits

Every time the head steps onto a cell no earlier step has reached, on the right and in state B, the tape is

S(c, a, b) = 0^∞ (10)^c 1^a 0 (10)^b 1 B>0 0^∞        (a even, a ≥ 2)

a left block of c pairs 10, a pool of a ones, a single 0, a right block of b pairs, and a final 1 with the head just after it. Between two visits the head sweeps back to the left end of the right block and works its way out again. Inside a sweep the tape is

0^∞ (10)^c 1^(2p) A> 1 0 1^(4k+1) 0 (10)^z 1111 0^∞

with a middle block of 4k + 1 ones growing between the pool and what is left of the right block. Every move of the sweep is a Lean theorem for all values of its counts (lean/Rules.lean):

  • A round (round): two 1s of the pool and one pair of the right block become four 1s of the middle block. It costs 8k + 8 steps, because the head crosses the middle block, so a whole sweep costs of the order of v2 steps.
  • The end of the sweep (final_g): when the right block is used up, the middle block is rewritten as a right block twice as long, and the head reaches new ground: the next visit is S(c, a − 2b, 2b + 1).
  • A carry (carry, and carry_z2, carry_z1, carry_z0 when two, one or no pairs of the right block are left): when the pool runs out first, one pair of the left block is used up and the middle block becomes the new pool, which is why the pool jumps from a to 2a + 4.
  • A reset (reset, reset_z1): when the pool runs out and the left block is already empty, the middle block is rewritten as a new left block and a fresh pool of four 1s starts.
  • The halt (halt_R): if pool, left block and right block all run out in the same round, the head walks into the blank left part in state F and finds the undefined transition.

Level 2

Three wheels

Forget the steps and keep only the three numbers c, a, b of each visit. The sweep moves of the previous section add up to one rule for the next visit (F in lean/Map.lean, proved for every visit):

2b < a          next visit S(c, a − 2b, 2b + 1)
2b ≥ a, c ≥ 1   carry: go on with (c − 1, 2a + 4, max(b − a/2 − 1, 0))
2b ≥ a, c = 0   halt if b = a/2,
                else reset: go on with (a, 4, b − a/2 − 1)
  • Fast wheel. If the pool is big enough (2b < a), one sweep doubles the right block, b ↦ 2b + 1, paying 2b ones from the pool.
  • Middle wheel. If the pool is too small and c ≥ 1, one pair of the left block is used up: the pool becomes 2a + 4 and b drops by a/2 + 1; then the fast wheel tries again.
  • Slow wheel. If the pool is too small and the left block is empty, the whole pool becomes the new left block (c = a), a new pool of 4 starts, and b drops by a/2 + 1, unless b is exactly a/2: then the machine halts.

So the run is a sequence of resets, each one making a left block as long as the pool was. From blank tape the first left blocks are 2, 8, 4, 24, 52, 44, 120, …; after 1011 operations there have been 54 resets and the latest left block is 34,047,591,832 pairs long.

Level 3

Doubling modulo a growing number

Change variables: put u = b + 1 and v = a/2 + b + 2 (so a = 2(v − u − 1) and b = u − 1). The odometer's condition 2b < a becomes 2u < v, and its three wheels become one operation on (c, u, v) (op in lean/Doubling.lean):

2u < v          u ← 2u,  v ← v + 1                          a visit
2u > v, c ≥ 1   u ← 2u − v,  v ← v + 1,  c ← c − 1          a carry
2u = v, c ≥ 1   u ← 1,  v ← v + 2,  c ← c − 1               an emptying carry
2u > v, c = 0   c ← 2(v − u − 1),  u ← 2u − v,  v ← u + 3   a reset
2u = v, c = 0   halt

In words: double u; if that reaches v, subtract v and use up one unit of c; add one to v. So u is doubled modulo v, and the modulus creeps up by one at every operation. The left block c only counts the wrap-arounds: c of them are allowed, and the next one is a reset, which refills c with 2(v − u − 1) and drops the modulus to (2u − v) + 3. The machine halts exactly when a reset finds 2u = v, that is when u sits exactly at the middle of [0, v] at the moment the left block is empty.

The quantity that matters is the fraction x = u/v. Doubling sends x to about 2x when x < 1/2 and to about 2x − 1 when x > 1/2: the binary shift, the textbook example of chaos. The small corrections from v growing are what keep it exactly computable and unpredictable at once. One thing is conserved: c + v = 5 + visits + resets + emptying carries, so the modulus is essentially a clock that counts visits.

The strip shows where u sits in [0, v], with the middle marked; the trail is the last few positions. Step through operations and watch u jump around, wrap past the middle, and use up the left block.

Level 4

Laps, and why they get rarer

Cut the operations at the resets and call each piece a lap. A reset sets u = 2u − v and then v = u + 3, so every lap starts at u = v − 3, just below the top of the interval, and its left block c and starting modulus v satisfy c + v = V + 1, where V is the modulus at the reset that began it. A lap is therefore fixed by two numbers, its starting modulus and its allowance of wrap-arounds, and it ends at its (c + 1)-th wrap-around, which is the next reset. The machine halts iff some lap ends with an exact hit, 2u = v at that last wrap-around.

How long a lap lasts depends on how many wrap-arounds it is allowed, and that is set by where the previous reset landed: a reset that finds 2u − v small makes a long left block and a long next lap. Roughly, a lap starting at modulus v ends near v(3 − 2x) for a fraction x between 1/2 and 1, so the modulus at a reset grows by a factor between 1 and 2 from lap to lap: about 1.47 on average in theory, about 1.52 in the 54 laps of the run. This is why resets are logarithmically rare: reset k comes after about 1.5k operations, and 1011 operations reach only 54 of them.

The wrap-arounds of a lap are binary digits

Write a 1 for each wrap-around of a lap and a 0 for each plain doubling. Doubling modulo a fixed v would give exactly the binary digits of u/v; because v grows by one at every operation they are instead the digits of a nearby number x with |v0x − u0| ≤ 1 (between emptying carries). So the first log2 v digits agree with those of u0/v0 and then the two drift apart. The lap ends at its (c + 1)-th 1, and an exact hit at that point means the digits from there on start with a 1 followed by about log2 v zeros, to the exact integer. Deciding the machine is deciding where an implicitly defined binary expansion has such a pattern: a question of the same kind as whether a given number is normal, for which there is no known method.

The bottom line

The whole question in one definition

Everything above comes down to the following. There is no tape and there are no states left, only three whole numbers and five cases.

start with  c = 0,  u = 2,  v = 5

repeat:
    if 2u < v:                          u ← 2u,  v ← v + 1
    otherwise, if c > 0:
        if 2u > v:                      c ← c − 1,  u ← 2u − v,  v ← v + 1
        if 2u = v:                      c ← c − 1,  u ← 1,       v ← v + 2
    otherwise (c = 0):
        if 2u > v:                      c ← 2(v − u − 1),  u ← 2u − v,  v ← u + 3
        if 2u = v:                      stop

Kernel The machine 1RB1RA_0LC0RE_1LF1LD_1LA0LA_1LC1RB_---1LC halts from a blank tape if and only if this loop stops. (In the reset line the new v uses the new u, so v becomes 2u − v + 3 in terms of the old values.)

The same thing as a recursive definition

Write H(c, u, v) for "the loop stops when started from (c, u, v)". Then H is the predicate defined by

H(c, u, v) = H(c, 2u, v + 1)                        if 2u < v
H(c, u, v) = H(c − 1, 2u − v, v + 1)                if 2u > v and c ≥ 1
H(c, u, v) = H(c − 1, 1, v + 2)                     if 2u = v and c ≥ 1
H(c, u, v) = H(2(v − u − 1), 2u − v, 2u − v + 3)    if 2u > v and c = 0
H(c, u, v) = true                                   if 2u = v and c = 0

The machine halts  ⟺  H(0, 2, 5).

H is defined by unbounded recursion, so "H(0, 2, 5) is false" means "the recursion never terminates". Each case moves to a state with a larger v except the reset, which can lower it; that is why there is no measure that would make the recursion obviously finite or obviously infinite.

One lap at a time

Since every reset leaves u = v − 3, the loop can also be read as a recursion on laps. Lap(c, v) runs the loop from (c, v − 3, v) up to its (c + 1)-th wrap-around, the first one with c = 0; say that happens at (0, u, V). If 2u = V the machine halts; otherwise the next lap is Lap(2(V − u − 1), 2u − V + 3). The run from blank tape is lap 1 from (0, 2, 5) followed by Lap(2, 5), Lap(8, 6), Lap(4, 32), Lap(24, 16), …, and its 54th reset is at V = 35,143,437,691.

In Lean

This is the definition the theorem is about, copied from lean/Doubling.lean; none is the stop.

def op : Nat × Nat × Nat → Option (Nat × Nat × Nat)
  | (c, u, v) =>
    if 2 * u < v then some (c, 2 * u, v + 1)
    else match c with
      | c + 1 => if 2 * u = v then some (c, 1, v + 2) else some (c, 2 * u - v, v + 1)
      | 0 => if 2 * u = v then none else some (2 * (v - u - 1), 2 * u - v, 2 * u - v + 3)

def opIter : Nat → Nat × Nat × Nat → Option (Nat × Nat × Nat)
  | 0, s => some s
  | n + 1, s => (op s).bind (opIter n)

theorem halts_iff_op : HaltsFromBlank ↔ ∃ n, opIter n (0, 2, 5) = none

HaltsFromBlank is the machine's own halting from blank tape, defined from its transition table in lean/Machine.lean. The proof (lean/Map.lean for the tape, lean/Doubling.lean for the arithmetic) is checked by Lean's kernel with only the standard axioms; nothing about the run is assumed. What is not known is the value of H(0, 2, 5): the loop has been run for 1011 operations (107 of them inside Lean) without stopping.

The odometer rule

For S(c, a, b) = 0^∞ (10)^c 1^a 0 (10)^b 1 B>0 0^∞ with a even and a ≥ 2, the next right-end visit in state B is: if 2b < a, S(c, a − 2b, 2b + 1) (the right block doubles, eating 1s); if 2b ≥ a and c ≥ 1, apply the rule to (c − 1, 2a + 4, max(b − a/2 − 1, 0)) (a carry: one 10 of the left block becomes 1s); if 2b ≥ a and c = 0, halt when b = a/2 and otherwise apply the rule to (a, 4, b − a/2 − 1) (a reset: the 1s become the new left block). halts_iff, lean/Map.lean; halts_iff_op, lean/Doubling.lean
It halts iff the doubling map halts

HaltsFromBlank ↔ ∃ n, opIter n (0, 2, 5) = none, where op is the doubling map on (c, u, v) below. Two layers: the machine halts iff the orbit of the visit S(0, 4, 1) (step 43) under the visit map F reaches none (every value of F proved on the tape, for every visit, from the rules of Rules.lean); and, by arithmetic, one step of F is one or more operations of op, with a halt exactly when op halts.

The building blocks of the odometer, for all parameters

Each move between two visits is a Lean theorem about every value of its counts and every tape in its context: the start of a sweep, a round (two 1s of the pool and one pair of the right block become four 1s of the middle block), the end of a sweep (the doubled block), the carry (a pair of the left block and the middle block become the new pool), the reset (the middle block becomes the new left block), and the boundary cases: a pool of two, a carry with two, one or no pairs of the right block left (the last is the emptying carry), a reset with one pair left.

The odometer is a doubling map

With u = b + 1 and v = a/2 + b + 2: 2u < v is a visit with (2u, v + 1); 2u > v is a carry to (2u − v, v + 1) that uses one unit of c, or with c = 0 a reset to c = 2(v − u − 1), u = 2u − v, v = u + 3; 2u = v is a carry to (1, v + 2), or with c = 0 the halt. Proved for every visit (F_op), and evaluated: no halt in the first 104 operations (kernel) and 107 (native_decide). v + c = 5 + visits + resets + emptying carries.

Heuristic
A later halt has probability about 10−10

At a reset 2u − v is one of about v/2 values, one of which halts. The moduli at resets grow by a factor between 1 and 2 (about 1.47 on average), so the chances form a geometric series: about 6/v after a reset at v, 10−10 after reset 54. The same growth is why resets are logarithmically rare: reset k needs about 1.47k operations.

Heuristic
README, Structure of the map
Why no invariant is in sight

Every lap starts at u = v − 3, so a lap is a function of its modulus; its pattern of wrap-arounds is the binary expansion of a real number within 1/v of u/v, and an exact hit is a long run of zeros in that expansion, to the exact integer. Backwards, a state has exactly one predecessor when v is even, and two or none (u even or odd) when v is odd: the backward tree of the halting states is a critical branching process, which is where the 1/v density comes from. Every class of (u, v, c) modulo 8 contains states that halt, so no invariant modulo a power of 2 can rule the halt out.

Open
Does a reset ever land on 2u = v (b = a/2)?

No congruence excludes it: the halting state has predecessors under the map for every even v. Deciding the machine means deciding a chaotic doubling map with a moving modulus.
