The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in E reading 0. E is entered only from D1 (writes 1, moves L): 0 D>1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

What it does

A frozen trail and a small engine

Look at the tape each time the head turns at the left end, and draw the rows one under the other, each row slid 16 cells to the right of the one before. Almost everything then stands still: vertical stripes. Only a narrow band at the right edge flickers.

So the machine is a translating archive: between two round trips the tape is the same word moved 16 cells to the left, except for the first 67 cells (where one more copy of 1^5 0 1^6 0 1^4 0 1 0 appears per round trip) and a zone at the right end of 450 to 610 cells that keeps changing. That zone is the engine: it moves to the right with the edge of the tape and the cells it leaves behind never change again.

The sweeps

Two ways to write the same blocks

At a left turn the tape is blocks of ones separated by single 0s, for example 0 1^4 0 1^4 0 1^3 0 1^8 …. The rightward sweep turns each block into runs of exactly three ones separated by runs of 0s (at the right turn the tape looks like 1^6 0^2 1^3 0 1^3 0^2 1^3 0^3 …); the leftward sweep turns them back. Each sweep is a sequence of shift rules, proved in Lean for every repeat count:

rulefromtowhat it does
b_eraseL B> (1)^n RL (0)^n B> RRightward sweep: B erases a block of ones.
f_onesL (1)^n F> 1 RL F> 1 (1)^n RLeftward: F runs left over a block of ones, keeping them.
b_wordL B> (1101111)^n RL (1110000)^n B> RRightward: B crosses the word 1101111 (a block 1^4 after 1^2) and leaves 1110000.
b_word2L 0 B> (101111)^n RL (111000)^n 0 B> RRightward: B, with a 0 behind it, crosses 101111 and leaves 111000.
Can it halt?

Only the engine can stop it

The machine halts only from D reading a 1 with a 0 on its left (0 D>1). In the first 109 steps D reads a 1 about 49 million times, always with a 1 on its left. The frozen part of the tape is crossed by the sweep rules and cannot produce the pattern; the engine at the right end could. The engine is small (about 600 cells) but chaotic, and it never repeats, since the trail it leaves is not periodic. Deciding this machine means understanding that engine.

The sweep rules

Five-to-thirteen-step shift rules of the two sweeps, for every repeat count and any tape around: B erases a block of ones going right (b_erase), F keeps one going left (f_ones), and B crosses the words 1101111 and 101111 of the right sweep.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. A proof would have to control the right-end engine: show that the cells it leaves behind never change again and that it never writes 0 1 where D arrives. Its width looks bounded but creeps up slowly; no invariant for it is known.
