What is known about 1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---

1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. It belongs to a family of four holdouts that share their first four states, and works as two binary counters, one counting down in the blocks left of the head and one counting up in the gaps right of it. This page lists what is established, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Lean now proves the counter itself: read the tape as two binary numbers, E in the odd blocks left of the head and N in the gaps right of it, and every countdown of E, for every size of E and N, runs as the counter says (run3, run5). Lean also proves that the blank-tape run enters this counter after 160 steps and runs its first countdown. The pass rules (including the halting one) are proved too.

Coq now proves that halting is equivalent to a question about one explicit map, for 1a and 1b at once: the machine halts if and only if the round map of a closed counter model, iterated from (2, 5), reaches a corner X = 2L (halts_iff_corner). The second-overflow cascade is proved too, and it narrows the door to one equation: a corner comes only from rule R5 with the unread rest m of the right counter equal to (2D + 1)·2s − 1, D being how far the left counter is below full (halts_iff_r5_match, also in Lean for the model). Whether the orbit ever meets it is open; in 400,000 rounds (L up to 132 million) it stayed at least 0.47·L bits away.

The pass rules

Six rules on symbolic tapes, for all values of their parameters and any surrounding tape: a pass on an even top block (L 1 A>0 1^(2k+4) 0^(g+2) 1 R), on a block of 2, on an odd block crossing j small gaps, on a block of 3, a gap of length one closing, and pass_odd_halt: an odd pass whose D-run meets a gap one cell too short reaches F reading a 1, the only halting transition. Proofs by rfl steps and crossing lemmas, written by tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py.

The counter, proved for every size

Write E in p bits on the left (from the head outward, a 0 bit is 1 1 and a 1 bit is 1 0, above a floor that starts with a 1) and N in n bits on the right (a 0 bit is 0^3, a 1 bit is 0^2 1, followed by any tape X). In mode 3 (top block 1^3) the machine takes (E, N) to (E − k, N + k) for every k ≤ E with N + k < 2n, in at least 22·k steps (run3); in mode 5 (top block 1^5) it takes (E, N) to (E − k, N + 2k) in at least 48·k steps (run5). Proofs: three pass-pair rules written by tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py, the binary decrement and increment on bit lists, and induction on k.

Every corner halts, so halting is equivalent to reaching a corner (Coq)

halts_iff_corner bb : halts (machine bb) c0 <-> exists j len, iter_n j (cfgL 2 0 5) = Some (cfgL len 0 (2^len)), for both 1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB--- and 1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_1RC---. The new half is corner_halts: from a corner cfgL len 0 (2^len), for every len ≥ 1, the machine halts. The path is the same for every len: the overflow of the left counter (LC_Ov) turns the full right counter into a shifted counter that is twice too large, and the left counter is counted all the way down (2len − 1 pairs); the left counter overflows a second time, gains one digit (rule E1), and the right part becomes a counter of len − 1 digits ended by 0011; that counter is counted up through all its values twice, the carry between the two passes turning 0011 into 1011; then a fixed sweep halts (rule Hend). E1 and Hend are tape rules with free parameters, proved for both tables by busycoq's symbolic execution; the rest reuses the counter lemmas of SOC23Model.v. Print Assumptions: closed under the global context. Coq 8.20.1 on busycoq's BB6 branch (commit 33209bc), 31 seconds for the three files.

A corner can only come from one equation at one rule (Coq)

halts_iff_r5_match bb : halts (machine bb) c0 <-> exists j x, iter_n j (cfgL 2 0 5) = Some x /\ r5match x, for both 1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB--- and 1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_1RC---. After the left counter overflows, the right counter is read as a cascade of second overflows (the place where busycoq's SOC_2_3_v2 template needs its rules ROv2_0 and ROv2_1; here rules R1 to R5 of SOC23Model.v). Call a configuration doomed when its plain countdown ends at a corner (cfgR len k n with k + n + 1 = 2len). doom_step: the only step of the model that makes a doomed configuration out of one that is not doomed is rule R5, a shifted counter cfgS1 with an odd number h = 2a + 1 of digits running out, and only when the unread rest m of the right counter satisfies m + 1 = (2D + 1)·23a+3, where D = 2len − 1 − k is how far the left counter is below full (r5match). The three ends with nothing left to read (R1, R2, R3) never give a corner: the new left counter is an odd number times 2s, minus 1, so the countdown ends one or two short of a power of two. The one exception, cfgS0 len (2^len-1) 0 0 0, is reached by no step (bad0_step). So the second-overflow cascade is proved for every parameter, and what it would take to make a corner is exactly one equation; whether the orbit ever satisfies it is the open question. Print Assumptions: closed under the global context. Coq 8.20.1 on busycoq's BB6 branch (commit 33209bc), 2 seconds on top of the other three files.

No halt before 8.47·101907 steps

The round-2 accelerator py/jump2.py (with c/ps.c, in the folder of 1a) simulates directly from pass start to pass start until the tape is a counter configuration of lean/Counter.lean (top block 3 or 5, E and N readable with at least 12 bits), then jumps a whole countdown at once: k cycles with k up to E (or down to the next power of two) and within the room N has before its first defect. Each jump is an instance of run3/run5, so its end tape is proved; only the Python reading of the tape, the exact step count (fitted formula a + 4ν(E) + 6ν(N+1) per pair) and the direct simulation between jumps are unproved. Jumps of at most 108 steps were each re-run by direct simulation and matched token for token. In 25 minutes on one core it made 245 jumps and reached step 8.47·101907 (E up to 6,452 bits) with no halt.

Growth shape: exp/fixed

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.036).

The phase transition

When E reaches 0 the floor block 1^(2s) under it is popped into the top block, the next pass pushes s single 1s, and a new countdown starts with E = 2s − 1 (s one bits; floor 1^2) in the other mode (3 and 5 alternate); N stays on the right, but the cut leaves a gap of irregular size in front of it. Irregular gaps then act in a cascade of short sub-phases: a gap one too long (for 1a/1b; the analogous residue mod 5 for 1c/1d) collapses into a block 1^2, a gap one too short followed by a single 1 is cut down and passes its defect on to the next gap; sometimes the whole of N is pulled to the left and N restarts at 0. The floor grows by 2 whenever E passes a power of two, so it is about twice the bit length of the largest E so far. Seen at every transition of the runs above; not proved, and no closed formula for the new (E, N) as a function of the old one is known.

What a corner would take

Write the left counter by its deficit D = 2len − 1 − k. A round starts with D = 0; a countdown of n adds n to D, and an overflow that appends s digits sends D to (2D+1)·2s, so the round never needs L itself. The round reads X from its low end and ends either when all of X is read (rules R1, R2, R3 on an empty rest) or at rule R5, which leaves the unread top part m of X. Then X' = 2L' − (2D+1)·2s + m + 1 at R5, and X' = 2L' − (2D+1)·2s (+1 for R1) otherwise. So a corner can only come from R5 with m + 1 = (2D+1)·2s (the R1 case would need X = 1): the part of X a round leaves unread must equal a number computed from the part it read. That number has about 2.5 times as many bits as the part read (a zero run of length h adds about 2h+1 to D and then about 1.5h digits), so a corner needs one round to read about 0.29·L bits of X and the other 0.71·L bits to match exactly (a random match has odds of about 2−0.7L). Only round 10 read that much (53%, right after round 9 had read all of X); from round 35 on no round read more than 22.3%. Derived by hand from f in SOC23Model.v; c/model23.c computes rounds in this form and agrees with py/model23.py. No invariant that excludes the match is known: the low bits of X decide where a round stops, and the top bits are what earlier rounds left, which is what makes the question Collatz-like.

How a round reads X, and why sizes do not settle it

A round reads X from its low end as runs 0^h 1, and the model only looks at the parity of each h, so the cascade is a reader with four states (S0 or S1, h even or odd): in S0 an even run moves to S1 and an odd run stays in S0; in S1 with h even, a 0 next starts another run in S1 and a 1 next swallows a whole block 1^j 0 and moves to S0 with h = j; in S1 with h odd the round stops (rule R5). All 3,000 first rounds but two (rounds 3 and 8, which read all of X) end at R5. At R5 the equation m + 1 = (2D + 1)·2s first needs the low s bits of m to be 1s and the next one 0; that happens in 129 of the 2,998 R5 rounds (4.3%). Then the bits of 2D + 1 must match: the best of the 2,998 matched 6 bits (out of 10 to 73). Length alone does not decide it either: the target (2D + 1)·2s was longer than m + 1 in rounds 0, 1, 2, 4, 9 and 33 of the first 30,000 (round 33 read 515 bits of an X of about 1,560), so the two lengths cross; after that m + 1 was always longer. In 20,000 rounds (L up to 3.9 million) the low s bits matched in 887 of 19,998 R5 rounds, and the longest agreement was at round 6634, where all 5 bits of 2D+1 matched, but m+1 there is millions of bits longer than the target; in 30,000 rounds the target was longer than m+1 only in the same six early rounds. A proof of non-halting needs a reason why the unread top of X can never equal what the round built from its bottom; none is known.

How long a round reads: a power-law tail, and the odds of a match

Over 200,000 rounds from (2, 5) (L up to 58.6 million), the bits r read by one R5 round have a heavy tail: P(r ≥ x) is about 5.5·x−0.9 from x = 10 to 100,000. That is a power law, not the geometric tail of a reader that stops with a fixed chance at each run. The long reads are mostly a single run (in rounds that read 1,000 bits or more, the longest run is a median 66% of them), so they come from long runs of 0s or 1s that earlier rounds left in X. Per bit read, the target (2D+1)·2s grows by 1.5 to 2.5 bits (median 1.69). A match needs equal lengths, r + tbits = bits of X, but after round 35 the largest r + tbits was 0.78 of X, and the largest share read stayed 22.3%. Equal lengths came only in rounds 0, 1, 2, 4, 9 and 33, and none matched. Another necessary condition holds sometimes: m + 1 and the target must have the same number of 0s below their leading 1. That happened in 93 of the 200,000 rounds, so it does not rule a match out on its own. If later rounds read like the fitted tail, the expected number of rounds after 35 with equal lengths is about 0.0006. Even such a round would then need some 900 or more further bits to agree by chance. This is a heuristic for never halts, not a proof: a power law is just the kind of tail that a hidden structure could use.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. Coq proves the question equivalent to one equation in the model orbit from (2, 5): the machine halts exactly when, at some rule R5 (the end of a second-overflow cascade), the unread rest m of the right counter satisfies m + 1 = (2D + 1)·2s (halts_iff_r5_match). In 400,000 rounds X stayed at least 0.47·L bits away from a corner, and in 3,000 rounds no R5 matched more than 6 bits of 2D + 1 past the first s. No invariant ruling the equation out is known.
