1RB1LD_1LC0RD_0LA0RB_0LE1LF_1RF0LC_0RB---

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Its tape is a prefix of blocks 1^2 0 at the left end followed by a long word of the tokens 1 0, 1^2 0 and 0^3 1. The left end moves out three cells at a time, at irregular moments roughly 1.5 times apart; no counter, no fixed rule for the word, and no restructure rule has been found.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Lean proves no halt in the first 107 steps (native_decide); the C simulator finds no halt in the first 1011 steps.

What it does. Every time the left end moves, the tape reads C>0 0^2 1 0 (1^2 0)^m V with V a word of the tokens 1 0, 1^2 0, 0^3 1. The prefix length m goes up and down (16 to 18 in the last ten extensions). The left end moved at steps 3.2·109, 8.1·109, 2.3·1010, 3.6·1010, 4.3·1010, 6.5·1010: the gaps do not double, so this is not a plain counter.

Why it is hard. No macro structure has been found: no closed tape language from short windows was found, the word V changes everywhere between extensions, and the extension times follow no visible rule.

The left end grows three cells at a time

All 61 moments in the first 1011 steps when the head, in state C, reaches a new cell at the left end have the shape C>0 0^2 1 0 (1^2 0)^m V, with V made of 1 0, 1^2 0 and 0^3 1; each is followed within 12 steps by two more new cells. m is not monotone: it fell 14 times, the last time at step 35,830,421,385 (from 18 to 16).
