The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from E0 (writes 1, moves R): E>0 0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

Macro structure

What the machine does

Stop the machine whenever its head first reaches a new cell on the left. With a for 10, around step 9.85·107:

  • step 98,543,810: B>0^2 a^85 1^1663
  • step 98,605,493: B>0^2 a^173 1^1490
  • step 98,852,768: B>0^2 a^349 1^1141
  • step 99,842,939: B>0^2 a^701 1^440

Each time, the alternating block goes from x to 2x + 3 (85, 173, 349, 701) and the solid block loses the same number of cells (1663 − 173 = 1490, 1490 − 349 = 1141, 1141 − 701 = 440). Rewriting a block of length x costs about x passes of length x, so the step count between left growths grows like the square of the width (61,683, 247,275 and 990,171 steps here), and the width grows like the cube root of the step count. When the solid block runs out a new round starts. This is the same doubling seen in the three machines 1RB1RD_0LC1RA_…_1RF0LD_---0RE, with different states.

The halting transition

Where a halt would have to come from

The halting transition is F reading a 0. F is entered only from E reading a 0 (1RF), so a halt needs E>0 0: E on a 0 with another 0 just to its right. In the first 108 steps F was entered 30,382 times, every time on a 1, and 30,101 of those times at least 7 cells from the left end.

Whether the end of a round can leave E in front of two 0s is open. In the related machines 1RB1RD_0LC1RA_…_1RF0LD_---0RE the halt can only come at the moment the tape is all 1s, and only for a sparse list of tape lengths; whether the same holds here has not been checked.

Proof

The frame map in Lean

The frames (blank tape outside; X is the head in state X on the next cell; u^v is v copies of u, so 11^L is 2L ones; a frame counts only at a moment where the head turns round):

  • b: 1^L B 10 10^j 1^R
  • a: 1^L 10^j 110 A 1^R
  • c: 1^L 0 C 0 10^j 1^R
  • d: 1^L A 01 10^j 1^R
  • e: 1^L 10^j B
  • f: 1^L 10^j 1101 B

The map was found by fitting the machine's runs (py/framemap.py) and collapsing the fit into cases (py/pieces.py); Lean then checks every case for all sizes. It is undefined only at .b l j 2, .d l 0 1, .d l 1 0, and those halt. So the open question is exactly whether the orbit of Ph.e 1 0 reaches one of them; the first 108 frames do not.

The recursion, rule by rule

Exact tapes, exact step counts

The bbchallenge wiki page of this machine (analysis by Racheline) writes its recursion the way the wiki writes Antihydra's: one family of tapes, then rules. Here each rule also has the exact number of machine steps it takes. A visit is any moment at which the tape has the form

A(a, b) := 0∞ 1a 0 C> 1b 0∞ (a ≥ 1)

The Lean map of the section above is finer: A(a, b) is the Lean frame b(a − 2, 1, b − 1) three steps earlier (for a = 1, the frame d(0, 2, b − 1) five steps earlier), so each rule below is the run of Lean cases from one such frame to the next: mostly a zigzag of b and a frames (2j + 5 and 2j + 4 steps each) and a sweep of d and c frames (5 and 1 steps each).

The rules themselves are now proved in Lean for all sizes (lean/Rules.lean, next section). Below them is the frame map they are made of: every case of next for all sizes (next_sound in lean/Phases.lean), with the step count of each case in the statement of its piece theorem in lean/Pieces.lean (next_sound itself only says that some number of steps exists), the halts (none_halts; the halt row is .b l j 2) and halts_iff. python3 py/rules.py checks each row three ways: direct simulation from 3,418 tapes A(a, b) (a, b < 60) to the next visit or the halt, giving the target and the step count of the table; the Lean cases from the frame of A(a, b) to the next such frame, giving the same; and the run from blank tape, where the 238 visits of the first 107 steps are exactly the rules' orbit from A(2, 9) with the same step counts, and the same as the Lean map's frames. The Lean map followed for 5·105 frames (2.1·108 steps, 685 visits) agrees with the rules too. Lean now proves that the rules hold for all sizes, so the machine halts iff they lead from A(2, 9) to some A(a, 3).

For example A(1, 7) = 1 0 C> 17: A(5, 6) after 27 steps, A(4, 14) after 82, A(12, 9) after 79, A(4, 21) after 176, A(12, 16) after 79, A(28, 3) after 431, and the halt 11 steps later: 885 steps in all, which is what direct simulation gives.

Can b = 3 be ruled out?

Looking for an invariant that excludes the halt

lean/Rules.lean proves each rule above for all sizes from the frame map (a zigzag of b/a frames, a sweep of d/c frames, a few single cases), and with it halts_iff_rules: the machine halts from blank tape if and only if the rules lead from A(2, 9) to some A(a, 3). The only rule that can give b = 3 is the growing rule from A(a, a + 4) with a even. So the whole question is whether the orbit ever lands exactly on that diagonal.

Following the orbit is cheap: one visit is a few integer operations, while the sizes grow only about linearly in the number of visits. no_b3 checks the first 108 visits in Lean (about 6.8·1023 machine steps), and C runs the first 3·1012 (c/abrun.c; about 1.8·1037 steps, a and b near 1013). Half the visits are swaps and almost all the rest growing rules; restarts are rare (8 in the first 107 visits).

Why it looks random. Every rule adds a fixed amount to s = a + b (3 for a growing rule, 4 for a swap; proved in Lean as op_sum), so s is a clock, about 3.5 per visit. Measured against it, the swap is a ↦ 2a − s + 1 and the growing rules are a ↦ 2a + 4 or 2a + 3: the ratio x = a/s just doubles mod 1, the binary shift, with a little noise from the +3s and +4s. The sequence of swaps and growths is the binary expansion of x, and its blocks of 12 digits come out equidistributed. A halt needs the orbit to land on one exact point, a = (s − 4)/2 with a even, which is the same kind of question as whether a Collatz orbit ever hits a given number.

No congruence does it. The residues of a and b − a at the visits are far from uniform (when a is 2 or 3 mod 4, b is always odd), but for every modulus tried, the classes with a even and b − a ≡ 4 get their full share of visits, and the rules applied to residues reach them too.

The halting visit looks like a rare random event. For each small even g, visits with a even and b − a = g come about 0.11 times per decade of the visit index, steadily from 105 to 1010. For g = 4 that predicts about 1.3 halts so far, so having seen none says little. Orbits from random starts of size 109 do halt by this rule (5 of 9,000 within 106 visits; for example A(264421744, 92483937) after 485,721 visits). If the orbit behaves like a random one, it halts with probability 1, but the median wait is about 2·1018 visits, about 1055 machine steps, beyond any direct run. Proving that it never halts would need an argument that the visits always miss one exact diagonal, and nothing of that kind is in sight.

The long run

Many visits at once

The rules move along a clock: every rule adds a fixed amount to s = a + b (3 for a growing rule, 4 for a swap). Away from the rare resets the orbit is one of two affine maps, picked by one digit:

  • grow (b > a): A(a, b) ↦ A(2a + 4, b − a − 1) if a is even, A(2a + 3, b − a) if a is odd;
  • swap (b ≤ a): A(a, b) ↦ A(a − b + 1, 2b + 3).

The smaller coordinate doubles and the larger pays for it. The grow/swap digits are the binary expansion of b / (a + b), up to carries from the small constants, which reach only the digits about log2(a + b) places down. So the next 32 or more digits can be read off at once as the top bits of 2K b / (a + b), applied together with tables, and checked: c/abfast.c keeps a block of K guessed digits only if both coordinates end at least 16·2K. lean/Block.lean proves that check sound whatever digits were guessed (block_ok): one formal step at most doubles min(a, b) plus 4, so a large end forces every step of the block to end with both coordinates at least 28, and such a step is the true rule. None of those visits has 1 ≤ b − a ≤ 27 (block_far), so no halt (b − a = 4 with a even), reset or near visit is skipped. Refused blocks, about 1 in 100, are redone one visit at a time with the full rules.

The run so far: 6.6·1012 visits, no halt, ending at A(8096170023751, 15003829348773). Near visits (1 ≤ b − a ≤ 8) keep coming at about the rate of the earlier decades; none has a even and b − a = 4.

Heuristic
c/abres.c
No congruence invariant excludes b = 3

The halt needs a visit A(a, a + 4) with a even. Over the visits with b > a and b − a ≤ 106 among the first 3·109, the residues (a mod M, (b − a) mod M) are far from uniform, yet for every M tried (2k up to 256; 48, 90, 120, 210, 252) the classes with a even and b − a ≡ 4 get the same share as any other even residue. The rules applied to residues mod every even M up to 128 also reach them.

Heuristic
c/abdig.c
A doubling map in disguise

With s = a + b as the clock, the swap is a ↦ 2a − s + 1 and the growing rules a ↦ 2a + 4 or 2a + 3: x = a/s follows x ↦ 2x mod 1 up to O(1/s). The swap/grow digits are its binary expansion; their blocks of 12 are equidistributed over the first 109 visits (chi-square 3,956 on 4,095 degrees of freedom). The halt asks for one exact point of this map.

Heuristic
c/abstat.c, c/abrand.c
The halting visit looks like a rare random event

Visits with a even and b − a = g, for each small even g, come about 0.11 times per decade of visit index, steadily from 105 to 1010 and flat in g (c/abstat.c). For g = 4 that predicts about 1.3 halts in the 12 decades run, so none is unremarkable. Of 9,000 orbits from random A(a, b) with a, b < 109, run 106 visits each, 5 halt by this rule (c/abrand.c; for example A(264421744, 92483937) reaches A(358606020, 3) after 485,721 visits). In that model the run halts with probability 1, with a median of about 2·1018 visits (about 1055 steps).
