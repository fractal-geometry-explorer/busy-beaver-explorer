Open: a cryptidIts halting is thought to come down to a Collatz-like question that no known method decides (the community's label, not a result).

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape. Its run is a sequence of epochs, each a Collatz-like countdown; an epoch ends either in a halt or in a new, vastly longer epoch. The bbchallenge community lists it as probviously halting: with fair-coin parities every epoch halts with probability 1/3.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. In configurations X(n, m) = C>0 0^n 1^m 0 1 the machine sends n to about 3n/2 and m down by 1 or 2 according to n mod 4. When m runs out it halts or starts a new epoch with m ≈ 3n/2.

Where the run is (computed exactly). Epochs start with m = 5, 57, 210,273,201. The third epoch lasts 140,180,848 macro steps (the number reaches 82 million bits) and ends in a new epoch, whose countdown has about 24,684,623 decimal digits. Its end is out of reach of any computation. Heuristically each epoch halts with probability 1/3, so the machine halts with probability 1, after a number of steps with tens of millions of digits in its exponent.

Other proofs and outside reports

Community label

In the community's BB(6) tracker the machine's class is Bell-like. Its 3 November 2026 edition lists the machine as a cryptid: its halting is thought to come down to a Collatz-like question that no known method decides. These are labels read off the run by others, not results.

The macro rules

For X(n, m) = 0^∞ C>0 0^n 1^m 0 1 0^∞ with n ≥ 2 even: for m ≥ 2, n ≡ 0 (mod 4) goes to X(3n/2 + 4, m − 1) and n ≡ 2 to X((3n + 10)/2, m − 2); for m = 1, n ≡ 0 gives X(3n/2 + 4, 0) and n ≡ 2 a new epoch X(4, 3n/2 + 6); for m = 0, n ≡ 0 gives X(4, 3n/2 + 5) and n ≡ 2 halts. Checked against direct simulation for every even 2 ≤ n < 60 and m < 12. Inside a step the head fills the run of 0s with 1s from the left, two cells per round trip while the tape grows by one at the left; then a 1 is taken off the countdown block.

The first three epochs, exactly

From blank tape the run reaches X(4, 5) after 50 steps; epochs start with m = 5, 57 (step 2,619) and 210,273,201. The third epoch was run exactly with a divide-and-conquer parity iteration (the parities of t steps of y ↦ ⌈3y/2⌉ depend only on y mod 2t; blocks are combined with powers of 3, GMP through gmpy2): it ends after 140,180,848 macro steps at m = 1 with n ≡ 2 (mod 4), so a fourth epoch starts with m = 3n/2 + 6, a number of 82,000,544 bits (about 24,684,623 digits; m mod 264 = 15369781771884450229). Two runs with different block sizes agree; the fast iteration agrees with plain big-integer iteration on epochs with m up to 105.

heuristic Evidence only

Halts with probability 1, but not soon

With y = n/2 + 4 the rule is y ↦ ⌈3y/2⌉, with m −1 for y even and −2 for y odd. With fair-coin parities the countdown lands on m = 1 with probability 2/3 and on 0 with probability 1/3; m = 1 continues to 0 or starts a new epoch, and m = 0 halts or starts a new epoch, each with probability 1/2. So an epoch halts with probability 1/3 and the machine halts with probability 1, the community's reading. The fourth epoch alone lasts about 1024,684,623 macro steps.
