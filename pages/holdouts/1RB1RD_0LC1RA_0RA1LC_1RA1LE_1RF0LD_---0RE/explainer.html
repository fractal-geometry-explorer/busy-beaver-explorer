Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1RD_0LC1RA_0RA1LC_1RA1LE_1RF0LD_---0RE

Nobody knows, but the reason is now clear. The tape is an alternating block next to a solid block; the machine doubles one at the other's expense, back and forth. It can only halt right after the tape becomes all ones, and only if the number of ones is in a sparse list (3, 13, 35, 81, 175, …).

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from E0 (writes 1, moves R): E>0 0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The run at a glance

The diagram stacks the tape at many moments. Sampling at each new cell shows the shape of the run as the visited region grows; sampling evenly in time shows where the head spends its time. The records list every step at which the head reaches a cell no earlier step reached, with the side, the width so far and the whole tape: the first place to look for a repeating macro structure.

Macro structure

Two blocks

Stop the machine whenever its head first reaches a new cell and the tape always looks the same: an alternating block 10 10 10 … followed by a solid block of 1s. Writing a for 10, here are four moments around step 7·107:

  • step 69,526,461: a^8 1^1695 A>0
  • step 69,528,539: E>0 a^18 1^1673 1110
  • step 69,537,687: E>0 a^84 1^1545 1110
  • step 70,183,249: E>0 a^700 1^319 1110

The alternating block grows from 8 copies to 18, 40, 84, 172, 348, 700: it roughly doubles each time, eating the solid block. When the solid block is too short for another doubling the machine turns to the right end and the roles swap: now the solid block doubles (a^541 1^642, a^220 1^1286) and eats the alternating one.

The two phases

Doubling, back and forth

Write E(x,z) for the tape E>0 (10)^x 1^z, the head in state E at the left end, and A(x,y) for (10)^x 1^y A>0, the head in state A just past the right end.

  • Left phase. E(x,z) → E(2x+4, z−2x−6), as long as z ≥ 2x+8. The head bounces between the alternating block and the solid block; each bounce moves one 10 from the left part to the right part and turns two solid 1s into another 10. It takes 4x²+24x+30 steps.
  • Right phase. A(x,y) → A(x−y/2, 2y+2) for even y, as long as x is large enough: the solid block doubles, eating the alternating block. It takes y²+2y+6 steps.

When a doubling no longer fits, the machine switches phase, and the width of the tape grows by a couple of cells. Both rules held at every one of the 1,041 doublings in the first 109 steps. Since a doubling of a block of size x costs about x² steps and the tape grows by a few cells per phase change, a tape of width w takes about w³ steps: that is why the width grows like the cube root of the step count.

In effect the machine runs the doubling map: the split point between the two blocks moves like x ↦ 2x around a circle of the tape's width, the classic example of a chaotic map. The exact remainders at each overflow decide everything.

The one way to halt

Halting needs a perfect landing

The halting transition is F reading a 0. State F is entered only from E reading a 0 (1RF), and E and F together walk right over 0 1 0 1 …. So the machine halts only when E starts at the left end with at most one 1 after the alternating block: E>0 (10)^x or E>0 (10)^x 1. A left phase reaches that when z − 2x − 6 = 1: the doubling has to use up the solid block exactly, leaving a single 1.

Parity forbids it in the normal course of things. Probing every two-block tape with x, y, z < 150: if z is even at the left phase and y is even at the right phase, the next two-block tape is again of that kind, or the tape becomes all ones. Only an all-ones tape 1^n can start a left phase with z odd, and from 1^n A>0 the run halts exactly when

n = 1, 3, 6, 13, 35, 81, 175, 365, …   (n = 12·2k − 2k − 9 for k ≥ 0, and 1, 6),

checked directly for every n ≤ 400. The reason: from 1^n the alternating block restarts at x = 2 and goes 2, 8, 20, 44, 92, … (x ↦ 2x+4), each doubling taking 2x+6 ones; the run halts if the ones run out leaving exactly one.

Evidence

Rare resets, sparse targets

The tape becomes all ones only when a right phase lands exactly on x = 0. That happened eleven times in the first 109 steps, at n = 5, 10, 14, 30, 61, 89, 169, 222, 382, 542, 781, and the last of them was at step 6.7·106. Between 109 and 1015 steps there were only five more: n = 9705, 59501, 77694, 271974, 311881. None was in the halting list.

A crude model: a phase change lands on an exact value with chance about 1/w on a tape of width w, so all-ones moments become rarer as the tape grows, and each one also has to hit a list whose members double. Under that model the expected number of halts from here on is small and shrinking: the machine very probably never halts. Turning that into a proof would mean controlling the exact remainders of a doubling map, which is as hard as questions about the binary digits of specific numbers.

Two near-duplicates, 1RB1RD_0LC1RA_1LA1LC_1RA1LE_1RF0LD_---0RE and 1RB1RD_0LC1RA_1RA1LC_0RB1LE_1RF0LD_---0RE, share the left phase (states D, E, F and B) and behave the same way, each with its own sparse list of fatal all-ones lengths.

Proof

The phase map in Lean

Every configuration the run passes through at a phase boundary is one of three shapes: E(x, z) = E>0 (10)^x 1^z, A(x, y) = (10)^x 1^y A>0 and the bounce frame Q(m, j, t) = (10)^m 1 D>0 0 (1010)^j 1^t. The function next in lean/Phases.lean sends each one to the next shape the machine reaches, and next_sound proves each of its 21 cases for all parameters, from symbolic runs generated by py/pieces.py. Nothing is left out: next is undefined only at E(x, 0) and E(x, 1), and those halt.

So the question is exactly whether the orbit of A(0, 0) (the blank tape) under next reaches E(x, 0) or E(x, 1) (halts_iff). The first 108 iterates do not (native_decide); by the step counts that is about 4.1·1021 machine steps. The even phases cannot reach a halt without an all-ones tape in between, and for all-ones tapes shorter than 105 cells Lean checks which ones lead to a halt first: n = 1 or 12·2k − 2k − 9 (3, 13, 35, 81, 175, …). lean/Fatal.lean proves that list for every n: from E(x, z) with z odd the run is a chain of doublings E(x, z) → E(2x+4, z−2x−6), which halts exactly when z = zHalt(x, k) for some k (e_fate, strong induction on z), and zHalt(2, k) = 12·2k − 2k − 11 (zHalt_closed). The last open step is to show that the run never hits a fatal length.

Kernel
lean/Rules.lean, lean/EPhase.lean
The two phase rules, for all sizes

Lean proves, with rfl steps, the crossing lemmas and induction on the number of bounces: E(x,z) reaches E(2x+4, z−2x−6) whenever x ≥ 1 and z ≥ 2x + 7 (ephase), and A(x,y) reaches A(x−y/2, 2y+2) whenever y ≥ 4 is even and 2x ≥ y + 2 (aphase). Both phases are made of the same bounce, (10)^(m+1) 1 D>0 0 (1010)^j 1^(r+3) to (10)^m 1 D>0 0 (1010)^(j+1) 1^(r+1) in 8j + 10 steps, plus a start and an end piece generated by py/gen_rules.py. The parity analysis is proved separately, on the whole phase map (even_next in lean/Phases.lean).

Step counts of the phases

Probing from those tapes, E(x,z) → E(2x+4, z−2x−6) takes 4x² + 24x + 30 steps and A(x,y) → A(x−y/2, 2y+2) takes y² + 2y + 6 steps (checked for small x, y; the step counts of the Lean pieces add up to the same formulas). Doubling costs the square of the block, so a tape of width w takes about w³ steps to grow: the width grows like t1/3.

py/phases.py
Halting needs an all-ones tape from a sparse list

From every two-block tape E(x,z) and A(x,y) with x, z, y < 150 the next two-block tape was computed. Call E(x,z) with z even and z ≥ 2, and A(x,y) with x ≥ 1 and y even, even: every even tape leads to an even tape or to an all-ones tape 1^n A>0, never to a halt. The machine halts only from E(x,0) or E(x,1). From 1^n A>0 it halts exactly when n is 1, 3, 6, 13, 35, 81, 175 or 365 (n ≤ 400), the list n = 12·2k − 2k − 9 plus 1 and 6. Not proved for all sizes.
