OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. The head zig-zags across the whole tape; read in words of 4 and 7 cells, the tape has a regular left part and a regular right part that both grow, and an irregular zone between them. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. Lean proves the exact way a halt would happen (C reading 1 with an isolated 0 after it) and the main shift rules; the C simulator finds no halt in the first 1011 steps. The middle zone looks irregular and no invariant that keeps the pattern away is known.

The halting configurations

The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (B1); the generated statement covers every configuration.

How a halt comes about

B is entered only from A reading 0, so the machine halts exactly at A>0 1. A is entered from A reading 1 (moving left, leaving a 0 behind, so the next cell to the right is 0) and from C reading 1 (moving right), so a halt needs C>1 0 1: C reads a 1 whose right neighbour is an isolated 0; halt 3 steps later.

enter_B, enter_A, halt_A, halt_C, lean/Halt.lean

Shift rules

For every n and every surrounding tape: C moving right rewrites (0 0 1 1)^n as (1 0 0 0)^n; A moving right fills (0 0 1)^n into (1 1 1)^n; A moving left erases a block 1^n; F crosses 0^n to the right, three steps a cell.

cconv, afill, aerase, fzero, lean/Rules.lean

No halt in the first 1011 steps

The C simulator (tools/c/tm.h) runs 1011 steps from blank tape without a halt; the visited tape then spans 58,066 cells, 27,999 of them 1.

c/longrun.c

C never meets an isolated 0

In the first 108 steps C reads a 1 about 8.75 million times; each time A then reads a 0 and B a 0 (B reads 1 never), so after a 1, C always sees two 0s. The tape itself has many isolated 0s (at the moments the head reaches a new cell on the right, about one run of 0s in seven has length 1), but C never reaches one from a 1.

c/trfreq.c, c/runhist.c

Where C meets a 1 followed by 0

In the first 109 steps C reads a 1 with a 0 after it only 693 times. 649 of them are at the right end of the visited tape, where the next cell is blank, so A then reads 0 0. The other 44 are inside the tape, at the join between the run of x (written 1000 at that moment) and the irregular middle, and there too the next cells are 0 0. So the halting pattern can only arise in the interior, next to the irregular middle; the right end, where the tape is periodic, cannot produce it.

c/evt.c

The right end is periodic

Recording the tape each time the head reaches a fresh cell at the right end (5,402 times in the first 3·108 steps) and cutting it after the last two copies of the 26-cell word z x x y x: from the 112th time on, the part to the right repeats exactly with period 13.

heuristic Evidence only

Two growing regular parts and an irregular middle

At the right end the tape is 1^3 x^2 z x^N (middle) (z x^2 y x)^K with x = 0001, y = 0001111, z = 0111111: the right end repeats every 13 visits and feeds new periods into the tail, while the middle zone eats into the tail's left end from time to time (K was 174 after 3·108 steps); the run of x grows more slowly and from time to time freezes (it stopped at 185 after about 6·107 steps, and a new run of x started to its right). The middle zone keeps a few dozen words of no visible pattern.

Growth shape

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.393). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.39).

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The halt needs C to read a 1 just before an isolated 0. Isolated 0s exist (inside the words 0111111), but C only ever reads 1s in front of two 0s. A proof would have to show why the sweeps never bring C there; window CTL up to 7 cells and n-gram CPS (widths up to 9, see the README for the larger runs) fail.
