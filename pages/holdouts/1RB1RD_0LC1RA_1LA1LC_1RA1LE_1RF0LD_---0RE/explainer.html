Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1RD_0LC1RA_1LA1LC_1RA1LE_1RF0LD_---0RE

Open: a cryptidIts halting is thought to come down to a Collatz-like question that no known method decides (the community's label, not a result).

Nobody knows, but the mechanism is clear. The tape is an alternating block next to a solid block; the machine doubles one at the other's expense, back and forth. It can halt only right after the tape becomes all ones, and only if the number of ones is in a sparse list (15, 41, 95, 205, …).

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from E0 (writes 1, moves R): E>0 0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

Macro structure

Two blocks, doubling

Stop the machine whenever its head first reaches a new cell and the tape is always an alternating block 10 10 10 … followed by a solid block of 1s. With a for 10, around step 4.8·107:

  • step 48,123,183: a^637 1^226 A>0
  • step 48,175,627: a^522 1^458 A>0
  • step 48,388,151: a^291 1^922 A>0
  • step 48,729,549: E>0 a^582 1^339 1110

At the right end the solid block doubles (226, 458, 922), eating the alternating block; when that no longer fits the head goes to the left end and the alternating block doubles instead (291 to 582), eating the solid one. Writing E(x,z) for E>0 (10)^x 1^z and A(x,y) for (10)^x 1^y A>0:

  • left phase: E(x,z) → E(2x+4, z−2x−6) while z ≥ 2x+8, in 4x²+24x+30 steps: the head bounces between the blocks, each bounce moving one 10 across and turning two 1s into another 10;
  • right phase: A(x,y) → A(x−y/2−2, 2y+6) for even y while it fits, in y²+6y+12 steps.

Each doubling of a block of size x costs about x² steps, and the tape grows by a few cells per phase change, so a tape of width w takes about w³ steps: the width grows like the cube root of time. The split between the blocks moves like the doubling map x ↦ 2x on a circle, a textbook chaotic map.

The one way to halt

Halting needs an exact landing

F reading 0 halts, and F is entered only from E reading 0; E and F together walk right over 0101…. So the run halts only from E>0 (10)^x or E>0 (10)^x 1: a left phase that uses up the solid block leaving exactly one 1.

Parity prevents that in the normal course of things: probing all two-block tapes with sizes below 150, if z is even in the left phase and y is even in the right phase, the next two-block tape is again of that kind or the tape becomes all ones. From 1^n A>0 the left phase restarts at E(3, n−2); the alternating block goes 3, 10, 24, 52, …, using 12, 26, 54, … ones, and the run halts if exactly one is left: n = 15, 41, 95, 205, 423, … (n = 28·2k − 2k − 13), and also n = 3 and 29 (checked for every n ≤ 400).

All-ones moments are rare: n = 4, 10, 58, 81, 278, 634 in the first 109 steps, and only 5881, 9702, 12113, 28074, 29561 and 405562 between 109 and 1015. None was in the list. A crude model (a phase change hits an exact value with chance about 1/width) makes such moments rarer and rarer, and each must also land on a list whose members double: the machine very probably never halts. A proof would have to control the exact remainders of a doubling map.

Its near-twins 1RB1RD_0LC1RA_0RA1LC_1RA1LE_1RF0LD_---0RE and 1RB1RD_0LC1RA_1RA1LC_0RB1LE_1RF0LD_---0RE share states B, D, E, F and the left phase, and behave the same way with their own fatal lists.

The recurrence in closed form

A doubling map with a growing circle

Look at the run only at its stops: the left phases E(x, z) with z even and the right phases A(x, y) with y even. Give each stop a position s and a circle length L: s = z + 2, L = 2x + z + 8 on the left, s = y + 6, L = 2x + y + 8 on the right. Then, at every stop except the few named below, the next stop has

L′ = L + 2,   s′ = 2s if 2s ≤ L,   s′ = 2s − L otherwise,

which is the doubling map s ↦ 2s mod L on a circle that grows by 2 cells a stop (doubling in lean/Doubling.lean, for all sizes). Whether the next stop is on the left or the right is decided by the branch: doubling without wrapping lands on the right, wrapping lands on the left. Each point below is one stop of the run from blank tape (stops 50,000 on): it sits on one of the two dashed lines.

The two exceptions are the ends of the circle. At the low end, s = 4 (E(x, 2), which goes to A(x − 3, 10): s′ = 16 = 4s) (low). At the midpoint 2s = L + 2 (the vertical line at 1/2) the next stop is an all-ones tape of odd length L − 5 (mid_ones): that is the only way a stop leads to an all-ones tape of the fatal parity, and after it a few phases run off the stops before the map resumes. A safe all-ones tape A(0, y) takes one extra step, to E(3, y − 2) with modulus L + 4 (ones_even). Those few phases can make another all-ones tape straight away, for a sparse list of lengths (2 → 7, 13 → 19, 39 → 47, 93 → 103, …: the odd targets are 7 and 7·2j − 9 (j ≥ 2), never fatal, since 7·2j − 9 = 28·2k − 2k − 13 has no solution; checked for n < 3·105). All-ones tapes of the other parity come from 2s = L and are ordinary stops.

So the machine halts only if the doubling map hits its midpoint exactly at a stop with L = 28·2k − 2k − 8, one stop out of a list that doubles. Unrolled, sj = 2js0 − Σ εi2j−1−iLi with Li = L0 + 2i and εi the wrap bits: the itinerary is a binary expansion, as chaotic as the doubling map itself.

No congruence helps. Taken mod N, both branches are always possible, so the reachable residues fill everything: the search over the whole phase map mod N (N ≤ 48, and 64 to 256) always reaches a fatal residue, and in the coordinates (s, L) the two branches reach every pair of the right parity mod every N ≤ 64. What decides the halt is the size of s, not a residue. The crude model (s uniform on the circle) predicts about ½ ln L midpoint all-ones tapes up to modulus L, 7.1 in the first 20,000,000 phases against 4 seen, and a chance below 10−10 of a fatal one after the 1011 phases already run.

The four machines 1RB1RD_0LC1RA_1RA1LC_0RB1LE_1RF0LD_---0RE, 1RB1LE_1RC1RA_0LD1RB_1LE1LD_1RF0LA_---0RE, 1RB1RD_0LC1RA_1LA1LC_1RA1LE_1RF0LD_---0RE, 1RB1RD_0LC1RA_0RA1LC_1RA1LE_1RF0LD_---0RE all run this map; only the offsets, the low end and the fatal list differ. The orbits of 1RB1LE_1RC1RA_0LD1RB_1LE1LD_1RF0LA_---0RE and 1RB1RD_0LC1RA_1LA1LC_1RA1LE_1RF0LD_---0RE even coincide, stop for stop, for most of their first 106 stops.

Proof

The phase map in Lean

Every configuration the run passes through at a phase boundary is one of three shapes: E(x, z) = E>0 (10)^x 1^z, A(x, y) = (10)^x 1^y A>0 and the bounce frame Q(m, j, t) = (10)^m 1 D>0 0 (1010)^j 1^t. The function next in lean/Phases.lean sends each one to the next shape the machine reaches, and next_sound proves each of its 25 cases for all parameters, from symbolic runs generated by py/pieces.py. Nothing is left out: next is undefined only at E(x, 0) and E(x, 1), and those halt.

So the question is exactly whether the orbit of A(0, 0) (the blank tape) under next reaches E(x, 0) or E(x, 1) (halts_iff). The first 108 iterates do not (native_decide); by the step counts that is about 4.1·1021 machine steps. The even phases cannot reach a halt without an all-ones tape in between, and for all-ones tapes shorter than 105 cells Lean checks which ones lead to a halt first: n = 3 or 28·2k − 2k − 13 (15, 41, 95, 205, …). lean/Fatal.lean proves that list for every n. lean/Ones.lean puts the pieces together (halt_needs_ones): the machine halts if and only if its run reaches an all-ones tape of fatal length. The last open step is to show that the run never hits one.

The fatal all-ones lengths, for every n

ones_fatal_all: for every n with n = 3 or 28·2k − 2k − 13 for some k, the machine halts from the all-ones tape A(0, n). ones_safe_all: for every other n, any iterate of next from A(0, n) that halts comes after another all-ones tape. The proof: from E(x, z) with z odd the run is a chain of doublings E(x, z) → E(2x+4, z−2x−6) that halts exactly when z = zHalt(x, k) (e_fate, strong induction), with zHalt(x, k) closed-form (zHalt_closed).

lean/Ones.lean
A halt needs an all-ones tape of fatal length

halt_needs_ones: the machine halts from blank tape if and only if some iterate of next from A(0, 0) (the blank tape) is an all-ones tape A(0, n) with n fatal (n = 3 or 28·2k − 2k − 13 (15, 41, 95, 205, …)). halt_passes_ones says the same in machine steps: a halting run passes through the configuration A(0, n) for a fatal n. The proof is a strong induction on the number of phases: by ones_safe_all a halt after a non-fatal all-ones tape comes after a later all-ones tape, so the last all-ones tape before the halt is fatal; conversely a fatal one halts (ones_fate).

lean/Doubling.lean
At its stops the phase map is the doubling map s ↦ 2s mod L

At a left stop E(x, z) (z even) put s = z + 2, L = 2x + z + 8; at a right stop A(x, y) (y even) put s = y + 6, L = 2x + y + 8. From every stop except the few below the next stop, one to three phases on, is again a stop, with L′ = L + 2 and s′ = 2s if 2s ≤ L, s′ = 2s − L otherwise (doubling, all sizes). The exceptions: the low end, s = 4 (E(x, 2), which goes to A(x − 3, 10): s′ = 16 = 4s) (low), and the midpoint 2s = L + 2, where the next stop is the all-ones tape of odd length L − 5 (mid_ones). A safe all-ones tape A(0, y) takes one extra step, to E(3, y − 2) with modulus L + 4 (ones_even). The all-ones tapes of the other parity, the safe one, come from 2s = L inside the map. So the recurrence behind the all-ones lengths, in closed form, is the doubling map with a modulus that grows by 2 a stop; 1RB1RD_0LC1RA_1RA1LC_0RB1LE_1RF0LD_---0RE, 1RB1LE_1RC1RA_0LD1RB_1LE1LD_1RF0LA_---0RE, 1RB1RD_0LC1RA_0RA1LC_1RA1LE_1RF0LD_---0RE run the same map with other offsets.

No congruence keeps the run off the fatal list

The residue search of the doubling-map trio, on the whole phase map: for every modulus N from 2 to 48 and for N = 64, 96, 128, 192 and 256, the phase map taken mod N (every branch the residues allow, both halves of every bounce count) is run to a closed set of classes from the start; in every case some reachable all-ones class is a fatal length mod N. In the coordinates (s, L) there can be none: the two branches s ↦ 2s and s ↦ 2s − L, with L ↦ L + 2, reach every pair of residues of the right parity mod every N ≤ 64. What keeps the run off the list is only which branch is taken, that is the size of s, not a residue (24 November 2026).

The chance of a halt from here on

Leaving aside the relay lengths, whose targets are never fatal, a halt needs the midpoint 2s = L + 2 at a stop whose modulus is L = 28·2k − 2k − 8 (then L − 5 is a fatal length); those moduli double from one to the next. If s sits at a random place below L, the midpoint at a given stop has chance about 1/L, so the expected number of midpoint all-ones tapes up to modulus L is about ½ ln L: 7.1 for the first 20,000,000 phases (L up to 13,300,000), against 4 seen. The chance of ever reaching a fatal one beyond the 1011 phases already run (L about 6.7·1010) is about the sum of 1/L over the fatal moduli above it, below 10−10.
