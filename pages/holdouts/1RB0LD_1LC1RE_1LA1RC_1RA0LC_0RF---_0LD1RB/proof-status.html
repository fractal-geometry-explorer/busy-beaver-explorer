1RB0LD_1LC1RE_1LA1RC_1RA0LC_0RF---_0LD1RB

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The pieces of a sweep are proved in Lean for every size (leftward recoding of even blocks into pairs, the turn at an odd block, the rightward regrouping of pairs by threes); the global dynamics, a few defects drifting through a background of 1^8 blocks, is described from simulation only. No halt in the first 1011 steps (The C simulator tools/c/tmrun.c runs 1011 steps from blank tape without a halt (522,424 cells visited).).

The pieces of a sweep

For every k and every surrounding tape: leftward, an even block 1^(2k+2) becomes the segment (1 0)^k 1 0 0 and C moves on (left_even); an odd block 1^(2k+1) becomes the same kind of segment, glues two 1s to the block on its left and turns the head right (left_odd); rightward, every three pairs 1 0 1 0 1 0 become 1^5 0 (right_group) and a segment end 1 0 0 met in phase 0 becomes 1 1 1 (right_seg0).

heuristic Evidence only

Blocks of 3d+2, defects in a background of 1^8

At the right-end moments of the first 3·107 steps every block right of the start cell but the first (cut at the start cell) and the last (3) has 3d+2 ones; 1^8 (d = 2) is the background: its 3 pairs plus segment end regroup to 1^5 plus 3 = 1^8 again. A handful of defects (digit groups such as 4 1, 7, 1 5, 3 1, 6, 1 2 1) sit between long runs of 8s; the runs to their left shrink and those to their right grow by one per pass, so the defects drift left, and the leftmost group of digits changes slowly, like the top digits of a counter.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Open. A proof would need the rules for every phase of the rightward pass and a closed description of how defects move, collide and are created; the dynamics is a recoding between halving and grouping by threes, of the Collatz-like kind.
