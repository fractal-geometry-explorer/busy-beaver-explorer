One map on words, and a counter in base 4 inside it

1RB1LF_1LC1RB_1LF1LD_1LE0RD_1LB---_1LA0LA

Nobody knows whether it halts. Cut the tape into pairs of cells: every visit of the growing left end then applies one recursive map to the tape, and that map makes a counter in base 4 out of long blocks of the word 111010. The counter is proved, phase by phase; the rare moments when the head reaches the other end of the tape are not understood, and they decide the question.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in E reading 1. E is entered only from D0 (writes 1, moves L): 1 D>0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

How to read the tape

Pairs of cells: X = 10, Y = 11

Each time the head is in state F on a 0 with only 0s to its left (a wall visit) the tape to its right is a word of the pairs X = 10 and Y = 11. The wall is the growing end: it moves one pair to the left at every visit. The right end of the tape stays where it is for very long stretches.

From one wall visit to the next the word S becomes G(S):

G(Y^a X X U)     = X^a Y Y Y U                 turn back at the first double X
G(Y^a X Y^k X U) = X^a Y G^k(Y Y U)            a digit k: k visits of the rest
G(Y^a)           = X^a Y Y                     the blank right end, reached in B

What the head does: B runs right over the Y's. At a double X it turns back. At X Y^k X it erases the k Y's and refills two pairs, so k − 1 blank pairs stay behind it. On the way home F swaps X and Y in every pair, and a blank pair sends it right again: each blank pair is a small wall for the rest of the tape. These moves are proved in Lean (turn, digit, flip, reflect).

What the map does

A counter in base 4, level after level

Write the word as digits (the number of Y's before each X). The six-cell word 111010 is the digit pair 1 0, and the tape is 000 (1 0)N1 w ... (1 0)N2 w ... (1 0)N3 w .... The digit word w behind a block goes through four phases:

0000000  ->  3 00000  ->  0000 3  ->  3 00 3  ->  0000000 and a carry

One phase is two wall visits. The block gains one 1 0 in the second and in the fourth phase, and in the fourth the head goes on into the rest of the tape. For the first block all of this is a Lean theorem for every block length n and every tape R behind it: eight visits take 000 (1 0)n 0000000 R to 000 (1 0)n+2 0000000 R', and R' is exactly what one visit makes of R. So the rest of the tape lives the same life, eight visits to one: the second block grows four times more slowly than the first, the third sixteen times, and so on (5998, 1492, 372, 88, 21 copies after 109 steps).

What is not understood

When the head reaches the right end

When the last level overflows the head arrives at the blank right end in state D, and the recursion no longer applies. In the first 2·1010 steps this happened at steps 1,937, 57,942, 61,755, 63,313, 83,647, 125,133, 48,737,226. Some of these events only rewrite the last few digits; others are resets: a stretch of blank pairs is left inside the tape, a new counter starts to its right and uses the stretch as its wall, and the old blocks stay frozen until the stretch is used up.

The machine stops only when E reads a 1. The counter never brings that about, but some words of pairs do: on the tape X Y^7 X X a single wall visit halts after 253 steps (a Lean theorem; this tape does not occur in the run from blank). So whether the machine halts depends on the words that the right-end events leave behind, and nobody knows a rule for them.

Where a halt can come from

A closed tape language from windows of 6 cells (closed: yes) covers the run; the only places it lets the halting transition E1 fire are E reading a 1 with the abstract left side in a window of 0s, followed by at least six 1s. With windows of 4 and 8 the same picture.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The counter cannot halt; a right-end event could leave a halting word, and no rule for these events is known.

README, Next steps
The right-end events

When the last level of the counter overflows the head reaches the blank right end in D; what follows (parity changes, blank stretches inside the tape, resets of the counter) has no known rule, and it decides whether a halting word ever appears.
