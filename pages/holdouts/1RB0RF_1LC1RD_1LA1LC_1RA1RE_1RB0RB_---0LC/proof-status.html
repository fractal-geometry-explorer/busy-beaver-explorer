What is known about 1RB0RF_1LC1RD_1LA1LC_1RA1RE_1RB0RB_---0LC

1RB0RF_1LC1RD_1LA1LC_1RA1RE_1RB0RB_---0LC

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The head does not sweep the whole tape each time: it zigzags, rewriting the tape between two forms (blocks 1^a 0^b at the left turn, blocks of ones of length 3k+2 separated by single 0s at the right turn). Turns at the left end are rare (9 in the first 3·106 steps); the left end grows in bursts whose sizes are multiples of 1,065 cells, after which the first hundreds of cells of the tape are exactly as before. The sweep rules are proved in Lean; the burst mechanism is not understood.

Lean proves no halt in the first 107 steps (10,000 by the kernel alone); the C simulator finds none in 109.

How it works

The tape alternates between two encodings. At the left turn it is blocks of ones and blocks of zeros, 0 1^13 0^5 1^4 0^5 1^4 0^5 1^4 0^2 1^12 0^4 …; at the right turn it is blocks of ones whose lengths are mostly 3k+2 (2, 5, 8, 11, …) separated by single 0s. The rewriting is done by a zigzagging head, not one clean sweep, so turns at the ends are rare.

The left end grows in bursts. From the second burst on, every burst adds a multiple of 1,065 cells, and after each one the first hundreds of cells of the tape are exactly as before: a burst rebuilds the left structure further out. How it does so, and why 1,065, is the open part.

Growth shape: sqrt/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.406). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.559).

The left end of the visited tape grows in bursts: after 125,047 steps by 3,410 cells, then by 1,065, 1,065, 1,065, 5,325, 5,325, 8,520 and 2,130 cells (at 5.5·106, 1.5·107, 3.0·107, 4.5·107, 2.3·108, 3.5·108, 5.9·108 steps): from the second on, all multiples of 1,065.

A burst rebuilds the left end

The tape at the left turn after a burst starts with the same 699 cells (1,613 at the next burst) as the tape at the left turn before it, although the left end has moved thousands of cells: the leftmost structure is re-created at the new end.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The burst sizes suggest an exact recursive structure (multiples of 1,065 = 3·5·71), which has not been found.
