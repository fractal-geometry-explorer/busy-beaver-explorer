What is known about 1RB1RA_1LC0RD_1LD1LB_1LE0LF_0RA---_0LB1LF

1RB1RA_1LC0RD_1LD1LB_1LE0LF_0RA---_0LB1LF

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 26 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The tape is an irregular list of runs with one long periodic block in it, (001111111001)^3766 at the left end at step 882,788,555 (and its translation (011011101011)^2687 at the right end), which grows steadily while the rest stays irregular. Measured, not proved. Halting needs E on a 1, entered only from D on a 0; E has always found a 0. DeepMind's PR 93 engine does not carry over: the table is PR 93's T3 with one transition changed, and that change breaks the engine's batched calls (Lean proves the changed rules). No halt in 1012 steps (direct C simulation), nor in 3.5·1018 steps (accelerated simulator with a chain-phase macro step); an exact engine with no jumps and proved rules reaches 2·1017 (halving-chain engine, 22 November 2026).

Shift rules of the sweeps

Lean proves, for every count and every tape around, the shift rules of the leftward sweep in B: it turns (01)^n into (11)^n (2 steps per copy) and (011)^n into (001)^n (5 steps per copy).

DeepMind's PR 93 macro rules on this machine

With E and F exchanged and the detour D0 1LE, E0 0RA, A1 1RA replaced by one step 1RA (it ends in the same configuration unless the cell left of D is 1, where the machine halts), the table is 1RB1RA_1LC0RD_1LD1LB_1RA0LE_0LB1LE: the table T3 of DeepMind's halting proof in busycoq PR 93 with E1 changed (T3: 0LF, then F0 0LB). Lean proves 11 of PR 93's macro rules for this machine for every count, among them the four that change: where T3 writes the digit D (k+1) (D n = 1 (11)^n 00) on entering CfgJ = l <C 100 r, this machine writes D k 10.

pr93_collisionD, pr93_collide, pr93_defect0D, pr93_defect0, pr93_finishP and six unchanged rules, lean/EngineRules.lean (written by py/engine_rules.py)

The halving-chain engine's rules

Lean proves, for every count and every tape around, the two moves by which the halving-chain engine c/hce.cpp crosses a digit D n to the right (pr93_rightD0, pr93_rightD), and the 100 rules it learned and used most on the way to 1013 steps (74% of the uses of learned rules). symtm proves all 3,481 learned rules; the kernel check of the rest is not done (some proofs exceed the kernel's time limit).

PR 93's engine does not carry over

Of PR 93's 18 basic rules, 14 hold unchanged (direct simulation, counts up to 4, random tape around), and none of the 18 has D reading 0 next to a 1, so a halt can only come where an engine steps cell by cell. But a collision now leaves 10 10 (PR 93's call D_j 10 D0 D0 r to D_(j+1) D2 10 r becomes D_(j+1) D1 10 10 r), and a call that meets 10 10 runs on into the tape beyond instead of returning. PR 93's batched calls have no counterpart, so its engine cannot be patched the way it was for T4.

No halt in the first 1018 steps

The accelerated simulator rsim.py runs 984,180,560,109,706,163 steps without a halt (3 hours). Its faster C++ port c/rsim_fast.cpp follows the same trajectory (same step counts, rules and jumps at 106 to 1013; tape equal to direct simulation at 8 points up to 3·1010) and runs 1,000,000,000,008,656,179 steps without a halt (65 minutes). The rules it applies are exact; its jumps over repeated tape shapes are validated, not proved.

c/rsim_fast.cpp, py/rsim_fast_validate.py

No halt in the first 3.5·1018 steps

The chain-phase engine c/rsim_chain.cpp adds to rsim_fast.cpp a macro step for the phases where its jumps never fire. It reads the right tape as a chain of levels 1^(2a+1) 00, predicts one increment with a learned radius-1 automaton on the separators and an affine step cost, and uses a pattern only after two checks against exact increments. Up to 9.84·1017, all 2,796 chain-phase exits (step count and tape hash) are the same with and without macro steps. It runs 3,588,977,699,892,617,375 steps without a halt (20 minutes). The automaton and costs are checked, not proved.

c/rsim_chain.cpp

No halt in 2·1017 steps, without jumps

The halving-chain engine c/hce.cpp, exact and with no jumps over repeated tape shapes, runs 200,000,000,629,086,925 steps of the reduced table without a halt (27 minutes). It crosses digits by the Lean-proved rules, simulates everything else cell by cell or by its learned rules (proved by symtm), and its tape equals direct simulation at four points up to 3·108 steps.

heuristic Evidence only

Growth shape: power/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like a power of the step count other than 1/2 (fitted exponent 0.663). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.473).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

A periodic block in an irregular list

At the last left extension before 109 steps (step 882,788,555) the tape is E>0 1^17 0010 1^3 0 (01)^3 1^4 00 1^5 (001)^4 1^8 … (001111111001)^3766 00 (10011)^3 …: runs of 1s of length 3a+1 (7,550 of 7,582) separated by 00 (7,574 of 7,581 runs of 0s have length 2), and a periodic block of 3,766 copies of 1^7 00 1 00. At the last right extension (step 999,852,606) the same block reads (011011101011)^2687 followed by (01)^1608.

py/observe.py, tools/c/tmrun.c

Never one step from a halt

The machine halts when E reads a 1, and E is entered only from D reading a 0 (D0 → 1LE). In the first 3·106 steps D reads a 0 297,649 times, always with a 0 to its left.

direct simulation

Digits, halving chains and periodic blocks

At left-end visits up to 3·1010 steps the tape is a list of digits D n with a few 10 defects: halving chains such as D5773 D0 D0 D2884 10 D1440 10 D0 D0 D0 D720 D1 D359 D1 D179 … and growing periodic blocks (D2 D1)^15875, (D3 D0)^3841.

direct simulation (C)

A left-end counter

Near 1012 steps the left end of the tape is exactly periodic: every 83 far passes it eats 4 copies of D7 D0 D0 D2 and the right end gains 166 D0. By 1013 it has become an aperiodic, growing counter that eats (D3 D0 D0)^58372 one copy per two far passes; the middle of the tape (mostly D0) passes through unchanged.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The halving-chain engine runs it exactly to 2·1017 steps, but a proof needs the left-end counter, which is not understood.
