1RB1RC_1LC0RE_1LD0LB_1RA1LC_1RA1RF_---1RD

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 14 November 2026.

In short

The question is open, but the machine is now well understood. Behind a block of 1s that grows by two each sweep sits a chain of small counters that each count 0, 1, 2, 3, followed by a chaotic tail. The chain is exactly predictable, so the run was followed, with exact step counts, through 2.80·1036 sweeps (2.35·1073 steps): no halt, and the third event turned the block into a chain of 4.67·1035 counters. Lean proves that the machine can only halt on the blank tape beyond the far end, which the head will not see again for more than (4/3)4.67·10^35 sweeps. Whether that contact, or a later one, halts depends on the chaotic tail.

A halt can only happen beyond the far end

Every configuration of the run from blank tape lies in a closed tape language (windows of six cells on each side of the head plus the parity of the distance to the next 0; 5,545 abstract configurations). In it, the halting transition can only fire with blank tape from the head on. So the machine halts if and only if F steps onto the blank tape right of everything.

holdouts/triage.json

The region is a chain of mod-4 counters and a tail

At each visit of the left end in state D the tape is D>0 1^n R; in pairs a = 01, b = 11, R = aaa S[s_0] ... S[s_m-1] junk with S = [baabaa, abbabb, bbbbbb, aaaaaa]. A double visit adds 1 to s_0 and goes on to the next counter unless the counter was 0; the junk changes only at sweeps that pass the whole chain, by a map of its own (3,000 recorded passes replayed behind four different chains). The reference implementation agrees with the direct run at every visit up to 6,000 (step count, block, region), through two events.

The next contact: more than (4/3)4.67·10^35 sweeps away

A sweep passes a chain of m counters once in about (4/3)m double visits (exact formula in jump); the new chain has 4.67·1035 counters, and the 221-cell junk of epoch 3 needed 2.6·107 passes before the event.

py/chain.py

In short-block models every run halts, at the far end

The real machine with the block cut back to 4 cells at every visit, from random regions of 8 pairs: all of 2,000 runs halt within 12 events, with blank tape right of the head. With the last 21 or 41 cells of the true tail of epoch 4 at the far end, none of 400 runs halts at the first contact.

holdouts/1RB1RC_1LC0RE_1LD0LB_1RA1LC_1RA0RF_---0RC/c/smallblock.c

Growth shape: fixed/sqrt

The head sweeps across the whole tape and adds two cells per sweep at the left end, so the tape grows like the square root of the step count.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. It comes down to the junk: an autonomous map on a few thousand cells, applied once per pass of the chain, with no regular digits found (blocks of 18, 28, 28 cells with 55, 181, 292+ states, then no pattern). Which of its far-end contacts halt is the whole question.
