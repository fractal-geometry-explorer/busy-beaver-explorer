Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_1LE---

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether it halts. Its engine is decoded and proved: a base-3 counter on the left and a chain of binary words on the right tick together, and each word that wraps sends 3 to the next. So between two changes of era the whole right side is one counter. A simulator built on this has followed the run for 5.1·1041 events without a halt; what a change of era hands on is the open question.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from D1 (writes 1, moves L): 1 D>1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

Decoded

Three counters on one tape

At the moments we call events (the head in state A, on a 0, just right of the left counter) the tape reads

0∞ 1 0 [N] A> 0 [M] 1 [R] …

  • N, a base-3 counter: 3-cell digits 100 = 0, 110 = 1, 010 = 2, most significant first, its lowest digit next to the head;
  • M, a binary counter: 4-cell digits 0010 = 0, 1010 = 1, lowest digit first (next to the head);
  • R, a second binary counter after a single 1: 4-cell digits 1000 = 0, 1010 = 1, lowest digit first.

In the words a = 10, b = 110 of the older description, M's digits read as sections a| and aa, which is why the tape looked like sections of a's split by 000.

The rule

One event: N + 1, M + 1, and R + 3 when M wraps

From one event to the next the head runs right over M (and over R only when M wraps), comes back, crawls into N, adds one there and returns. The net effect:

counterchange per event
N+1 in base 3; trailing 2s carry; when N = 2…2 it becomes 0…0 with one digit more (the tape grows 3 cells at the left)
M+1 in binary; trailing 1s carry; from 1…1 it wraps to 0…0 (same width)
Runchanged, except when M wraps: then +3 in binary

So N counts events (mod 3width), M counts them mod 2width, and R gains 3 every 2width of M events: two different bases running side by side. Example from the run of 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB--- (M with 2 digits, R with 8): at steps 12,203, 12,223, 12,243, 12,253, 12,345 the triple (N, M, R) is (5, 0, 92), (6, 1, 92), (7, 2, 92), (8, 3, 92), (9, 0, 95).

Each case of this rule is a theorem in lean/Events.lean, proved for every size: 9 cases for N (lowest digit 0 or 1 after 0, 1 or k+2 twos, or the wrap of 2…2 at the left end), times 6 for M and R (no wrap, carry over i+1 ones, or a wrap of M with i+1 digits combined with the 4 cases of R's two low bits and a carry over j ones). 54 cases (exhaustive by construction of events.py), one theorem each, with its exact step count, e.g. 12i + 12j + 10k + 116 steps for a carry over k twos in N, a wrap of M with i+1 digits and R ending in 10 followed by j ones. When M does not wrap, nothing right of M is read, so these rules hold whatever lies beyond.

Checked

How long the run keeps this shape

In the clean shape R is followed by 1 0 1 and blank tape. This run reaches it at step 132,141 (N = 35 (5 digits), M = 2 (2 digits), R = 0 (12 digits)) and then 5,556 (to step 3·106) consecutive events follow the rule, until R = 4,095 = 212 − 1 at step 3·106, about to overflow.

An overflow of R (its carry running into the cells after it) is not covered by the rules: there the head goes past R and rebuilds the right part; in 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB--- the transition D1 (the only way into F, whose 1 halts) is used in the first 105 steps only at steps 138, 1,008, 3,177, 7,571 (before the clean shape) and 20,756, 53,353, 92,444, 92,861 (at the first three overflows of R). After the first overflows the cells right of R are no longer just 1 0 1: they hold a further structure starting 1 0 1 1 0 …, and M and R take other widths. Counting every event whose left part is N and whose right part starts 0 [M] 1, 42,230 events in the first 3·106 steps follow from the previous one by the rule and 3,227 do not (the wraps of M that touch the undecoded part); at step 3·106: N = 18,571 (9 digits), M with 1 digit, R with 5 digits.

The picture

Two words and a separator

Write a for 1 0 and b for 1 1 0. Apart from a few cells at the ends, the tape is a string of a's and b's broken into sections by extra 0s (0 0 0 between two 1s). At its visits to the left end the first section is a b b … b followed by a's and b's, and the later sections are mostly runs of a's. Writing 1 for a and 2 for b, the tape at step 645,244,842 of the first sibling is 1222222222222222 | 111 | 111 | 11111 | 111 | 12 | 11111112 | 111111111 | 11111 | 11111 | 1 | 111 | 1 | 11.

Mechanism

Sweeps and an inner counter

The head moves through runs of words in one state, rewriting each copy the same way, for example A turning 0 1 1 into 1 0 0 or B turning 1 0 into 0 1 (proved in Lean for any number of copies: a_right_01, a_right_011, b_right_10, b_right_11, b_right_110, e_right_10, e_right_11, e_right_110). Its excursions do not usually reach the ends: the turning points far from the left end follow the ruler pattern of a binary counter with digits four cells wide (in a stretch of 17,000 steps from step 28,601 of the first sibling the far turning points are 43, 47, 43, 51, 43, 47, 43, 55, … cells from the left end). The ends are visited rarely, and each visit to the left end finds the whole tape rearranged.

Halting

Where it could halt

The machine halts only when F reads a 1. A closed tape language with windows of six cells (an over-approximation of every configuration reachable from blank tape, found and checked by bbtools.ctl) allows that only with 110001, 110011 from the head on: F on the first 1 of a word 1 1 0 that ends a section. Whether the run ever puts F there is the open question.

After the overflows

A cascade, then layers of words

After the first overflows of R a counter C appears between M and R. In its plain form an event reads

0∞ 1 0 [N] A> 0 [Z] 1 1 [C] 0 1 [R] 0∞

with N in base 3 as before, Z the counter called M above, a new binary counter C (4-cell digits 0001 = 0, 0101 = 1) and R as before. The rule becomes a cascade: every event adds 1 to N and to Z; when Z wraps, C gets 3; when C wraps, R gets 3. That is 162 cases, each a Lean theorem with its exact step count, and the same for all six siblings.

In this sibling the plain layout occurs only early in the run. The full events of the later run carry more words between Z and R (in tokens: Zk, Ck words of k digits, Xk a word of k a z/a a digits, Rk the last one):

Decoded

One law for every era

All the layouts of the run, the plain cascade, the X layer and everything after, are one shape: Z followed by a chain of binary words, alternately of two kinds (W with digits 0001, 0101 and U with digits 1000, 1010), separated by the blocks 1 1, 0 1 and 1 0 1 1, any of them possibly empty:

0∞ 1 0 [N] A> 0 [Z] 1 1 [W₁] 0 1 [U₁] 1 0 1 1 [W₂] 0 1 [U₂] … 1 0 1 1 [Wm] 0 1 [Um] 0∞

Every word is a binary counter, and every wrap sends 3 to the next word. Each event adds 1 to Z; when Z wraps, W₁ gets 3; when W₁ wraps, U₁ gets 3; and so on. An empty word wraps at once, so it passes 3 times what it got. In one number: Z + 2|Z|/3 · (W₁ + 2|W₁|/3 · (U₁ + …)) grows by exactly 1 per event. The rules of the earlier sections are special cases: the cascade is one pair (C, R), the X layer two pairs (∅, X) and (C, R), and for the X layer the law gives the odd-looking C + 24/2k (an empty W makes the 3 into 9, X of k digits takes 9 mod 2k and passes the rest on, times 3).

The law is proved in Lean for every number of words and every width (lean/Layers.lean), from 23 visit rows of blocks of 1, 2 and 4 cells composed by induction.

Because the right side is one counter, a simulator can jump straight to the last event before a carry would leave the last word, and run only the change of era cell by cell. The first eras of the run:

Open

What is left

The engine is proved for every layout: N + 1 on the left, and on the right the chain law. A halt can only come during a change of era, when a carry runs off the last word: every event that follows the law is a run of the machine that never reads the halting 1. At the end of an era all words are near their largest values; after the change they hold small values, the boundaries between words have moved by a digit, the last word has a digit more, and N has either gained 1 or given 2 to 9 of its lowest base-3 digits to new words. Open: (1) a rule for this hand-over; (2) whether some change of era can ever put the head on the halting 1.

New view

One visit of a stream

Cut the tape right of the event cell into tokens of two cells: z = 00, a = 10, c = 01. Everything from R on, the blank tape included, is one stream of a and z, and one visit of the head in state B does this to it: the first z becomes a, and every second a before it becomes z.

If the first z is at an odd place the visit comes out in state A: with digits a z = 0 and a a = 1 this is the binary increment of R. If it is at an even place (all digits 1 up to the blank tape) the visit comes out in state C and leaves the digits shifted by one token. So R has no overflow into something else; the rare case is a visit that comes out in C.

Both cases, and M's increment and wrap (three visits of the stream), are proved in Lean for every size from 32 token rows, each of which is a small theorem about one token and the nested visits of the rest. What the c tokens of M do after a visit that comes out in C is in those rows but not yet composed; that is where the question of halting now sits.

The chain law, for every layout

At an event the right side is 0∞ 1 0 [N] A> 0 [Z] 1 1 [W₁] 0 1 [U₁] 1 0 1 1 [W₂] 0 1 [U₂] … 1 0 1 1 [Wm] 0 1 [Um] 0∞: Z (digits 0010, 1010), each W (0001, 0101) and each U (1000, 1010) binary, lowest digit first, of any width, W and U possibly empty, any number m of pairs. What a change of era hands on

At the end of an era the words are at or near their largest values; after the change they hold small values, the boundaries between words have moved by a digit, the last word has one digit more, and N has either gained 1 (the change is one event, and two words disappear) or lost 2 to 9 base-3 digits, depending on N mod 9 and more, which become new words. chain_event, chain_event_open, lean/Stage.lean; chain_event_junction, lean/Junction.lean
The stage law, for every layout

The stages are events of the chain law with the word Z empty, and with a chain of base-3 words on the left (parted by 1010) in place of the single counter N. From one event to the next the left words get one increment, and a word that wraps sends 2 to the next, because a separator passes two increments on (five left rows, lv_d0 … lv_top). With Z empty, the right side gets one unit (Q + 3, a wrap sends 3 to the next word). The head passes the turning cell twice per event. So the fitted gifts of the stages are this law read two visits at a time. Proved for every Z, every list of left words and any tape after either chain. A second form, chain_event_open, lets the chain end in an open item 11 [Q] followed by any tape. A unit into it adds 3 to Q, up to a wrap of Q. This covers the half-digit item 11 [Q] 00 11 [Q'] 01 [P] met at about 10137 events up to a wrap of Q. A third form, chain_event_junction, covers the wraps: the cells 0011 after Q act as one more digit of Q. The first wrap turns them into 0111 and stops; the second turns them into the Q digit 0 and adds 2 to Q'. Then the tape reads in the grammar again, as one item with Q, 0 and Q' as its first word. So the item heals after two wraps, and every event still adds exactly one to the number of the chain.

What a change of stage hands on

A stage ends at the event where a carry leaves R, the last word. That event runs to the blank after R and comes back in state C, through every item to the event cell. At that moment each item is in the middle of one unit of the chain law: the a-th increment of Q wrapped it, and the b-th increment of P sent the unit on (a and b are 1, 2 or 3, and follow from the chain law). The return in C moves the 11 that opens each item 2 cells right if a = 1 and 2 cells left otherwise, and the 01 before P the same way by b. It rewrites Q as 2 if a ≥ 2 and 0 if a = 1, and P likewise by b, with the carries of the chain law; R keeps its width and only its top digit is 1. In 150 stages from about 10128 events, 186 changes of stage have Z empty; the rule covers 116, and all 116 match it cell by cell. Set apart: 65 at an empty word, 4 where the head goes past R, 1 where the new event cell lies in the first item.
