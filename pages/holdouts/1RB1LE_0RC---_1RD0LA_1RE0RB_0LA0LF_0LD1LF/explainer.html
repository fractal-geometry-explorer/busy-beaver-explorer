Started on a blank tape, this machine builds blocks of the word 100 separated by walls, and the walls are the digits of a base-4 odometer that the head counts up one trip at a time; the blocks drain at rates 4, 12, 48, … per visit of the left end. The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in B reading 1. B is entered only from A0 (writes 1, moves R): A>0 1; D1 (writes 0, moves R): D>1 1. So a halt is the moment one of these patterns appears, with the head on the cell after >.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.056). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.468).

After 109 steps the tape spans 66,928 cells (50 left of the start, 49,870 right), with a density of 1s of 0.333.

Down the columns, the first block loses 4 words per visit, the second 12, the third 48, then 192, 768. Between two such visits the head shuttles between the walls and the right end on a base-4 schedule: four short trips for each trip one wall further, and so on. Each trip moves a wall by one word.

The machine halts exactly when A reads a 0 followed by a 1 or D reads a 1 followed by a 1. Whether the way exhausted blocks are refilled ever produces that is the open question.

How it works

A base-4 odometer of walls

Look at the tape each time the head arrives back at the right end. It is always L an1 w1 an2 w2 … wk ank+1 1 D>1 0: blocks of the word a = 100 separated by walls, and every wall has one of four shapes. The shapes are the digits of a base-4 counter. One trip of the head (from the right end and back) adds 1 to that counter: the head runs left over every wall showing digit 3, turning it into digit 0 (a carry), changes the first wall that is not a 3 to its next shape, and comes back. The last wall is the least significant digit.

Each change moves words between the two blocks beside the wall. Over a full cycle of four changes a wall takes 3 words from the block on its left and adds 6 to the block on its right. The left end 10 is one more digit, a binary one: a carry turns it into 00, and the next carry makes the head run to the left end (the visit of the left end) and back, after which every wall shows 0 again. So one period from one visit to the next is 2·4k trips, and over a period the first block loses 4 words, block j loses 12·4j−2 and the last block gains 12·4k−1: the countdown seen in the table above, now derived.

When a block between two walls runs out, the two walls become one wall (1010)r made of r walls with nothing between them. That wall is again a base-4 digit (its four shapes change only at its two ends) with the same totals −3 and +6, so the blocks to its right move one level down and drain four times slower. After every second visit of the left end the right end grows a new wall 1010 with a block of 5 words. All of these rules are proved in Lean for every block length (and every r), in lean/Odometer.lean.

Far out

The countdown, followed exactly far out

Because these rules apply many trips at once in closed form (all walls are digits; a jump stops only where a block gets short, a wall has a shape whose rule is not yet known, or the left end overflows), the run can be followed exactly far beyond any simulation: the macro in py/odometer/ reached step 101520 (1521 digits, given exactly in data/odometer/summary.json) after 8,776 visits of the left end, with 1261 walls on the tape, and no halt. Every trip that is not a plain digit change (a visit of the left end, a new wall, a block running out, walls merging) is run cell by cell on a copy of the tape whose long blocks are cut down to the same length modulo 12, and its step count is corrected by the measured 8 or 19 steps per word. The macro agrees with the plain simulation at every visit of the left end up to 5·1010 steps, step count included.

The run-outs of the first block. The first block counts down by 4 per visit. When it runs out, it and the walls next to it fold into the left end, which shrinks back to 10 over a few visits, and the next block becomes the first one, counting down by 4 again. The slider shows the tape at visits of the left end (the first 60 blocks, bar length on a logarithmic scale; the thin dark lines are the walls, taller for a wall made of more merged walls). Past the start-up, the first block ran out at visits 22 (step about 108), 35 (step about 1011), 37 (step about 1011), 437 (step about 1077), 445 (step about 1078), 8,516 (step about 101476). Each run-out so far folded the same way, and the run went on without a halt. At the end of the run the first block has 104,965 words left, so the next run-out comes about 26,241 visits later.

Halting. The machine halts only when A reads 0 followed by 1 or D reads 1 followed by 1. No digit change, carry, visit of the left end or new wall does that, and every wall shape met so far, (1010)r for r up to 13 and their four phases, is a digit whose rule is known. A halt would need a new local shape: for instance a block running out in a way not seen yet, or two short blocks next to each other. Whether that can ever happen is the remaining question; it is now a question about the list of block lengths and the order in which they run out, not about the machine.

It keeps the tape run-length coded and, for each stretch of tape from the left end, a macro (the run until the head first leaves that stretch) computed by a symbolic run of the machine with the long runs as variables; a macro is used only when the linear inequalities its symbolic run assumed hold, otherwise the stretch is run again by the same recursion. The countdown, derived

If no block runs out during a period, a period is 2·4k trips and the blocks change by −4, −12, −48, …, −12·4k−2, +12·4k−1 (from the digit totals −3, +6 per cycle). A wall of r merged walls is again a digit with the same totals.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. It halts exactly at A>0 1 or D>1 1; no rule of the odometer does that, so a halt needs a wall shape or a run-out not met yet.
