What is known about 1RB0LD_1RC1RA_1LD0RB_1LE1LA_1RF0RC_---1RE

1RB0LD_1RC1RA_1LD0RB_1LE1LA_1RF0RC_---1RE

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open, and reduced to a parity question about one fixed integer sequence. Lean proves (kernel) that the machine halts from blank tape if and only if some epoch ends with an even number of 1 0 pairs (halts_iff), and (native_decide) that the first three epochs end odd, so a halt can come at the end of the fourth epoch at the earliest, after more than 1079,981 steps; its outcome depends on the parity of a term of the sequence with an index of 39,991 digits, which nobody can compute.

Other proofs and outside reports

Community label

In the community's BB(6) tracker the machine's class is Bell-like. Its 3 November 2026 edition lists the machine as a cryptid: its halting is thought to come down to a Collatz-like question that no known method decides. These are labels read off the run by others, not results.

kernel Proved in Lean, kernel-checked

The macro step

From every phase start 0^∞ 1^a 0 0 (10)^K 1^(2j+1) C> 0^∞ the machine reaches the next phase start given by mstep, for all a, K, j, or halts exactly when a = 0 and K is even. The pieces (one sweep, the two phase ends, the reset, the halt) are runs proved for all values of the variables.

mstep_sound, mstep_halt, lean/Macro.lean; inner, end_even, end_odd, reset_a, reset_b, to_halt, lean/Rules.lean

Halting reduced to the macro machine

The machine halts from blank tape iff the orbit of mstep from (3, 0, 1) (reached after 18 steps) gets stuck.

halts_iff_mstep, lean/Macro.lean

Halting reduced to a parity question

Let seq be the orbit of the map g(K, j) = (j + 3⌊K/2⌋ + 1, 0) for even K and (j + 3⌊K/2⌋ + 3, 1) for odd K, from (0, 1). The walls are A₁ = 3 and Ai+1 = 2(j + 3⌊K/2⌋) + 7 where (K, j) = seq Ai, as long as K is odd. The machine halts iff some (seq Ai).K is even.

halts_iff, lean/Macro.lean

The first two epochs

The walls of epochs 2 and 3 are 25 and 227,097 (epochs 1 and 2 end with K = 7 and K = 75,697, both odd).

epochs_small, lean/Macro.lean

No halt in the first 10,000 steps

The list simulator of the shared library, proved to agree with the machine's semantics, runs 10,000 steps from blank tape without a halt.

blank_runs_kernel, lean/Blank.lean

native Proved in Lean with native_decide

The third epoch ends odd too

(seq 227097).K, a number of 132,845 bits, is odd, so the machine survives the third epoch; if it halts, it is at the end of epoch 4 or later.

epoch3_odd, halts_late, lean/Epoch3.lean

How long that is

With the exact step counts of the rules, epoch 2 ends after about 1010 steps and epoch 3 after about 1079,981 (py/macro.py epochs, 5 minutes of big-integer arithmetic); the fourth epoch has a wall of about 5.6·1039990 ones, so it takes more than 10^(10^39989) steps. The macro step and its step counts agree with the direct simulator for the first 18 phases (1.8 million steps) (py/macro.py check).

heuristic Evidence only

Probably halts, unimaginably late

If the parity of K at the end of each epoch behaves like a fair coin, the machine halts at the end of epoch 4 with probability 1/2, of epoch 5 with probability 1/4, and so on: it halts with probability 1, but no one can compute when, nor prove it.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known: it halts iff (seq A).K is even for one of the walls A = A₄, A₅, …; A₄ has 39,991 digits, so even the next parity is out of reach of direct computation.
