Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LC_1RC1RA_1LD0RE_1LA1LD_0RA1RF_0LC---

Nobody knows. The head sweeps over a counter and a list of runs of 1s; in every epoch each group of runs is halved and moved up one length, and the counter is rebuilt, so that everything grows by 5/4 every two epochs. It stops only if state B ever reads 0 1 1 1, which has not happened in 6.3×1014 steps.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from E1 (writes 1, moves R): E>1 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

The records list every step at which the head reaches a cell no earlier step reached, with the side, the width so far and the whole tape: the first place to look for a repeating macro structure.

What it does

A counter and runs of 1s whose lengths step by 6

After a sweep to the right the tape is a counter (100)^n followed by runs of 1s separated by 00; almost all runs have a length 6k+1 and they are sorted into groups: all the 7s, then the 13s, the 19s and so on, with a few odd runs between the groups. After a sweep to the left the same list is written differently: the counter is (011)^n and a run of length v is an alternating stretch 0101…0 of v cells, with 11 between stretches.

The head goes back and forth over all of it. Its right turning point (the front) moves slowly to the right through the list; behind it the runs are regrouped two by two into units that contain a timer, a run of 1s that loses 3 cells on every round trip and so tells the front when to move on. A unit made of runs of length 6j+1 holds the front for about 3(j−1) round trips (one trip for the 7s), and leaves one run of length 6j+7: the group is halved and moved up one length.

The cycle

Two phases per epoch

Phase 1. The head turns at the left end of the tape; every round trip adds 2 to the counter and moves the front. When the front reaches the right end of the tape, one sweep to the left regroups the whole list.

Phase 2. Now the head turns at the right end of the counter: every round trip takes 2 from the counter and makes one new run of 7. In each of these trips state B reads 0 1 1 0 twice (the F-event): B reading 0 1 1 0 gives 1 0 0 0 with A on the right (6 steps: B0 C1 E1 F0 C1 E0). When the counter is used up, phase 1 starts again.

Machines a2, a3 and a4 share one orbit: a4 has the same list at all 97 visits of the right end up to 1014 steps, a3 has it too with 5/4 of the step count.

The halving is not exact: a group sometimes loses a run, and the odd runs between the groups change from epoch to epoch with no visible rule. That is the chaotic part. Because no pattern repeats, the simulator of this family finds its shortcuts on the fly: it notices that a stretch of round trips repeats with the counts changing by constants and applies many of them at once.

Halting

What would make it stop

It halts exactly when state B reads 0 1 1 1: B0 C1 E1 put F on a 1 (F1 is the missing transition). Lean checks this path; that there is no other follows from the table: F is entered only by E1, E only by C1, and C by B0 (moving right), by A1 (moving left; then C1 puts E on the 0 that A has just written, and E0 goes on) or by F0 (moving left onto the 1 that E1 wrote; then C1 puts E on a 0 again).

In the run this has not happened in 6.3×1014 steps. The community tracker says “Several tables share one chaotic Bell orbit. The interior is chaotic.” The shared orbit is explained below; the two tables between a1 and a2 (E1 = 1RF with F0 = 0LD, and E1 = 0RF with F0 = 0LC) both halt at step 1,272.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.511). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.06).

After 109 steps the tape spans 38,771 cells (27,584 left of the start, 467 right), with a density of 1s of 0.634.

lean/Paths.lean, generated by holdouts/1RB0LC_1RC1RA_1LD0RE_1LA1LD_0RA1RF_0LC---/py/gen_paths.py
The local paths and the halting path

Outside the sweeps, the machine does a handful of local things, each proved for every tape around it: A on a 1 bounces (bounce_on_1, bounce_on_0_0, bounce_on_0_1); B reading 0 1 1 0 gives 1 0 0 0 with A on the right (6 steps: B0 C1 E1 F0 C1 E0) (fevent, the F-event, twice per round trip in the second half of an epoch); and B reading 0 1 1 1 halts: B0 C1 E1 put F on a 1 (fevent_halt). By the table this is the only way to halt: F is entered only by E1, E only by C1, and C by B0 (moving right), by A1 (moving left; then C1 puts E on the 0 that A has just written, and E0 goes on) or by F0 (moving left onto the 1 that E1 wrote; then C1 puts E on a 0 again).

The exact accelerated simulator agrees with direct simulation

bell_wsim.py keeps the tape as repeated words, crosses a repeated word in one move (an exact shift rule found on the fly), and at the left turns looks for a stretch of round trips after which the tape has the same shape with counts changed by constants; it then applies many such stretches at once, with the exact step count. Its tape, state and step count were compared with a plain C simulator at 704 tapes up to 2.04×1010 steps: all equal.

The shared orbit

Machines a2, a3 and a4 share one orbit: a4 has the same list at all 97 visits of the right end up to 1014 steps, a3 has it too with 5/4 of the step count. The reason is in the tables: a4 does with E, F what a2 does with C, E on the bounce of A, a3 replaces the step C0 of a2 by three steps with the same result, and all three turn B> 0 1 1 0 into 1 0 0 0 A>. They could part only where B reads 0 1 0 (a2 and a4 differ there: b_010), 0 1 1 1 (a2 and a4 halt, a3 goes on) or 0 0 1 (a3 halts); in 109 steps of a2 and a4, B never reads 0 1 0 and reads 0 0 only at the blank right end.

Heuristic
data/far_1e14.json
The epoch law

Read off the recorded lists, not proved: with n the counter and c7, c13, … the numbers of runs, one epoch gives c7′ ≈ n/2, c6j+7′ ≈ c6j+1/2 (the half, or one or two less) and n′ ≈ (5/2)·c7 (within 10−4 at 1014 steps). Hence the two interleaved geometric series in the table of counts, the factor 5 per +12 in length, growth by 5/4 every two epochs and step counts growing by 25/16. Between the groups sit a few odd runs that change from epoch to epoch without a visible rule; they are the chaotic part, and the halting pattern can only arise there.

Heuristic
direct C runs (not kept in the repository)
Where B reads 0 1 1

In the first 109 steps of a2 state B reads 0 1 1 20,972 times; in 20,941 of them the cells around are 111101[0]110101, in 29 001101[0]110101, and twice (steps 104 and 1,264) something else; the next cell is always 0. a1 has 20,998 such events with the windows 111100[0]110101 and 001100[0]110101. So after the first 1,300 steps the F-events seen are all of two harmless kinds; whether that holds for ever is the open question.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. It halts exactly if B ever reads 0 1 1 1. A proof of non-halting needs an invariant for the odd runs between the groups, which no closed rule describes so far; the simulator slows down beyond 1015 steps because those runs pile up at the right end.
