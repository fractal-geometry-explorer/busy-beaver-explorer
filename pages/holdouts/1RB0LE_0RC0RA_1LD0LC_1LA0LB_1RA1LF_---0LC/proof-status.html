1RB0LE_0RC0RA_1LD0LC_1LA0LB_1RA1LF_---0LC

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 9 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The machine is a binary counter: left of a periodic wall the tape reads as six-cell units holding one or two bits, and every three sweeps add one. When the counter is full, the carry rewrites the left part of the tape and the counting starts over. Rarely the carry runs to the left end (an S event): the old counter freezes into blocks and the wall, by then astronomically long, becomes the new counter. Lean proves every rule on the way from blank tape through the first S event, for all counter and wall lengths: no halt in the first 8p0+1 steps, p0 = 321,163,084,821,590,067,792,163 (a number with about 2.9·1023 digits). A trusted simulator gets through the next three S events without a halt. Each S event is a new, apparently random trial for halting, and the left part never settles into a pattern.

Other proofs and outside reports

Community label

In the community's BB(6) tracker the machine's class is Shift-overflow bouncer counter. These are labels read off the run by others, not results.

The halting configurations

The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (F0); the generated statement covers every configuration.

step_none_iff, lean/Blank.lean

No halt in the first 10,000 steps

The list simulator of the shared library, proved to agree with the machine's semantics, runs 10,000 steps from blank tape without a halt, evaluated by Lean's kernel.

blank_runs_kernel, lean/Blank.lean

Two sweeps that only touch the right end

From the phase P right end 111011 (101011)^n 1 B>0 one sweep gives phase Q 101010 (111010)^n 111 B>0, and the next gives phase R 111110 (101110)^n 10111 B>0, in 16n+18 and 16n+26 steps, for every n and any tape left of them.

sweep_PQ, sweep_QR, lean/Rules.lean

One increment of the counter, for every carry

Read the cells left of the wall in six-cell units, alternately X (111110 = 0, 111011 = 1) and Y (111011, 111010, 111111, 111110 = 0..3). Three sweeps from phase R add one to this mixed-radix (binary) number and one unit to the wall: X0 becomes X1, or a carry over a last X1 and j pairs Y3 X1 stops at a Y below 3 or at an X0. 48n+122, 60j+48n+154 (or +162 for Y1, +182 for an X stop) steps, for all j, n and any tape left of the counter.

cyc_x0, cyc_y0, cyc_y1, cyc_y2, cyc_x, lean/Rules.lean; inc_x0 … inc_x, lean/Counter.lean

The increment as a program

A program next on the list of units computes one increment and its number of steps; next_sound proves that the machine makes exactly that move whenever next answers, which it does unless the carry runs off the end of the list.

next_sound, run_sound, lean/Counter.lean

A full count, for any counter

From any valid list of m units, the counter counts up to all ones in at least capP(m) − 1 − value steps (capP = number of values, 2·8k for 2k+1 units), for every m, every wall length and any tape left of it.

up, lean/Count.lean

The 17 carries into the left part, and the first S event

With the counter all ones, the carry into the left part Lk gives the zero counter and Lk+1 (k = 0..16), for every even counter length and wall; the carry into L17 runs to the left end: the counter becomes the blocks of L1 = P (1^9 0 1 0)^j Q and a wall of 2p+2 units becomes the counter 1,3,0,3,0,… of 2p+3 units, for every j and p. Generated with bbtools.symtm by py/gen_left.py.

fe, s1, lean/Left.lean; lean/LeftRules.lean

The first six carries into L1

With an odd all-ones counter, the first six carries into L1 only rewrite its last ten cells Q (Q0 to Q6), for every counter length, wall and tape left of Q.

fo, lean/Left.lean

trusted Computed, not proved

The second S event

A memoized simulator of the carry map on the left part (the counter between carries is skipped) finds 2,266,846,918 carries into L1 before the second S event, without a halt. It gives a new irregular part P2 of 406 cells, p0+1 blocks and Q, and a new counter from the wall.

py/accel/acc.cpp (ODD=1), README, section Level 1

Jumps, and the third and fourth S events

The blocks are transparent: a carry crosses them in state C and returns in state A. A carry into P (a jump) changes P by a map J that depends on P only. J was checked against the direct run for the first 11 jumps with 300 blocks, and the result of the S event was checked to be independent of the number of blocks and of the cells right of them. Iterating J: 170,793,835 jumps on P2, no halt, then the third S event, giving P3 of 460 cells; 2,048,289,505 jumps on P3, no halt, then the fourth, giving P4 of 500 cells.

py/accel/acc.cpp (JUMP=1), jmap.py, README, section Level 2

heuristic Evidence only

Halting is possible at the left end

The machine halts exactly when E reads a 1 with a 0 on its left, and this can happen when a carry arrives at the left end: for the synthetic left part P B^2 Q with an even counter it halts after 1,129 carries. From blank tape all counters after S1 have odd length.

README, section Where it could halt

No closed family

The irregular part is new at each level (28, 406, 460, 500 cells) and the number of jumps until the next S event is irregular (about 2.3·109 carries, then 1.7·108 and 2.0·109 jumps). Each S event looks like a fresh random trial for halting, and the time between them grows like a tower; no invariant family is in sight.

README, section Why it does not close

open Open

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. Deciding it needs either the outcome of every future S event (each computable by iterating J on a few hundred cells, but with ever more jumps), or a reason why the left end never shows E over a 1 next to a 0.
