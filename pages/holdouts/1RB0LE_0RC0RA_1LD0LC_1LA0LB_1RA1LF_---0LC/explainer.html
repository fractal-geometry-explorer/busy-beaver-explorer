A 6-state Busy Beaver machine · Status as of 9 November 2026.

Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LE_0RC0RA_1LD0LC_1LA0LB_1RA1LF_---0LC

Nobody knows yet, but we know what it does: it counts in binary, and every time the counter fills up the carry scribbles on the far left of the tape. Very rarely the carry reaches the left end; then the whole counter freezes into the left part and the wall, by then unimaginably long, becomes a new counter. Lean follows the machine through the first of these events, past 8p0+1 steps with p0 ≈ 3.2·1023. What happens at the later ones looks random.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from E1 (writes 1, moves L): 0 E>1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.488).

After 109 steps the tape spans 39,053 cells (50 left of the start, 28,650 right), with a density of 1s of 0.668.

The parts

What the tape is made of

The right part of the tape is a wall: the six cells 010111 over and over, two cells longer after every trip of the head. Left of the wall, cut the tape into pieces of six cells, starting at the wall. Each piece, a unit, is one of a handful of words, and the units take turns being of two kinds:

X unit (1 bit)cellsY unit (2 bits)cells
X0111110Y0111011
X1111011Y1111010

Nearest the wall is an X unit (bit 0), then a Y unit (bits 1 and 2), then X (bit 3), Y (bits 4 and 5), and so on: together a binary number. Between the counter and the wall sits one edge unit, 111110 once every three trips. Far to the left is a short irregular piece that is not made of units: the left part.

The motion

Three trips, one increment

Two of every three trips are short: the head walks left over the wall, turns inside the edge unit, and walks back. Each adds two cells to the wall and changes nothing else. The third trip goes deeper: it crosses every unit that holds its maximum (X1 or Y3), turns at the first unit that does not, and on the way back rewrites the crossed units to their minimum. That is exactly adding one, with a carry.

Above: the counter X0 Y3 X1 (from the left; the value 0·8 + 3·2 + 1 = 7) and the edge and wall, before and after one increment. The X1 and Y3 carry, the X0 becomes X1: the value 8, and the wall one unit longer. In blocks of ones the counter is the odd blocks of the simulator records; the long run of 1^5 blocks is a stretch of high bits X0 Y3.

Exactly (proved in Lean for every value, with n the wall length and j the number of pairs Y3 X1 crossed):

The wall grows by one unit per increment, so the k-th increment takes about 48k steps: the run is quadratic and the tape grows like the square root of the time.

The run

From blank tape

The first 145 trips are a scramble in which a small counter grows against the left end. From step 59,129 on the tape is a 28-cell left part L0, a counter of 50 units and the wall (reach_s0, checked by compiled Lean). The counter is 8.4·1019 increments from full; then the carry reaches the left part for the first time, around step 1041.

The big picture

Counters inside counters

When the counter is all ones, the next carry has nowhere to go inside it. It runs on into the left part and rewrites a few of its cells, and the counter starts again from zero, now with a longer wall. Proved in Lean: from blank tape this happens 17 times, each time rewriting the left part Lk into Lk+1 by a rule that holds for every counter length and wall length.

The 18th carry does something new: it runs all the way to the left end of the tape. Call this an S event. The old counter is frozen into identical blocks 1^9 0 1 0, and the wall, which by then has 2·p0+2 units with p0 = 321,163,084,821,590,067,792,163, becomes the new counter.

The new counter has 2·8p0+1 values, so the time to the next carry into the left part is beyond astronomical. Each carry either stays near the right end of the blocks or makes a jump: it crosses the blocks without changing them and rewrites the irregular piece P on the far left by a map J that depends on P alone. When a jump comes back in the wrong state (B instead of A) it is the next S event, and the whole picture repeats one level up with a new P.

P never settles: 28, 406, 460, 500 cells of what looks like noise, and J scrambles it. That is why the question stays open: every S event is a new roll of the dice for a halt.

The question

Where it could halt

The missing transition is F reading 0, and F is entered only from E reading 1, which steps left. So the machine halts exactly when E reads a 1 with a 0 on its left. In every proved rule F lands on a 1. The place where it could fail is the left end of the tape, at an S event: what state the carry arrives in there depends on the irregular cells of P. For a made-up left part (P B^2 Q with an even-length counter) the machine does halt, after 1,129 carries; the configurations reached from blank tape have so far always been safe.

The proof so far

What is proved

Lean, kernel. The two short trips and the five kinds of three-trip increment, for every wall length and carry length (lean/Rules.lean); the increment as a program on the units (next_sound, lean/Counter.lean); a full count of a counter of any length (up, lean/Count.lean); the 17 carries into the left part, the first S event and the first six carries into L1, for all counter and wall lengths (fe, s1, fo, lean/Left.lean).

Lean, kernel on top of one native_decide. Blank tape reaches the first S event and goes on to the seventh carry into L1: no halt in the first 8p0+1 steps (no_halt_early, lean/Frontier.lean), where only the first 59,129 steps (reach_s0) use compiled evaluation.

Trusted computation. The second S event after 2,266,846,918 carries into L1, the third after 170,793,835 jumps into P2 and the fourth after 2,048,289,505 jumps into P3, none halting (py/accel/).

Open. All later S events. Each one is a finite computation on a few hundred cells, but the number of jumps before it is large and unpredictable, and nothing suggests a pattern that a proof could follow.

Computed
py/accel/acc.cpp (ODD=1), README, section Level 1
The second S event

A memoized simulator of the carry map on the left part (the counter between carries is skipped) finds 2,266,846,918 carries into L1 before the second S event, without a halt. It gives a new irregular part P2 of 406 cells, p0+1 blocks and Q, and a new counter from the wall.

Computed
py/accel/acc.cpp (JUMP=1), jmap.py, README, section Level 2
Jumps, and the third and fourth S events

The blocks are transparent: a carry crosses them in state C and returns in state A. A carry into P (a jump) changes P by a map J that depends on P only. J was checked against the direct run for the first 11 jumps with 300 blocks, and the result of the S event was checked to be independent of the number of blocks and of the cells right of them. Iterating J: 170,793,835 jumps on P2, no halt, then the third S event, giving P3 of 460 cells; 2,048,289,505 jumps on P3, no halt, then the fourth, giving P4 of 500 cells.

Heuristic
README, section Where it could halt
Halting is possible at the left end

The machine halts exactly when E reads a 1 with a 0 on its left, and this can happen when a carry arrives at the left end: for the synthetic left part P B^2 Q with an even counter it halts after 1,129 carries. From blank tape all counters after S1 have odd length.

Heuristic
README, section Why it does not close
No closed family

The irregular part is new at each level (28, 406, 460, 500 cells) and the number of jumps until the next S event is irregular (about 2.3·109 carries, then 1.7·108 and 2.0·109 jumps). Each S event looks like a fresh random trial for halting, and the time between them grows like a tower; no invariant family is in sight.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. Deciding it needs either the outcome of every future S event (each computable by iterating J on a few hundred cells, but with ever more jumps), or a reason why the left end never shows E over a 1 next to a 0.
