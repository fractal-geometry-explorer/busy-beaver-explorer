1RB0LC_0RC1RF_1RD0RE_1LE0RA_0LA0LE_1RE---

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 13 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The machine runs a base-5 counter written as markers between blocks; Lean proves its regular sweeps for all block lengths, and an exact fast simulator finds no halt in 1016 steps. It can only halt if two of the rare merges of drifting markers happen close together, which nothing here decides.

Family F05. One of 40 holdouts on the same five-state core; the family's exact block-list map runs this machine for 3.4·10200 steps without a halt.

The family's visit rule and its closed form

In frame A a visit starts with the head in state A on the 0 in front of plain blocks (10)^b1 1 (10)^b2 1 … 1 and ends when the head first steps left of that cell. go_spec proves for every depth that a clean visit turns the list l into go n l in exactly goT n l steps (the first block is spread, the rest gets five visits, the first block is folded back 5 shorter); go_closed gives the closed form (b1−5, b2−18, b3−90, …, bk+12·5k−2) when b1 is odd and at least 7, the other blocks even and block i at least 2+18·5i−2. visit states it as a run of this machine. Also proved: the birth of a block, and that the entry into F after a visit that ends in state C on a phase-1 tail reads a 0 (fevent, fevent_reads_zero). The proofs are generated for this machine's own frame-A table; they never use F0.

go_spec, go_closed, visit_closed, visit, birth, fevent_reads_zero, lean/Family.lean (written by holdouts/1RB0LC_0RC0RB_1LD0RE_0LE0LF_1LA0LB_0RA---/py/gen_family_lean.py)

What a clean visit conserves

For the blocks after the first, the last one left out: a clean visit (a list the closed form applies to) changes neither the residue of block i modulo 18·5i−2 nor the drift bi+1 − 5·bi of two neighbours. So these numbers change only at the exceptional events (a block running out, a birth, an entry into F, the left end). This is a law between events, not a proof that the machine never halts.

heuristic Evidence only

Halting would need two exceptional events close together

A clean visit cannot halt, and in the runs every exceptional event with long blocks around it ends without a halt; short lists of the same shape do halt. When block i runs out, the next block has about the length of their drift, so a second event close by needs a drift near 0 at that moment. Observed at the left-end events of this run: after step 1020 the smallest drift in absolute value is 300,922, after step 10100 it is 708,572. New drifts are made of sums and differences of earlier blocks, which grow with the run, so close pairs of events should become ever rarer; but nothing proved keeps a drift away from 0, and the figures are sampled at the left-end events only.

data/f05_run.json (drift), holdouts/1RB0LC_0RC0RB_1LD0RE_0LE0LF_1LA0LB_0RA---/py/f05_run.py

Growth shape: fixed/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.557). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

tools/py/bbtools/triage.py

Halting needs two merges close together

51 of 2,000 random short tapes of the same shape halt within 20,000 steps, while separated merges never halt. Whether the drifting counter ever brings two merges together is a question about its arithmetic that nothing here settles.
