Antihydra: a Turing machine that is secretly a coin-flipping walk

1RB1RA_0LC1LE_1LD1LC_1LA0LB_1LF1RE_---0RA

Nobody knows whether it halts. Lean proves that this machine halts exactly when a simple walk on numbers does: a number is multiplied by 3/2 again and again, and a counter goes up 2 for every even result and down 1 for every odd one. The machine halts only if the counter ever drops below zero. It has climbed past 500,000 and keeps climbing, but nobody can prove it never comes back.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from E0 (writes 1, moves L): 0 E>0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

What the machine does

Three blocks of ones and a sweeping head

Watch the run for a while and the tape settles into three blocks of 1s separated by single 0s, with the head bouncing at the right end. Write it as 1^p 0 1^a 0 1^b.

Each round trip of the head (a sweep, 2b + 13 steps) takes two 1s from the middle block and adds three to the right block. So while the middle block lasts, the right block grows by 3 for every 2 the middle loses: the middle number gets multiplied by 3/2.

When the middle block is down to one or two 1s, the head goes further left and touches the left block:

  • if a was odd (one 1 left), the left block gains 2;
  • if a was even (two 1s left), the left block loses 1 — and if there is no left block at all, the machine halts.

Then the right block becomes the new middle block, and a fresh right block of two 1s starts. In numbers: a = 2k + 1 gives (p + 2, 3k + 4), and a = 2k gives (p − 1, 3k + 2). The run from blank tape reaches (p, a) = (2, 7) after 62 steps, then (4, 13), (6, 22), (5, 35), (7, 55), (9, 85), …

The emergent behaviour

A number times 3/2, and a counter that flips coins

Set x = a + 5. Then each step is just x ↦ ⌊3x/2⌋, and the counter p goes up 2 when x is even and down 1 when x is odd. The machine halts if and only if p would go below 0. Lean checks this equivalence for the whole infinite run (halts_iff).

Nobody can predict the parity of ⌊3x/2⌋ iterates: they look like fair coin flips. If they are, p is a random walk with drift +½ per step, and once it is high it almost surely never returns. After a million steps p is near 500,000, and the chance of a walk like that ever coming back to −1 is about 0.618500,000. That is why the community calls Antihydra probviously non-halting, and why proving it is out of reach: it is a problem of the same kind as Mahler's 3/2 problem, open since 1968.

Try the walk. Start from the machine's own values or your own, and step it. The machine halts exactly when this says halt.

The sweep and its two endings
For every p, a, b, with Q p a b = 0^∞ 1^p 0 1^a 0 1^b B> 0^∞: Q p (a+3) (b+1) reaches Q p (a+1) (b+4) in 2b + 13 steps (one sweep: the middle block loses 2, the right block gains 3), and by induction k sweeps at once; a middle block of 1 gives Q (p+2) (c+4) 2 from Q p 1 (c+2), a middle block of 2 gives Q p (c+5) 2 from Q (p+1) 2 (c+2), and Q 0 2 (c+2) halts after c + 8 steps. The shift rules are generated from the table by bbtools.symtm and proved by rfl and the crossing lemmas.

Halting is equivalent to the Antihydra walk
After 62 steps from blank tape the run is at Q 2 7 2. With nxt (p, a) = (p + 2, 3k + 4) for a = 2k + 1, (p − 1, 3k + 2) for a = 2k and p ≥ 1, and no successor for a even and p = 0: the machine halts from blank tape if and only if the orbit of (2, 7) under nxt stops. Both directions: a stop gives a halting run; an orbit that never stops gives runs of every length.

Heuristic
holdouts/known.json
Why a halt is not expected
With x = a + 5 the map is x ↦ ⌊3x/2⌋, and p goes up 2 when x is even and down 1 when x is odd. If the parities are independent fair coins (they look so: no bias is known), p is a random walk with drift +½ per step; from height p it ever reaches −1 with probability φ−(p+1), φ = (1+√5)/2, and p is already about 500,000. This is the bbchallenge community's reading too (Antihydra is listed as a cryptid, probviously non-halting).

    
Open
Does the walk ever stop?
Open. Proving it never stops would need to show that the parities of the iterates of ⌊3x/2⌋ from x = 12 are not too often odd, an unsolved problem of the same kind as Mahler's 3/2 problem.
