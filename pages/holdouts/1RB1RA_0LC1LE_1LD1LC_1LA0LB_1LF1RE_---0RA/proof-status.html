Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape. It is the machine the bbchallenge community calls Antihydra, a cryptid: a machine whose halting is equivalent to an open Collatz-like problem.

Lean proves the equivalence here, for every step of the run: the machine halts from blank tape exactly when a simple arithmetic walk on two numbers reaches its halting case.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open, and is now a question about numbers. Lean proves (halts_iff, kernel) that the machine halts from blank tape if and only if the orbit of (2, 7) under the map (p, a) ↦ (p + 2, 3k + 4) for a = 2k + 1, (p − 1, 3k + 2) for a = 2k ever meets an even a with p = 0.

Why nobody expects a halt. The parity of a behaves like a fair coin, so p is a random walk with steps +2 and −1 and an upward drift of ½ per step. After 106 steps of the walk (a computation, not a proof) p is 498,505; a walk of that kind started at height p ever reaches −1 with probability about 0.618p+1. Deciding it would need control of the parity sequence of ⌊3x/2⌋ iterates, a famously hard problem (Mahler's 3/2 problem is of this kind).

Other proofs and outside reports

Community label

In the community's BB(6) tracker the machine's class is Bell-like. Its 3 November 2026 edition lists the machine as a cryptid: its halting is thought to come down to a Collatz-like question that no known method decides. These are labels read off the run by others, not results.

Halting is equivalent to the Antihydra walk

After 62 steps from blank tape the run is at Q 2 7 2. With nxt (p, a) = (p + 2, 3k + 4) for a = 2k + 1, (p − 1, 3k + 2) for a = 2k and p ≥ 1, and no successor for a even and p = 0: the machine halts from blank tape if and only if the orbit of (2, 7) under nxt stops. Both directions: a stop gives a halting run; an orbit that never stops gives runs of every length.

Why a halt is not expected

With x = a + 5 the map is x ↦ ⌊3x/2⌋, and p goes up 2 when x is even and down 1 when x is odd. If the parities are independent fair coins (they look so: no bias is known), p is a random walk with drift +½ per step; from height p it ever reaches −1 with probability φ−(p+1), φ = (1+√5)/2, and p is already about 500,000. This is the bbchallenge community's reading too (Antihydra is listed as a cryptid, probviously non-halting).

Does the walk ever stop?

Open. Proving it never stops would need to show that the parities of the iterates of ⌊3x/2⌋ from x = 12 are not too often odd, an unsolved problem of the same kind as Mahler's 3/2 problem.
