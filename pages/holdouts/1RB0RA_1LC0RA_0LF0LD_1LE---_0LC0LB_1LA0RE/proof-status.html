What is known about 1RB0RA_1LC0RA_0LF0LD_1LE---_0LC0LB_1LA0RE

1RB0RA_1LC0RA_0LF0LD_1LE---_0LC0LB_1LA0RE

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The machine is a sweep counter: each sweep adds a 1 to the youngest block and moves a one or two 1s between older blocks at a place fixed by the binary digits of the sweep number. Lean proves ten whole-sweep rules for every block length (the generic parity patterns), and that the run from blank tape reaches such a sweep; the C simulator finds no halt in the first 1.03·1011 steps (226,517 sweeps).

The halting configurations

The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (D1); the generated statement covers every configuration.

step_none_iff, lean/Blank.lean

Where a halt can come from

If the machine halts on its third step from a configuration, that configuration is one of: B with 1 two left, 1 left, 0 under the head; E with 1 two left, 1 left, 0 under the head. So a run can reach a halt only through one of these patterns, and a proof that the machine never halts only has to rule them out. The proof splits on the state and the five cells around the head.

halt_route, lean/Route.lean (written by py/route.py)

Ten whole-sweep rules

With block counts written last block first, a sweep from B>0 at the right end back to it changes them by: (E | O ?) → (+1, +1, −1); (E | E E ?) → (+1, −1, +2, −1); (E | E O E ?) → (+1, −1, 0, +2, −1); (O | E E E ?) and (O | E O O E) → (+1, 0, 0, +1, −1); (O | E E O E E) → (+1, 0, 0, −1, +2, −1), where E/O is the parity of a count (inner counts at least 4, the last at least 5), for every value of the counts and every tape further left; the step counts are linear (e.g. 8c + 16y1 + 76 for the first). Written by bbtools.symtm.prove.

The sweep number is a binary counter

In the 31,158 sweeps of the first 2·109 steps, every sweep adds 1 to the last block and, except a handful where the number of blocks changes, makes exactly one more change: a move Mk (block 2k+1 gains a 1, block 2k+2 loses one; blocks counted from the last, which is block 0) or a split Sk,j (block 2k+1 loses one, a block j places further left gains two, the next one loses one). The level k is the number of trailing zeros of the sweep number n in binary (n odd: k = 0; n = 2 mod 4: k = 1; ...), and M or S follows n mod 2k+2 and the parities of the blocks further left. Frequencies halve with each level, like the carries of a binary counter.

Does it halt from blank tape?

The only halting transition is D reading 1: the left-moving head (C, D, E, B rotating over the 10s) meeting two adjacent 1s. None of the generic sweep rules does that; whether a sweep over short blocks (counts below 4, the empty blocks that sometimes appear) or the arrival of a carry at the left end can do it is not settled.
