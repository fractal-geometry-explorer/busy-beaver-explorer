Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1LB_1LC1LA_1RD0LB_1RA1RE_1RF0RD_---1RD

Nobody knows, and the reason is a number-theory puzzle in disguise. The machine keeps a counter that grows by a factor of about 5/2 each epoch; whether the counter is odd or even decides how a carry ripples into a tail of digits. It halts only if one carry ever runs all the way through that tail.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 0. F is entered only from E0 (writes 1, moves R): E>0 0. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.494). Right of the start it stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half).

After 109 steps the tape spans 61,750 cells (44,912 left of the start, 13 right), with a density of 1s of 0.559.

The tape

A block, a counter and a tail of digits

Everything happens left of the start. Write a for the pair 01. At the start of an epoch the tape reads

0^inf 1 B> 1 01 0101 1^5 a^Y | 1^(5d1) a^e1 | 1^(5d2) a^e2 | ... | 1^4 a

a counter of Y a's, then a tail of digits (d, e) = 1^(5d) a^e ending in the fixed word 1^4 a. From blank tape the first epoch starts at step 494 with Y = 5 and tail (1,8).

Inside an epoch

Rounds: two a's become ten ones

Between the head's left end and the counter sits a block of ones, empty at first. In each round the head runs right through the block (turning it into a's as it goes, 11 → 10), zigzags back over it turning the a's back into ones, and adds five. Every second round two a's of the counter are used up. So after Y/2 pairs of rounds the block holds 5Y ones (5Y+5 if Y was even, after one more half round) and the counter is empty. A round costs about three times the block length, so an epoch costs about 3.75·Y2 steps and the visited tape grows like the square root of the time.

Lean: round_a, round_b (one round, any j, Y) and epoch (any number of rounds, by induction).

The end of an epoch

The reset: halve, and carry

When the counter is empty, one sweep turns the block of 10j+5 ones into a's again: two ones per a, so the new counter is about half the block, Y' = 5⌊Y/2⌋ + c with c ∈ {0, 5, 10}. Then the sweep runs on into the tail. It passes a digit only if its block of ones is odd and it has exactly one a; otherwise it turns back and rewrites the digits it passed and the one it stopped at. Which digits it meets first depends on the parity of Y: for even Y the counter's 1^5 merges with the first digit, for odd Y one digit (1,1) is left in front.

4,000 epochs

The counter explodes, the tail grows slowly

The counter gains about 0.4 decimal digits per epoch (a factor of 5/2). The tail gains a digit about every three epochs, because new digits are made at its front and the carry almost never reaches far: in 4,000 epochs it changed at most the first three digits.

The open question

Where it could halt

The only halting transition is F reading 0, and F is entered only from D> 1 0; the sweep halts exactly when it meets 1 0 0, i.e. if it passes every digit of the tail and arrives at the right end out of step. That needs a long run of special parities of Y. If we pick the parities ourselves, we can make the machine halt within ten epochs of the real start; the real parities come from Y → 5⌊Y/2⌋ + c, a sequence of the same kind as Mahler's 3/2 problem, about which nothing is known. Treating them as coin flips, the carry reaches depth 5 a few times in 5,000 epochs and the tail is already 1,311 digits long, so a halt is extremely unlikely, but it is not excluded.

The two halves of a round

From 0^inf 1 B> 1 01 (1^10)^j 0101 1^5 (01)^Y R the machine reaches the same tape with five more ones and the marker 01 (30j+19 steps), and from there, with two a's fewer and the marker 0101 again, (1^10)^(j+1) (30j+51 steps). For all j, Y and every tape R.

Resets, for families of first digits

When the counter runs out, a sweep turns the 10(j+1)+5 ones back into 5(j+1)+c a's and rewrites the front digits: (2k+2, e+1) → (2k+3, e) with c = 0; (3, e+3) → (1,4)(1,e+1) with c = 5; (1,1)(1,e+3) → (2,2)(1,e+1) and (1,1)(3,e+3) → (2,7)(1,e+1) with c = 0; for all j, k, e and R.

search over both parities, notes in README.md
Halting is possible in principle

Choosing the parity of Y freely at every epoch (both branches), some choices from the real starting tail make the carry run off the end and halt after 10 epochs (e.g. tail (1,1)(19,0) 1^4). So non-halting would have to use the actual parity sequence.

Heuristic
Why it probably never halts

With the parity of Y treated as a fair coin, the carry depth has an exponential tail (depth ≥5 a few times in 5,000 epochs) while the tail grows linearly; the chance that a carry ever crosses the 1,311 digits now present is astronomically small. Like Mahler's 3/2 problem, the parities themselves are out of reach.
