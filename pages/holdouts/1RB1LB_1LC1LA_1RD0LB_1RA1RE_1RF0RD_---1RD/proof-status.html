What is known about 1RB1LB_1LC1LA_1RD0LB_1RA1RE_1RF0RD_---1RD

1RB1LB_1LC1LA_1RD0LB_1RA1RE_1RF0RD_---1RD

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open, and looks hard. The run is a sequence of epochs; each multiplies a counter Y by about 5/2 (Y → 5⌊Y/2⌋ + c, c ∈ {0, 5, 10}) and lets a carry, steered by the parity of Y, rewrite the front of a tail of digits. The machine can halt only if a carry runs through the whole tail. For some parity sequences that happens within ten epochs, so no argument that ignores the parities can work; along the real run the tail has grown to 1,311 digits by epoch 4,000 and no carry has gone deeper than three digits. Lean proves the rounds of an epoch, the epoch itself by induction, and several reset shapes.

The two halves of a round

From 0^inf 1 B> 1 01 (1^10)^j 0101 1^5 (01)^Y R the machine reaches the same tape with five more ones and the marker 01 (30j+19 steps), and from there, with two a's fewer and the marker 0101 again, (1^10)^(j+1) (30j+51 steps). For all j, Y and every tape R.

round_a, round_b, lean/Rules.lean (generated by lean/rules.py)

An epoch, by induction

k rounds take c0 j (Y+2k) R to c0 (j+k) Y R: the counter's a's are turned into ones, ten for every two a's, for any k.

epoch, lean/Epoch.lean

Resets, for families of first digits

When the counter runs out, a sweep turns the 10(j+1)+5 ones back into 5(j+1)+c a's and rewrites the front digits: (2k+2, e+1) → (2k+3, e) with c = 0; (3, e+3) → (1,4)(1,e+1) with c = 5; (1,1)(1,e+3) → (2,2)(1,e+1) and (1,1)(3,e+3) → (2,7)(1,e+1) with c = 0; for all j, k, e and R.

4,000 epochs without a halt

Iterating the epoch map 4,000 times (Y reaches 1,593 decimal digits) never halts; the tail grows to 1,311 digits, about one digit every three epochs, and the carry changes at most the first three digits.

python3 py/epochs.py 4000

Halting is possible in principle

Choosing the parity of Y freely at every epoch (both branches), some choices from the real starting tail make the carry run off the end and halt after 10 epochs (e.g. tail (1,1)(19,0) 1^4). So non-halting would have to use the actual parity sequence.

Why it probably never halts

With the parity of Y treated as a fair coin, the carry depth has an exponential tail (depth ≥5 a few times in 5,000 epochs) while the tail grows linearly; the chance that a carry ever crosses the 1,311 digits now present is astronomically small. Like Mahler's 3/2 problem, the parities themselves are out of reach.
