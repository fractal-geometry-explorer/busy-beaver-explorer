Does this machine ever halt?

1RB0LD_0RC1RD_1LA1RA_1LE0LE_1RF0LA_0RA---

Nobody knows yet, but its map is now fully known. A block of 101s grows by 4/3 each round, while a short word at the right end, the tail, gets rewritten; the machine could only stop inside such a rewrite, and none of the 90,000 rewrites tried stops.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from E0 (writes 1, moves R): E>0 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

What it does

A 4/3 counter and a tail

Look at the tape whenever the head is at its left end in state D. It always reads 1 00 (10)^m T: a long run of m copies of 10 and then a short tail word T. Between two such moments (a round) the head:

  1. sweeps right, turning every three 10 into two 101 (the same length, 6 cells);
  2. runs into the tail and rewrites it, a few cells at a time;
  3. comes back and, one 101 at a time, carries the 101 block back to the left end as pairs of 10 (the D-loop, which walks to the left end and back for every copy, so a round costs about 3m² steps).

Net effect: m grows by a factor 4/3 each round, and the tail changes. Which change happens depends only on the tail and on the remainder of m mod 3.

With the tail 1 this is the P-map of the first analysis: P(n) = (101)^n 01 A> is the moment just before DL(2n+1, 1).

The tail

A word that wanders and comes back

The tails are lists of small blocks separated by runs of zeros, like (010)^3 0^4 (10)^2 1 or 1^2 (01)^6. Because m mod 3 behaves like a coin with three sides, the tail does a random walk among these words: from blank tape it is back to 1 in 9,214 of the first 100,000 rounds, and it is never longer than 106 cells.

The round map is exact: a round depends on m only through m mod 3 (checked on four values of m for every tail used), so a round with an m of twelve thousand digits is computed from one with m ≈ 30. It agrees with every event of the direct simulation up to 5·109 steps.

Halting

Where a halt would have to come from

The machine halts exactly when state D reads a 0 that has another 0 to its left (D writes 1, E steps left onto the 0, F steps back onto the new 1 and has no rule). That can only happen while a tail is being rewritten. Exploring every tail reachable from 1 breadth first, with all three remainders allowed at every round (90,000 rounds in all), not one round halts. So the run is probviously non-halting, and more: no way to halt is known at all, which suggests an invariant waiting to be found.

In Lean

What is proved

The kernel checks the P-map for every n: P(3k) → P(4k+2), P(3k+1) → P(4k+3), P(3k+2) → (101)^(4k+4) 110101 A>, with exact step counts, and the run from blank tape to that first excursion (27,264 steps). The D-loop lemma holds with any tail to its right, so the remaining step to a full proof of a round is the right sweep through each tail, stated with a symbolic k.

First look

The shape of the run

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.509). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.079).

After 109 steps the tape spans 46,085 cells (33,996 left of the start, 124 right), with a density of 1s of 0.583.

Computed
py/roundmap.py (round_rep)
A round depends on m only mod 3

For each (r, tail) pair used, four representatives m = 3j + r (j = 10..13) give the same tail τ(r,T), the same c = 3m' − 4m and a step count quadratic in j; the iteration relies on this. A Lean proof would state each round with symbolic k (the D-loop lemma already allows any tail).

Heuristic
py/roundmap.py bfs 30000 90 (25 min)
No halting round is known at all

The tail behaves like a recurrent random process (driven by m mod 3, which is pseudo-random) on a language of block lists. Exploring the tail graph breadth-first with every residue allowed (30,000 tails, 90,000 rounds) finds no round that halts. So a halt is not merely unlikely: no reachable way to halt is known; this hints at an invariant that a stronger decider could prove.

Open
Does it halt from blank tape?

Not proved either way. The round map is a finite-state rewrite of the tail word, so a proof of non-halting would be an invariant on the tail language that excludes the halting pattern (D reading 0 with a 0 on its left).
