What is known about 1RB1LF_1LC1RB_1LE1LD_1LB0RD_1LF---_1LA0LA

1RB1LF_1LC1RB_1LE1LD_1LB0RD_1LF---_1LA0LA

OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape. Seen in pairs of cells, each visit of the growing left end applies one recursive map to the tape; that map runs a counter in base 4 whose levels are long blocks of the word 111010. The counter itself can never halt; what the head does on the rare occasions it reaches the right end is not understood.

Status as of 22 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The map from one wall visit to the next is known exactly as a recursion on words of pairs, with exact step counts, and the counter phases are proved in Lean. An accelerated simulator that finds its own rules, checked against direct simulation at every one of 1,399 wall visits (word and step number), runs to wall visit 4.233·102237 (step about 3.1·104475) without a halt. It is exact by construction but has not been re-derived independently. The right-end events do not stop, each one can in principle leave a halting word (such words exist, proved in Lean), and no invariant is known. Every halt seen comes from one local pattern, a cascade with 3 blank cells, and the run's events so far meet only even counts.

The pair moves

With X = 10, Y = 11, O = 00: F moving left swaps X and Y and turns round on a blank pair (reflect, flipX0/1, flipY0/1); Y B> X X becomes <F Y Y Y (turn0/1); Y B> X Yd X becomes X Od-1 Y Y Y B> (digit1, digit). For every count and every surrounding tape.

A word of pairs that halts

The counting laws of the level-stripping accelerator

first_pass_correct: a level with cycle length p that passes one hit per cycle at position t makes its m-th pass at its hit p(m − 1) + t. blocks_closed: the hits that K super-cycles of an eater need from above, when each block maps x ↦ (c + 1)x + b, are a geometric sum in closed form, for every K. Arithmetic only: the constants come from symbolic runs of the digit engine.

Two hits that halt whatever is to their left

The hit of a last run of 13 Y's, L Y B> X Y13, halts after 690 steps, and the hit L Y B> X Y8 X X after 254, for every tape L to the left (the head never reads L). So any visit, at any depth of the recursion, that hands on one of these hits stops the machine.

hit13_halts, hit800_halts, lean/Events.lean

What an event does, for every tape to the left

For b = 11, 12 and 14 to 20 and every tape L: L x Y B> X Yb comes back over the Y in F, as every regular hit does, leaving a word fixed by b; in the recursion this is one more rule, h(b) = g(b). For b = 0 to 10, every prefix Ya (a ≥ 1) and every tape beyond four blank pairs: the wall visit of the word (a, b) ends 7 cells to the left instead of 2, so the pairs to its right are shifted by one cell.

The cascade, case by case

A pass of the head to the left reads the tape in pairs (A-cell, F-cell). Where it turns on a pair 01 instead of a blank pair, the head runs a cascade whose result depends only on the number m of blank cells to its left, proved for every tape on both sides: m = 0 is a hit in the new pair grid, m = 1 one more sub-visit, m = 2 a hit in the old grid, m = 3 halts, m = 4 and 5 hand the pass on to the left, m ≥ 6 gives a wall visit 7 cells on with the word Y Y Y R.

Where a halt can come from

A closed tape language from windows of 6 cells (closed: yes) covers the run; the only places it lets the halting transition E1 fire are E reading a 1 with the abstract left side in a window of 0s, followed by at least six 1s. With windows of 4 and 8 the same picture.

The visit map is a recursion on words

V(S) = G(S) with G(Ya X X U) = Xa Y Y Y U, G(Ya X Yk X U) = Xa Y Gk(Y Y U), G(Ya) = Xa Y Y: checked against the machine on 20,000 random words and on every covered wall visit of the run from blank tape (the recursion does not cover the right-end events).

After the reset, a plain counter again

After the reset at step 19,980,179,598 the new counter eats the blank stretch and then the frozen first block (turned into blanks from its right end); at 1011 steps the tape is again a counter with the wall at the left end. At the border of the rest of the tape the head of a carry crosses in the states D, F, B, D, D, F (in, out, in, out, in, out) at all 733 carries of a test, so a level does not depend on what lies to its right. An accelerator that strips levels stalls at stretches between levels that change from hit to hit.

The recursion with step counts, checked against the machine

On digit words: G(a T) = 0^a h(T) in 16 + 4a steps, h(0 T) = 3 + T, h(k T) = 1 + G^k(2 + T) in 2k steps plus the sub-visits, a unit 1 0 passes a hit in 26 steps. Fitted and then checked on 3,995 random words; the accelerated simulator built on it agrees with direct simulation, word and step number, at 1,399 wall visits (visits 1 to 1,396 (3·106 steps) and visits 298,224 to 298,226 (step 100,001,215,143, after the reset)), with jumps of 1, 7, 50 and 1,000 visits.

Accelerated run: no halt up to wall visit 4.233·102237 (step about 3.1·104475)

The digit engine finds cycle, run and recursion rules by symbolic execution and the macro simulator does the right-end events on cells. The run passes the events after wall visits 3, 14, 23, 114, 117, 107,452 and 6.354·102236 and stops at the event after 4.233·102237, where a fuel run of more than 1014 units needed a rule the engine did not have. That rule (frozen words emitted by cycle rules, 16 November 2026) now exists, and the run of 16 November 2026 gets past that point and well into the return sweep of the event, with no halt: after 2 h 35 min, 14,635 runs of cells are left of the head, from 18,141 when the stall point was passed. It is slow there, not stuck. Each wall visit costs about 200,000 explicit operations of the recursion, and rule discovery is not the bottleneck. Exact by construction, validated only on the range direct simulation can reach, not re-derived independently: read it as strong evidence, not as a proof.

From a wall visit straight to the next right-end event

The level-stripping accelerator treats the ladder of levels as a mixed-radix counter and jumps an eater's K super-cycles at once (hP(E[m] X) = Blk E[m − dm] hc(X), derived with the fuel m symbolic). From the word at visit 101600 it finds the seventh event at exactly wall visit 6.354265240826449…·102236, all 2,237 digits as the digit engine found them by running every visit: in 128 s with plain batches of hits, in 9 s with 7 nested jumps (eaters ×256, ×16, ×1).

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The counter cannot halt. Every halt seen is a cascade with m = 3: an event with last digit b ≤ 10 that comes back to a frame with exactly 5 blank cells, after an earlier change of grid in that frame (or a last digit 13). The run's first events have only even m; in the eighth, frame (6, 0) is left with 5 blank cells but gets no event there, and the first 28 minutes of its return have only even m.

README

The right-end events

A right-end event is a hit that reaches the last digit b of the word; at its bottom is always a sub-visit of a two-digit word (a, b). For b ≥ 11 it is an ordinary hit h(b) = g(b), or a halt at b = 13. For b ≤ 10 the head comes home in the other pair grid and runs a cascade at the first blank stretch; the cascade is now a proved local rule with seven outcomes. What is still missing is the recursion that keeps the grid and the blank cells of every frame, so that the events can be followed as digit work instead of on cells.
