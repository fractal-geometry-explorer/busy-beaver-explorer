Does this machine ever halt?

1RB1RD_1LC0LE_1RA0LB_1RA1RF_0RA0RC_1RE---

Nobody knows. This machine plays a game much like the 3n+1 problem: a block of m ones is ground down three at a time, and then rebuilt as a block of about 8m/3 ones, with the details set by the remainder of m divided by 3. This page runs the real machine in your browser and explains the game.

The machine

6 states, two symbols, 1 halting transition

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and which state to enter. The machine starts in A on a blank tape (all 0s).

The machine stops when it is in F reading 1. F is entered only from D1 (writes 1, moves R): D>1 1. So a halt is the moment this pattern appears, with the head on the cell after >.

Remainder classes

Some remainder classes halt, 107 rounds away

A generic test used on the whole family: sample the run whenever the head is in state D reading 1 with only 0s to its right. Read leftwards from the head, the tape is then A Um B, one long periodic block between two short context words A and B. The round to the next sample is computed class by class: for a class (A, U, B, m mod 3) six values of m are simulated, and when they agree the round is taken to be an affine map of m. Against the direct run to 3·109 steps the class map is right in all 7 rounds it applies to. It is an observed law, not a proved one.

The orbit. Iterated with exact integers from the event at step 3,238 of the run (A = (empty), U = 1, m = 109, B = 0 16): 185,703 rounds without meeting a halting class; m then has 262,784 bits (about 10158,210 steps of the machine). 119 distinct contexts occur, the longest of 28 cells.

Can any class halt? Breadth first from the starting context, with every remainder at every context (whether or not the numbers can really produce that sequence of remainders, and only for m large compared with the contexts): 5,000 contexts expanded, 5,440 found, to depth 43. None of them halts; the search is not complete (the set of contexts keeps growing), and it says nothing about a halt that needs a context as long as the block itself.

A halt is reachable. The same search restricted to contexts of at most 26 cells runs to the end: 10,061 contexts (1,649 longer ones are reached but not expanded), and 5 of their classes halt. The nearest is 107 rounds from the starting context: after the remainders 1 22 1 2 12 2 13 22 1 22 12 22 1 2 12 22 1 22 13 22 1 22 1 22 12 22 13 22 1 2 13 22 1 22 12 22 1 2 12 22 1 22 13 22 1 22 1 22 12 22 1 2 12 22 1 22 13 22 1 22 1 2 (mod 3) the tail is 01011011, and with m ≡ 1 (mod 3) the machine halts before the next event (all six simulated values of m halt). So, granted the class map, a halt is possible, but only through one prescribed sequence of 107 remainders or a longer one; with remainders like fair dice that is one chance in 3107 per try. The orbit computed has not come near it.

That makes the machine a cryptid: whether it halts is a question about one orbit of a Collatz-like map, and nothing known decides it.

What the tape looks like

A block of ones turning into pairs

Every time the head reaches the right end of the tape in state D, the tape reads Q 0 1x (01)y 1 D>0: a short prefix Q, a solid block of x ones, and a run of y pairs 01. The head walks left over the pairs, eats three ones off the end of the block, and walks back writing four new pairs:

1x+3 (01)y 1 D>0  →  1x (01)y+4 1 D>0   (6y + 39 steps)

This one cycle is proved in Lean for all x, y (cycle, lean/Cycle.lean). A phase is the run of cycles from a full block down to fewer than three ones: a block of m = 3k + r ones lasts k cycles and leaves about 4k pairs.

Macro structure

Phases: a Collatz-like map that multiplies by 8/3

When fewer than three ones are left (r = m mod 3 of them), the head runs into the prefix, rebuilds it, and turns the long run of pairs back into a solid block: about two ones per pair, so the next block is m' = 8k + c, roughly 8m/3. The constant c and the new prefix depend only on the old prefix and on r. For the empty prefix:

m mod 3next prefixnext block m'

1empty8k + 14

So the machine is iterating a map like the famous 3n+1 problem: what happens next depends on a remainder, and the numbers grow by a factor of 8/3 per phase. A counter inside the counter

The prefix is a smaller copy of the same machine

The prefix Q has the same shape as the main tape, 1a (01)b. A phase ending with remainder 2 pushes work into it (the empty prefix becomes 16, then 13(01)2, ...: three ones become two pairs, just like a cycle); remainder 0 takes one pair off; remainder 1 usually runs the prefix's own little phase and empties it. Some cases nest a third level (16 0 14, ...). Over 3,000 phases the prefix was empty 62% of the time and never longer than about 20 cells.

The halting transition is F reading a 1, reached only as D>1 1. In all 68 (prefix, remainder) cases met in 3,000 phases, and in the 120 prefixes reached breadth first from the empty one, the phase ends in a new phase start: no case halts.

Why it is still open

What is proved and what is not

  • Proved (kernel): the halting configurations, no halt in the first 10,000 steps (107 with native_decide), and the cycle rule for all sizes.
  • Computed: the phase map, fitted case by case on small blocks (four sizes each, step count included) and checked against the direct simulation step for step through phase 9 (1.67·1010 steps). Iterated 3,000 phases (about 102557 steps) it never reaches a case that halts.
  • Open: each case of the map is fitted, not proved, and the prefix can in principle grow without bound (a long run of remainder-2 phases), so there are infinitely many cases. A proof of non-halting would need every case, by induction on the prefix, and an argument that the run never meets a halting one.

Heuristic. If the remainders behave like fair random digits, the prefix behaves like a walk pushed back towards empty, and a halting case, if one exists at all, would have to be reached through an unusually deep prefix. No such case is known; the map so far gives no sign that it halts.

Growth shape: fixed/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.505).
