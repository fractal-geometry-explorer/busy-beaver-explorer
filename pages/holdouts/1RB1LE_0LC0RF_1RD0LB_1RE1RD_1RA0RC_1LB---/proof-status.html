OpenNobody has proved whether this machine halts. Everything below is what is known so far.

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. This page lists what has been established about it, sorted by what each result rests on.

Status as of 8 November 2026.

In short

The question is open. The tape is a list of numbers in unary (blocks of 4k+3 ones) that each round trip rewrites; the sweep rules over the blocks are proved in Lean, but the rewriting of the list has no known closed form and looks chaotic. No halt in 1010 steps.

Where a halt can come from

If the machine halts on its third step from a configuration, that configuration is one of: A with 0 under the head, 1 right, 1 two right; F with 1 left, 0 under the head. So a run can reach a halt only through one of these patterns, and a proof that the machine never halts only has to rule them out. The proof splits on the state and the five cells around the head.

halt_route, lean/Route.lean (written by py/route.py)

The sweep rules

10 rules for all values of the repeat counts and every tape around them: the leftward sweep turns each 11 of a block into 10 (B) or 01 (C), the rightward sweep turns 001, 010, 100 into 110, 101, 011 (C, D, E), the left end of a block, and the turns at the two ends.

The halting state is never close

F1 is the only halting transition and F is entered only by B1 (0RF): a halt needs B on a 1 with a 1 after it. In the first 109 steps F is entered 117,032,159 times and reads 0 every time.

Regular-language deciders tried (F11)

None of these closes: each reaches a halting configuration in its abstraction, which says nothing about whether the machine halts. n-gram CPS (tools/c/ngramcps.c), equal widths on both sides: a halting local configuration is reached with plain up to width 24, history 1 up to width 24, history 2 up to width 24, history 3 up to width 24, LRU history up to width 7 (larger widths ran out of the 150 s or 60 million configuration budget). n-gram CPS with every cell tagged by its position mod p: fails for p = 2 up to width 20, p = 3 up to width 20, p = 4 up to width 20, p = 6 up to width 20. n-gram CPS where each side also carries the state of a small automaton that has read that side from the far end (every automaton with up to 3 states on both sides, or 4 states on one side), with position mod 1..4 and widths 4, 6, 8: no closed set without a halt in all 120 parameter sets. Run-length windows (the K nearest runs of equal macro cells on each side, run lengths kept exactly below T and mod M above): fails for all 72 parameter sets (cells of 1 to 4 bits, K = 2, 4, 6, T = 2, 4, M = 1, 2, 4). Two-automaton closed tape language by SAT (a deterministic automaton reading each side inward, n states each, CaDiCaL): no solution for n = 2..8; n = 9 not finished in 150 s.

Growth shape: sqrt/sqrt

Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.496). Right of the start it grows like the square root of the step count, the signature of a head sweeping back and forth across the whole tape and adding a cell or a few at the end of each sweep (a bouncer-like shape) (fitted exponent 0.503).

Blocks of 4k+3 ones

At every 7th visit to the right end in the first 3·108 steps, 553,908 of the 554,278 blocks of ones between single 0s have a length 4k+3; the few others are where the head was still working. After a leftward sweep a block of 4k+3 ones has become 2k+1 single 1s (000 (10)^(2k+1)), the groups separated by 000.

Does it halt from blank tape?

Not known. The list of numbers is rewritten each round trip by a sequential process with a finite carry (the zigzag of the rightward sweep), but how the list evolves is not understood; a proof would need an invariant of the list (a closed language of lists) that keeps B away from two adjacent 1s.
