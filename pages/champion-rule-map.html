What each rule in the BB(6) champion does

The champion's table has twelve entries: six states, each with one rule for reading 0 and one for reading 1. Sorted by what they do, they form three ways to move along the tape plus a few short fixed sequences. Every part of the calculation uses the same routines in a different order, and the 0s on the tape decide which routine runs next.

Each entry says what to write, which way to move and the next state. Because that last letter names the state that acts next, rules always chain: C0 is followed by a D rule, E1 by a C rule. Every sequence on this page is written in that order. Four rules belong to two routines.

Crawl leftD1 E1 C0
D erases the cell and moves left, E steps back right, and C writes the 1 back and moves left again. The head ends one cell further left every three steps and the tape is unchanged. The crawl ends when E reads a 0.
Run rightA1
A keeps the 1 and moves right, one cell per step. The run ends when A reads a 0.
Recode rightC1 F1 E1
C writes 0, F writes 0 and E keeps the 1, so every 111 becomes 100, one cell per step. The recode ends when C, E or F reads a 0. When F reads it, F0 hands the head to the run.
FillE0 D0 A0 B0
A crawl that reaches the 00 of a 100 fills both 0s, and B writes back the cell D erased. The 100 becomes 111.
AppendA0 B0 C0
A run that reaches blank tape writes 111, and C turns the head into a crawl.
HaltB1
B normally lands on a cell that is 0, often one that D has just erased. Reading a 1 there stops the machine.

E1 is where the crawl and the recode meet. E always moves right into C, and C's cell decides which routine carries on. In a crawl that cell is the one D just erased, so C reads 0 and turns left. In a recode it is a fresh 1, so C reads 1 and keeps going right.

The 3-state cycles set the digit size. One turn of C, F and E rewrites exactly three cells, which is why both counters use 3-cell digits: 100 for 1 and 111 for 0.

The 0s are the signposts

From step 821 on, the tape has this shape between events. This is the real run at step 1,013, with the head on the counter's last cell. Every routine passes over 1s, so the 0s, shown dark, are where things change.

  • The 00 inside each 1-digit. A crawl that reaches it fills it in, which is how both counters subtract.
  • The left marker 100 10. Only a crawl through an empty counter gets here. It crosses the marker and goes on into the countdown.
  • The header's 0 and the blank tape beyond it. A crawl only reaches them when the countdown has no 1-digit left, and that starts a reset or the halt.
  • The right marker 0 11100. Recodes and runs heading right stop at its first 0. The state that gets there picks what happens next.
  • The blank tape after the pile. The run stops here and appends a group.

The pile holds one more 111 group than its value. A convert or reset turns that spare group into the next right marker.

Why the phase decides between reset and halt

The countdown and the left marker sit on one 3-cell grid. The middle counter, the right marker and the pile sit on another. The phase is the offset between the two grids, 0, 1 or 2 cells, and the decoder shows it as the 1s between the left marker and the counter.

Ticks never compare the two grids. A convert makes the stretch between the markers 3b + 5 cells longer. Five cells is one digit plus two, so the offset steps from 2 to 1 to 0 and back to 2.

In a reset or a halt, the long recode starts on the left grid and runs all the way to the right marker. Its three states repeat every three cells, so the offset decides which state reaches the marker's first 0. A tick's recode starts on the right grid, so it always arrives as C.

After the warm-up the countdown never runs out in phase 2. mxdys's analysis has it running out twice in phase 1, which gives two resets, and then in phase 0, which gives the halt. It does happen once during the warm-up, at step 140, while both counters are still empty. There the sweep arrives as C and the event ends like a tick.

Where the steps go

Almost all the time goes into ticks, and a tick is mostly travel. In the first 20 million steps of the real run, 74.9% are crawl steps and 24.9% are run steps. Fills, appends, recodes and the other fixed sequences share the last 0.2%.

The 3-to-1 split comes from the costs: each tick crawls back over every pile cell at three steps per cell and runs out over the same cells at one step per cell. That is about 12 steps per pile group, and the pile grows by one group every tick.
