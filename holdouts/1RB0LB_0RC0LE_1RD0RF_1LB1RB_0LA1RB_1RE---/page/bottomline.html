It halts.

Started on a blank tape, the machine 1RB0LB_0RC0LE_1RD0RF_1LB1RB_0LA1RB_1RE--- stops: in round 161,211 a visit meets the block 1011, and the head, in state F, reads a 1. That happens after between 5.9 × 1024,266 and 9.2 × 1024,266 steps (computed, not proved; see below).

theorem halts_from_blank : HaltsFromBlank :=

native_decide lean/Halt.lean. HaltsFromBlank is the machine's own halting from blank tape, defined from its transition table in lean/Machine.lean.

So everything is checked by Lean's kernel except the evaluation of a checker that is itself proved correct. That evaluation is by native_decide, compiled code, and the theorem depends on eleven native_decide axioms, one per evaluation (trust in the Lean compiler; the comment in Halt.lean names it Lean.ofReduceBool), besides the three standard axioms. The halting theorem is checked by lean/check_halt.sh, about 3 hours of computing; the repository's automatic checks run check.sh, the kernel-only part, but not this evaluation.

Elsewhere. The same visit law, in a Coq proof on busycoq's definitions (tools/coq/busycoq/VisitLaw2/, halts : halts tm c0, Coq 8.20.1, no axioms): the checker of busycoq PR59 extended to two forms of visit, with the whole run as one vm_compute of about 15 minutes. And the three machines of busycoq PR59 (1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_0RD1RF_0RC--- and two relatives) obey the same law with one form of visit, entered one cell further left; PR59 proves in Rocq that they halt in round 161,211. The Lean proof here does not use PR59, so that both land on the same round is a cross-check (as is the C simulator holdouts/1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_1RB0RD_0RB---/c/vis.c, which halts in that round on the PR59 machine). The twin 1RB0LB_0RC0LE_1RD0RF_1LB1RB_0LA0RE_1RE--- halts in the same round by the same checker run; that is a separate theorem about a different table, not a consequence of this one.

How many steps Computed

Between 5.9 × 1024,266 and 9.2 × 1024,266 steps from blank tape to the halt, that is 178.4·280605 to 274.6·280605. A number with 24,267 digits (or 24,268): large, but an ordinary power, not a power tower.

Every visit costs a fixed number of the machine's own steps, apart from its nested visits: 4 or 8 steps for 0 → 1 (entered in C or F), 6 for 100, 8 for 110, 20 for 1010 (the step numbers in the proofs of lean/Visit.lean, the ones the panel on rounds and visits checks against the machine). So the step count is the number of visits of each kind times these costs. c/steps.c in holdouts/1RB0LB_0RC0LE_1RD0RF_1LB1RB_0LA1RB_1RE---/ repeats the checker's pass for all 161,210 rounds and adds up the counts per rule: in the completed rounds there are 5.9519·280605 visits each of the kinds 0 → 1, 100 and 1010 (the largest single count is 280605, in round 161,210: the count in round r peaks at 2r/2), and at most 2.1164·280605 more of each in the halting round, which stops part of the way through. The lower bound takes the completed rounds at the cheaper cost, the upper bound adds the whole halting round at the dearer cost.

The program's word after 161,210 rounds agrees cell for cell with the word in VisitW161210.lean, which the Lean segments check; the sums are kept as a mantissa and an exponent in floating point (the bounds are widened by 0.1% for that). Lean's theorems say "after some number of steps" and do not state the numbers. Nothing in these paragraphs is a Lean theorem.
