Before the visit law was found, the run was described as two counters ticking together. That description is kept here: it is how the run looks at the level of the machine, and parts of it are Lean theorems of their own (lean/Rules.lean, lean/Events.lean).

Two counters and an engine

Most of the time the tape is L · engine · R. Each visit of the head to the engine is an event, and every event adds 1 to both counters. In the reference machine of the family (1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_0RD1RF_0RC---), L is written in blocks of four cells, 0101 for a 1 and 0001 for a 0, lowest digit next to the engine, and R keeps one cell per digit, three cells apart (the cells 9, 12, 15, … right of the head), lowest digit nearest the engine. This machine is a relative in different clothes, the twin of 1RB0LB_0RC0LE_1RD0RF_1LB1RB_0LA0RE_1RE--- (E1 = 1RB against 0RE): the same clockwork left side (3·2k turns, each count twice, the second time 3 less) and the same tapes (at its left-end visit at step 12,788,128 its tape is exactly the twin's at step 14,008,454).

The left side is clockwork

When L overflows, the carry runs to the left end of the tape, the tape grows by two cells there, and L starts again. The lowest digit of L is the cell where the head turns most often, and the number of its turns between two consecutive left-end extensions follows a strict pattern, without a single exception in the first 1.5·109 steps (ymax.c):

48 96 96 192 192 384 384 … 3·221 3·221 3·222

So the periods of the left side are powers of two in events, each length twice (the counter gains one digit every two periods). The time per event is not constant, because a carry on the right costs more when it crosses junk, so the steps between left-end extensions grow by factors between about 1.35 and 2.05 instead of exactly 2.

A counter with junk between its digits

The cells that change between events are the digits of the counters: each digit changes about half as often as the one before (chg.c counts, over a whole period, how often each cell changes from one event to the next). On the right, between some digits of R, there are stretches of fixed cells (0101010101 00 1 00 1 00 1 and the like) that the carries cross without changing. They are written when a carry runs into the end of the tape, at irregular event counts, and their layout is what makes the run irregular. How they are produced is not decoded yet.

The dangerous moments

The machine halts only in state F reading a 1, and F is entered from C1 (0RF) only. After C1, used about 3.1·107 times in 109 steps, the head has read a 0 every time (F0 is used exactly as often); no transition is rare. Halting would need that ordinary step to find a 1. In every run short enough to simulate directly this has not happened. The proof on this page shows that it does happen, in round 161,211.

The increments, proved for every size

As in its twin, the tape holds the reference's two counters in other words; here one visit to the engine (A on cell 3) does the left increment and then the right one in a single pass. lean/Events.lean proves the combined visit for every size.

Eight machines, one mechanism

The family (G11-Y): 1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_0RD1RF_0RC--- (the reference), 1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_0RD1RB_0RC--- (the same run), 1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_0RD0LC_0RC---, 1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_1RB0RD_0RB---, 1RB0LB_0RC0LF_1LD1RE_0LA0RB_0RD0RF_0LA--- (all with left-end extensions after 2k events, each twice, to within a few hundred steps of each other); 1RB0LB_0RC0LE_1RD0RF_1LB1RB_0LA0RE_1RE--- and 1RB0LB_0RC0LE_1RD0RF_1LB1RB_0LA1RB_1RE--- (the same right part, a differently written left counter); and the mirror case 1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RB_0RD1RF_1LA---, with the junk on the left.
