"Does this machine halt?" is answered here by walking down five levels of description. Each level forgets detail the one above it keeps, and each step down is a Lean theorem that carries the answer back up: a halt found at a lower level is a halt of the level above. At the bottom there is no machine left, only one computation, and running that computation is the last link of the proof (the bottom line).

Level 1The machine
A tape of cells and a head in one of six states. One unit of time is one step.
From blank tape to the halt: between 5.9·1024,266 and 9.2·1024,266 steps (computed, not proved).
 Level 2Rounds and visits
At the start of every round the tape is 0∞ A> 1 0 0 S; a round is one visit to the word 1 0 S, which makes visits of its own, entered in state C or F. One unit is one round.
Kernel a halting round is a halt of the machine: start, round, round_halts, V_*, W_* Level 3Four rules on words
The visit as a rewriting system on words of 0s and 1s, with a trap 1011, and no machine in it. One unit is one visit.
Kernel every abstract visit is a visit of the machine: law, Law.av, Law.ah Level 4Counting visits
One pass per round carries the number of visits, written 2e − c, through the word. One unit is one block of the word.
Kernel the pass gives what the visits give: scan_sound, hscan_sound, halts_of_check Level 5One evaluation
