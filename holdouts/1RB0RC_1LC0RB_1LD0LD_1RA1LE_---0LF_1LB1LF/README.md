# 1RB0RC_1LC0RB_1LD0LD_1RA1LE_---0LF_1LB1LF

A translating archive with a chaotic engine at its right end. Between round trips the whole tape, except its first 67 cells and a zone of 450 to 610 cells at the right end, is the same word moved 16 cells to the left; the left end gains one copy of the word `1^5 0 1^6 0 1^4 0 1 0` per round trip. Halting is open; it can only happen inside the engine.

| state | read 0 | read 1 |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1RB | 0RC |
| B | 1LC | 0RB |
| C | 1LD | 0LD |
| D | 1RA | 1LE |
| E | --- | 0LF |
| F | 1LB | 1LF |

## What it does

**The two forms of the tape.** At a left turn (head at the left end, turning right) the tape is blocks of ones
separated by single 0s: `0 1^4 0 1^4 0 1^3 0 1^8 0 1^6 0 1^4 0 1 0 1^5 0 1^9 0 1^4 0 1 0 (1^5 0 1^6 0 1^4 0 1 0)^n …`.
The rightward sweep rewrites each block into runs of three ones separated by runs of 0s (at the right turn:
`1^6 0^2 1^3 0 1^3 0^2 1^3 0^3 …`); the leftward sweep, with short zigzags (`C>`, `D>`, `E>`, `F>` going left, `A>`
bouncing right), rebuilds blocks of ones. Within the sweeps, B erases blocks of ones going right and F crosses them
going left; these and two longer crossings are the shift rules of `lean/Rules.lean`:

| rule | from | to | what it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `b_erase` | `L B> (1)^n R` | `L (0)^n B> R` | Rightward sweep: B erases a block of ones. |
| `f_ones` | `L (1)^n F> 1 R` | `L F> 1 (1)^n R` | Leftward: F runs left over a block of ones, keeping them. |
| `b_word` | `L B> (1101111)^n R` | `L (1110000)^n B> R` | Rightward: B crosses the word 1101111 (a block 1^4 after 1^2) and leaves 1110000. |
| `b_word2` | `L 0 B> (101111)^n R` | `L (111000)^n 0 B> R` | Rightward: B, with a 0 behind it, crosses 101111 and leaves 111000. |

**Emergent behaviour (observed).** Compare the tape at consecutive left turns (one round trip apart):

- the first 67 cells: the left end moves 36 cells left per round trip and the word `1^5 0 1^6 0 1^4 0 1 0` (20 cells)
  is added once per round trip, n = 61 at 2.96·10^6 steps;
- everything after that, except the right end, is the previous tape moved **16 cells to the left**, cell for cell
  (cells 67..3391 of 3,833 at 2.96·10^6 steps);
- the last 450 to 610 cells change: this is the *engine*. Measured with a C scanner against the shifted previous tape:
  450 cells at 1.9·10^7 steps, 577 at 2.1·10^8, 589 at 1.2·10^9, 609 at 2.0·10^9, 605 at 3.0·10^9 (tape 128,771 cells).

In a frame moving with the archive, the picture is a chaotic band at the right edge that moves right and leaves a
frozen, non-periodic trail (the explainer has the picture). In the first ~55 round trips the whole tape is active;
then the middle freezes at once and only the right-end engine remains.

**Halting.** The only halting transition is E0, reached from `0 D>1` (D reads a 1, writes 1, moves left onto a 0).
In 10^9 steps D reads a 1 49,299,233 times, each time with a 1 on its left. The frozen archive is crossed by the shift
rules and cannot make the pattern; only the engine (or a turn) could.

## For later

- Prove the archive: a rule for a whole round trip over the frozen part (the sweep rules cross it; what is missing is a
  statement of the frozen word's language that both sweeps preserve).
- The engine is a bounded-looking chaotic system in a moving frame; its width creeps up (450 → 605 over 10^9 steps).
  A proof of non-halting probably needs a description of the engine's states, which is not known.
