The bookkeeping of a visit (a count loses one, a cleared bit moves one unit up) looks fiddly in the counts nj of the tape. In the coordinates

N_0 = n_0 − s_1,    N_j = n_j + s_j − s_(j+1),    N_k = n_k + s_k          (s_j the bits)

it becomes plain: at every visit N0 gains 2, and Nj−1 loses 1 each time bit j is set. Bit j is set once every 2j visits, so Nj−1 is a fuel tank that drains at that rate, and after V visits everything is known in closed form: bit j has seen about V/2j−1 increments, half of which set it (addV in lean/Jump.lean).

Kernel jump: if the bits do not overflow within V visits and every Nj (j ≥ 1) is still at least 2 after them, the machine goes from the zone to the zone after V visits. The proof is an induction on V: addV_succ says that one more increment after addV V is addV (V + 1), and inc_physP that the increment in these coordinates is the visit rule of the tape. The Coq proof adds the number of steps of V visits in closed form, a sum over the levels of the odometer.

The panel starts from the zone of visit 94,957 of the run, where the program jumps 16,383 visits in one go. Move V. The bars are the tanks; the lamps are the bits. The browser computes the zone after V visits with the closed form, again by V single visits of the rule, and, when it is short enough, by running the machine itself.
