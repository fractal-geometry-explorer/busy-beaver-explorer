The question "does this machine halt?" can be asked at four levels of description. Each level throws away detail the one above it keeps, and each step down is a theorem: the answer is the same at every level. The page walks down the ladder: the six-state machine on its tape, the tape at the moments it reaches new ground, the three numbers that describe those moments, and the long laps those numbers make. At the bottom the question fits in five lines of arithmetic (the definition).

Level 1The machine
A tape of cells and a head in one of six states. One unit of time is one step.
The run so far: far beyond 1030 steps (an estimate).
 Level 2Visits S(c, a, b)
The tape each time the head reaches a new cell on the right in state B. One unit is one visit, about v2 steps.
Kernel same answer as level 1: halts_iff Level 3The doubling map (c, u, v)
u is doubled modulo v and v grows by one. One unit is one operation: a visit or a carry.
Kernel same answer as level 2: halts_iff_op Level 4Laps
The same operations cut at the resets. Every lap starts at u = v − 3. One unit is one lap.
54 laps in 1011 operations, none ending in a halt.
