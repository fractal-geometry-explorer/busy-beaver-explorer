# 1RB1LF_1RC1RB_1RD0RB_0LE0LF_---1LD_0LE1LA

bbchallenge id 60424. Open. A **bouncer with merging counters**: the tape is a row of blocks `(01)^x (1)^y`; every
round trip of the head grows each `01`-block by one pair and shrinks each `1`-block by two cells; an emptied `1`-block
makes its two neighbours merge by a zigzag, and a merge at the left end **halts** the machine if its left block is not
longer than the right one (proved in Lean). The whole run is followed exactly by a *block machine* proved correct in
Lean (`lean/Macro.lean`); its C twin runs it to 10^18 steps with no halt. At all 49 merges at the left end in that range
the right block was at most 0.6 times the left one (at the tiny early merges), at most 0.346 once the left block had 50
pairs, and 0.22 to 0.35 once it had 100 (halting needs about 1); the run is roughly self-similar: from one left-end merge to the next the left block grows by a factor 1.3 to 1.5.

| state | read 0 | read 1 |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1RB | 1LF |
| B | 1RC | 1RB |
| C | 1RD | 0RB |
| D | 0LE | 0LF |
| E | --- | 1LD |
| F | 0LE | 1LA |


## The tape as words

At every visit of the left end (state A on the new leftmost cell) the tape is

    (01)^x0 s1 (01)^x1 s2 (01)^x2 ... sk (01)^xk T

where each separator `s` is a block of ones `1^y` with y odd, or one of the two short-lived markers `[0]` (just the
cell `0`) and `[10]` (the cells `1 0`), and the end T is either nothing or a single `1` before the blank tape. (Checked
at all 6,960 left-end visits of the first 10^8 steps: the separators were `1^y` (y >= 3) 27,476 times, `1` 4,434,
`[0]` 7,329 and `[10]` 2,689 times; the last block always has 1 to 3 pairs.) For example, at step 9,999,886,792:
`(01)^38561 (1)^14643 (01)^10801 (1)^8987 (01)^1723 (1)^3639 (01)^112 (1)^23 (01)^29 (1)^55 (01)^6 (1)^9 (01)^3 1 0 1 0 0 1 0`.

Going right, B turns every `01` into `10` (`b_cross`) and passes over 1s; it turns back at the first marker (or at the
right end); coming back E/D walk over the pairs and D, F, A eat into each block of ones.

**Sweep law** (checked 289,603 times in 10^10 steps, no exception): from one left-end visit to the next,
`x -> x+1` for every 01-block and `y -> y-2` for every 1-block left of the turning point. Every boundary moves one cell:
the right end of each 01-block moves right, the right end of each 1-block moves left, so each block of ones closes
from both ends at one cell per sweep and the pairs between them widen.

**Life of a separator.** `1^y -> 1^(y-2) -> ... -> 1^1 -> [0] -> [10] -> merge`. A typical stretch at the left end
(steps 11,213 to 12,950):

    (41) 1^1 (13) 1^5 (6) 1^3 (3) T
    (41) [0] (14) 1^3 (7) 1^1 (3) 1^1 (1) [0] (1)        the 1 is gone; the left block waits
    (42) [10] (13) 1^3 (7) 1^1 (3) 1^1 (1) [0] (1)       the head turned at [0]: the right part was not swept
    (29) 1^55 (6) 1^9 (3) 1^1 (1) [0] (1)                merge: 42 - 13 = 29 and 4*13 + 3 = 55

(`(x)` is `(01)^x`.) The right end of the tape keeps making small blocks, which merge into larger ones further left,
like the low digits of a counter carrying into the high ones.

## Merges

When the head going right meets a `[10]` it zigzags in place (`zig`, `lean/Rules.lean`, all a, n, k):

    L (01)^(a+1) A>0 1 (11)^n 0 (01)^(k+1) R   ->   L (01)^a A>0 1 (11)^(n+2) 0 (01)^k R      (4n+10 steps)

taking a pair from each side and adding four 1s; `zig_iter` (`lean/Merge.lean`) iterates it j times. When the right
block `(01)^b` is used up, sweeping resumes; observed at every merge:

    (01)^a [10] (01)^b (1)^c   ->   (01)^(a-b) (1)^(4b+c)

The length 2a + 2b + c is kept: the right block of pairs turns into ones, and the left block pays for the zigzag.

## The exact block machine

`lean/Macro.lean` (generated by `py/macro_lean.py`, kernel only) defines the machine that the whole description
above is a reading of: the tape cut into blocks of two cells, each side of the head run-length coded as a list of runs
`(block, count)`. One step (`mstep`) runs the Turing machine on the block the head faces until it leaves that block,
a lookup in two 48-entry window tables (every entry checked by `rfl`, `winR_spec`, `winL_spec`); when the head leaves
on the far side in the state it entered with, the whole run of equal blocks is crossed at once (`TM.cross_r`, and
`crossLeft` for the leftward direction). Proved for every configuration:

* `mstep_spec`: each block-machine step is a run of the Turing machine between the encoded configurations, and a
  block-machine halt is a halt of the machine;
* `init_cfg`: the empty block configuration is the blank tape; `mrun_blank`: any run of the block machine from it is a
  run of the machine from blank tape, with the step count it reports.

So the abstract machine is exact for the whole run, blank tape included; the blocks `(01)^x`, `(1)^y` above are its
runs `01^x` / `10^x` and `11^m`. Evaluated in Lean (`lean/MacroRun.lean`): 30,000 block steps by the kernel alone
(no halt in 1,199,390 steps), 18,000,000 by `native_decide` (no halt in 124,890,717,366 steps).

`c/msim.c` is the same machine in C (window tables computed the same way); it agrees with Lean's evaluation of `mrun`
on the step counts after 1,000, 4,635,164 and 18,000,000 block steps (5,097, 10,000,000,158 and 124,890,717,366), and
runs 10^10 steps in 0.03 s, 10^18 in about 8 minutes.

## The way to halt

The merge needs a > b. The only halting transition is E on 0, and E reads a 0 only on blank tape. Lean proves
(kernel only, `left_merge_halts`, `lean/Merge.lean`) that at the left end

    0^inf 1 1 1 (01)^a A>0 1 (11)^n 0 (01)^(a+k+2) R      halts, for every a, n, k, R:

the zigzag eats the left block, F walks left over the ones onto the blank cell, and E reads 0. Simulations of real
tapes with the left block shortened agree: with right block 28, left blocks 20..28 (and 30) halt, while 29, 31..34,
40, 60 and 83 run on for at least 3*10^6 steps. Inside the tape a merge with a short left block does not halt at once
(the zigzag walks on into the block of ones further left), but none has happened: at every internal merge the left
block was at least 1.67 times the right one, and at least 2.89 times when the right block had 15 pairs or more
(10^18 steps).

**Reduction.** Let X0, X1, X2 be the first three blocks of pairs at a merge at the left end (X1 is the right block of
the merge). Right after it the left block is X0 - X1 and the next right block is X2. Until the next left-end merge
both of them gain a pair at every sweep, and merges further right can only shorten the right one; so X0 - X1 never
goes down between two left-end merges (checked at 11,085,706 consecutive left-end visits to 10^15 steps; the only
exception is at step 2,871 with blocks of at most 10 pairs). Hence **if (X0 - X1)/X2 > 1 at a left-end merge, the next
one is safe.** This is an argument from the sweep and merge laws, which are checked by simulation, not proved.

**To 10^18 steps** (`c/msim.c`, `py/leftmerges.py`): 49 merges at the left end, no halt (the run stops at step
1,000,000,000,801,139,239, after 83,709,420,367 block steps). Some of them:

Over all of them with a left block of at least 100 pairs X1/X0 is between 0.22 and 0.35, and (X0-X1)/X2 is at least
5.8 (all 47 left-end merges to 2·10^17 steps; `py/leftmerges.py 2e17`); it is at least 2 at every one of them, the
tiny early ones included. The left
block at successive left-end merges grows by factors 1.3 to 1.5, often exactly 3/2 up to rounding
(571,835 -> 857,753 -> 1,286,629; 5,432,441 -> 8,148,662), and obeys X0' = X0 + X1 + m2 (±1), m2 the half-length of the
block of ones after X1: the new leftmost block of ones has 4·X1 + y2 cells and closes at one pair of sweeps per two
cells. The deeper structure is not a scaled copy: below the first two blocks the ratios change from merge to merge.

## Why it is hard

In light-cone coordinates each block of ones is a pair of numbers (α, β) = (left end − t, right end + t) that stay
fixed between merges; a merge of block i replaces α of block i+1 by its mirror image 2α_i − α_(i+1) and drops β_i;
the left end of the tape is a wall at β = 0, and the right end of the tape emits new small blocks. The machine halts iff
some mirror image crosses the wall. This is a deterministic, roughly scale-invariant cascade (log-periodic, with ratios
like 3/2), the kind of system for which no inductive invariant is known: the simple candidates fail. "Each block of
pairs is longer than all later ones together" holds at every visit (smallest ratio 1.65, to 10^14 steps) and survives merges, but not
the sweeps (the sum grows faster); "the blocks of pairs decrease" survives sweeps but not merges. A proof would need the
whole distribution of the cascade, as for Collatz-like machines.

## Status

Open. Proved in Lean, kernel only: the halting configurations, no halt in 10,000 steps (`lean/Blank.lean`), 10^7 by
`native_decide`; the zigzag, its iteration and the halting merge (`lean/Rules.lean`, `lean/Merge.lean`); the exact
block machine and its correctness from blank tape (`lean/Macro.lean`); no halt in 1,199,390 steps (kernel) and in
124,890,717,366 steps (`native_decide`, `lean/MacroRun.lean`). Trusted (C): no halt in 10^18 steps (`c/msim.c`), the
sweep law and merge law at every check (`c/ev.c` + `py/analyze.py`, 10^10 steps), X0 − X1 non-decreasing between
left-end merges. Heuristic: the a/b ratio at merges stays near 3; each left-end merge leaves a margin of at least 5.8
for the next one.

## For later

* State the item-level round trip (the separator life cycle above, the two ends of the tape) as rules on the block
  list and prove them from `Macro.lean` or directly with `bbtools.symtm`; the merge exit depends on the separator after
  the right block, which needs a case for each kind.
* Model the right end as a finite automaton feeding new blocks (its patterns recur); then the cascade is a system on
  the numbers (α, β) alone, and could be iterated much faster than the block machine (one step per merge).
* Look for a potential that mixes the two partial invariants above (a weighted sum of pairs and remaining lifetimes
  y/2) and survives sweeps, merges and the blocks the right end makes.
