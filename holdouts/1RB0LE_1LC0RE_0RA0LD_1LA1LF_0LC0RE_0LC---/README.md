# 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_0LC---

bbchallenge id 9169. One of the six-state holdouts whose halting from blank tape is open (group G11, family *two-word sweeper*). It differs from its siblings in F0 = 0LC, E1 = 0RE.

| state | read 0 | read 1 |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1RB | 0LE |
| B | 1LC | 0RE |
| C | 0RA | 0LD |
| D | 1LA | 1LF |
| E | 0LC | 0RE |
| F | 0LC | --- |

## What it does

Write a = `10` and b = `110`. Apart from a few cells at the ends, the tape is a string of a's and b's split into
sections by `000`. The head crosses runs of words in one state (the sweep rules `a_right_01`, `a_right_011`, `b_right_10`, `b_right_11`, `b_right_110`, `e_right_10`, `e_right_11`, `e_right_110`,
proved in Lean for any number of copies). Its turning points far from the left end follow the ruler pattern of a
binary counter with 4-cell digits, and the ends are visited rarely.

At its visits to the left end (state E) the tape starts `E>0 0 1 0 (1 1 0)^k ...`. Record values of k in a
3e9-step run (`tmrun 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_0LC--- 3000000000 at E L`): k=9 at step 41,565,138, k=10 at step 113,646,421, k=11 at step 293,313,138, k=12 at step 347,514,165, k=13 at step 510,117,516, k=14 at step 997,927,371. Each new b costs about 2 to 3 times the steps of the
previous one, as a counter would; the rest of the tape is rearranged between visits, so the counter's digits are
not plain blocks.

**Where it can halt.** `python3 -m bbtools.ctl 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_0LC--- 6` finds a closed tape language (windows of 6 cells; not yet in
Lean). It allows a halt only with F reading a 1 and `110001`, `110010` from the head on, that is
F on the first 1 of a word `110` that ends a section.

**Larger windows (worker 2, second round).** `python3 -m bbtools.ctl 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_0LC--- K` for larger K: K = 7: closes, 768 abstract configurations where a halt can fire; K = 8: no closed set within the default 100 rounds. So larger windows do not remove the halting contexts (their number grows with K) and soon stop closing: this route gave no certificate. These machines are counters, whose halting depends on a global count, which a language of K-cell windows cannot see; a certificate will have to use the macro structure.

## The engine (decoded and proved, worker 2, third round)

**Events.** At an *event* the head is in state A on a 0 just right of a base-3 counter:

    0^inf 1 0 [N] A> 0 [M] 1 [R] ...

- `N`: base 3, 3-cell digits `100` = 0, `110` = 1, `010` = 2, most significant first (lowest digit next to the head);
- `M`: binary, 4-cell digits `0010` = 0, `1010` = 1, lowest digit first, any width;
- `R`: binary, 4-cell digits `1000` = 0, `1010` = 1, lowest digit first.

(In the words a = `10`, b = `110` of the first description, M's digits are the sections `a|` and `aa`.)

**Event rule (Lean, every size).** From one event the machine reaches the next with N + 1 (trailing 2s carry; 2...2
wraps to 0...0 with one digit more, the tape growing 3 cells at the left end), M + 1 (trailing 1s carry; 1...1 wraps to
0...0), and, only when M wraps, R + 3. When M does not wrap nothing right of M is read. So N counts events in base 3,
M counts them in binary, and R gains 3 every 2^(width of M) events. `lean/Events.lean`: 54 theorems, one per case (exhaustive by construction of `events.py`; 9 cases of N:
lowest digit 0 or 1 after 0, 1 or k+2 twos, or the wrap at the left end; times 6 of M and R: no wrap, carry over i+1
ones, or a wrap of i+1 ones with the 4 cases of R's two low bits and a carry over j ones), each with its exact step
count (for example 12i + 12j + 10k + 116), generated by
`python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/events.py 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_0LC--- NAMESPACE`.
Example (run of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`, M with 2 digits, R with 8): at steps 12,203, 12,223, 12,243, 12,253, 12,345 (N, M, R) =
(5, 0, 92), (6, 1, 92), (7, 2, 92), (8, 3, 92), (9, 0, 95).

**The run.** In the clean shape R is followed by `1 0 1` and blank tape. This run reaches it at step 533
(N = 1 (1 digit), M = 4 (4 digits), R = 0 (2 digits)); then 28 consecutive events follow the rule, until the first overflow of R (2 digits)
(`python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/track.py 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_0LC--- 3000000`). An overflow of R (the carry running past R) is not covered: the head
goes past R and rebuilds the right part, and later the cells after R hold a further structure starting `1 0 1 1 0`, with
M and R of other widths. Counting every event whose right part starts `0 [M] 1`: in the first 3·10^6 steps
5,729 follow from the previous event by the rule and 1,022 do not (the wraps of M that reach the
undecoded part); at step 3·10^6, N = 150 (5 digits), M with 1 digit.

**Siblings.** All six machines of the family satisfy the same 54 rules (the transitions in which they differ are never

3,177, 7,571 (before the clean shape) and 20,756, 53,353, 92,444, 92,861 (at the first three overflows of R).

**What is open.** No event can halt (every rule is a run); a halt can only come from an overflow of R or from the

decode the layered right part (it seems to hold further counters), then iterate.

## The right side as a recursive visit law (worker 2, round 2 of G11)

Idea (from the busycoq proof of `1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_0RD1RF_0RC---`): instead of rules for events plus
separately decoded overflows, describe what **one visit** of the head into a stream does, for every stream.
`Vis q x r x'` (`lean/Visit.lean`): the head enters the stream `x` from the left in state `q`, and the first time it
leaves `x` to the left it is in state `r` and the stream is `x'`.

**Token rows (Lean, kernel).** Cut the tape right of the event cell (cell -17) into tokens of two cells,
`z = 00`, `a = 10`, `c = 01`, `d = 11`. A visit of `token x` is a few steps inside the token and some nested visits
of `x`. In 3·10^7 steps of the run exactly 22 rows occur (`py/laws.py` of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`); each is a theorem of
`lean/Visit.lean` (written by `py/gen_visit.py`), conditional on the nested visits and valid for every stream, e.g.
`v_B_10_Bc`: if `B> x` comes out in C leaving `x'`, then `B> a x` comes out in A leaving `a x'`.

**The a/z stream (Lean, kernel, `lean/Stream.lean`).** Everything from R on, blank tape included (blank is
`z z z ...`), is one stream of `a` and `z`, and a visit in state B does this: the first `z` becomes `a`, and every
second `a` before it becomes `z`:

    B> (a a)^k z x    ->  (z a)^k a x      out in C     (vb_even)
    B> a (a a)^k z x  ->  a (z a)^k a x    out in A     (vb_odd; the same for a visit in state E: ve_odd)

With R's digits `a z` = 0, `a a` = 1 the odd case is the binary increment. **So R never overflows into something
else: there is no end of R.** What was called the overflow of R is the even case: when all digits up to the blank
tape are 1 the first `z` stands at an even place, the visit comes out in C instead of A, and leaves `(z a)^k a`:
the digits shifted by one token. (First time in the run of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`: step 20,645, the stream `a^18` then blank, comes
out in C as `(z a)^9 a`.)

**M (Lean, kernel).** `m_inc`: `A> (c c)^k z c y` leaves `(z c)^k c c y`, out in D (M + 1, digits `z c` = 0,
`c c` = 1, for every k and every `y`). `m_wrap`: `A> (c c)^(k+1) c x` leaves `(z c)^(k+1) c x3`, out in D, where
`x3` is `x` after three visits (E, B, B) that each come out in A. With `vb_odd`/`ve_odd` this is the right half of the
event rule (M + 1, R + 3) in visit form, and it shows exactly where the event rule stops: at a visit of the a/z
stream that comes out in C.

**What is still open.** After a visit that comes out in C the token rows with other nested results apply (they are
in `Visit.lean`, among them one row that leaves a token in state F, the state whose 1 halts), and the layer of `c`
tokens is rebuilt with other widths. These rows are proved one by one but not composed into a law for the whole
right side, and the left side (N, tokens of three cells) has its own table (`py/laws.py` on the mirrored machine:
37 rows for `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`), not yet in Lean. There is no token-level simulator with jumps yet, so no run beyond the
3·10^9 steps of `tmrun`. A decision needs: the composed law for the `c` layer after a C exit, the left side's law,
then either an invariant or a far run.

## The overflows of R and the eras after them (November 2026)

The later layout of the family is

    0^inf 1 0 [N] A> 0 [Z] 1 1 [C] 0 1 [R] ...

with N in base 3 as before, Z the counter called M above (digits `0010` = 0, `1010` = 1), a new binary counter C
(digits `0001` = 0, `0101` = 1, closed by `0 1`) and R as before, all lowest digit first. **Cascade rule (Lean,
every size):** from one event to the next N + 1 and Z + 1; when Z wraps, C + 3; when C wraps, R + 3.
`lean/Cascade.lean`: 162 theorems, one per case, each with its exact step count, generated by
`python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/events2.py MACHINE NAMESPACE` (the full description, the checker
`py/cascade_check.py` and the eras of the sibling with F0 = 0LB are in
[its README](../1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/README.md)).

**This machine.** In the first 3·10^8 steps the full events (the tape left of the head exactly `0^inf 1 0 [N]`) have the plain layout only at steps 1,456 to 9,824 with C of 5 digits and R of 3 (216 events); 214 consecutive pairs of them follow the cascade rule, with no mismatch (`py/cascade_check.py`). All other full events carry more words between Z and R. Their commonest shapes, in tokens (Zk, Ck: words of k digits; Xk: a word of k `a z` / `a a` digits; Rk: the last such word; `c`, `a`, `d`: single tokens):

| steps | shape | full events |

| 13,432,394 – 289,977,742 | `Z1 d c a d c a X5 d C7 a X5 d C4 a X4 d C5 R3` | 226,544 |
| 289,979,828 – 299,999,968 | `Z1 d C1 a X5 d C8 a X5 d C4 a X4 d C5 R3` | 98,252 |

## One law for every era: a chain of binary counters (November 2026)

The layered shapes of this sibling's later run are all one layout, the one decoded for
`1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---` (see its README). At a full event the cells right of the head read
`A> 0 [Z] 11 [W1] 01 [U1] 1011 [W2] 01 [U2] ... 1011 [Wm] 01 [Um] 0^inf` (Z: `0010`/`1010`, W: `0001`/`0101`, U:
`1000`/`1010`, lowest digit first, any widths, any W or U possibly empty). The **chain law** holds: each event adds 1
to Z, and each word that wraps sends 3 to the next word, so Z + 2^|Z|/3 · (W1 + 2^|W1|/3 · (U1 + ...)) grows by
exactly 1 per event, as long as no carry leaves the last word.

- Lean (kernel): `lean/Layers.lean`, `layer_event` and `layer_event_n0t0` … `layer_event_novk`, for every layout.
  Generated by `python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/layers_gen.py MACHINE NAMESPACE`.
- The siblings with E1 = 0RE make one more visit in E (a bounce) before each increment of a W and inside each unit; the generator handles them (`py/layers_bounce.json`).
- Trusted: `python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/wordchain.py MACHINE jump ERAS 10` follows the
  run from blank tape, jumping over each era by the law and running each change of era cell by cell.
- `python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/wordchain.py 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_0LC--- check 50000000`: 42,877 pairs of consecutive full events in one layout follow the law, 0 mismatches; 12 changes of layout, 15 full events in no layout.
- `python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/wordchain.py 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_0LC--- jump 60 10`: 60 changes of era, 8.0·10^29 events from blank tape (so at least that many steps), no halt; 20,232,708 steps run cell by cell; at the end the chain has 17 words with 124 digits in all; spot checks: 20,803 events, 0 mismatches.

After the last of these changes of era the run reaches full events outside the chain grammar: somewhere along the chain the tape is out of phase by half a digit. Seen so far: `... U a c W ...` where the grammar has `... U a d W ...`; a W followed by U digits with no `c` between them; a half digit (`z` or `a`) at the end of Z before its `d`; and a stock of `c z` tokens after the last W, which the last W takes one digit at a time, one per overflow, until the stock is used up.

**The law up to a defect (Lean, every layout).** `lean/Layers.lean` now proves the chain law with any tape after the last word (`encT B`): no visit reads past the last word unless a carry leaves it. So the chain law holds on the longest prefix of the chain that is in the grammar, whatever defect comes after it, until a carry leaves that prefix. `py/prefixjump.py` jumps with this and runs only the crossings of a defect cell by cell:

- `python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/prefixjump.py 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_0LC--- 600 2`: 600 jumps, about 10^77 events from blank tape (so at least that many steps), no halt; jumps from a prefix with a defect after it: 457, from a chain wholly in the grammar: 145; 16,738 full events had no prefix that could jump and were run cell by cell; spot checks: 5,487 events, 0 mismatches.

**What a carry does at a defect (trusted, `py/defects.py`).** Every defect met is one of two kinds. (1) One erased cell in a separator: after Z `d` = 11 read as `c` = 01, after a W `c` = 01 read as `z` = 00, after a U `ad` = 1011 read as `ac` = 1001. The law for these: carries follow the chain law up to the defect, and when a carry of c > 0 reaches it the separator is restored and the carry goes on as c − 3. So the defect takes exactly one unit, once, and then the chain is the plain chain again. (2) A half-digit shift: a separator missing altogether (a W running straight into its U), or a separator after Z read as `a`, `ad`, `zd` or `ac`. When a carry meets one, the words after it are read again with W and U swapped and the widths move by a digit, as at a change of era. This kind is not decoded.

- `python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/defects.py 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_0LC--- check 30000000`: 30,000,000 steps: 26,504 consecutive pairs of full events follow the law, 3 of them crossing a defect, 0 mismatches; 19 events are not covered (a carry leaves the chain or meets an undecoded defect).
- `python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/defects.py 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_0LC--- run 2000 2` (stopped by a time limit): about 10^927 events from blank tape (so at least that many steps), no halt; 6,844 jumps, 5,278 crossings by the defect law, about 1,700 events run cell by cell; self-checks (random events of each jump and a fifth of the crossings, rebuilt and run cell by cell): 14,729 events, 0 mismatches.

Open: the half-digit shifts.

A halt can only come when a carry leaves the last word of the grammar prefix: at a change of era or when it crosses a defect. What a change of era hands on is open here; for the E1 = 0RA machines see `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`.
