Leave out the states, the step counts and the two forms, and what remains is a rewriting system on words. In VisitLaw.lean it is two inductive relations on streams with no machine in them: AV s s', "one visit rewrites s to s'", and AH s, "a visit to s halts".

A visit also halts if one of its nested visits halts (AH.h, AH.g, AH.l1, AH.l2). So a block 000 or 100 is a binary digit: it flips on every visit and passes every second visit on. A block 1010 is a doubler: it passes every visit on twice. A block 1011 is the trap.

The guard. The doubler rule carries the side condition "the stream behind does not start with 11" (Guard), because the first of its two nested visits is made in form W. In this machine form W has no rule for 110, so the doubler rule can only be turned into visits of the machine when the stream behind the doubler does not start with 11. Lean proves that after two visits a stream never starts with 11 (av2_guard), and the checker below tests the condition every time it uses the rule.

From the rules to the machine. Law lists what a machine's two forms must satisfy, one field per rule, and Law.av, Law.ah turn every abstract visit into a visit of the machine in form V (and in form W when the guard holds). lean/Law.lean fills in the fields with the theorems of the table above (law); everything is checked by the kernel.
