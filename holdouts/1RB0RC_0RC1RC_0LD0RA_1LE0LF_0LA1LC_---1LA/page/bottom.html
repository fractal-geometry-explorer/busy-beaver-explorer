It never halts.

Started on a blank tape, 1RB0RC_0RC1RC_0LD0RA_1LE0LF_0LA1LC_---1LA runs for ever. From step 1,818,198,163,340,705 on its run is the growth cycle: C(r) comes back as C(r + 321,042,928) after 116,320,154 arrivals of state E at the left end, and so on without end.

The Lean theorem is nonhalt : ¬ HaltsFromBlank in lean/Replay.lean, ledger level native_decide. What it rests on, exactly:

  • The Lean kernel checks the definitions of the machine, the soundness of the symbolic executor (run_sound, run_pos, rule_instN, ruleOK_of_run in lean/Exec.lean), the 31 shift rules (lean/ExecData.lean, each by rfl) and the last implication, nonhalting_of_growth_cycle (lean/Cycle.lean).
  • Fourteen evaluations by native_decide, which trust Lean's compiler as well as its kernel: the twelve cycle rules (rule0_ok … rule11_ok, from 422 to 201,007 executor moves each), the arrival from blank tape (arrive_run, 5,037,044 moves) and the period (period_run, 5,581,714 moves). #print axioms nonhalt lists propext, Quot.sound and these fourteen evaluations; there is no sorry.
  • Not used: any simulator outside Lean. The executor's strategy (which rule, how many times, how to re-bracket the tape) is not trusted; only its soundness is, and that is proved.
  • Not stated in Lean: the numbers of steps and events, such as the step 1,818,198,163,340,705 at which C(16,797,709) is first reached; Lean says only "reaches, in at least one step". Those counts come from py/closure/accel.py, py/replay/engine.py and verify/sim3.cpp, which agree (computed, not proved).

The short cycle rules can be checked by the kernel alone (decide +kernel was tried on rule 4, 422 moves, and rule 11, 3,255 moves); the drain rule and the two long runs have not been, so there is no kernel-only proof of nonhalt yet. A second proof of the same statement, in Coq on top of busycoq (coq/H65826.v, nonhalt : ~ halts tm c0), evaluates all its runs inside Coq's kernel with vm_compute and reports no axioms; the ledger lists it as kernel level.
