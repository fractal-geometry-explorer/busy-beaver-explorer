# 1RB1RF_0RC0RE_0RD1RA_1LE0LA_1LB0LD_0RD---

bbchallenge id 124060. One of the six-state holdouts whose halting from blank tape is open. Its tape is a
**queue of blocks (01)^k whose sizes are mostly powers of 4: blocks are used up one copy at a time from a cursor
and rebuilt four times larger at the right end**; the feeding rule is proved in Lean, the global schedule is not
understood, halting is open.

| state | read 0 | read 1 |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1RB | 1RF |
| B | 0RC | 0RE |
| C | 0RD | 1RA |
| D | 1LE | 0LA |
| E | 1LB | 0LD |
| F | 0RD | --- |


## What it does

Write `a` for the word `01` and `b` for `10111`. After the first few hundred steps the tape is

    a 1 1 (a b)^p a^2 (b a^4)^q b a^8 (b a^16)^r ... b a^k 1 a^m 1 F> 0^inf

a list of blocks a^k separated by b, with k mostly a power of 4 (1, 4, 16, 64, 256, 1024, ...) or twice one (2, 8,
32, 128, ...), and one or two *cursors* (a single `1` in place of a `b`) near the right end. The left end has not
moved since step 1022; the right end grows like the square root of the step count.

**The feeding rule.** Every time the head comes to the right end (state F on the blank cell), the block before the
last cursor gives up one copy of `01` and the block at the end gains three:

    L a^(k+1) 1 a^m 1 F> 0^inf  ->  L a^k 1 a^(m+3) 1 F> 0^inf      in 6m+24 steps

(`feed` in lean/Rules.lean, generated by py/rules.py with bbtools.symtm; `feed'` and its iteration `feeds` in
lean/Phase.lean). So a block of k copies is converted, over k visits, into k·3 copies at the end, which with the
copies already there and the cursor gives the factor 4 between block sizes. When the block is used up, the cursor
moves to the next b to its left and the next block is fed, a deeper cursor appears when a whole run of equal blocks
is used up, and the head's round trips get longer as the end moves away (hence the square-root growth).

With two cursors the end looks like `a^k 1 a^m b a^n 1 F>`, and per visit the k-block loses one copy, the middle
block gains four and the last block loses one (seen in the run, e.g. `a^103 1 a^612 b a^616 1` at step 4,793,306
becomes `a^3 1 a^1012 b a^516 1` 100 visits later); this rule is not proved yet.

The picture is that of a counter whose digits are the numbers of blocks of each size, in base 4-like fashion: a
run of (b a^(4^j))^c blocks is turned, one block at a time, into blocks of size 4^(j+1) at the end.

## Where it could halt

The machine halts only on F reading 1: A reads a 1 and moves right onto another 1. In the run F is entered at the
right end, on blank tape; a halt needs a `1 1` met by A in the middle of the tape, which the block structure (every
`1 1` sits inside a `b = 10111` or the left prefix) does not offer to A in the observed phases.

## Status

Open. Proved in Lean: the halting configurations, no halt in 10^7 steps (`lean/Blank.lean`), the feeding rule and its
iteration (`lean/Rules.lean`, `lean/Phase.lean`). Observed (not proved): the block sizes, the two-cursor rule, the
structure of the queue. Not understood: the order in which blocks are fed and new cursors are created (the global
schedule), so there is no macro map yet.

### Next steps

- Prove the two-cursor rule (state it after the cursor block is used up: the symtm run needs `n+1` split cases) and
  the transitions when a block is used up, then write the queue as a counter and find its update rule.
- A CTL certificate on the language of blocks `(01)^* (10111 (01)^*)^*` with cursors, which would prove the machine
  never meets the `1 1` that halts if the phases seen are the only ones.
