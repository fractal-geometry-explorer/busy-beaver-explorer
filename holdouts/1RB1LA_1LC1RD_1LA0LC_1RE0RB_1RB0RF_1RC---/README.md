# 1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_1RC---

Nobody knows whether this six-state, two-symbol Turing machine halts when started on a blank tape: it is one of the holdouts left open by the bbchallenge project. It belongs to a family of four holdouts that share their first four states, and works as two binary counters, one counting down in the blocks left of the head and one counting up in the gaps right of it. This page lists what is established, sorted by what each result rests on.

| state | read 0 | read 1 |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1RB | 1LA |
| B | 1LC | 1RD |
| C | 1LA | 0LC |
| D | 1RE | 0RB |
| E | 1RB | 0RF |
| F | 1RC | --- |

**Verdict.** **The question is open.** Lean now proves the counter itself: read the tape as two binary numbers, E in the odd blocks left of the head and N in the gaps right of it, and every countdown of E, for every size of E and N, runs as the counter says (`run3`, `run5`). Lean also proves that the blank-tape run enters this counter after 160 steps and runs its first countdown. The pass rules (including the halting one) are proved too. What is not proved is what happens between countdowns. When E runs out the tape is reorganised; the reorganisation leaves *defects* on the right (gaps of an irregular size, blocks of two 1s), and the machine halts only if such defects meet in one particular way. No invariant excluding this is known. An accelerated simulation, whose jumps are instances of the Lean countdowns, reaches 2.53·10^819 steps with no halt and with never more than one defect at a time. Coq now proves that halting is equivalent to a question about one explicit map, for 1a and 1b at once: the machine halts if and only if the round map of a closed counter model, iterated from (2, 5), reaches a corner X = 2^L (`halts_iff_corner`). The second-overflow cascade is proved too, and it narrows the door to one equation: a corner comes only from rule R5 with the unread rest m of the right counter equal to (2D+1)·2^s − 1, D being how far the left counter is below full (`halts_iff_r5_match`, also in Lean for the model). Whether it does is open; in 400,000 rounds (L up to 132 million) it stayed at least 0.47·L bits away, and a hit would need the unread top of X to equal, exactly, a number of about 0.7·L bits computed from its bottom.

## What it does

One of four holdouts (1a `..._1RB0RF_0RB---`, 1b `..._1RB0RF_1RC---`, 1c `..._1RF0LE_1RA---`, 1d `..._1RF1RC_1RA---`)
that share states A–D, `1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB`. The family's scripts are in `holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/`.

At the start of a *pass* (head in state A on a 0, a block `1^m` to its right) the tape is

    0^inf 1^(2s) 0 1^(2v_1+1) 0 1^(2v_2+1) ... A>0 1^m 0^(3q_1+2) 1 0^(3q_2+2) 1 ... 0^inf

A pass eats the top block two cells at a time (A, B, C carry 1s to the left part; D runs right into the first gap):
m even: two 1s go left, the first gap shrinks by one; m odd: D crosses all gaps of the smallest size and cuts the
first larger one; m = 2, 3: the block merges with the left part (`lean/Rules.lean`, all proved for every parameter).

Read the odd blocks on the left as the binary digits of a number E (a block `1^(2v+1)` is a 1 bit followed by v zero
bits) and the gaps on the right as the digits of F. One *cycle* of passes maps (E, F) to (E − 1, F + 1) in
a + 4ν(E) + 6ν(F+1) steps (ν = 2-adic valuation; a = 54 in the commonest shape of 1a): two binary counters. When E runs
out, a *phase transition* starts a new countdown (round 2: the new E comes from the floor block, see below) (1a: phases end at 4,071,811, 8,593,882,720, ~3·10^23, ~10^42, ...).
Transitions are irregular: they can leave a long gap (a reservoir that later overflows of F eat 3 cells at a time) and
short sub-phases in other shapes.

The machine halts only when F reads a 1, and from a pass start that happens exactly when an odd pass, after the small
gaps, meets a gap one cell too short (`pass_odd_halt`). During a countdown all gaps are at least 2 long, so only a
phase transition can create the fatal gap. Whether one ever does is the open question.

### Round 2: the counter exactly, and the transitions

Read the tape at the start of a pass as two binary numbers. Left of the head, going outward, every pair of cells is one bit of *E*, lowest bit first: `1 1` is a 0 and `1 0` is a 1, down to an even block, the *floor*. Right of the head, after the top block, every group of 3 cells is one bit of *N*: `0^3` is a 0 and `0^2 1` is a 1. (So an odd block `1^(2v+1)` is v zero bits and a one bit, and a gap is a run of zero bits closed by a one bit.)

With top block `1^3` (mode 3) one pair of passes subtracts 1 from E and adds 1 to N; with `1^5` (mode 5) two pairs subtract 1 from E and add 2 to N. A pair costs a constant plus 4 steps per trailing zero of E plus 6 per trailing one of N, exactly the work of borrowing and carrying.

When E reaches 0 the floor `1^(2s)` is popped into the top block; the next pass pushes s single 1s, which is E ≈ 2^s − 1, all ones, and the mode switches between 3 and 5. N is left on the right, but the pass that cut it leaves a gap of the wrong size, a *defect*, in front of it.

Defects are worked off in a cascade of short sub-phases: a gap one cell too long collapses into a block `1^2`; a block `1^2` followed by a gap makes the next gap one too short; a gap one too short followed by a single 1 is cut and hands its defect to the next gap. Sometimes N is swallowed into the left part entirely and restarts at 0. Then a clean countdown runs again. Since the floor gains 2 cells each time E passes a power of two, the floor is about twice the bit length of the largest E so far, and phases grow fast.

The machine halts only when F reads a 1. From a pass start that happens exactly when an odd pass, after the gaps of the smallest size, meets a gap of length one followed by a block of two or more 1s (`pass_odd_halt`, proved). In the language above that needs *two* defects at once: a gap cut down to length one, directly in front of the `1^2` left by another defect. In every transition seen so far a single defect was worked off without harm.

During a countdown there are no defects at all, so a halt can only come inside a transition cascade. In the 6.3 million passes that the round-2 run of 1b simulated directly (between its 107 jumps), the right part never held more than one defect, never more than one `1^2` block, and the fatal precursor (a gap one cell short directly followed by a block of two or more) never occurred.

## What is established

**Proved in Lean, kernel only:**

- **The halting configurations.** The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (F1); the generated statement covers every configuration. (`step_none_iff`, `lean/Blank.lean`)
- **No halt in the first 10,000 steps.** The list simulator of the shared library, proved to agree with the machine's semantics, runs 10,000 steps from blank tape without a halt, evaluated by Lean's kernel. (`blank_runs_kernel`, `lean/Blank.lean`)
- **The pass rules.** Six rules on symbolic tapes, for all values of their parameters and any surrounding tape: a pass on an even top block (`L 1 A>0 1^(2k+4) 0^(g+2) 1 R`), on a block of 2, on an odd block crossing j small gaps, on a block of 3, a gap of length one closing, and `pass_odd_halt`: an odd pass whose D-run meets a gap one cell too short reaches F reading a 1, the only halting transition. Proofs by `rfl` steps and crossing lemmas, written by `tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py`. (`lean/Rules.lean`, `holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/py/gen_rules.py`)
- **1a and 1b agree on F0 followed by 0.** From `L x F>0 0 R` both 1RB1LA_..._1RB0RF_0RB--- and 1RB1LA_..._1RB0RF_1RC--- reach `L A>x 1 1 R` in 3 steps (`f00_0`, `f00_1`). The two machines differ only when F reads a 0 followed by a 1. (`lean/Rules.lean`)
- **The counter, proved for every size.** Write E in p bits on the left (from the head outward, a 0 bit is `1 1` and a 1 bit is `1 0`, above a floor that starts with a 1) and N in n bits on the right (a 0 bit is `0^3`, a 1 bit is `0^2 1`, followed by any tape X). In mode 3 (top block `1^3`) the machine takes (E, N) to (E − k, N + k) for every k ≤ E with N + k < 2^n, in at least 22·k steps (`run3`); in mode 5 (top block `1^5`) it takes (E, N) to (E − k, N + 2k) in at least 48·k steps (`run5`). Proofs: three pass-pair rules written by `tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py`, the binary decrement and increment on bit lists, and induction on k. (`lean/Counter.lean`, generated by `holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/py/gen_counter.py`)
- **The blank-tape run enters the counter.** After 160 steps from blank tape the machine is in mode 5 with E = 17 (5 bits) and N = 1 (`init_counter`), and it then counts E down to 0, reaching N = 35 after at least 816 further steps (`first_countdown`). (`lean/Init.lean`, generated by `holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/py/gen_init.py`)
- **The cascade lemma, for the model.** `lean/Cascade.lean` copies the model step `f` of `SOC23Model.v` case for case and proves `doom_step`, `bad0_step` and `corner_iff_r5`: the orbit from `cfgL 2 0 5` reaches a corner iff it reaches an R5 configuration with m + 1 = (2D+1)·2^(3a+3). It assumes that each step keeps the invariant P, which Coq proves (`f_spec`); the link to the machines is Coq's `halts_iff_corner`. (`holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/lean/Cascade.lean`)

**Proved in Coq (busycoq definitions):**

- **A halt needs a corner round (Coq).** `halts_needs_corner b : halts (machine b) c0 -> exists j len, iter_n j (cfgL 2 0 5) = Some (cfgL len 0 (2^len))` for 1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB--- (b = false) and 1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_1RC---. `SOC23Model.v`: a closed counter model (left digits 10/11, right digits 100/000, two shifted right counters), eight raw rules proved for every parameter by busycoq's symbolic execution for both tables, a computable step `f` with invariant `P` and `f_spec` (f stops only at a corner `cfgL len 0 (2^len)`). `SOC23_Holdouts.v`: both machines reach `cfgL 2 0 5`. (`holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/coq/SOC23Model.v`, `holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/coq/SOC23_Holdouts.v`, `holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/coq/build.sh`)
- **Every corner halts, so halting is equivalent to reaching a corner (Coq).** `halts_iff_corner bb : halts (machine bb) c0 <-> exists j len, iter_n j (cfgL 2 0 5) = Some (cfgL len 0 (2^len))` for both machines. The new half is `corner_halts`: from `cfgL len 0 (2^len)`, for every len ≥ 1, the machine halts. The path is the same for every len: `LC_Ov` turns the full right counter into a shifted counter twice too large and the left counter is counted all the way down (2^len − 1 pairs); the left counter overflows again and gains a digit (rule `E1`), and the right part becomes a counter of len − 1 digits ended by `0011`; that counter is counted up through all its values twice, the carry between the passes turning `0011` into `1011`; then a fixed sweep halts (rule `Hend`). `E1` and `Hend` are tape rules with free parameters, proved for both tables by busycoq's symbolic execution; the rest reuses the counter lemmas of `SOC23Model.v`. Closed under the global context, Coq 8.20.1, busycoq BB6 branch 33209bc, 31 s for the three files. (`holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/coq/SOC23Corner.v`, `holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/coq/build.sh`)
- **A corner can only come from one equation at one rule (Coq).** `halts_iff_r5_match bb : halts (machine bb) c0 <-> exists j x, iter_n j (cfgL 2 0 5) = Some x /\ r5match x` for both 1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB--- and 1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_1RC---. After the left counter overflows, the right counter is read as a cascade of second overflows (where busycoq's `SOC_2_3_v2` template needs `ROv2_0`/`ROv2_1`; here rules R1–R5 of `SOC23Model.v`). Call a configuration doomed when its plain countdown ends at a corner (`cfgR len k n` with k + n + 1 = 2^len). `doom_step`: the only step that makes a doomed configuration out of one that is not doomed is rule R5 (a shifted counter `cfgS1` with an odd number h = 2a+1 of digits running out), and only when the unread rest m of the right counter satisfies m + 1 = (2D+1)·2^(3a+3), D = 2^len − 1 − k (`r5match`). The blank ends R1, R2, R3 never give a corner (the new left counter is an odd number times 2^s, minus 1); the one exception `cfgS0 len (2^len-1) 0 0 0` is reached by no step (`bad0_step`). Closed under the global context, Coq 8.20.1, busycoq BB6 branch 33209bc, 2 s on top of the other files. (`holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/coq/SOC23Match.v`, `holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/coq/build.sh`)

**Proved in Lean with `native_decide`:**

**Heuristic:**

- **Growth shape: exp/fixed.** Left of the start, the visited part of the tape stopped growing in the second half of the run (bounded so far, or growing so slowly that no record fell in the last half). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a *counter* or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.036). (`tools/py/bbtools/triage.py`)
- **The phase transition.** When E reaches 0 the floor block `1^(2s)` under it is popped into the top block, the next pass pushes s single 1s, and a new countdown starts with E = 2^s − 1 (s one bits; floor `1^2`) in the other mode (3 and 5 alternate); N stays on the right, but the cut leaves a gap of irregular size in front of it. Irregular gaps then act in a cascade of short sub-phases: a gap one too long (for 1a/1b; the analogous residue mod 5 for 1c/1d) collapses into a block `1^2`, a gap one too short followed by a single 1 is cut down and passes its defect on to the next gap; sometimes the whole of N is pulled to the left and N restarts at 0. The floor grows by 2 whenever E passes a power of two, so it is about twice the bit length of the largest E so far. Seen at every transition of the runs above; not proved, and no closed formula for the new (E, N) as a function of the old one is known. (`holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/py/jump2.py` (log of the tapes at each counter start))
- **What a corner would take.** Count the left counter by its deficit D = 2^len − 1 − k: a round starts at D = 0, a countdown of n adds n, an overflow that appends s digits sends D to (2D+1)·2^s. A round reads X from the low end and ends when all of X is read (R1, R2, R3 on an empty rest) or at R5, which leaves the top part m of X unread; then X' = 2^L' − (2D+1)·2^s + m + 1 (R5), or 2^L' − (2D+1)·2^s (+1 for R1). So a corner can only come from R5 with m + 1 = (2D+1)·2^s (R1 would need X = 1): the unread top of X must equal a number built from the bits read. That number has about 2.5 times as many bits as the part read, so a corner needs one round to read about 0.29·L bits and the remaining 0.71·L bits to match exactly (random odds about 2^(−0.7L)); only round 10 read that much (53%, right after a full read), and from round 35 on no round read more than 22.3%. Derived by hand from `f`; `c/model23.c` computes rounds in this form. No invariant excluding the match is known: low bits decide where a round stops, top bits are what earlier rounds left, which makes the question Collatz-like. (`holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/c/model23.c`)
- **How a round reads X, and why sizes do not settle it.** The cascade reads X from its low end as runs `0^h 1` and looks only at the parity of each h: a reader with four states. In S0 an even run moves to S1 and an odd run stays in S0; in S1 with h even, a 0 next starts another run in S1 and a 1 next swallows a block `1^j 0` and moves to S0 with h = j; in S1 with h odd the round stops (R5). All of the first 3,000 rounds but two (3 and 8, which read all of X) end at R5. At R5 the equation first needs the low s bits of m to be 1s and bit s to be 0: 129 of the 2,998 R5 rounds (4.3%). Then the bits of 2D+1 must match: the best matched 6 (round 776, out of 45). The target (2D+1)·2^s was longer than m + 1 in six rounds (0, 1, 2, 4, 9, 33) and shorter in all others, so the lengths cross and size alone cannot exclude a match. In 20,000 rounds (L up to 3.9 million) the low s bits matched in 887 of 19,998 R5 rounds, and the longest agreement was at round 6634, where all 5 bits of 2D+1 matched, but m+1 there is millions of bits longer than the target; in 30,000 rounds the target was longer than m+1 only in the same six early rounds. (`holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/py/r5_match.py`)
- **How long a round reads: a power-law tail, and the odds of a match.** Over 200,000 rounds from (2, 5) (L up to 58.6 million), the bits r read by one R5 round have a heavy tail: P(r ≥ x) ≈ 5.5·x^−0.9 for x from 10 to 100,000. That is a power law, not geometric. Long reads are mostly one long run (median 66% of a read of 1,000 bits or more), left in X by earlier rounds. Per bit read, the target (2D+1)·2^s grows 1.5 to 2.5 bits (median 1.69). A match needs r + tbits = bits(X), but after round 35 the largest r + tbits was 0.78 of X, and no round read more than 22.3%. Equal lengths came only in rounds 0, 1, 2, 4, 9 and 33, and none matched. The zeros under the leading 1 agreed between m + 1 and the target in 93 rounds, so that condition alone excludes nothing. If later rounds follow the fitted tail, the expected number of rounds after 35 with equal lengths is about 0.0006, and each would then need some 900 or more further bits to agree. This is a heuristic for never halting, not a proof. (`holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/c/r5tail.c`, `holdouts/1RB1LA_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_1RE0RB_1RB0RF_0RB---/py/r5_tail.py`)

**Open:**

- **Does it halt from blank tape?.** Not known. Coq proves the question equivalent to one equation in the model orbit from (2, 5): the machine halts exactly when, at some rule R5 (the end of a second-overflow cascade), the unread rest m of the right counter satisfies m + 1 = (2D+1)·2^s (`halts_iff_r5_match`). In 400,000 rounds X stayed at least 0.47·L bits away from a corner, and in 3,000 rounds no R5 matched more than 6 bits of 2D+1 past the first s. No invariant ruling the equation out is known.

## For later

Coq (SOC23Model.v, SOC23Corner.v, SOC23Match.v) proves halting equivalent to the model orbit reaching a corner X = 2^L, and that a corner needs an R5 round whose unread top part m of X satisfies m + 1 = (2D+1)·2^s (`halts_iff_r5_match`). The cascade itself is a four-state reader of the parities of the runs of X (see "How a round reads X"). Next: the bits a round reads near the bottom of X are, up to a borrow, the complement of the previous round's D, so the reader's path through them is set by the previous round's runs; but the read lengths have a power-law tail (see "How long a round reads"), so a cap on the share read would have to come from structure, not from the reader stopping with a fixed chance per run. Also: look for structure in the top bits of X (after round 34 they are isolated 1s left by earlier rounds, plus long runs of 1s where a borrow went through a gap) that a round's D can never match, or a reason that no round reads more than a bounded share of X. The earlier plan, in the language of passes: the counter is proved (`lean/Counter.lean`), so what is left is the transitions. Next steps: (1) prove the transition events as symtm rules (floor pop, the cut that leaves a defect, the gap-collapse and defect-passing events; the event patterns need an explicit continuation after the gap or symtm stops with TouchContext); (2) define a defect-aware configuration (counter plus at most one defect, with its position and type) and try to show it is closed under a countdown followed by a cascade; if two-defect configurations are unreachable this gives non-halting with Lean, combined with `init_counter`; (3) failing that, log (E, N, defect) at each counter start from `py/jump2.py` and look for a closed form of the transition map, which would turn the question into a number-theoretic one.
