# 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_1LE---

bbchallenge id 64715. One of the six-state holdouts whose halting from blank tape is open (group G11, family *two-word sweeper*). It differs from its siblings in F0 = 1LE, E1 = 0RE.

| state | read 0 | read 1 |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1RB | 0LE |
| B | 1LC | 0RE |
| C | 0RA | 0LD |
| D | 1LA | 1LF |
| E | 0LC | 0RE |
| F | 1LE | --- |

## What it does

Write a = `10` and b = `110`. Apart from a few cells at the ends, the tape is a string of a's and b's split into
sections by `000`. The head crosses runs of words in one state (the sweep rules `a_right_01`, `a_right_011`, `b_right_10`, `b_right_11`, `b_right_110`, `e_right_10`, `e_right_11`, `e_right_110`,
proved in Lean for any number of copies). Its turning points far from the left end follow the ruler pattern of a
binary counter with 4-cell digits, and the ends are visited rarely.

At its visits to the left end (state E) the tape starts `E>0 0 1 0 (1 1 0)^k ...`. Record values of k in a
3e9-step run (`tmrun 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_1LE--- 3000000000 at E L`): k=10 at step 6,741,512, k=11 at step 41,755,007, k=12 at step 523,217,185, k=13 at step 1,019,661,408, k=14 at step 1,421,446,771, k=15 at step 2,626,803,218. Each new b costs about 2 to 3 times the steps of the
previous one, as a counter would; the rest of the tape is rearranged between visits, so the counter's digits are
not plain blocks.

Its left-end configurations coincide with those of `1RB---_1LC0RF_0RA0LD_1LE1LA_1RF0LF_0LC0RA` at 47 of its first 50 visits, in the same order (step ratio about 1.12): the two machines do the same thing.

**Where it can halt.** `python3 -m bbtools.ctl 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_1LE--- 6` finds a closed tape language (windows of 6 cells; not yet in
Lean). It allows a halt only with F reading a 1 and `110001`, `110011` from the head on, that is
F on the first 1 of a word `110` that ends a section.

**Larger windows (worker 2, second round).** `python3 -m bbtools.ctl 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_1LE--- K` for larger K: K = 7: closes, 576 abstract configurations where a halt can fire; K = 8: no closed set within the default 100 rounds. So larger windows do not remove the halting contexts (their number grows with K) and soon stop closing: this route gave no certificate. These machines are counters, whose halting depends on a global count, which a language of K-cell windows cannot see; a certificate will have to use the macro structure.

## The engine (decoded and proved, worker 2, third round)

**Events.** At an *event* the head is in state A on a 0 just right of a base-3 counter:

    0^inf 1 0 [N] A> 0 [M] 1 [R] ...

- `N`: base 3, 3-cell digits `100` = 0, `110` = 1, `010` = 2, most significant first (lowest digit next to the head);
- `M`: binary, 4-cell digits `0010` = 0, `1010` = 1, lowest digit first, any width;
- `R`: binary, 4-cell digits `1000` = 0, `1010` = 1, lowest digit first.

(In the words a = `10`, b = `110` of the first description, M's digits are the sections `a|` and `aa`.)

**Event rule (Lean, every size).** From one event the machine reaches the next with N + 1 (trailing 2s carry; 2...2
wraps to 0...0 with one digit more, the tape growing 3 cells at the left end), M + 1 (trailing 1s carry; 1...1 wraps to
0...0), and, only when M wraps, R + 3. When M does not wrap nothing right of M is read. So N counts events in base 3,
M counts them in binary, and R gains 3 every 2^(width of M) events. `lean/Events.lean`: 54 theorems, one per case (exhaustive by construction of `events.py`; 9 cases of N:
lowest digit 0 or 1 after 0, 1 or k+2 twos, or the wrap at the left end; times 6 of M and R: no wrap, carry over i+1
ones, or a wrap of i+1 ones with the 4 cases of R's two low bits and a carry over j ones), each with its exact step
count (for example 12i + 12j + 10k + 116), generated by
`python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/events.py 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_1LE--- NAMESPACE`.
Example (run of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`, M with 2 digits, R with 8): at steps 12,203, 12,223, 12,243, 12,253, 12,345 (N, M, R) =
(5, 0, 92), (6, 1, 92), (7, 2, 92), (8, 3, 92), (9, 0, 95).

**The run.** In the clean shape R is followed by `1 0 1` and blank tape. This run reaches it at step 132,141

## The right side as a recursive visit law (worker 2, round 2 of G11)

Idea (from the busycoq proof of `1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_0RD1RF_0RC---`): instead of rules for events plus
separately decoded overflows, describe what **one visit** of the head into a stream does, for every stream.
`Vis q x r x'` (`lean/Visit.lean`): the head enters the stream `x` from the left in state `q`, and the first time it
leaves `x` to the left it is in state `r` and the stream is `x'`.

**Token rows (Lean, kernel).** Cut the tape right of the event cell (cell -41) into tokens of two cells,
`z = 00`, `a = 10`, `c = 01`, `d = 11`. A visit of `token x` is a few steps inside the token and some nested visits
of `x`. In 3·10^7 steps of the run exactly 32 rows occur (`py/laws.py` of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`); each is a theorem of
`lean/Visit.lean` (written by `py/gen_visit.py`), conditional on the nested visits and valid for every stream, e.g.
`v_B_10_Bc`: if `B> x` comes out in C leaving `x'`, then `B> a x` comes out in A leaving `a x'`.

**The a/z stream (Lean, kernel, `lean/Stream.lean`).** Everything from R on, blank tape included (blank is
`z z z ...`), is one stream of `a` and `z`, and a visit in state B does this: the first `z` becomes `a`, and every
second `a` before it becomes `z`:

    B> (a a)^k z x    ->  (z a)^k a x      out in C     (vb_even)
    B> a (a a)^k z x  ->  a (z a)^k a x    out in A     (vb_odd; the same for a visit in state E: ve_odd)

With R's digits `a z` = 0, `a a` = 1 the odd case is the binary increment. **So R never overflows into something
else: there is no end of R.** What was called the overflow of R is the even case: when all digits up to the blank
tape are 1 the first `z` stands at an even place, the visit comes out in C instead of A, and leaves `(z a)^k a`:
the digits shifted by one token. (First time in the run of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`: step 20,645, the stream `a^18` then blank, comes
out in C as `(z a)^9 a`.)

**M (Lean, kernel).** `m_inc`: `A> (c c)^k z c y` leaves `(z c)^k c c y`, out in D (M + 1, digits `z c` = 0,
`c c` = 1, for every k and every `y`). `m_wrap`: `A> (c c)^(k+1) c x` leaves `(z c)^(k+1) c x3`, out in D, where
`x3` is `x` after three visits (E, B, B) that each come out in A. With `vb_odd`/`ve_odd` this is the right half of the
event rule (M + 1, R + 3) in visit form, and it shows exactly where the event rule stops: at a visit of the a/z
stream that comes out in C.

**What is still open.** After a visit that comes out in C the token rows with other nested results apply (they are
in `Visit.lean`, among them one row that leaves a token in state F, the state whose 1 halts), and the layer of `c`
tokens is rebuilt with other widths. These rows are proved one by one but not composed into a law for the whole
right side, and the left side (N, tokens of three cells) has its own table (`py/laws.py` on the mirrored machine:
37 rows for `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`), not yet in Lean. There is no token-level simulator with jumps yet, so no run beyond the
3·10^9 steps of `tmrun`. A decision needs: the composed law for the `c` layer after a C exit, the left side's law,
then either an invariant or a far run.

## The overflows of R and the eras after them (November 2026)

## One law for every era: a chain of binary counters (November 2026)

The layered shapes of this sibling's later run are all one layout, the one decoded for
`1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---` (see its README). At a full event the cells right of the head read
`A> 0 [Z] 11 [W1] 01 [U1] 1011 [W2] 01 [U2] ... 1011 [Wm] 01 [Um] 0^inf` (Z: `0010`/`1010`, W: `0001`/`0101`, U:
`1000`/`1010`, lowest digit first, any widths, any W or U possibly empty). The **chain law** holds: each event adds 1
to Z, and each word that wraps sends 3 to the next word, so Z + 2^|Z|/3 · (W1 + 2^|W1|/3 · (U1 + ...)) grows by
exactly 1 per event, as long as no carry leaves the last word.

**The law up to a defect (Lean, every layout).** `lean/Layers.lean` now proves the chain law with any tape after the last word (`encT B`): no visit reads past the last word unless a carry leaves it. So the chain law holds on the longest prefix of the chain that is in the grammar, whatever defect comes after it, until a carry leaves that prefix. `py/prefixjump.py` jumps with this and runs only the crossings of a defect cell by cell:

- `python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/prefixjump.py 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_1LE--- 600 2`: 270 jumps, about 10^128 events from blank tape (so at least that many steps), no halt; jumps from a prefix with a defect after it: 42, from a chain wholly in the grammar: 230; 4,524 full events had no prefix that could jump and were run cell by cell; spot checks: 2,625 events, 0 mismatches. Then the run went 10^8 steps without a full event, and this simulator stops there.

**What a carry does at a defect (trusted, `py/defects.py`).** Every defect met is one of two kinds. (1) One erased cell in a separator: after Z `d` = 11 read as `c` = 01, after a W `c` = 01 read as `z` = 00, after a U `ad` = 1011 read as `ac` = 1001. The law for these: carries follow the chain law up to the defect, and when a carry of c > 0 reaches it the separator is restored and the carry goes on as c − 3. So the defect takes exactly one unit, once, and then the chain is the plain chain again. (2) A half-digit shift: a separator missing altogether (a W running straight into its U), or a separator after Z read as `a`, `ad`, `zd` or `ac`. When a carry meets one, the words after it are read again with W and U swapped and the widths move by a digit, as at a change of era. This kind is not decoded.

**The stage after 10^128 events (trusted, `py/latestage.py`).** The head now turns back at a cell 17 cells right of the last event cell. Call a *visit* the head at that cell in state C on a 0. At a visit the tape reads

    left:   0^inf 1 0 [L2] 1010 [L1] 1010 [L0] 101010101010101 | head
    right:  head | 0 b 1  W1 c U1 a d W2 c U2 a d ...       (W1 empty)

On the left, L0, L1 and L2 are base-3 words in the digits of N (100 = 0, 110 = 1, 010 = 2, lowest digit next to the head), parted by the four cells `1010`, so each word sits one cell off from the next. L2 is what is left of the old N. At the start of the stage the carries of N rebuild its lowest digits into L0 (10 digits) and L1 (4 digits), and after that the widths stay fixed. On the right, b is one cell and the words are those of the chain, from W1 on. The law is two chain laws run at once, over each pair of visits (from b = 0):

- left: L0 gets 16, and a word that wraps sends 2 to the next (base-3 words, so the left side is one counter);
- right: the word (b, U1) gets 2 (b is its lowest bit), W2 gets 6, and a word that wraps sends 3 to the next, as in the chain law.

**The stages after it (trusted, `py/stages.py`).** The stage ends the way the first one did: a carry runs off the right end of the chain, and the turning cell moves. The tape around the new cell has the same two chains, with other word widths and other gifts. On the left, each word that wraps sends 2 and an empty word passes twice what it got. On the right, each wrap sends 3. Over the stages the cell moves both ways, mostly by 2 cells but sometimes by tens of cells. Next to it lies a block `1010…1`, which can be read two ways:

- As fixed cells: in the run from the 10^128 tape the block shrinks from 15 cells to 11, 7, 3 and then none, and the left gift per visit halves with each step (16, 8, 4, 2, 1).
- As left words: the same tape reads with the block as left words, with the lowest digit ending at the turning cell itself. Then the left gift is 1 at every stage.

So every 4 cells of the block act as one more empty left word, which passes on twice what it got. In the run from blank tape the right gifts take three forms: (b, W1) + 5 when b = 1 in 396 of the 400 stages, the first word + 17 in 3, and the first word + 1 and the next + 3 in 1. `py/stages.py` follows the stages with this form of law and fits its numbers per stage:

- At each move it takes the cell with the most visit-shaped passes where the tape reads as the two chains, and fits the smallest gifts that match 40 consecutive visits run cell by cell.
- It checks every later visit run cell by cell against those gifts, and fits again after a mismatch.
- It jumps by pairs of visits as far as no carry leaves either chain, and checks itself: random points of each jump are rebuilt and run cell by cell for 3 pairs.

- `python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/stages.py run 400 3`: from blank tape to about 10^128 events with `defects.py`, then 400 stages of this kind, about 10^409 visits (so at least that many more steps), no halt. 494 fits of the gifts (94 visits run cell by cell left a fitted law and started a new fit), and 18,882 self-check pairs with 0 mismatches.

A fitted law is a description, not a proof: each stage's gifts are checked on its own cell-by-cell visits and spot checks, not derived. The next section derives them.

**Why the gifts take these forms (Lean, `lean/Stage.lean`).** The stages are not a new mechanism: they are events of the chain law with the word Z **empty**, and with a chain of base-3 words on the left in place of the single counter N. Call e the cell right of the turning cell. At an event the head is in state A on a 0 at e, and

    left of e:   0, then base-3 words (digits 100/110/010, lowest first) parted by 1010, ending at the top 0^inf 1 0 or at any tape;
    right of e:  [Z] 11 [Q1] 01 [P1] 10 11 [Q2] 01 [P2] ... 11 [Q] 01 [R], then any tape.

With b = 1 the stage reading `0 b 1 W1 c U1 a d W2 ...` is exactly this with Z empty (W = Q, U = P); with b = 0 it is the same tape halfway through an event.

- **Right.** With Z empty, every event wraps Z, so every event sends one unit into the chain: Q1 + 3, a wrap of Q adds 3 to P, a wrap of P sends a unit to the next item (`dU` of `Layers.lean`). The head passes the turning cell in state C on a 0 twice per event, once in the middle of the unit and once at the end. So the pair of visits that `py/stages.py` jumps by is one event, and its fitted gifts are this unit read with b as the lowest bit: (b, W1) + 5 at a visit with b = 1 is W1 + 3, and + 17 with W1 empty is U1 + 9.
- **Left.** The event's last part (the cell left of e in state D) is an increment of the left words, done by visits leftwards, the mirror image of `Vis` (`LVis`). Cut the left tape into units, each beginning with a 0 cell: a digit, a separator `1010`, or the top `10`. Five rows, proved for any tape further left: a digit 0 or 1 entered in D adds 1 and leaves in E (`lv_d0`, `lv_d1`); a digit 2 becomes 0 and passes one increment on (`lv_d2`); a separator passes **two** increments to the next unit (`lv_sep`); the top makes a new digit 0 below it (`lv_top`). So each event adds 1 to the lowest left word, and a word that wraps sends 2 to the next. The block `1010…1` of `py/stages.py` is a run of separators with empty words between them, and each passes twice what it got, which gives its gifts 16, 8, 4, 2, 1.
- **The law** (`chain_event`, kernel): from one event to the next the left words get one increment (`linc`) and the right side moves by `evNext` (Z + 1, or with Z empty one unit into the chain). This holds for every Z, empty or not, for every list of left words, and for any tape after R or after the last left unit (`rest`): no visit reads that tape unless a carry reaches it. It contains `layer_event` and its cases, and is stated for every layout at once.

**The run against the proved law (trusted, `py/stage_law.py`).** This script does what `py/stages.py` does, but with the ported Lean functions `linc` and `evNext` instead of fitted gifts. At each stage it finds the event cell. It checks every event run cell by cell against the one before it. It jumps by the law iterated (+1 per event on the left with ×2 at each wrap, Z + 1 on the right with ×3 at each wrap), as far as no carry leaves either chain. Each jump is spot-checked: random points are rebuilt and 3 events are run cell by cell.

- `python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/stage_law.py run 500 3` (with the open item below): from blank tape to about 10^128 events with `defects.py`, then 500 stages (500 event cells; 124 times a carry left a chain and the run came back to the same cell) and about 10^480 events (so at least that many more steps), no halt. 4,558 consecutive pairs of events run cell by cell follow the law and none fail it; 34 events at wraps of a half-digit item follow `chain_event_junction` and none fail it; 23,450 spot-check events, 0 mismatches. This goes past the 10^409 of `py/stages.py`, with a proved law in place of fitted gifts. (Without the open item the same script stalls at about 10^137 events, in stage 13.)

So up to that point the run from blank tape follows a proved law. What is trusted is that each tape is in the layout (the tape is read off at every event), the port of the Lean functions, and the jump arithmetic.

**The half-digit item (Lean, `chain_event_open`).** Without it the proved law stalls at about 10^137 events, in stage 13. There the chain read in the layout ends at R, and after R the tape reads `10 11 [Q] 00 11 [Q'] 01 [P] 10 11 …`. It is an item whose Q runs into `0011` where the grammar has `01 [P] 10 11`: half a digit out of phase. Every carry out of R goes into that item, so without a law for it each jump only reaches the next carry out of R.

- What a carry does there: run cell by cell, each carry out of R is one unit into the item, and it only adds 3 to its Q (in that run Q has 31 digits and goes 74, 77, 80, 83, …). Q is followed by any tape at all: no visit reads past Q unless Q wraps.
- The law: `Stage.lean` proves the chain law with such an *open* last item, `11 [Q]` and then any tape (`encO`, `dUo`, `evNextO`, `chain_event_open`), for every layout. It stops (`none`) only where that Q would wrap.
- When Q wraps (Lean, `Junction.lean`, `chain_event_junction`): the four cells after Q, `0011`, act as one more digit of Q with two extra states. The first wrap of Q turns `0011` into `0111` and the carry stops there (`A> 00` → `01`, row `l_c_A0`). The second wrap turns `0111` into the Q digit `0001` and sends **two** increments into Q' (row `j_A1`: `A> 0111 x`, nested visits E→C A→D E→C A→D, out in D leaving `0001 x'`). After it the tape reads in the grammar again, as one item whose Q is Q, the digit 0 and Q' (`encJ_hld`). So the half-digit item heals after two wraps, and in value it was the word Q + 2^|Q|·(2 + b) + 2^(|Q|+1)·Q' all along (b = 0 for `0011`, 1 for `0111`): every event still adds exactly one to the number of `Layers.lean`. `chain_event_junction` proves this for such an item anywhere in the chain, for every width, with items of the grammar before it and the item (Q', P) and the rest of the chain up to R after it. It is `none` only where a carry leaves R, or where the junction would wrap into an empty Q' (not met in the run).
- Found by following both half-digit items of the run (stages 11 and 13) cell by cell, and by 96 set-up tapes (Q near its wrap, both junctions, every Q' of 3 digits), all as above.

**The change of stage (trusted, `py/stage_change.py`).** A stage ends at the event where a carry leaves R. That change of stage is a single event (about 8,000 to 12,000 steps in the stages checked). The carry reaches the blank tape after R in state B or E and comes back in state C: R is all ones there, so the a/z stream of R and the blank is in the even case (`vb_even`). This return in C runs back left through every item to the event cell, and it rewrites every word on the way.

- The phases: when the carry leaves R, every item k is in the middle of one unit. The a-th increment of Q (a = 1, 2 or 3) wrapped Q and sent three increments into P, and the b-th of those (b = 1, 2 or 3) wrapped P and sent the unit on. (a, b) for every item follows from the chain law alone.
- The rule: after the event, the cells `11` that open item k have moved 2 cells right if a = 1 and 2 cells left if a ≥ 2. The cells `01` between Q and P have moved 2 cells right if b = 1 and 2 cells left if b ≥ 2. So every width changes by at most one digit. Q is rewritten as 2 if a ≥ 2 and 0 if a = 1, P as 2 if b ≥ 2 and 0 if b = 1, with the carries of the chain law (a word that wraps at its new width sends 3 to the next word). R keeps its width and only its top digit is 1. This holds for item 0, the first item after the event cell, too (its carry goes into item 1), and for a word that becomes empty.
- The tape is read the same at a shift of one cell (a Q word is a P word one cell to the left), so when the event cell moves by an odd number of cells the new tape is read with Q and P swapped. The rule is therefore checked on the cells, not on a reading.
- `python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/stage_change.py check 150`: from about 10^128 events, 150 stages. Every change of stage with Z empty is run cell by cell on a copy up to the first event at the new event cell, and the cells of items 1 to R are compared with the rule. Of the 186 changes of stage with Z empty, 116 are covered by the rule and all 116 match it, cell by cell; 0 differ. Set apart: 65 with a word that is empty before the change or an item 0 whose word becomes empty, 4 where the head goes past the end of R before it comes back, 1 where the new event cell lies in item 1.

Open: the changes of stage at a word that is empty before (there widths can go negative and two items merge, much as the half-digit item does), at an item 0 whose word becomes empty, where the head goes past the end of R, and where the new event cell lies in item 1; what is left of item 0 and where the new event cell lies, the changes of stage with Z not empty, a Lean proof of the return in C, the other half-digit shifts (a separator missing altogether, or a separator after Z read wrongly), and whether the stages go on forever.

A halt can only come when a carry leaves the last word of the grammar prefix: at a change of era or when it crosses a defect. What a change of era hands on is open here; for the E1 = 0RA machines see `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`.
