# 1RB0RE_1LC1LF_1RA1LD_0LB---_1RF1LE_0LB0RA

bbchallenge id 129533. A six-state holdout of BB(6). **Open.** It is a list of blocks that behaves like a base-4 counter: between two visits of the head to the left end, block i loses about 4^i words.

| state | read 0 | read 1 |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1RB | 0RE |
| B | 1LC | 1LF |
| C | 1RA | 1LD |
| D | 0LB | --- |
| E | 1RF | 1LE |
| F | 0LB | 0RA |

## What it does

At the left end the tape reads `10 (110)^x0 1010 (110)^x1 1010 ... (110)^xr 10 11`. Write it as [x0, ..., xr].
On the way right, the blocks are rewritten in the word `100` (`sweep_right`). On the way left, they are turned
back into `110` (`sweep_left`). At each block boundary the head zigzags: every zigzag takes a bit of the block on
the left, through a small "digit" gadget, and adds one word to the block on the right. This is why most sweeps do
not reach the left end.

The late rows differ by -1, -4, -16, -64, -256 and about -1024: block i loses about 4^i words per visit. New
blocks have lengths like 1023 = 4^5 - 1, 4095 = 4^6 - 1 and 16387 = 4^7 + 3. In the early rows the differences
are close to these but not equal. A block that runs low is refilled, and the refill depends on the rest of the
list.

## Rules proved in Lean

`lean/Rules.lean` is generated by `lean/rules.py` (symbolic runs, `tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py`). Every rule holds for all values of its variables and all tapes L, R:

| theorem | from | to | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sweep_left` | `L 1 (001)^n B> 0 R` | `L 1 B> 0 (011)^n R` | Leftward, B turns a block of `100` words into `110` words, three steps per word. |
| `sweep_right` | `L 0 0 A> (110)^n+1 1 0 R` | `L 0 0 (100)^n 1 0 0 0 1 F> R` | Rightward, A and E turn a block of n+1 words `110` back into `100` words, seven steps per word. |

## Second round: exact pieces of the list map

Lists at visits to the left end, to step 1.86*10^10 (`edges`, then `tok.py a=110,b=10`, then a checker in scratch):

    1,657,141,917   [115, 328, 18, 587, 5070, 17411, 4095]
    2,625,151,334   [114, 324, 2, 523, 4814, 16386, 16387]
    3,400,038,339   [113, 319, 496, 4706, 15954, 14658, 13059]
    4,589,471,184   [112, 315, 480, 4642, 15698, 13634, 8963, 16383]
    18,558,703,739  [111, 311, 464, 4578, 15442, 12610, 4866, 65539]

- **Interior law.** Of 13 pairs of consecutive visits whose lists parse as plain block lists, 10 follow these two rules exactly: block i loses 4^i words. A new last block
  4^r - 1 appears. At the next visit it is 4^(r+1) + 3, and its left neighbour loses 4^(r-1) + 1.
- **A block runs out** (2 at step 2,625,151,334): it disappears and the blocks to its right move one place left,
  keeping their weights. The deficit shows as 27, 108, 432, 1729 = 27*4^j (+1 at the end). For example
  523 -> 496, 4814 -> 4706, 16386 -> 15954, 16387 -> 14658.
- **The first block runs out** (rows starting `b^3`, `a^4`, `0^2 a^2`, steps 4.7*10^7-2.6*10^8 and
  3.6*10^8-1.4*10^9): the next block is taken over as the new first block, through intermediate shapes that are
  not yet decoded.

Taken together, the tape is a base-4 counter that counts down. Position i has weight 4^i: one visit to the left
end removes one unit from every digit. Halting would need one of the borrow events to produce D reading a 1. An
exact borrow rule, and its Lean proof (as for `halts_iff` in `1RB0RE_1LC1LD_0RA0LD_1LB0LA_1RF1RA_---1LB`), is the
remaining work.

## Status

Open. Lean: the halting configurations and no halt in 10^7 steps (`lean/Blank.lean`), and the two shift rules of a
sweep (`lean/Rules.lean`). C: no halt in 10^9 steps. The base-4 picture is a heuristic read off the run.

## For later

- Find the exact zigzag rule at a block boundary, i.e. how many words the left block loses per word added to the
  right block, and prove it with symtm. This needs the digit gadget of about four shapes (`c^3`, `c^2 b 1`,
  `a c^2`, `c^2 0`, ...), seen between steps 944 and 2,569.
- From that rule, derive the map on lists, including refills, and check it against the rows above.
- Then ask whether a refill can ever produce D reading a 1 (the halting transition).

## Deep exact run

No halt in the first 1.68x10^19 steps: the macro simulator `holdouts/1RB0LB_1RC0LA_1LB0RD_1RE1RD_1RB1RF_1LA---/c/msim.c` with blocks of 6 cells ran
from blank tape to step 16,848,971,644,337,937,408. It is exact, and at this block size it agrees with the
direct simulator after 2,000,004,826 steps (`python3 holdouts/1RB0LB_1RC0LA_1LB0RD_1RE1RD_1RB1RF_1LA---/py/deep.py run 240`, then `verify`).
