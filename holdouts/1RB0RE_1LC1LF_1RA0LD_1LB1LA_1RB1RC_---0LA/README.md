# 1RB0RE_1LC1LF_1RA0LD_1LB1LA_1RB1RC_---0LA

bbchallenge id 80070. A six-state holdout of BB(6). **Open.** It is a list of blocks of the word `110` with one-cell separators that flip between 0 and 1. Its main move is an exact transfer cycle: (x, y, z) -> (x-2, y+3, z-1) for three neighbouring blocks, in 28y+111 steps. What happens when a cycle ends has not been worked out.

| state | read 0 | read 1 |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1RB | 0RE |
| B | 1LC | 1LF |
| C | 1RA | 0LD |
| D | 1LB | 1LA |
| E | 1RB | 1RC |
| F | --- | 0LA |

## What it does

At the right end the tape is `10 (110)^x0 0 (110)^x1 0 ... 0 (110)^3 1`, with every separator a 0. At the left end
the same kind of list has separators that are a mix of 0 and 1:

    right 97,433,463    555 401 749 316 218 99 221 70 160 20 30 12 6 5 3
    left 112,083,053    831 324 517 370 167 178 41 21 168 102 80 8 10 5 ...
    right 468,333,147   4587 369 679 1056 9 126 178 65 46 46 19 22 2 3 13 8 3
    left 746,418,191    1226 2184 120 2572 19 200 243 62 271 60 189 91 5 13 13 ...
    right 1,539,966,585 1633 4080 1838 305 1874 709 298 311 75 214 79 206 7 3 10 1 3

Crossing the inside of a block is a plain shift by one cell (`a_right`, `d_left`, `c_left`). Everything else
happens at the separators, where the head turns, a separator is rewritten, and neighbouring blocks trade length.
Most sweeps turn before they reach an end. The tape grows like the square root of the step count.

## Transfer cycles

Most of the work happens in one kind of local cycle between three neighbouring blocks. When the tape around the
head reads

    (110)^x 0 (110)^y 1 A> 1 0 1 0 1 1 0 0 (110)^z 0

the head zigzags out and back. The next time it turns in state A at the same place, the tape is the same with
**(x, y, z) -> (x-2, y+3, z-1)**, and exactly **28y + 111** steps have passed. In the first 10^8 steps there are
8,397 pairs of such consecutive turns, and every one follows this law, step count included
(`c/hturn.c`, then `hturn M 100000000 6 | python3 py/transfer.py`). So the block left of the head pays two words and the block
right of it pays one, and the middle block gains three. This is a 3-for-2 exchange, as in Collatz-like machines.
The cycle stops when x < 2 or z = 0, and what happens then depends on the blocks further out.
`py/septurns.py` logs every turn with the separator and the two block lengths next to it. That is the raw material
for the remaining boundary rules.

## Rules proved in Lean

`lean/Rules.lean` is generated by `lean/rules.py` (symbolic runs, `tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py`). Every rule holds for all values of its variables and all tapes L, R:

| theorem | from | to | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `a_right` | `L 0 A> (110)^n 0 R` | `L 0 (011)^n 1 B> R` | Going right, A (with E) shifts a block of `110` words by one cell, three steps per word. |
| `d_left` | `L 1 (110)^n D> 1 R` | `L 1 D> 1 (011)^n R` | Going left, D (with B, F) crosses a block of `110` words, shifting it by one cell. |
| `c_left` | `L 1 (101)^n C> 1 R` | `L 1 C> 1 (110)^n R` | Going left, C (with A, D) crosses a block written as `101` words. |

## Status

Open. Lean: the halting configurations and no halt in 10^7 steps (`lean/Blank.lean`), and three shift rules
(`lean/Rules.lean`). C: no halt in 3*10^9 steps. One exact local rule is known, the transfer cycle (checked on every instance up to 10^8
steps). The rules for the ends of a cycle, and so a map on whole lists, are not known yet.

## For later

- Prove the transfer cycle in Lean. `symtm` does not fold the `1 1 0` words that pile up between x and y back into
  `(110)^y`, so the proof needs a hand-written fold lemma or an intermediate rule for one zig.
- Classify what happens when a transfer cycle ends (x < 2 or z = 0), using the `py/septurns.py` log. That gives
  the next boundary rules. The aim is a map on block lists with the transfer cycle as its main move.
- Find out whether the separators act as the bits of a counter (which ones flip on the way left, and why).
