## Status

**Open; no halt in 3.16·10^15 steps.** The machine runs a base-4 counter whose digits are defects in a periodic background and whose blocks
run out at a critical rate, so digits collide. Lean proves (kernel) the counter's rounds for all block lengths, and
(native) that a tape of the same shape halts, so a proof must track the block lengths exactly.

## What it does

Read the tape as the list of gaps between consecutive 1s. Almost every gap is 2 (the background `001001...`), and
every time the head, in state D, is at the left end of the tape the gap list is

    2^n0  d1  2^n1  d2  2^n2 ...  dk  2^nk  END

where each defect `d_i` is one of four kinds, `q = (1,0)`, `r = (4)`, `s = (1,1)`, `p = (3)`, and `END = (1)` is
the last 1. From one such moment to the next (a *round*):

- the defects are the digits of a **base-4 counter**, least significant first, and the round adds 1:
  `q → r → s → p → q`, with a carry to the next digit on `s → p`;
- the first block `n0` grows by one period (the tape grows by 3 cells at the left);
- whenever a digit goes `q → r`, the block after it loses one period.

When the carry runs through every digit and reaches END, it comes back and a new least-significant digit `q`
appears at the left end (all the old digits are then `p`, and the old first block becomes the second): the counter
gains a digit at the bottom. With K digits, an overflow comes about every 4^K rounds, while block `n_i` loses one
period every 4^i rounds. Right after an overflow the new second block is about 4^K long and is spent at about
4^K per cycle: **the blocks run out at a critical rate**, so some block reaches 0 and two digits touch. A compound
defect then cycles on its own (seen: `rr → sr → ps → qs → pt → qt → p`, two digits merging into one, and
`rq → sq → pr → qr → (6) → (1,3)`, which reflects the carry like END does) and the counter goes on with fewer
digits. Block lengths seen at overflows: 64, 260, 1028, 4100, 4612 (near powers of 4).

`py/counter.py` checks this against the C simulator: of the 94,852 rounds in the first 10^10 steps, the counter rule
predicts 94,716 exactly and makes no wrong prediction; the other 136 are 31 overflows and 100 collisions (and 5
rounds of the first 120 steps). At most 8 digits so far.

**Where it can halt.** The halting transition is A1, reached only through `B> 0 1 1`. Random tapes of the
counter's shape halt in about 5% of trials (`lean/Example.lean`: `2^4 r 2^2 END` halts after 234,048 steps, when
the digits drift into END and wreck it). The run from blank tape keeps its blocks just long enough; whether it
always will is a question about the exact arithmetic of the block lengths.

## Results

- `lean/Rules.lean` (generated by `py/rules.py`): the rounds `round_q`, `round_r`, `round_p` (first digit, no
  carry), `carry_q`, `carry_r`, `carry_p` (a carry into the second digit) and `overflow` (one digit and END), for
  all block lengths (kernel).
- `lean/Example.lean`: `ex_halts`, the tape `2^4 r 2^2 END` (the counter's shape) halts (native_decide).
- `py/counter.py 1e10`: the counter rule against the C simulator, as above (trusted).
- `lean/Rules.lean` also has the four sweeps through a block (kernel): the pass to the right `B> (010)^n -> (111)^n B>`
  (`sweep_right`) and the pass back in state D, E or F, which writes the background in phase 010, 001 or 100
  (`sweep_left_D/E/F`). The state of the pass back carries the result of the round to the left.
- `py/far.py 3.2e15` (the macro simulator `tools/py/bbtools/macro.py`, exact; 8 minutes): **no halt in the first
  3,162,277,665,785,965 steps**. The block before END then holds 9 periods.
- What an overflow does (seen in `py/counter.py`'s rounds up to 10^10 steps, not proved): between two overflows with
  the same digits and no collision the block before END loses 1 period and the j-th block from the right 4^j
  (1, 4, 16, 64, 256 at the overflows of steps 1.6·10^7 to 4.4·10^7). The block
  before END went 8, 7, ..., 1 (overflows at steps 22,585 to 6,922,775), then END absorbed the last digit: its
  cluster went `1 1 1`, `3 1`, `1 0 1`, `3`, `1 0`, `|2`, `1` over the next overflows (steps 9.8·10^6 to
  7.3·10^7), during which an overflow makes no new digit and the first block keeps growing. The next block before
  END went 42, 41, ..., 29 by 10^10 steps and is at 9 after 3.16·10^15 steps.
- Missing for a decision: (1) every collision and the absorption by END as local rules of the two passes (what the
  pass to the right does at each boundary between two phases of the background, and which state comes back), for
  any number of digits; (2) an invariant on the block lengths closed under them. The blocks are spent at a
  critical rate (roughly: a block born at an overflow with K digits holds of the order of 4^K periods and loses 4^(K-1) per overflow),
  so collisions never stop and the invariant has to be exact; `lean/Example.lean` shows the shape alone is not
  enough. (3) Cheaper first: run `py/far.py` to about 10^17 steps (hours) to see the next absorption by END.
