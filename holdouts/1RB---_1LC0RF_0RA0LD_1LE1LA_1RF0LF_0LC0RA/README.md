bbchallenge id 22247. One of the six-state holdouts whose halting from blank tape is open (group G11, family *two-word sweeper*). It differs from its siblings in a relabelled relative (its F plays E's part).

## What it does

Write a = `10` and b = `110`. Apart from a few cells at the ends, the tape is a string of a's and b's split into
sections by `000`. The head crosses runs of words in one state (the sweep rules `a_right_01`, `a_right_011`, `b_right_10`, `b_right_110`, `f_right_10`,
proved in Lean for any number of copies). Its turning points far from the left end follow the ruler pattern of a
binary counter with 4-cell digits, and the ends are visited rarely.

At its visits to the left end (state F) the tape starts `F>0 0 1 0 (1 1 0)^k ...`. Record values of k in a
3e9-step run (`tmrun 1RB---_1LC0RF_0RA0LD_1LE1LA_1RF0LF_0LC0RA 3000000000 at F L`): k=10 at step 6,019,226, k=11 at step 37,281,281, k=12 at step 467,158,233, k=13 at step 910,412,010, k=14 at step 1,269,148,937, k=15 at step 2,345,360,050. Each new b costs about 2 to 3 times the steps of the
previous one, as a counter would; the rest of the tape is rearranged between visits, so the counter's digits are
not plain blocks.

Its left-end configurations coincide with those of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RE_1LE---` at 47 of its first 50 visits, in the same order (step ratio about 1.12): the two machines do the same thing.

**Where it can halt.** `python3 -m bbtools.ctl 1RB---_1LC0RF_0RA0LD_1LE1LA_1RF0LF_0LC0RA 6` finds a closed tape language (windows of 6 cells; not yet in
Lean). It allows a halt only with A reading a 1 and `110001` from the head on, that is
A on the first 1 of a word `110` that ends a section.

**Larger windows (worker 2, second round).** `python3 -m bbtools.ctl 1RB---_1LC0RF_0RA0LD_1LE1LA_1RF0LF_0LC0RA K` for larger K: K = 7: closes, 384 abstract configurations where a halt can fire; K = 8: no closed set within the default 100 rounds. These machines are counters, whose halting depends on a global count, which a language of K-cell windows cannot see; a certificate will have to use the macro structure.

## The engine (decoded and proved, worker 2, third round)

**Events.** At an *event* the head is in state A on a 0 just right of a base-3 counter:

    0^inf 1 0 [N] A> 0 [M] 1 [R] ...

- `N`: base 3, 3-cell digits `100` = 0, `110` = 1, `010` = 2, most significant first (lowest digit next to the head);
- `M`: binary, 4-cell digits `0010` = 0, `1010` = 1, lowest digit first, any width;
- `R`: binary, 4-cell digits `1000` = 0, `1010` = 1, lowest digit first.

(In the words a = `10`, b = `110` of the first description, M's digits are the sections `a|` and `aa`.)

**Event rule (Lean, every size).** From one event the machine reaches the next with N + 1 (trailing 2s carry; 2...2
wraps to 0...0 with one digit more, the tape growing 3 cells at the left end), M + 1 (trailing 1s carry; 1...1 wraps to
0...0), and, only when M wraps, R + 3. When M does not wrap nothing right of M is read. So N counts events in base 3,
M counts them in binary, and R gains 3 every 2^(width of M) events. `lean/Events.lean`: 54 theorems, one per case (exhaustive by construction of `events.py`; 9 cases of N:
lowest digit 0 or 1 after 0, 1 or k+2 twos, or the wrap at the left end; times 6 of M and R: no wrap, carry over i+1
ones, or a wrap of i+1 ones with the 4 cases of R's two low bits and a carry over j ones), each with its exact step
count (for example 12i + 12j + 10k + 116), generated by
`python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/events.py 1RB---_1LC0RF_0RA0LD_1LE1LA_1RF0LF_0LC0RA NAMESPACE`.
Example (run of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`, M with 2 digits, R with 8): at steps 12,203, 12,223, 12,243, 12,253, 12,345 (N, M, R) =
(5, 0, 92), (6, 1, 92), (7, 2, 92), (8, 3, 92), (9, 0, 95).

**The run.** In the clean shape R is followed by `1 0 1` and blank tape. This run reaches it at step 118,003
(N = 35 (5 digits), M = 2 (2 digits), R = 0 (12 digits)); then 5,556 (to step 3·10^6) consecutive events follow the rule, until R = 4,095 = 2^12 − 1 at step 3·10^6, about to overflow

goes past R and rebuilds the right part, and later the cells after R hold a further structure starting `1 0 1 1 0`, with
M and R of other widths. Counting every event whose right part starts `0 [M] 1`: in the first 3·10^6 steps

## The right side as a recursive visit law (worker 2, round 2 of G11)

Idea (from the busycoq proof of `1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_0RD1RF_0RC---`): instead of rules for events plus
separately decoded overflows, describe what **one visit** of the head into a stream does, for every stream.
`Vis q x r x'` (`lean/Visit.lean`): the head enters the stream `x` from the left in state `q`, and the first time it
leaves `x` to the left it is in state `r` and the stream is `x'`.

**Token rows (Lean, kernel).** Cut the tape right of the event cell (cell -41) into tokens of two cells,
`z = 00`, `a = 10`, `c = 01`, `d = 11`. A visit of `token x` is a few steps inside the token and some nested visits
of `x`. In 3·10^7 steps of the run exactly 30 rows occur (`py/laws.py` of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`); each is a theorem of
`lean/Visit.lean` (written by `py/gen_visit.py`), conditional on the nested visits and valid for every stream, e.g.
`v_B_10_Bc`: if `B> x` comes out in C leaving `x'`, then `B> a x` comes out in E leaving `a x'`.

**The a/z stream (Lean, kernel, `lean/Stream.lean`).** Everything from R on, blank tape included (blank is
`z z z ...`), is one stream of `a` and `z`, and a visit in state B does this: the first `z` becomes `a`, and every
second `a` before it becomes `z`:

    B> (a a)^k z x    ->  (z a)^k a x      out in C     (vb_even)
    B> a (a a)^k z x  ->  a (z a)^k a x    out in E     (vb_odd; the same for a visit in state F: ve_odd)

With R's digits `a z` = 0, `a a` = 1 the odd case is the binary increment. **So R never overflows into something
else: there is no end of R.** What was called the overflow of R is the even case: when all digits up to the blank
tape are 1 the first `z` stands at an even place, the visit comes out in C instead of E, and leaves `(z a)^k a`:
the digits shifted by one token. (First time in the run of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`: step 20,645, the stream `a^18` then blank, comes
out in C as `(z a)^9 a`.)

**M (Lean, kernel).** `m_inc`: `A> (c c)^k z c y` leaves `(z c)^k c c y`, out in D (M + 1, digits `z c` = 0,
`c c` = 1, for every k and every `y`). `m_wrap`: `A> (c c)^(k+1) c x` leaves `(z c)^(k+1) c x3`, out in D, where
`x3` is `x` after three visits (F, F, B) that each come out in E. With `vb_odd`/`ve_odd` this is the right half of the
event rule (M + 1, R + 3) in visit form, and it shows exactly where the event rule stops: at a visit of the a/z
stream that comes out in C.

**What is still open.** After a visit that comes out in C the token rows with other nested results apply (they are
in `Visit.lean`, among them one row that leaves a token in state A, the state whose 1 halts), and the layer of `c`
tokens is rebuilt with other widths. These rows are proved one by one but not composed into a law for the whole
right side, and the left side (N, tokens of three cells) has its own table (`py/laws.py` on the mirrored machine:
37 rows for `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`), not yet in Lean. There is no token-level simulator with jumps yet, so no run beyond the
3·10^9 steps of `tmrun`. A decision needs: the composed law for the `c` layer after a C exit, the left side's law,
then either an invariant or a far run.

## The overflows of R and the eras after them (November 2026)

The later layout of the family is

    0^inf 1 0 [N] A> 0 [Z] 1 1 [C] 0 1 [R] ...

with N in base 3 as before, Z the counter called M above (digits `0010` = 0, `1010` = 1), a new binary counter C
(digits `0001` = 0, `0101` = 1, closed by `0 1`) and R as before, all lowest digit first. **Cascade rule (Lean,
every size):** from one event to the next N + 1 and Z + 1; when Z wraps, C + 3; when C wraps, R + 3.

`python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/events2.py MACHINE NAMESPACE` (the full description, the checker

[its README](../1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/README.md)).

## The layer law: one rule for every layout (November 2026)

**The layout.** Every full event of the later run reads

    0^inf 1 0 [N] A> 0 [Z] (1 1 [Q] 0 1 [P] 1 0)* 1 1 [Q] 0 1 [R] 0^inf

Z (digits `0010` = 0, `1010` = 1), then a *chain of items*, each a pair of binary words Q (digits `0001` = 0,
`0101` = 1) and P (digits `1000` = 0, `1010` = 1), all lowest digit first, of any width; Q and P may be empty; the
last item's P is R, followed by blank tape. The plain cascade layout is one item; the tokens of the table above
are this layout written in two-cell tokens (`d c a` is `1 1 [Q empty] 0 1 [P empty] 1 0`), so for example
`Z2 d c a d c a X14 d C2 R13` is Z of 2 digits and three items (Q, P) = (empty, empty), (empty, 14 digits),
(2 digits, R of 13).

**The law (Lean, every layout).** From one event to the next N + 1 and Z + 1. When Z wraps it sends one *unit* into
the first item. A unit adds 3 to the item's Q; every wrap of Q adds 3 to its P; every wrap of P sends one unit into
the next item. In one line: the separators `1 1`, `0 1` multiply a carry by 3, and

    Z + 2^|Z|/3 · (Q1 + 2^|Q1|/3 · (P1 + 2^|P1|/3 · (Q2 + ...)))

grows by exactly 1 per event, as long as no carry leaves R. (In the late layout a wrap of Z adds 3·3·3·3 = 81 to the
14-digit word: 6,400, 6,481, 6,562, ... in the run, every four events.)

`lean/Layers.lean` (written by `py/layers_gen.py` from the template `py/Layers.lean.tpl`) proves it for every number
of items and every width: 23 visit rows of blocks of 1, 2 and 4 cells (`py/rowgen.py`, each a run of the machine
between nested visits, by `rfl`), composed by induction over the digits of each word (`p_inc`, `q_inc`, `z_inc`)
and over the list of items (`dU_sound`). The functions `incB`, `pInc`, `pUnit`, `qInc`, `qUnit`, `dU`, `evNext`
compute the next layout on the digit lists in the order the machine does it; `layer_event` is the event, and
`layer_event_n0t0` ... `layer_event_novk` the same for each of the 9 cases of N, with the end of the event
`ln_*` proved by `bbtools.symtm`. A visit of a P word in state F or B is one increment (`PS`), three visits F, F, B
are a unit's 3 (`UP`), a unit into an item is three visits B, A, B of its block `1 1` (`UD`), and Q's increments
are visits in state A. Axioms: `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` only.
