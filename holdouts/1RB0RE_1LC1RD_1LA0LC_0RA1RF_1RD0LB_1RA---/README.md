# 1RB0RE_1LC1RD_1LA0LC_0RA1RF_1RD0LB_1RA---

bbchallenge id 152969. A six-state holdout of BB(6). **Open.** The tape is made of the words `100` and `101`, grouped into regions that count each other down, like nested counters. Full sweeps of the tape are rare, and the macro structure is not yet decoded.

| state | read 0 | read 1 |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1RB | 0RE |
| B | 1LC | 1RD |
| C | 1LA | 0LC |
| D | 0RA | 1RF |
| E | 1RD | 0LB |
| F | 1RA | --- |

## What it does

Read the tape as words `10` followed by a 0 or a 1, that is `100` and `101`. Going right, B turns `100` into
`101` (`b_right`); going left, it turns them back (`b_left`); A shifts blocks of `100` (`a_right`). The head works
for long stretches at the boundary between two regions, and the boundary moves by one word at a time.

The head reaches the left end, after having been at the right end, at steps 38, 110, 416, 660, 1,070, 1,904,
3,712, 8,212, 25,912, 827,042 and 1,021,956,316. The tape width at those times is 12, 19, 37, 44, 55, 71, 102,
149, 238, 1,499 and 37,210. The gaps grow faster than geometrically, which is how nested counters behave.

## Rules proved in Lean

`lean/Rules.lean` is generated by `lean/rules.py` (symbolic runs, `tools/py/bbtools/symtm.py`). Every rule holds for all values of its variables and all tapes L, R:

| theorem | from | to | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `b_right` | `L B> (100)^n R` | `L (101)^n B> R` | Going right, B turns every `100` into `101`, three steps per word. |
| `b_left` | `L 0 (101)^n B> 0 R` | `L 0 B> 0 (001)^n R` | Going left, B turns a block of `101` words into `100` words, read one cell further on. |
| `a_right` | `L A> (100)^n R` | `L (010)^n A> R` | Going right, A shifts a block of `100` words by one cell. |

## Status

Open. Lean: the halting configurations and no halt in 10^7 steps (`lean/Blank.lean`), and three shift rules
(`lean/Rules.lean`). C: no halt in 3*10^9 steps.
