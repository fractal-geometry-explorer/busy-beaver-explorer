# 1RB0RE_1LC---_0RA0LD_1LC1LD_1RF0LA_1RC1RE

bbchallenge id 135173. One of the six-state holdouts whose halting from blank tape is open (group G09, visited tape growing like the square root of the step count on both sides).

**In one sentence.** A particle system on the background `(1110)^n`: walls drift left and turn into runners at the left end, the head shuttles between the left end and the first runner, runners carry walls past them and merge in pairs into new walls; a halt needs three 0s in a row inside the tape, which never forms in 1.2·10^11 steps, but no proof covers every collision yet.

| state | read 0 | read 1 |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1RB | 0RE |
| B | 1LC | --- |
| C | 0RA | 0LD |
| D | 1LC | 1LD |
| E | 1RF | 0LA |
| F | 1RC | 1RE |

## What it does

### Blocks

Cut the tape into blocks `1^k 0` and write each block as its k. Almost every block is a 3 (the background word `1110`); write a run of n of them as `(n)` (`.` for one). What is left are a few kinds of *particles*, each a block or two adjacent blocks:

| block | particle | phase jump (k − 3 mod 4) |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 5 | a **wall**, in its two forms | 2 |
| 2 | a **runner** | 3 |
| 0 | a runner fused with a 1-wall (2 + 1) | 1 |
| 4 | a runner fused with a 5-wall (2 + 5) | 1 |
| 7, 9 | two or three walls in contact (rare) | 0, 2 |

The leftmost block is the *left gadget* (4 when idle). The far right holds a periodic *crystal* of walls (`1551.` repeated; earlier in the run `1.1.55.55.1.1(2)` repeated), which grows as the tape grows.

### One cycle

At the start of a sweep the head is in F on the second cell of the gadget. Rightward, F reads the tape two cells at a time (E, A and C only make one-cell back-steps): every `1110` becomes `0111`, every 1-wall `10` becomes `11` and every 5-wall `111110` becomes `010111`, so F crosses walls but turns at the first block with an odd phase jump, which is the first runner (2, 0 or 4). Leftward, D moves every 0 one cell back. The round trip leaves the background where it was and *flips every wall it crossed* (1 ↔ 5), and the gaps change so that each wall drifts one block to the left every two cycles. Everything right of the first runner is never read: it is frozen until a runner gets there.

So the tape is: the gadget, an *active* stretch of walls (flipping every cycle and drifting left), the first runner, and a *frozen* stretch that holds older runners, the walls they left behind, and the crystal.

Read together: **the runner** moves one block right per cycle; it passes through a wall in two cycles (fusing into a 0 or a 4 for one cycle) and leaves the wall behind it, flipped; when it reaches a resting runner the two **merge into a 1-wall**. **Walls** left of the runner flip every cycle and drift one block left every two cycles. When a wall reaches the left end, the gadget **absorbs it in three cycles** (4 1 → 6 → 2 → 3) and **emits a new runner** (3 → 4 2). The new runner is now the first one: the head turns there, and the old runner rests where it is until the new one catches it up (and they merge) — unless yet another wall reaches the left end first and a third runner is born. Phase jumps add up: wall (2) = runner (3) + runner (3) mod 4, and the gadget turns jump 2 into jump 3. (The rules are stated with a single wall in the active stretch; with several, each is crossed and flipped independently, as the sweep rules below show.)

## Halting

The machine halts only when B reads a 1, and Lean proves that the only way there is F reading `0 0 0 1` (`halt_needs_pattern`, `lean/Halt.lean`): in pairs, F reads `0 0`, switches to A, and A reads `0 1`. In block terms that is two 0-blocks in a row (three 0 cells) inside the stretch F crosses. A 0-block is a runner fused with a 1-wall and lasts one cycle (`runner_hits_1`, `runner_leaves_1`), and F turns at the first odd block, so a halt needs two fused runner-walls side by side, or a 0-block that rests in the frozen stretch next to another one when a runner arrives. In the first 1.2·10^11 steps F never reads `0 0 0` strictly inside the tape (`py/inside000.c`; F reads `0 0 0` only on blank cells past the ends, 31,214 times, where the next pair is blank too and the head turns); and at none of the 448,994 sweep starts of the first 10^9 steps are two 0-blocks adjacent anywhere inside the tape.

**What a proof needs.** A list of the forms the frozen stretch can take (resting runners in every stage of passing a wall, the walls between them, the crystal and the right end), closed under one cycle, with no two 0-blocks adjacent. The rules above cover a runner meeting one wall or one resting runner across a gap of at least one background block; what is missing is every contact case with gap 0 (a runner arriving at a resting runner that is itself fused with a wall, `2 0`, `2 4`, `2 52`, …), the right end, and walls in contact (`55`, `7`). In the run the frozen stretch is far from arbitrary: random tapes built from the observed adjacent pairs of particles do reach `0 0 0` and halt, so the invariant has to remember more than neighbouring pairs.

**Attempts at a closed tape language (all failed, recorded here).** (1) Sliding windows of K cells (`tools/py/bbtools/ctl.py`, and a faster rewrite): the abstract closure produces `000` already at K = 6, 8, 10 (at K = 8: 121 left and 384 right windows, 1344 abstract configurations that can halt). Windows lose the difference between a tape and its copy shifted by one cell, which is exactly what a sweep does. (2) Windows over blocks, with runs of background blocks counted up to a cap T (`N` blocks of lookahead, N ≤ 8, T ≤ 4): the closure grows runs of 1s without bound. (3) K cells plus a summary of the far side (the last M particles with their gaps, the set of particle kinds present; `py/ctl_search.py`): fails within a few hundred abstract steps for K ≤ 10, M ≤ 2, gaps ≤ 2, because the summary cannot stop two 0-blocks from being guessed next to each other. (4) The abstract states actually visited in a run of 2·10^5 steps (K = 8, M = 2): 104,329 triples, and 45,278 abstract successors outside them, almost all left/right combinations not yet seen together, so a certificate of this kind would have millions of triples (too large for `TM.CTL.check` by `decide`, whose membership test is a list search).

## Status

Open. Proved in Lean, kernel only: the halting configurations (`step_none_iff`, `lean/Blank.lean`), no halt in 10,000 steps, the 7 sweep rules (`lean/Rules.lean`), the 11 one-cycle particle rules (`lean/Cycles.lean`), and that every halt from blank tape passes through F reading `0 0 0 1` (`halt_needs_pattern`, `lean/Halt.lean`). With `native_decide`: no halt in 10,000,000 steps. C: no halt in 1,000,000,000 steps (`tools/c/triage.c`), and F never reads `0 0 0` inside the tape in 1.2·10^11 steps (`py/inside000.c`).

## For later

Write the frozen stretch as a regular language of particles (resting runner in each stage, walls, crystal), prove the gap-0 contact rules with `py/cycles.py`, and prove that one cycle maps the language into itself with no two 0-blocks adjacent. A certificate at the level of single steps would need a checker faster than `TM.CTL.check` (a hash set or a sorted array for the abstract configurations).
