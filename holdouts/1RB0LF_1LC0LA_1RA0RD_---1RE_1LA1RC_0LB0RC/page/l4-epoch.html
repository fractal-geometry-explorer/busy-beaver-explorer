Round trips need k ≥ 1. When k reaches 0 (a = 4) the countdown cannot go on. What happens next has three parts, and Lean checks them together as one end case per template. We call the stretch between two such events an epoch.

  1. The reset (three returns to the left end). The last 4 copies of 01 in a are used up and the short word in front of b is rewritten. The old b ends up first in the list, with a few more copies. In the example below the new a is 724 + 6 = 730.
  2. The transient (up to about 145 round trips). Each trip still takes 3 from the new a, as before. But the tail of the list is in disorder after the reset, so it does not yet follow any of the 62 templates. The new b starts small and grows. Now and then two pieces of the tail merge.
  3. Settling. The tail falls into one of the four regimes, and the tape is a template again: endT t, with a new k that is large and a new j that is small.

Lean proves, for each template t and every j: from template t with numbers (0, j + 200), the machine reaches the template endT t with numbers (j + endC1, endC2). These are the 62 end cases, checked together as ends_ok: runs of the symbolic checker of 4,200 to 127,000 moves each, 2.5 million in all, evaluated by compiled code because the kernel runs out of memory on runs this long. native_decide That is the step from level 3 to level 4: an epoch is k round trips followed by one end case, and at level 4 the whole epoch is one move, from (t, k, j) to (endT t′, …) where t′ is the template reached at k = 0.

Why "j + 200". During the transient the new a, which comes from the old b, still loses 3 every round trip. It must not run out before the tail has settled, or the end case would be a different computation. The symbolic checker cannot ask whether a count is zero, so it needs a guarantee up front. The end cases are stated for an old b of at least 3 · 200 + β copies (j ≥ 200). The longest transient takes under 150 round trips, so it uses fewer than 450 of those copies. The potential (level 5) shows that j is always at least 200 when an epoch ends.

The second runs them on the shift-rule simulator at any j you choose. These are independent checks of the claims behind ends_ok, at one size each; Lean's check covers every size.
