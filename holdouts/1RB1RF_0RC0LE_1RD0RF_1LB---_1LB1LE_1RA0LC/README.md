# 1RB1RF_0RC0LE_1RD0RF_1LB---_1LB1LE_1RA0LC

bbchallenge id 42815. One of the six-state holdouts whose halting from blank tape is open (group G09, visited tape growing like the square root of the step count on both sides).

**In one sentence.** A particle system on the background `(1110)^n`: walls drift left and turn into runners at the left end, the head shuttles between the left end and the first runner, runners carry walls past them and merge in pairs into new walls; a halt needs three 0s in a row inside the tape, which never forms in 1.2·10^11 steps, but no proof covers every collision yet.

**Twin.** This is the table of [`1RB0RE_1LC---_0RA0LD_1LC1LD_1RF0LA_1RC1RE`](../1RB0RE_1LC---_0RA0LD_1LC1LD_1RF0LA_1RC1RE/README.md) with the states renamed (A→C, B→D, C→B, D→E, E→F, F→A); this machine starts in the state that one calls F. The two runs from blank tape differ in detail (no common configuration at the left end in their first 300,000 steps), but the particles, the cycle and the eleven particle rules are the same after renaming; the full analysis is written up there, and this page gives it in this machine's state names.

## What it does

Cut the tape into blocks `1^k 0` and write each block as its k; a run of n background blocks `1110` is written `(n)` (`.` for one). The other blocks are particles: 1 and 5 are **walls** (phase jump 2), 2 is a **runner** (jump 3), 0 and 4 are a runner fused with a 1-wall or a 5-wall, 7 and 9 walls in contact. The leftmost block is the left gadget (4 when idle); the far right holds periodic crystals of walls (`1.1.55.55.1.1(2)` repeated, or `1551.` repeated).

At the start of a sweep the head is in A on the second cell of the gadget. Rightward, A reads the tape two cells at a time (F, C and B only make one-cell back-steps): every `1110` becomes `0111`, every 1-wall `10` becomes `11`, every 5-wall `111110` becomes `010111`, so A crosses walls and turns at the first block with an odd phase jump, the first runner. Leftward, E moves every 0 one cell back. The round trip leaves the background in place and flips every wall it crossed (1 ↔ 5); each wall drifts one block left every two cycles. Everything right of the first runner is frozen until a runner gets there.

Read together: **the runner** moves one block right per cycle; it passes through a wall in two cycles (fusing into a 0 or a 4 for one cycle) and leaves the wall behind it, flipped; when it reaches a resting runner the two **merge into a 1-wall**. **Walls** left of the runner flip every cycle and drift one block left every two cycles. When a wall reaches the left end, the gadget **absorbs it in three cycles** (4 1 → 6 → 2 → 3) and **emits a new runner** (3 → 4 2). The new runner is now the first one: the head turns there, and the old runner rests where it is until the new one catches it up (and they merge) — unless yet another wall reaches the left end first and a third runner is born. Phase jumps add up: wall (2) = runner (3) + runner (3) mod 4, and the gadget turns jump 2 into jump 3. (The rules are stated with a single wall in the active stretch; with several, each is crossed and flipped independently, as the sweep rules below show.)

## Halting

The machine halts only when D reads a 1, and Lean proves that the only way there is A reading `0 0 0 1` (`halt_needs_pattern`, `lean/Halt.lean`): in pairs, A reads `0 0`, switches to C, and C reads `0 1`. In blocks that is two 0-blocks side by side inside the stretch A crosses; a 0-block is a runner fused with a 1-wall and lasts one cycle. In the first 1.2·10^11 steps A never reads `0 0 0` strictly inside the tape (`py/inside000.c`; it reads `0 0 0` only on blank cells past the ends, 31,400 times), and at none of the 452,253 sweep starts of the first 10^9 steps are two 0-blocks adjacent anywhere inside the tape.

What a proof needs, and the closed-tape-language searches that failed, are written up for the twin ([README](../1RB0RE_1LC---_0RA0LD_1LC1LD_1RF0LA_1RC1RE/README.md), "What a proof needs" and "Attempts"); they apply here word for word after renaming.

## Status

Open. Proved in Lean, kernel only: the halting configurations (`step_none_iff`, `lean/Blank.lean`), no halt in 10,000 steps, the 7 sweep rules (`lean/Rules.lean`), the 11 one-cycle particle rules (`lean/Cycles.lean`), and that every halt from blank tape passes through A reading `0 0 0 1` (`halt_needs_pattern`, `lean/Halt.lean`). With `native_decide`: no halt in 10,000,000 steps. C: no halt in 1,000,000,000 steps (`tools/c/triage.c`), and A never reads `0 0 0` inside the tape in 1.2·10^11 steps (`py/inside000.c`).

## For later

As for the twin: a regular description of the frozen part of the tape (resting runners in every stage, walls, crystals), the gap-0 contact rules, and a proof that one cycle maps the description into itself with no two 0-blocks adjacent.

## Scripts

- `py/particles.py STEPS [EVERY] [WIDTH]`: the tape in block notation at each sweep start.
- `py/cycles.py [show]`: writes `lean/Cycles.lean`, the particle rules (or prints them).
- `py/inside000.c`: counts the times A reads `0 0 0` inside the tape (C, see the first lines for usage; pass `A`).
- `py/passes.py blocks K | cross | back D | tape STEPS N`: two-cell behaviour of every state, the words crossed whole, the way back from a halt, run-length tapes.
- `py/rules.py`: writes `lean/Rules.lean` and `lean/Halt.lean`.
