The machine 1RB0LE_1RC---_1RD1RE_1LA0RF_0RC0LA_0LC0RB, started on a blank tape, never halts.

Kernel Lean theorem nonhalting : ¬ HaltsFromBlank in lean/Closed.lean. It rests on Lean's kernel alone: #print axioms for it lists only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound, the three standard axioms of Lean's logic. There is no native_decide and no compiled code anywhere in the proof.

The argument, in three sentences. From the blank tape the machine reaches a tape that is good: one of ten forms, with numbers that satisfy that form's inequalities (table_from_blank, good_last). From any good tape it makes at least one step, without halting, and reaches a good tape again (good_step). Repeating the second sentence gives runs of every length, and a machine that halts has runs only up to some fixed length.

Other checks, separate from Lean Computed: the first 49 states of the replay match the plain run up to 3·1010 steps (c/checkpoints.c), and your browser repeats the first ones in the level 3 section and every rule turn up to 108 steps in the first panel. The replay itself goes on without a gap to 10300 steps (2,281 states). What the proof does not do is explain the growth rate; the replay suggests that each pair of phases multiplies the counts by a factor that depends on the residues taken.

The last lines of the proof, in words

The end of lean/Closed.lean, theorem by theorem:

Good t
t is one of the ten forms, with numbers that satisfy that form's inequalities (gOp, gO0, gS1, gRF, gEF, gT3, gT2, gU3, gRG, gRG3).
good_step
from a good t, at least one step leads to a good t′. One case per form (step_Op … step_RG3), each split by remainder or parity into the 20 cases.
table_from_blank
some run from the blank tape ends at the last state of the replay (blank_O then table_run).
good_last
that last state is good.
nonhalting
¬ HaltsFromBlank. By induction on N: for every N there is a run from blank of length at least N ending at a good tape. So runs of every length exist, and the machine does not halt.
