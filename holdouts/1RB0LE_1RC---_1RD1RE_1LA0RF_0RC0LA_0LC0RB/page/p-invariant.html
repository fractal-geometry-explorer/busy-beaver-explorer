The 196 moves cover a finite stretch of the run. To go on forever the proof needs one argument that works for all later moves. That is level 4, the invariant Good in lean/Closed.lean: for each form, a list of 3 to 16 linear inequalities on its numbers. They say three kinds of things:

shape
the growing numbers keep their ratios inside a fixed region (a polyhedral cone). For O a b c, b stays between about 1.7 and 4.2 times a, and c between about 0.3 and 2.3 times a, in the shaded region of the picture below.
small parts
the bounded numbers stay in range (in RF, k is 4 or 5; in RG and RG3, n is 4 or 5; in T3, 8 ≤ y ≤ 15), and some numbers keep exact relations (in T3, x = 2a + 4; in U3, x − 2a is between 0 and 2; in RG3, 4y + 21x − 49a is between −63 and 19).
size
one weighted sum is at least 50,000,000 or 100,000,000, such as 71·b ≥ 50,000,000 for O.

The picture shows, for the forms O and S1, the region that the ratio inequalities allow (shaded), and the ratios of every O or S1 state of the replay (dots). The ratios move around from phase to phase but stay in the region. Filled dots are good states. Hollow dots are early states, which are still too small for the size condition.

Why do the inequalities keep holding? Each of the 20 cases of level 3 is a fixed affine map on the numbers. So the step from Good to Good is a question about linear inequalities over the whole numbers, which Lean's omega procedure decides. Kernel Each case works because:

  • the map sends each form's region strictly inside the next form's region, with room to spare;
  • the size grows by a factor of at least 1.1 per case, so the room to spare grows faster than the fixed offsets in the formulas (the "+6", "+13" and so on);
  • the inequalities imply every condition a case needs (b ≥ 6 and so on), and that T3 has c ≥ 2⌊d/5⌋ + 3, enough 14-cell words for all its turns. That rules out the gaps of level 3.

That is good_step: from a good tape the machine makes at least one step and reaches a good tape. Its proof has one step_* lemma per form, each split by the residue into its cases, and each case is one mv_* lemma and one call of omega. The coefficients (up to 1,321) were found by linear programming on the replay's numbers, with scripts outside the proof. Lean does not trust them: if one were wrong, omega would fail.

The last state of the replay is good: Lean evaluates its 16 inequalities with decide (good_last). Kernel Along the replay every entry from the 39th on is good, and the panel above lets you check any of them, and the case that follows.
