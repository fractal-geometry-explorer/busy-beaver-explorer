Level 2 counts time in rules, and a rule may span millions of steps. What makes that possible is that the head crosses long runs in a simple way, and Lean proves each crossing once for every length:

  • pass_10_right: C crosses (10)^n to the right in 2n steps and leaves it unchanged.
  • pass_10_left: A crosses (01)^n to the left in 8n steps (it carries 10 along).
  • fill_F: F turns (1000)^n into (1110)^n, 10 steps a copy.
  • fill_B: B turns (0001)^n into (0111)^n, 10 steps a copy.
  • clear_A: A, going left, turns (0111)^n into (0010)^n, 10 steps a copy.

Each is proved by running one copy for real (rfl: the kernel computes those few steps) and then a general lemma: if one copy takes the head through in the same state, n copies do too, in n times the steps (lean/Rules.lean). Kernel Pick a pass and play it on the real machine with n = 3.
