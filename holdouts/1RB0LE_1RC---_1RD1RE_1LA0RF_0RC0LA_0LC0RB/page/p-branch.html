Level 3 forgets the turns inside a phase and keeps only the form at the start of each phase. Put the rules together and every form leads to another form. The graph shows all of them. A loop on a form is a cycle rule, which runs as long as there are words left to use up. Each arrow out of a form is an exit, chosen by what is left over: how many 14-cell words remain (c = 0 or 1), how many 0010 remain in T3 (d = 0 to 4), or whether the run just built has even or odd length. The thicker an arrow, the more often the first 196 moves of the run use it. Click a form to see its rules and its cases.

Each case runs one cycle to its end with its k-fold iterate (cycle_iter, cycle2_iter, cycle3_iter, ucyc_iter, rcyc_iter, gcyc_iter, g3cyc_iter) and then applies one exit rule. Which exit is decided by a residue: c mod 2 for S1, T2 and U3, d mod 5 for T3, and the parity of the run being built for RF, RG and RG3. Once the residue is fixed the new numbers are affine functions of the old ones, so the 20 cases mv_* are exact formulas, proved by chaining the rules of level 2 (for every value of the variables). Kernel

Not every case is safe for every size. A case needs its numbers to be large enough (for mv_oentry, b ≥ 6 and c ≥ 2), and some combinations have no case at all, for example T3 running out of 14-cell words while 5 or more 0010 are left. Those are the gaps the invariant of level 4 closes.
