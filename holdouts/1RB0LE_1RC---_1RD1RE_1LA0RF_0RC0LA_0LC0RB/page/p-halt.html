At the top of the ladder is the machine itself: a tape of 0s and 1s, a head, and the table above. Its table has one missing entry: B reading a 1. That is the only way to halt. Two transitions lead into B: A reading a 0 (writes 1, moves right) and F reading a 1 (writes 0, moves right). So the machine halts exactly when the head reads A> 0 1 or F> 1 1 (Lean: halt_A, halt_F in lean/Rules.lean). Kernel

The proof never looks for these patterns directly. It shows something stronger: from the blank tape, for every number n, the first n steps are all defined. That is the Lean statement ¬ HaltsFromBlank, where HaltsFromBlank means "some step reaches a configuration with no transition".

Without the proof, the run gives only bounds: no halt in the first 104 steps (kernel) and 107 steps (native_decide), both in lean/Blank.lean, and none in 109 steps of the C simulator. The simulator above (Watch it run) is this level.
