## Status

**The question is open.** Lean (kernel) proves the six regular macro steps and the two main shapes of the anomaly for all parameter values, and no halt in the first 10,000 steps (10^7 with `native_decide`). Lean also shows that the pattern of the word and of the gaps is not an invariant by itself: a short Q configuration of the same form halts after 190 steps. Non-halting therefore depends on sizes: every collision cascade must end before the word runs out. In the simulated run it is always won, by at least 8.0 bits. **Update, 12 November.** Lean now also proves this machine's own form of the anomaly with a one-unit counter, 20 collision rules, and a partial macro map (a function from one word-and-counter configuration to the next, proved to be followed by the machine wherever it is defined). The map is far from complete: of the 236 collisions in the simulated run, 187 follow a proved rule. For the sister machine 1RB0RF_1LC1RE_0LD0LC_1RD0RB_1RA0RB_0LC--- the map is complete; here the collisions have many more cases, and they keep a misaligned gap alive instead of clearing it.

- **No halt in the first 10,000 steps.** The list simulator of the shared library, proved to agree with the machine's semantics, runs 10,000 steps from blank tape without a halt, evaluated by Lean's kernel. (`blank_runs_kernel`, `lean/Blank.lean`)
- **The six regular Q-steps.** From `W 0 1^(3a) B>0 R` the machine reaches the next Q configuration after a step count linear in the parameters, in each of the six cases (last block `2j+5`, 3, or a 1 after `2i+1`; counter low part `a+2`, or one unit then `t` gaps of 2): shift rules generated by `bbtools.symtm.prove`. (`q_big_wide` … `q_one_unit`, `lean/Rules.lean`)
- **Q-step = φ twice, counter plus one.** The same six steps restated on Q configurations `qc W a P T`: the word goes to `phi (phi W)` (phi defined on lists) and the counter's low part and gaps update as a binary counter. (`qstep_big_wide` … `qstep_one_unit`, `lean/Macro.lean`)
- **The anomaly, exactly.** When the word has run out the machine rewrites it in one sweep, for every value of the parameters and every tape beyond: from `1^(2c+2) 0 1^(3(a+1)) B>0 R` (blank to the left) it reaches `1^2 (0 1)^c 0 1^3 B>0 0^(3a) 1 R`, one cell further left, in `3a+8c+17` steps; from the word `1^(2c+2) 0 1` with `a+2` units it reaches the word `2 1…1 3` with a gap of `3a+1` in `3a+8c+46` steps. Its word is so short that it runs out a second time before the misaligned gap made by the first anomaly is cleared; the second anomaly leaves gaps of 1 and 0 in a row, and the collision on them halts. So no proof can use only the shape of the word and of the gaps (even/odd blocks, gaps ≡ 2 mod 3): it has to carry the sizes. (`qc_short_halts`, `lean/Transients.lean`)
- **Pieces of the irregular steps.** Five rules that hold for all six machines of the family and for every value of the parameters: the head rewrites the end of the word as φ applied twice and comes back, without reading the tape on either side (3 shapes of the word's end); and the way a regular step with a one-unit low part ends (next gap at least 3 cells, or blank tape). Ten cases, each with a next gap of at least 5 cells or blank tape (20 rules): misaligned gap 0 with 0, 1, 2 or 3 gaps of 2 behind when `t` is even and 0, 1 or 2 when `t` is odd; misaligned gap 1 with at least one gap of 2 behind when `t` is even (any number) and with 0 or 1 when `t` is odd. ### Heuristic (observed, not proved)

- **Growth shape: exp/exp.** Left of the start, the visited part of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.0). Right of the start it grows like the logarithm of the step count: each new cell costs a roughly constant factor more time, the signature of a counter or of a Collatz-like map whose values are written on the tape (fitted exponent 0.039). (`tools/py/bbtools/triage.py`)
- **How the other collisions differ.** With a long next gap the table continues regularly: each further pair of gaps of 2 behind the misaligned gap adds a block of 5 to the word. These rows (a block of 5 repeated) and the cases with a next gap shorter than 5 cells are not proved. Found by simulating each case for a few sizes; not proved. (`python3 py/collision_table.py 1RB1RF_1LC1RE_0LD0LC_1RD0RB_1RA0RB_1RC---` in 1RB0RF_1LC1RE_0LD0LC_1RD0RB_1RA0RB_0LC---)
- **Gap alignment.** In every run the gaps after the first misaligned gap have length ≡ 2 (mod 3), at least 2. The halting patterns of the collisions that we found need a gap of length 0 there. (observed over all simulated transients; not proved)

### Open

- **Does it halt from blank tape?.** Not known. What is missing, in order: (1) the remaining collision cases as Lean rules. Unlike in the 0LC machine, a collision here does not clear the misaligned gap: it shortens the next gap by 3 to 7 cells, which is misaligned in turn, so gaps of every small length occur and each needs its own case; in several cases the head also goes back into the word. A finer description (the configurations with an empty low part that follow an F event) would probably replace this case list by a few rules. (2) the one-unit anomaly and those rules inside the macro map, so that it follows the run from step 27. (3) the size argument: every cascade of collisions ends before the word runs out. For the 0LC machine, where (1) and (2) are done, (3) is the open part and looks like a Collatz-type question; the same is to be expected here.

## How it works

### A word and a counter

This machine is one of six G12 holdouts that share every transition except two: A1 (`0RF` or `1RF`) and F0. Here A1 is `1RF` and F0 is `1RC`. Both are used only at an F event, when state A reads a 1, which is rare (see the last section). Everything else is the same for the six machines, and so is this page up to that point.

Most of the time the tape reads `W 0 1^(3a) B>0 R`. We call this a Q configuration. `W` is a word: blocks of 1s separated by single 0s, with a blank tape to its left. The first block is even and every later block is odd. `R` is a run of gaps `0^(3p+2) 1`. The run of `3a` ones before the head and the gaps together hold a binary counter: `a` and the gap sizes `p` are its digits, read in parts. The head stays between the word and the counter. It goes left and rewrites the end of the word, then goes right and adds one to the counter.

On the word the machine applies a fixed map `φ`. It removes the last block `b`, adds 2 to the block before it, and appends `(b−1)/2` blocks of a single 1. The total width stays the same. Applying it repeatedly, a word with blocks `2 3 1 5` goes like this:

| word (blocks) | tape |
|---|---|
| `2 3 1 5` | `1^2 0 1^3 0 1 0 1^5` |
| `2 3 3 1 1` | `1^2 0 1^3 0 1^3 0 1 0 1` |
| `2 3 3 3` | `1^2 0 1^3 0 1^3 0 1^3` |
| `2 3 5 1` | `1^2 0 1^3 0 1^5 0 1` |
| `2 3 7` | `1^2 0 1^3 0 1^7` |

Repeated `φ` carries every block into the first one: the word eventually becomes a single block. The number of steps this takes is exactly `N(W) = Σi≥2 2^(Si−1)/2`, where `Si` is the width of the word from block `i` to the end. So `φ` is itself a counter, written in a mixed radix.

### One Q-step: φ twice, the counter plus one

From one Q configuration to the next, the machine applies `φ` twice to the word and adds one to the counter. The step count is a linear function of the parameters. The step depends only on the last one or two blocks of the word (a last block of `2j+5`, a last block of 3, or a last 1 after an odd block) and on the lowest part of the counter (at least two units, or one unit followed by `t` gaps of length 2). That gives six cases.

| word ends | counter | steps |
|---|---|---|
| `1^(2j+5)` | `a+2 ≥ 2` | `3a + 8j + 41` |
| `1^(2j+5)` | one unit, `t` short gaps | `8j + 3t + 41` |
| `1^3` | `a+2` | `3a + 33` |
| `1^3` | one unit, `t` | `3t + 33` |
| `1^(2i+1) 0 1` | `a+2` | `3a + 8i + 33` |
| `1^(2i+1) 0 1` | one unit, `t` | `8i + 3t + 33` |

All six are Lean theorems checked by the kernel, for every value of the parameters and every tape beyond. `lean/Rules.lean` states them as shift rules. `lean/Macro.lean` restates them on Q configurations `qc W a P T`, with `φ` defined as a Lean function on lists, for example `qstep_big_wide`.

### Long cycles, anomalies and collisions

Between Q events the counter climbs. When it overflows, the run of ones `1^(3k)` gets longer by one unit and the counter starts again from zero. A full cycle with counter length `k` applies `φ` about 2^k times, and each extra unit roughly doubles the time. That is why the right side of the tape grows like the logarithm of the step count.

The regular Q-steps break down in two ways, called transients. An anomaly happens when the word has become a single block: `φ` has nothing left to carry, and the block restarts as `2 1 1 …` one cell further left. A collision happens when the counter overflows into a gap that is too short. The accelerated simulator `py/hyb.py` (in the folder of 1RB0RF_1LC1RE_0LD0LC_1RD0RB_1RA0RB_0LC---) jumps over regular stretches with the closed forms above and runs every transient cell by cell. It agrees with direct simulation on every F event up to 2·10^10 steps, and on direct runs of 3·10^8 steps from Q events picked at random in the middle of phases.

For this machine it ran 300 transients without a halt, up to about 2^2836 steps, with the tape 5664 cells wide.

### The anomaly: the word starts again

Each φ moves width into the first block, so after `N(W)` steps the word is a single block (or a block and a single 1). The next Q-step cannot apply φ and does something else, the anomaly. It is short and exact. The head erases the counter's low part, slides the block one cell to the right through the hole it leaves, and comes back rewriting the block as `1 1 0 1 0 1 …`:

| before | after | steps |
|---|---|---|
| `1^(2c+2) 0 1^(3a+3) B>0 R` | `1^2 (0 1)^c 0 1^3 B>0 0^(3a) 1 R` | `3a+8c+17` |
| `1^(2c+2) 0 1 0 1^(3a+6) B>0 R` | `1^2 (0 1)^c 0 1^3 0 1^3 B>0 0^(3a+1) 1 R` | `3a+8c+46` |

The new word `2 1 1 … 1` has the same width `w`, and `N(W) = 2^(w−2)/2 − 1` for it: it lasts that many φ steps. The counter starts again from one unit. Where the old low part was there is now a gap of `3a` or `3a+1` cells. That is not of the form `3p+2`: it is the misaligned gap. All of the old counter's gaps are still behind it. These are Lean theorems for every `a`, `c` and every tape `R` (`anomaly_one`, `anomaly_two` in `lean/Transients.lean`). The third shape, a block and a single 1 with a one-unit counter, goes through an F event and depends on A1 and F0, so it is not among them.

As the new counter overflows, the misaligned gap shrinks by 3 each time. When it is down to 0 or 1, the next overflow is a collision. The head carries that part of the counter into the end of the word, and the next old gap, a few cells shorter, becomes the misaligned one. This cascade continues until the old gaps are used up and the counter is aligned again.

### A race between the word and the cascade

Shape alone does not keep the machine running. Lean checks that the Q configuration `1^2 0 1^3 0 1^3 B>0` halts after 190 steps (`qc_short_halts`). Its word is valid and its counter is aligned, but the word is so short that it runs out again while the misaligned gap made by the first anomaly is still there. The second anomaly then leaves gaps of 1 and 0 in a row, and the collision on them halts. The real run avoids this only because each cascade ends long before the word runs out.

For this machine the cascade after an anomaly ends at most 2^278 steps later. The epoch, from one anomaly to the next, lasts up to 2^2762 steps. The margin (bits of the epoch minus bits of the cascade) is at least 8.0, and is that small only in the first epochs. A proof would need a bound on the cascade, set by the size of the counter at the anomaly, that stays below the word's `N(W)`.

The last epochs of the simulated run:

| word width after the anomaly | collisions | log2 epoch | log2 cascade | margin (bits) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4892 | 4 | 2472.5 | 9.2 | 2463.2 |
| 4940 | 1 | 2477.5 | 6.2 | 2471.2 |
| 4948 | 2 | 2485.5 | 7.7 | 2477.7 |
| 4970 | 40 | 2688.5 | 13.1 | 2675.4 |
| 5372 | 6 | 2720.5 | 10.2 | 2710.3 |
| 5440 | 6 | 2761.5 | 10.3 | 2751.2 |

### Where it could stop

F is reached only from A reading a 1, and F halts on a 1. So a halt needs A to read `1 1`. Regular Q-steps never let A read a 1. Only transients do; such a moment is an F event. In the first 2·10^10 steps this machine has 7 F events, and at every one the cell after the 1 is a 0.

All the F events we saw happen in collisions. A collision starts when the counter's low part is one unit followed only by gaps of length 2 and then a misaligned gap of length 0 or 1. The head sweeps that part of the counter into the word, and the next gap becomes the misaligned one, shortened by a few cells. A halt needs a gap of length 0 right after such a gap. The gaps behind the misaligned one come from the counter as it was at the anomaly. In every run they have length ≡ 2 (mod 3) and at least 2, so the halt never comes.

**Status: open.** Lean proves the regular dynamics, the anomaly and that the word-and-gap pattern alone does not prevent a halt. The collisions, the race and the alignment of the gaps are simulated, not proved.

## The Lean files, in plain words

- `Machine.lean`, `Blank.lean`: the machine and its first steps (generated).
- `Rules.lean`, `Macro.lean`: the six regular steps; `Macro.lean` says each one applies φ twice to the word and adds one to the counter.
- `Transients.lean`: the anomaly with a wide low part (two shapes) and the short configuration that halts.
- `Collide.lean`: statements of the form "from this tape the machine reaches that tape in so many steps, for all values of the parameters": the rewriting of the word's end, the end of a regular one-unit step, this machine's anomaly with a one-unit counter, and the collision cases listed above.
- `Abstract.lean`: the notation `qr W a G` for the tape `W 0 1^(3a) B>0` followed by gaps of the listed lengths.
- `CollideQ.lean`: the rules restated in that notation.
- `Full.lean`: the partial macro map `astep` and `astep_sound` (the machine follows it wherever it is defined). The file also contains `not_halts_of_arun` (if the map could be applied for ever from step 27 the machine would never halt); for this machine that hypothesis fails at once, because the map is not defined on the first configuration, so the theorem is true but gives nothing yet.
