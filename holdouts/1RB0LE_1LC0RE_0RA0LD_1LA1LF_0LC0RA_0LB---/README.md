# 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---

bbchallenge id 66280. One of the six-state holdouts whose halting from blank tape is open (group G11, family *two-word sweeper*). It differs from its siblings in F0 = 0LB, E1 = 0RA.

| state | read 0 | read 1 |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1RB | 0LE |
| B | 1LC | 0RE |
| C | 0RA | 0LD |
| D | 1LA | 1LF |
| E | 0LC | 0RA |
| F | 0LB | --- |

## What it does

Write a = `10` and b = `110`. Apart from a few cells at the ends, the tape is a string of a's and b's split into
sections by `000`. The head crosses runs of words in one state (the sweep rules `a_right_01`, `a_right_011`, `b_right_10`, `b_right_110`, `e_right_10`,
proved in Lean for any number of copies). Its turning points far from the left end follow the ruler pattern of a
binary counter with 4-cell digits, and the ends are visited rarely.

At its visits to the left end (state E) the tape starts `E>0 0 1 0 (1 1 0)^k ...`. Record values of k in a
3e9-step run (`tmrun 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB--- 3000000000 at E L`): k=11 at step 31,482,224, k=12 at step 56,276,639, k=13 at step 138,079,678, k=14 at step 271,307,991, k=15 at step 645,244,842, k=16 at step 1,767,055,517. Each new b costs about 2 to 3 times the steps of the
previous one, as a counter would; the rest of the tape is rearranged between visits, so the counter's digits are
not plain blocks.

Its first 21 left-end configurations also occur in the run of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LE---`; later the runs differ.

**Where it can halt.** `python3 -m bbtools.ctl 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB--- 6` finds a closed tape language (windows of 6 cells; not yet in
Lean). It allows a halt only with F reading a 1 and `110000`, `110001`, `110101` from the head on, that is
F on the first 1 of a word `110` that ends a section.

**Larger windows (worker 2, second round).** `python3 -m bbtools.ctl 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB--- K` for larger K: K = 7: closes, 960 abstract configurations where a halt can fire; K = 8: no closed set within the default 100 rounds. So larger windows do not remove the halting contexts (their number grows with K) and soon stop closing: this route gave no certificate. These machines are counters, whose halting depends on a global count, which a language of K-cell windows cannot see; a certificate will have to use the macro structure.

## The engine (decoded and proved, worker 2, third round)

**Events.** At an *event* the head is in state A on a 0 just right of a base-3 counter:

    0^inf 1 0 [N] A> 0 [M] 1 [R] ...

- `N`: base 3, 3-cell digits `100` = 0, `110` = 1, `010` = 2, most significant first (lowest digit next to the head);
- `M`: binary, 4-cell digits `0010` = 0, `1010` = 1, lowest digit first, any width;
- `R`: binary, 4-cell digits `1000` = 0, `1010` = 1, lowest digit first.

(In the words a = `10`, b = `110` of the first description, M's digits are the sections `a|` and `aa`.)

**Event rule (Lean, every size).** From one event the machine reaches the next with N + 1 (trailing 2s carry; 2...2
wraps to 0...0 with one digit more, the tape growing 3 cells at the left end), M + 1 (trailing 1s carry; 1...1 wraps to
0...0), and, only when M wraps, R + 3. When M does not wrap nothing right of M is read. So N counts events in base 3,
M counts them in binary, and R gains 3 every 2^(width of M) events. `lean/Events.lean`: 54 theorems, one per case (exhaustive by construction of `events.py`; 9 cases of N:
lowest digit 0 or 1 after 0, 1 or k+2 twos, or the wrap at the left end; times 6 of M and R: no wrap, carry over i+1
ones, or a wrap of i+1 ones with the 4 cases of R's two low bits and a carry over j ones), each with its exact step
count (for example 12i + 12j + 10k + 116), generated by
`python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/events.py 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB--- NAMESPACE`.
Example (run of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`, M with 2 digits, R with 8): at steps 12,203, 12,223, 12,243, 12,253, 12,345 (N, M, R) =
(5, 0, 92), (6, 1, 92), (7, 2, 92), (8, 3, 92), (9, 0, 95).

**The run.** In the clean shape R is followed by `1 0 1` and blank tape. This run reaches it at step 7,591
(N = 4 (2 digits), M = 2 (2 digits), R = 2 (8 digits)); then 433 consecutive events follow the rule, until the first overflow of R (8 digits, R = 254, at about step 20,500)
(`python3 holdouts/1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---/py/track.py 1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB--- 3000000`). An overflow of R (the carry running past R) is not covered: the head
goes past R and rebuilds the right part, and later the cells after R hold a further structure starting `1 0 1 1 0`, with
M and R of other widths. Counting every event whose right part starts `0 [M] 1`: in the first 3·10^6 steps
5,820 follow from the previous event by the rule and 1,045 do not (the wraps of M that reach the
undecoded part); at step 3·10^6, N = 2,159 (8 digits), M with 3 digits.

**Siblings.** All six machines of the family satisfy the same 54 rules (the transitions in which they differ are never
used inside an event); they differ in the overflows of R and after. This one: F0 = 0LB, E1 = 0RA. In
`1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---` the transition D1 (the only way into F, whose 1 halts) is used in the first 10^5 steps only at steps 138, 1,008,
3,177, 7,571 (before the clean shape) and 20,756, 53,353, 92,444, 92,861 (at the first three overflows of R).

**What is open.** No event can halt (every rule is a run); a halt can only come from an overflow of R or from the
undecoded layout after it. Next steps: write the overflow of R as a rule (what N, M, R and the cells after R become),
decode the layered right part (it seems to hold further counters), then iterate.

## The right side as a recursive visit law (worker 2, round 2 of G11)

Idea (from the busycoq proof of `1RB0LB_0RC0LD_1LD1RE_0LA0RF_0RD1RF_0RC---`): instead of rules for events plus
separately decoded overflows, describe what **one visit** of the head into a stream does, for every stream.
`Vis q x r x'` (`lean/Visit.lean`): the head enters the stream `x` from the left in state `q`, and the first time it
leaves `x` to the left it is in state `r` and the stream is `x'`.

**Token rows (Lean, kernel).** Cut the tape right of the event cell (cell -29) into tokens of two cells,
`z = 00`, `a = 10`, `c = 01`, `d = 11`. A visit of `token x` is a few steps inside the token and some nested visits
of `x`. In 3·10^7 steps of the run exactly 29 rows occur (`py/laws.py` of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`); each is a theorem of
`lean/Visit.lean` (written by `py/gen_visit.py`), conditional on the nested visits and valid for every stream, e.g.
`v_B_10_Bc`: if `B> x` comes out in C leaving `x'`, then `B> a x` comes out in A leaving `a x'`.

**The a/z stream (Lean, kernel, `lean/Stream.lean`).** Everything from R on, blank tape included (blank is
`z z z ...`), is one stream of `a` and `z`, and a visit in state B does this: the first `z` becomes `a`, and every
second `a` before it becomes `z`:

    B> (a a)^k z x    ->  (z a)^k a x      out in C     (vb_even)
    B> a (a a)^k z x  ->  a (z a)^k a x    out in A     (vb_odd; the same for a visit in state E: ve_odd)

With R's digits `a z` = 0, `a a` = 1 the odd case is the binary increment. **So R never overflows into something
else: there is no end of R.** What was called the overflow of R is the even case: when all digits up to the blank
tape are 1 the first `z` stands at an even place, the visit comes out in C instead of A, and leaves `(z a)^k a`:
the digits shifted by one token. (First time in the run of `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`: step 20,645, the stream `a^18` then blank, comes
out in C as `(z a)^9 a`.)

**M (Lean, kernel).** `m_inc`: `A> (c c)^k z c y` leaves `(z c)^k c c y`, out in D (M + 1, digits `z c` = 0,
`c c` = 1, for every k and every `y`). `m_wrap`: `A> (c c)^(k+1) c x` leaves `(z c)^(k+1) c x3`, out in D, where
`x3` is `x` after three visits (E, B, B) that each come out in A. With `vb_odd`/`ve_odd` this is the right half of the
event rule (M + 1, R + 3) in visit form, and it shows exactly where the event rule stops: at a visit of the a/z
stream that comes out in C.

**What is still open.** After a visit that comes out in C the token rows with other nested results apply (they are
in `Visit.lean`, among them one row that leaves a token in state F, the state whose 1 halts), and the layer of `c`
tokens is rebuilt with other widths. These rows are proved one by one but not composed into a law for the whole
right side, and the left side (N, tokens of three cells) has its own table (`py/laws.py` on the mirrored machine:
37 rows for `1RB0LE_1LC0RE_0RA0LD_1LA1LF_0LC0RA_0LB---`), not yet in Lean. There is no token-level simulator with jumps yet, so no run beyond the
3·10^9 steps of `tmrun`. A decision needs: the composed law for the `c` layer after a C exit, the left side's law,
then either an invariant or a far run.

## Status

- Lean (kernel): `Blank.lean` (the start configuration) and `Rules.lean` (sweep rules `a_right_01`, `a_right_011`, `b_right_10`, `b_right_110`, `e_right_10`).
- Trusted: no halt in 3e9 steps (`tmrun`); CTL with K=6 closed (`bbtools.ctl`).
- Lean (kernel): `Events.lean`, the event rule (N + 1, M + 1, R + 3 when M wraps) in 54 theorems for every size (one per case; the 54 cases are exhaustive by construction of `events.py`).
- Trusted: the run follows the rule (`track.py`, see above).
- Lean (kernel): `Visit.lean` (29 token rows of the visit law) and `Stream.lean` (`vb_even`, `vb_odd`, `ve_odd`, `m_inc`, `m_wrap`: the a/z stream and M for every size).
- Open: the composed law after a visit of the a/z stream that comes out in C (the former "overflow of R"), the left side in the same form; halting.
