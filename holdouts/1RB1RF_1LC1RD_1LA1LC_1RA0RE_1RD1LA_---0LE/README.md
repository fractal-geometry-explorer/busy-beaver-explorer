# 1RB1RF_1LC1RD_1LA1LC_1RA0RE_1RD1LA_---0LE

bbchallenge id 38519. One of the six-state holdouts whose halting from blank tape is open. Its run is a
**Collatz-like 3/2 map on one counter, driving a slowly growing stack on the right**; the macro rule is found and
checked, the inner sweep is proved in Lean, halting is open.

| state | read 0 | read 1 |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1RB | 1RF |
| B | 1LC | 1RD |
| C | 1LA | 1LC |
| D | 1RA | 0RE |
| E | 1RD | 1LA |
| F | --- | 0LE |


## What it does

Notation: `1^n` is n ones, `0^g` is g zeros, `A>` is the head in state A on the next cell. The right end of the
tape never moves after the first few thousand steps; everything happens at the growing left end.

**Epochs.** The run passes through configurations

    E(g, R) = 0^inf A> 0 1 0^g R

(the head just left of the tape, a single 1, a gap of g zeros, the rest R of the tape): E(8, `1^4`), E(18, ...),
E(24, ...), ... with g = 18, 24, 30, 42, 60, 84, 120, 174, 258, 378, 564, 840, 1254, 1878, 2808, 4206, 6306, 9450,
14172, 21252, 31872, 47802, ... (all multiples of 6 from 18 on).

**Inside an epoch: sweeps.** From E(g, R) the machine writes `1^12 000 1111` at the left end and enters

    X(n, m) = 0^inf A> 0 1^n 000 1111 0^m R      (first X(12, g-11))

and one sweep (the head runs right to the marker and back) is

    X(n, m+4) -> X(n+6, m)    in 3n+44 steps         (lean/Rules.lean `xstep`, lean/Phase.lean `sweep`)

so the block of ones grows by 6 while the marker 1111 eats 4 zeros of the gap; `sweeps` in Phase.lean iterates it
(j sweeps, j(3n+44) + 9j(j-1) steps). When fewer than 4 zeros are left (3 if g = 2 mod 4, 1 if g = 0 mod 4), the
marker hits R, the front of R is rewritten, the block of about 3g/2 ones is turned back into a gap, and the next
epoch starts.

**The epoch map.**

    E(g, R) -> E(3g/2 + c, R'),   (c, R') = T(g mod 4, R),   c in {-9, -6, -3, +6}

T depends on g only through g mod 4 (checked for g, g+4, g+8, g+12 for each R tried, and epoch by epoch along the
real run: `python3 py/epochs.py direct 30`, all 28 epochs past g = 100 agree). Examples:

    g = 2 mod 4, R = 1^11 0^8 1^4 0^16 S  ->  c = -3, R' = 1^23 0^2 1^4 0^16 S
    g = 0 mod 4, R = 1^11 0^8 1^4 0^16 S  ->  c = -6, R' = 1^11 0^8 1^4 0^4 1^4 0^16 S

So the counter g follows a Collatz-like map g -> 3g/2 + c (like Antihydra's 3/2 map), and its residues mod 4
(essentially the binary digits of g/2, read one per epoch) are fed into R. R is a list of runs (mostly `1^4`,
`1^8` separated by even gaps) that behaves like a stack: each epoch rewrites only its first few runs, pushing or
popping a little; the deep part of R is untouched for long times (the last 9 runs `1^4 0^12 1^4 0^18 1^8 0^28 1^4
0^2 1^4` have been fixed since 10^7 steps).

## Where it could halt

The machine halts only on F reading 0 (A reads a 1 and the next cell is 0). In random searches over short R
(`py/epochs.py`, function `epoch`) halts come only from R containing a lone `1` run; the R that occur have runs of
ones of length 2, 4, 6, 8, 11, 17, 23, ... Since R only changes at its front, a halt needs the front to eat its way
down into R far enough to produce such a configuration.

## Evidence

- The class map T, iterated with g as an exact integer (`python3 py/epochs.py abstract 300`): 300 epochs past
  g = 4206, up to g of about 10^55 (around 10^110 machine steps), with no halt. R grows steadily, from 13 runs
  to about 190 (about 0.6 runs per epoch); with random residues instead of the real ones it grows at the same rate.
- Direct simulation agrees with the macro rule epoch by epoch up to 3·10^11 steps.

Tape samples (runs of 1s separated by single 0s, left to right; `a^k` is k runs of length a; a long middle is cut with `...`):

- step 10,000,000: `2702 16 0^2 4 0^2390 11 0^7 4 0^23 4 0^11 4 0^17 8 0^27 4 0 4`
- step 100,000,000: `6006 6 4 0^10160 23 0^21 4 0^19 4 0^11 4 0^17 8 0^27 4 0 4`
- step 1,000,000,000: `44842 1688 0^2 4 0^848 11 0^7 4 0^15 4 0^17 4 0^19 4 0^11 4 0^17 8 0^27 4 0 4`

## Status

Open. Proved in Lean: the halting configurations, no halt in 10^7 steps (`lean/Blank.lean`), the sweep rule and its
iteration (`lean/Rules.lean`, generated by `py/rules.py` with `bbtools.symtm`, and `lean/Phase.lean`). Computed
(Python, not proved): the epoch map, its independence of g within a class mod 4, and 300 epochs of it without a
halt. Heuristic: the stack R grows linearly in the number of epochs, so the chance that its front ever reaches deep
enough to halt drops with every epoch; like Antihydra, a proof would need control of the residues of a 3/2 map.

### Next steps

- Prove the class map T in Lean for the R that occur (symbolic g in each class mod 4, with R as a context after
  its first few runs), and describe R as a stack automaton exactly: its alphabet and push/pop rules.
- Find the shortest residue sequence that would lead to a halt from the current R (a lower bound on how improbable a
  halt is).
