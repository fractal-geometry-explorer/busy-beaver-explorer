## The picture

At the moments the head is in A on the first blank cell right of everything written: The tape is then blocks of 1s separated by single 0s (`5^4` is four blocks of length 5, `…` an unchanged stretch). The fast counter is the run of blocks of length 2 at the right end, after the last 8: it grows by one block in every round, and each round takes about twice as long as the one before (T′ = 2T + 9 in the regular stretches).

| step | tape | |
|---|---|---|
| 178,406,562 | `6 2 6 5^4 8 8 5^6 11 11 11 17 17 5 5 2 2 2 8 2 11 2^9 8 2 1` | after a rebuild |
| 183,322,686 | `6 2 6 5^4 8 8 5 5 11 5^4 11 11 17 17 … 8 2^5 1` | the 11 walks left |
| 261,981,358 | `6 2 6 5 11 5 8 2 5^9 11 11 17 17 … 8 2^9 1` | it reaches the head |
| 849,298,943 | `6 2 12 5 5 5 8 8 5^8 11 11 17 17 … 8 2^12 1` |  |

Left of it the slow list is made of blocks of length 2 mod 3 (5, 8, 11, 17, …) behind a fixed head `6 2 6`. In each round one walker, an 11, moves one block to the left through the 5s (`5 11 → 11 5`), sometimes merging (`8 11 → … 14`); when it reaches the head of the list the list is rebuilt: the 2s of the fast counter are absorbed into new 5s and a new walk starts with more blocks to cross.

Rebuilds seen at about 2.0·10^5, 1.06·10^7 and 1.78·10^8 steps.

## What is established

**Proved in Lean, kernel only** — The halting configurations: The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (F1); the generated statement covers every configuration. (`step_none_iff`, `lean/Blank.lean`)

**Proved in Lean, kernel only** — No halt in the first 10,000 steps: The list simulator of the shared library, proved to agree with the machine's semantics, runs 10,000 steps from blank tape without a halt, evaluated by Lean's kernel. (`blank_runs_kernel`, `lean/Blank.lean`)

**Proved in Lean, kernel only** — Three rounds of the run, for any tape further left: Three consecutive rounds of the run from blank tape (between moments the head is in A on the first 0 right of everything written), each proved for every tape left of the cells it visits, with its exact step count. (`lean/Rounds.lean` (generated by `py/gen_rounds.py`))

**Proved in Lean with `native_decide`** — No halt in the first 10^7 steps: The same run, evaluated by compiled code. (`blank_runs_native`, `lean/Blank.lean`)

**Computed (trusted C/Python)** — No halt in the first 10^9 steps: The C simulator `tools/c/triage.c` runs 10^9 steps without a halt; at the end 287 cells have been visited and 213 of them hold a 1. (`holdouts/triage.json`)

**Computed (trusted C/Python)** — No halt in 10^9 steps; rebuilds: Direct simulation. The fast counter is the run of blocks of length 2 at the right end, after the last 8: it grows by one block in every round, and each round takes about twice as long as the one before (T′ = 2T + 9 in the regular stretches). The slow list was rebuilt at about 2.0·10^5, 1.06·10^7 and 1.78·10^8 steps; between rebuilds each round makes one local change in it. (`tools/c/tmrun.c` (`at A R`))

**Heuristic / observed** — Two-level counter: Left of it the slow list is made of blocks of length 2 mod 3 (5, 8, 11, 17, …) behind a fixed head `6 2 6`. In each round one walker, an 11, moves one block to the left through the 5s (`5 11 → 11 5`), sometimes merging (`8 11 → … 14`); when it reaches the head of the list the list is rebuilt: the 2s of the fast counter are absorbed into new 5s and a new walk starts with more blocks to cross. Not shown for all sizes. (`README.md`)

**Open** — Does it halt from blank tape?: Not known. See the heuristic claims above for what a halt would need.
