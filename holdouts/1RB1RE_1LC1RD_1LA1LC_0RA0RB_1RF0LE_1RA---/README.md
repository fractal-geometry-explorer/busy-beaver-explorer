bbchallenge id 20489, group G12. Whether it halts from blank tape is **open**. Family: two-level block counter (fast tail, walkers in a list of 1-mod-4 blocks, rebuilds).

## The picture

At the moments the head is in B on the first blank cell right of everything written: The tape is then blocks of 1s separated by single 0s (`1^11` is eleven blocks of length 1, `…` an unchanged stretch). The fast counter is the last block (followed by one more 1 and the head): it grows by 4 per round, and each round takes twice as long as the one before plus 4 steps in the regular stretches.

| step | tape | |
|---|---|---|
| 3,714,208 | `2 1^11 5 5 1^9 5 5 5 1 9 5 1 1 5 1^5 9 33 1 1 1 5 1^14 5 1` | just after a rebuild |
| 57,326,232 | `2 1^8 5 5 5 1^9 5 5 5 1 5 1 1 5 1 1 5 1^5 9 33 5 5 25 1 1 77 1` | walking |
| 861,641,606 | `2 5 1^8 5 5 1^9 5 5 5 1 5 … 25 1 1 93 1` | the walker at the front |
| 1,747,855,409 | `2 1^9 5 5 1^12 5 5 1^6 5 5 1^5 5 1 1 9 5 5 1 1 1 5 9 5 5 5 1^13 5 …` | after the next rebuild |

Left of it is a slow list of blocks of length 1 mod 4 (1, 5, 9, 13, …) behind a leading 2. In each round one walker moves one or two blocks to the left through the 1s, often growing by 4 as it swallows them (`1 1 x → x+4`), or a pair `5 5` moves left; when the walker reaches the leading part the list is rebuilt, the fast counter's cells becoming many new short blocks, and the walks start over.

Rebuilds seen at about 9.6·10^5, 3.7·10^6 and 1.75·10^9 steps.

## What is established

**Proved in Lean, kernel only** — The halting configurations: The machine stops exactly when it reads the symbol of a halting transition (F1); the generated statement covers every configuration. (`step_none_iff`, `lean/Blank.lean`)

**Proved in Lean, kernel only** — No halt in the first 10,000 steps: The list simulator of the shared library, proved to agree with the machine's semantics, runs 10,000 steps from blank tape without a halt, evaluated by Lean's kernel. (`blank_runs_kernel`, `lean/Blank.lean`)

**Proved in Lean, kernel only** — Three rounds of the run, for any tape further left: Three consecutive rounds of the run from blank tape (between moments the head is in B on the first 0 right of everything written), each proved for every tape left of the cells it visits, with its exact step count. (`lean/Rounds.lean` (generated by `py/gen_rounds.py`))

**Proved in Lean with `native_decide`** — No halt in the first 10^7 steps: The same run, evaluated by compiled code. (`blank_runs_native`, `lean/Blank.lean`)

**Computed (trusted C/Python)** — No halt in the first 10^9 steps: The C simulator `tools/c/triage.c` runs 10^9 steps without a halt; at the end 296 cells have been visited and 186 of them hold a 1. (`holdouts/triage.json`)

**Computed (trusted C/Python)** — No halt in 3·10^10 steps; rebuilds: Direct simulation. The fast counter is the last block (followed by one more 1 and the head): it grows by 4 per round, and each round takes twice as long as the one before plus 4 steps in the regular stretches. The slow list was rebuilt at about 9.6·10^5, 3.7·10^6 and 1.75·10^9 steps; between rebuilds each round makes one local change in it. (`tools/c/tmrun.c` (`at B R`))

**Heuristic / observed** — Two-level counter: Left of it is a slow list of blocks of length 1 mod 4 (1, 5, 9, 13, …) behind a leading 2. In each round one walker moves one or two blocks to the left through the 1s, often growing by 4 as it swallows them (`1 1 x → x+4`), or a pair `5 5` moves left; when the walker reaches the leading part the list is rebuilt, the fast counter's cells becoming many new short blocks, and the walks start over. Not shown for all sizes. (`README.md`)

**Open** — Does it halt from blank tape?: Not known. See the heuristic claims above for what a halt would need.
