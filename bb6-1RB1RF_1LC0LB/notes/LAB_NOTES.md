# Lab notes: 1RB1RF_1LC0LB_1RE0LD_1LB0RF_0RA0RC_1RE---

```
      0    1
A   1RB  1RF
B   1LC  0LB
C   1RE  0LD
D   1LB  0RF
E   0RA  0RC
F   1RE  ---
```

The machine halts only on F1. Trust levels as everywhere in the repository: **kernel** (Lean, kernel only),
**native** (Lean with `native_decide`), **computed** (C or Python here, tested, not proved), **heuristic** (patterns).

### The halting shape (kernel, `lean/Blank.lean`)

F is entered from A1 (`1RF`) and D1 (`0RF`) and reads the cell to their right. D is entered only by `C1 → 0LD`, a
left move that writes a 0 on the cell that is then D's right neighbour, so D never has a 1 to its right
(`d_right_zero`) and the D route never halts. A is entered only by `E0 → 0RA`. Hence `halt_from_e011`: in the run
from blank tape every halt comes two ordinary steps after a configuration `E>0 1 1`.

### Bounded runs

* kernel: 20,000 steps (`blank_runs_20000`, 20 s for the whole folder); native: 10^7 (`blank_runs_10M`).
* computed: no halt in 10^10 steps (`tools/c/tmrun.c`, 54 s; about 5 ns a step).

`runs/growth_1e10.txt` (`c/growth.c`): width, ones, left-end extensions at 1-2-5 steps.

| steps | width | ones | width / √steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10^6 | 2,377 | 1,188 | 2.38 |
| 10^7 | 7,474 | 3,732 | 2.36 |
| 10^8 | 25,548 | 12,775 | 2.55 |
| 10^9 | 76,681 | 38,339 | 2.42 |
| 10^10 | 258,671 | 129,335 | 2.59 |

So time is quadratic in the width (a sweeping machine), and half the cells are 1.

Transitions used in the first 10^9 steps: B0, B1, C1, D0 about 1.25·10^8 each, C0 and E1 about 2.5·10^8; the rare
ones are A0 (23,854), A1 (92,695), D1 (14,372), E0 (116,548), F0 (107,067). F1 never.

### Invariants and closed languages (computed)

* `bbtools.invariants`: no conserved a·ones + b·pos + W[state] (mod m) for m = 2..12.
* `bbtools.ctl` with windows of K = 2..7 cells: always closed, always allows a halt (K = 5, 6, 7: 3,285, 10,865,
  37,117 abstract configurations). The run's protection is the pair alignment below, which a window of raw cells
  does not see. A closed language over the words `10`, `110`, `0` (or windows that remember the cell's parity) is the
  natural next try.

### The tape: pairs, blocks, marks

Reading the tape with the words a = `10`, b = `110` (`python3 py/blank.py left`, or `tools/c/tokrun MACHINE N
a=10,b=110 L K`), every moment the head extends the tape at the left looks like

```
0^2 a^n1 0 a^n2 0 ... 0 a^nk 1          (D or B at the left end)
```

or the same with an occasional b right after a block (`a^10759 b a^3`) or a `0^2` boundary. The first ones:

```
32    0^2 a^3 0 a 1
275   0^2 a 0 a^7 0 a^8 1
1681  0^2 a^13 0 a^13 0 a^18 1
2184  0^2 a^3 0 a^12 0 a^15 0 a^22 1
5757  0^2 a 0 a^7 0 a^9 0 a^25 0 a^42 1
277787  0^2 a^257 0 a^123 0 a^192 1
```

The sweeps (kernel, `lean/Rules.lean`): E runs right over pairs shifting each by one cell, `E>(10)^n → (01)^n E>`
(`e_run`); at a boundary it marks it, `E>0 1 0 → 0 1 1 E>` (`e_mark`); B runs left over pairs, two at a time,
`1 (0101)^n B>0 → 1 B>0 (0101)^n` (`b_run`), through D in between, so the phase in which a left sweep reaches a
boundary depends on the parity of the block. The halting shape `E>0 1 1` is a mark that E meets **in step with its
own pairs**.

`py/blank.py turns FROM TO` prints the tape at every turn of the head. A typical stretch (steps 1681 to 2190): the
head sweeps right in E from the left end, marking every boundary (`a^3 b a^12 b a^12 b a^18 [C] 1^2`, read in the
shifted frame); excursions from the right end run left to a boundary, turn there (`0 [F] 0`), and return, the right
end growing by a pair or two each time; when the excursions reach the left end the next sweep starts. Positions of
the turns (`bbtools.explore turns`) show inner loops: between steps 277,787 and 1,942,279 the left turns come in
groups `D p`, `D q+4k`, `C p-1` with p fixed and q moving by 4 per group, so a block is counted up in unary while a
boundary near the left stays put.

At the 207 left-end moments up to 10^10 steps (`runs/left_1e10.txt`) the word b occurs 67 times, **always right after
a block of pairs, never right after a boundary 0**. After a pair, b is out of step with E (E arrives on its first 1,
`E1 → 0RC`, `C1 → 0LD`, and turns); after a boundary it would be the halting shape. (Heuristic: only the left-end
moments were looked at.)

### Walls and replays (heuristic)

`python3 py/replay.py` (on `runs/left_1e10.txt`): when a left-end moment reads `0^2 a^W 0 ...` with W ≥ 50 (a *wall*),
the following moments repeat the moments of the run from blank tape: moment i of the blank run is `0^2 P a^x 1`, and
the i-th moment after the wall starts `0^2 P a^y 0 ...`, the blank run's last block replaced by the wall. The wall
shrinks by the same amounts every time, whatever W and whatever lies beyond it:

```
step 277787:      wall 257:   256 253 251 247 245 235 229 219 213 211 205 193 187 173 167 163 161 157 155 145 139 129 123 93 75 45 27 21 5  (29 moments)
step 63654325:    wall 1547:  1546 1543 1541 1537 1535 1525 1519 1509 1503 1501 1495 1483 1477 1463 1457 1453 1451 1447 1445 1435 1429 1419 1413 1383  (24)
step 3999846159:  wall 43801: 43800 43797 43795 43791 43789  (5)
step 8246041679:  wall 99:    98 95 93 89 87 77 71 61 55 53 47  (11)
```

The decrements are 1, 3, 2, 4, 2, 10, 6, 10, 6, 2, 6, 12, 6, 14, 6, 4, 2, 4, 2, 10, 6, 10, 6, 30, 18, 30, 18, 6, 16.
What the wall has lost after moment i is about half the blank run's last block at moment i, plus one (last block 8,
lost 4; 12, 6; 18, 10; 22, 12; 42, 22): the wall gives up the room that the blank run's growing right end takes. The wall is not
literally blank tape to the machine (a run from `D>0 0 (10)^257` follows the blank run's head for only 17 steps); it
is the left-end configurations that agree. The replay counts are lower bounds: a left-end moment is taken where the
head extends the left end after extending the right end, and during a replay the left process grows into the wall
without extending the right end, so moments can merge.

Walls nest: at step 1.78·10^9 the tape starts `0^2 a^1768 0 a^6 0 a^10762 ...`, and at 7.0·10^9
`0^2 a^16920 0 a^698 0 a^6 0 a^5 b a^18873 ...`. The run looks like a recursive counter: the left end restarts the
blank run in front of each new wall and eats the wall at the blank run's pace; what happens when a wall is used up
(as at step 7.67·10^6, where a block that had been shrinking by these same decrements reaches 0, and the next
left-end moment starts with a wall of 1,752) is where the structure changes.

### Leads

1. **One excursion as a map.** Prove, from `e_run`, `e_mark`, `b_run` and the boundary cases (B or D arriving at a
   boundary or a mark, by parity), the effect of one excursion from the right end on the block list, with
   `bbtools.symtm.prove`. Then one sweep from the left end, then one left-end cycle.
2. **The replay.** State and prove: from `0^2 P a^W 0 S` with W large, the left-end configurations follow those of
   the blank run, the wall losing d_i. This would make the run a recursion on walls.
3. **Wall exhaustion.** Catalogue what happens when a wall runs out (finitely many cases if the blank run's state at
   that moment is determined by W modulo something), and look there for a mark after a boundary.
4. **A language over words.** A closed tape language over `10`, `110`, `0` that keeps the pair alignment might rule
   out `0 11` in front of E, or show where it can arise.
5. **Search.** Once the excursion map exists, a parts search over block lists (`tools/c/parts.h`) can look for lists
   that halt; until then a raw search has nothing to enumerate.

## 2 November 2026 (later): the right end is a base-3 counter

### A fast simulator

`tools/py/bbtools/macro.py` is a macro machine: the tape in blocks of k cells, run-length coded, the head between
blocks; a block transition is cached, and when the head leaves a block on the far side in the state it came in with,
the whole run of equal blocks is crossed at once. With k = 4 this machine's sweeps cross whole blocks of pairs:
10⁶ steps take 9,792 macro steps, 10¹⁰ take 2.2·10⁶ and 10¹² take 2.2·10⁷ (23 s in Python), with 15 to 35 runs on
the tape. It is exact (tested cell for cell against `bbtools.machine.Run`, and on the BB(2) to BB(5) champions).

### The levels

At each return of the head to the right end (C reading the `0` of `... 0 10 0^inf`), read the tape as blocks of pairs
and single cells (`py/counter.py levels`). At step 10¹⁰:

```
92 0 6 0 4 0 8 1 37 1 37 1 211 1 16721 0 1326 0 18236 0 1948 0 41212 0 8254 0 41236 0 1
```

Read from the right, the tape is a list of levels `s (10)^g s' (10)^W`; here (211, 16721) with seps `1 1`, then
(1326, 18236), (1948, 41212), (8254, 41236) with seps `0 0`. The next return borrows through the three digit-0
levels and turns at (211, 16721); from then on the seven seps right of that block run through

```
1111111 → 1111101 → 1111100 → 1110111 → 1110101 → 1110100 → 1110011 → 1110010 → 1110000 → 1101111 → ...
```

which is a base-3 count down with digit 2 = `1 1`, 1 = `0 1`, 0 = `0 0` per level. The lengths follow: when a
level's digit goes 2 → 1 or 0 → 2 one pair moves from its W to its g (the last level's W gains instead), and 1 → 0
changes no length (the last level's W gains 2). Every trip adds two pairs. The parities ride along: digit 2 has g and W
odd, digits 1 and 0 have them even.

### Proved (kernel, `lean/Counter.lean`)

The symbolic runs of `symtm.py` split one trip into eight pieces (`lean/Rules.lean`): `first0` and `walk0`, the walk
over a level with digit 0 (it leaves the level in a transit form `01 (0101)^c 0100 (1010)^(d+1) 1001` on the right of
the head and eats two pairs of the block before); `turn2`, `turn1`, the turn at a digit 2 or 1; `sweep0`, the reset of
a transit level on the way back; `sweepLast`; and `last2`, `last1`, the whole trip when the last digit is not 0. None
of them looks at the tape left of the turning level. `Counter.lean` chains them by induction on the number of borrows
(`walkN`, `sweepN`) and proves the tape equalities that bring the result back to the level form (`rep_rotate` and
friends, about 30 lines). The four theorems:

```
counter_last2   lv 1 1 (2a+1) (2b+1) L           →  lv 0 1 (2a+2) (2b+2) L
counter_last1   lv 0 1 (2a+2) (2b+2) L           →  lv 0 0 (2a+2) (2b+4) L
counter_borrow2 lv 0 0 (2c+2) (2d+2) (Zs zs (lv 1 1 (2a+1) (2b+3) L))
                                                 →  lv 1 1 (2c+3) (2d+3) (Rs zs (lv 0 1 (2a+2) (2b+2) L))
counter_borrow1 lv 0 0 (2c+2) (2d+2) (Zs zs (lv 0 1 (2a+2) (2b+2) L))
                                                 →  lv 1 1 (2c+3) (2d+3) (Rs zs (lv 0 0 (2a+2) (2b+2) L))
```

each from `⟨_, 0 1 0^inf, C⟩` to `⟨_, 0 1 0^inf, C⟩`, where `lv s s' g W L` is a level in front of the tape L, `Zs`
a list of digit-0 levels (g = 2c+2, W = 2d+4) and `Rs` the same levels reset (2c+3, 2d+3). Kernel only.

### Checked against the run (computed)

`python3 py/counter.py check 1e11`: 151,240 trips; 150,680 covered by a theorem, all of them ending as the theorem
says (comparing tapes, since two token lists can spell the same tape: `100 1 0 20` is `121`); 321 whose borrow runs
past the levels into the irregular left end (`left`); 239 that meet a level too short or of the wrong parity
(`short`). All 560 change the tape only in its first 24 tokens (the leftmost six levels'
worth), out of about 30 to 45. So everything the counter does is proved, and
what is left is its top: the left end, where the walls of the first entry live.

### Leads

1. **The top of the counter.** The `left` and `short` trips as maps on the block lengths: the left end is a short
   irregular prefix (`92 0 6 0 4 0 8 1 37 1 37` at 10¹⁰) followed by levels; a borrow into it should be one cycle of
   the blank-run replay of the first entry. `py/counter.py events` lists them.
2. **The replay, restated.** In front of a wall the left end repeats the blank run; with the counter, the blank run
   itself is the counter started from nothing, so the replay may be the statement that the counter below a wall runs
   as if the wall were the tape's end.
3. **A level running out.** A level's W loses one pair for every two of its digit changes, and the level to its right
   changes three times as often, so a level with a small W runs out. Catalogue what the run does then (`short`).
4. **Halting.** A halt needs `E>0 1 1`. The counter trips never halt (proved); a halt has to come from the top.

## 3 November 2026: an accelerated run, and what the top does to a wall

### Counter trips in closed form (`py/accel.py`)

The steps of one counter trip are a linear function of the lengths it touches, read off the pieces in `Rules.lean`:
last level digit 2: 4g+4W+18; digit 1: 4W+16; a borrow costs 8g+4W+23 at the last level and 8g+4W+8 at each other
digit-0 level, plus 4g+4W+3 (digit 2) or 4W+1 (digit 1) at the turn. This matches all 14,129 counter trips of the
first 10⁹ steps. Level p is touched by the trips whose counter value is a multiple of 3^p, its digit cycles 2, 1, 0,
and its lengths change by one pair at the 2s and 0s, so n trips of the counter can be applied at once, with their
exact step count, as long as no level runs short. `py/accel.py` does that and runs the other trips (the top) on the
macro simulator, built from the token list with long blocks still run-length coded. Every state it passes through
is a return of the plain macro run at the same step (checked to 10¹¹; `tests/test_machine.py` checks 10⁹).

Result (trusted Python): **no halt in the first 1.05·10¹⁵ steps** (1.6·10⁷ trips, 1,741 at the top), in five
minutes. The tape then has 53 tokens.

### The slow part: a wall drains into the left end

Most of those minutes go into a few top trips that last up to 10¹² steps without the head coming back to the right
end. Inside one (from step 1.42·10¹³, tape `92 0 6 0 4 0 8 1 37 1 1485 0 220 ... 107543 1 1 0 339982 0 ...`), after a
short rearrangement the head shuttles between the left end and the wall of 339,982 pairs; all levels in between have
seps `1 1` and do not change. Each round trip moves two pairs from the wall to the leftmost block (`94 1 8 ...`,
`96 1 8 ...`, `98 1 8 ...`), and takes 3,658,888, 3,658,920, 3,658,952, ... steps (32 more each time). That goes on
for about 170,000 round trips, until the wall is used up. This is the replay of the first entry seen from the other
side: the wall is spent at a fixed rate while the left end grows. A cycle accelerator (proved with `symtm.py`, then
applied k times at once) would make these phases instant; that is being built in the shared tools.
