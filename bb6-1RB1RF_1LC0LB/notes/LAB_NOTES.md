# Lab notes: 1RB1RF_1LC0LB_1RE0LD_1LB0RF_0RA0RC_1RE---

```
      0    1
A   1RB  1RF
B   1LC  0LB
C   1RE  0LD
D   1LB  0RF
E   0RA  0RC
F   1RE  ---
```

The machine halts only on F1. Trust levels as everywhere in the repository: **kernel** (Lean, kernel only),
**native** (Lean with `native_decide`), **computed** (C or Python here, tested, not proved), **heuristic** (patterns).

## 2 November 2026: setup and a first look

### Setup

The folder follows `tools/README.md` ("Adding a machine"): `lean/Machine.lean` generated by `bbtools.leangen`
(namespace `BB6e`), `lean/Blank.lean`, `lean/Rules.lean` (generated by `lean/rules.py`), `lean/Axioms.lean`,

because they are not about this machine: a word view of the tape (`bbtools.explore tokens`, and `tools/c/tokrun.c`
for billions of steps), a trace of the head's turns (`bbtools.explore turns`), and the status panel of an explainer

### The halting shape (kernel, `lean/Blank.lean`)

F is entered from A1 (`1RF`) and D1 (`0RF`) and reads the cell to their right. D is entered only by `C1 → 0LD`, a
left move that writes a 0 on the cell that is then D's right neighbour, so D never has a 1 to its right
(`d_right_zero`) and the D route never halts. A is entered only by `E0 → 0RA`. Hence `halt_from_e011`: in the run
from blank tape every halt comes two ordinary steps after a configuration `E>0 1 1`.

### Bounded runs

* kernel: 20,000 steps (`blank_runs_20000`, 20 s for the whole folder); native: 10^7 (`blank_runs_10M`).
* computed: no halt in 10^10 steps (`tools/c/tmrun.c`, 54 s; about 5 ns a step).

`runs/growth_1e10.txt` (`c/growth.c`): width, ones, left-end extensions at 1-2-5 steps.

| steps | width | ones | width / √steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10^6 | 2,377 | 1,188 | 2.38 |
| 10^7 | 7,474 | 3,732 | 2.36 |
| 10^8 | 25,548 | 12,775 | 2.55 |
| 10^9 | 76,681 | 38,339 | 2.42 |
| 10^10 | 258,671 | 129,335 | 2.59 |

So time is quadratic in the width (a sweeping machine), and half the cells are 1.

Transitions used in the first 10^9 steps: B0, B1, C1, D0 about 1.25·10^8 each, C0 and E1 about 2.5·10^8; the rare
ones are A0 (23,854), A1 (92,695), D1 (14,372), E0 (116,548), F0 (107,067). F1 never.

### Invariants and closed languages (computed)

* `bbtools.invariants`: no conserved a·ones + b·pos + W[state] (mod m) for m = 2..12.
* `bbtools.ctl` with windows of K = 2..7 cells: always closed, always allows a halt (K = 5, 6, 7: 3,285, 10,865,
  37,117 abstract configurations). The run's protection is the pair alignment below, which a window of raw cells
  does not see. A closed language over the words `10`, `110`, `0` (or windows that remember the cell's parity) is the
  natural next try.

### The tape: pairs, blocks, marks

Reading the tape with the words a = `10`, b = `110` (`python3 py/blank.py left`, or `tools/c/tokrun MACHINE N
a=10,b=110 L K`), every moment the head extends the tape at the left looks like

```
0^2 a^n1 0 a^n2 0 ... 0 a^nk 1          (D or B at the left end)
```

or the same with an occasional b right after a block (`a^10759 b a^3`) or a `0^2` boundary. The first ones:

```
32    0^2 a^3 0 a 1
275   0^2 a 0 a^7 0 a^8 1
1681  0^2 a^13 0 a^13 0 a^18 1
2184  0^2 a^3 0 a^12 0 a^15 0 a^22 1
5757  0^2 a 0 a^7 0 a^9 0 a^25 0 a^42 1
277787  0^2 a^257 0 a^123 0 a^192 1
```

The sweeps (kernel, `lean/Rules.lean`): E runs right over pairs shifting each by one cell, `E>(10)^n → (01)^n E>`
(`e_run`); at a boundary it marks it, `E>0 1 0 → 0 1 1 E>` (`e_mark`); B runs left over pairs, two at a time,
`1 (0101)^n B>0 → 1 B>0 (0101)^n` (`b_run`), through D in between, so the phase in which a left sweep reaches a
boundary depends on the parity of the block. The halting shape `E>0 1 1` is a mark that E meets **in step with its
own pairs**.

`py/blank.py turns FROM TO` prints the tape at every turn of the head. A typical stretch (steps 1681 to 2190): the
head sweeps right in E from the left end, marking every boundary (`a^3 b a^12 b a^12 b a^18 [C] 1^2`, read in the
shifted frame); excursions from the right end run left to a boundary, turn there (`0 [F] 0`), and return, the right
end growing by a pair or two each time; when the excursions reach the left end the next sweep starts. Positions of
the turns (`bbtools.explore turns`) show inner loops: between steps 277,787 and 1,942,279 the left turns come in
groups `D p`, `D q+4k`, `C p-1` with p fixed and q moving by 4 per group, so a block is counted up in unary while a
boundary near the left stays put.

At the 207 left-end moments up to 10^10 steps (`runs/left_1e10.txt`) the word b occurs 67 times, **always right after
a block of pairs, never right after a boundary 0**. After a pair, b is out of step with E (E arrives on its first 1,
`E1 → 0RC`, `C1 → 0LD`, and turns); after a boundary it would be the halting shape. (Heuristic: only the left-end
moments were looked at.)

### Walls and replays (heuristic)

`python3 py/replay.py` (on `runs/left_1e10.txt`): when a left-end moment reads `0^2 a^W 0 ...` with W ≥ 50 (a *wall*),
the following moments repeat the moments of the run from blank tape: moment i of the blank run is `0^2 P a^x 1`, and
the i-th moment after the wall starts `0^2 P a^y 0 ...`, the blank run's last block replaced by the wall. The wall
shrinks by the same amounts every time, whatever W and whatever lies beyond it:

```
step 277787:      wall 257:   256 253 251 247 245 235 229 219 213 211 205 193 187 173 167 163 161 157 155 145 139 129 123 93 75 45 27 21 5  (29 moments)
step 63654325:    wall 1547:  1546 1543 1541 1537 1535 1525 1519 1509 1503 1501 1495 1483 1477 1463 1457 1453 1451 1447 1445 1435 1429 1419 1413 1383  (24)
step 3999846159:  wall 43801: 43800 43797 43795 43791 43789  (5)
step 8246041679:  wall 99:    98 95 93 89 87 77 71 61 55 53 47  (11)
```

The decrements are 1, 3, 2, 4, 2, 10, 6, 10, 6, 2, 6, 12, 6, 14, 6, 4, 2, 4, 2, 10, 6, 10, 6, 30, 18, 30, 18, 6, 16.
What the wall has lost after moment i is about half the blank run's last block at moment i, plus one (last block 8,
lost 4; 12, 6; 18, 10; 22, 12; 42, 22): the wall gives up the room that the blank run's growing right end takes. The wall is not
literally blank tape to the machine (a run from `D>0 0 (10)^257` follows the blank run's head for only 17 steps); it
is the left-end configurations that agree. The replay counts are lower bounds: a left-end moment is taken where the
head extends the left end after extending the right end, and during a replay the left process grows into the wall
without extending the right end, so moments can merge.

Walls nest: at step 1.78·10^9 the tape starts `0^2 a^1768 0 a^6 0 a^10762 ...`, and at 7.0·10^9
`0^2 a^16920 0 a^698 0 a^6 0 a^5 b a^18873 ...`. The run looks like a recursive counter: the left end restarts the
blank run in front of each new wall and eats the wall at the blank run's pace; what happens when a wall is used up
(as at step 7.67·10^6, where a block that had been shrinking by these same decrements reaches 0, and the next
left-end moment starts with a wall of 1,752) is where the structure changes.

### Leads

1. **One excursion as a map.** Prove, from `e_run`, `e_mark`, `b_run` and the boundary cases (B or D arriving at a
   boundary or a mark, by parity), the effect of one excursion from the right end on the block list, with
   `bbtools.symtm.prove`. Then one sweep from the left end, then one left-end cycle.
2. **The replay.** State and prove: from `0^2 P a^W 0 S` with W large, the left-end configurations follow those of
   the blank run, the wall losing d_i. This would make the run a recursion on walls.
3. **Wall exhaustion.** Catalogue what happens when a wall runs out (finitely many cases if the blank run's state at
   that moment is determined by W modulo something), and look there for a mark after a boundary.
4. **A language over words.** A closed tape language over `10`, `110`, `0` that keeps the pair alignment might rule
   out `0 11` in front of E, or show where it can arise.
5. **Search.** Once the excursion map exists, a parts search over block lists (`tools/c/parts.h`) can look for lists
   that halt; until then a raw search has nothing to enumerate.

## 2 November 2026 (later): the right end is a base-3 counter

### A fast simulator

`tools/py/bbtools/macro.py` is a macro machine: the tape in blocks of k cells, run-length coded, the head between
blocks; a block transition is cached, and when the head leaves a block on the far side in the state it came in with,
the whole run of equal blocks is crossed at once. With k = 4 this machine's sweeps cross whole blocks of pairs:
10⁶ steps take 9,792 macro steps, 10¹⁰ take 2.2·10⁶ and 10¹² take 2.2·10⁷ (23 s in Python), with 15 to 35 runs on
the tape. It is exact (tested cell for cell against `bbtools.machine.Run`, and on the BB(2) to BB(5) champions).

### The levels

At each return of the head to the right end (C reading the `0` of `... 0 10 0^inf`), read the tape as blocks of pairs
and single cells (`py/counter.py levels`). At step 10¹⁰:

```
92 0 6 0 4 0 8 1 37 1 37 1 211 1 16721 0 1326 0 18236 0 1948 0 41212 0 8254 0 41236 0 1
```

Read from the right, the tape is a list of levels `s (10)^g s' (10)^W`; here (211, 16721) with seps `1 1`, then
(1326, 18236), (1948, 41212), (8254, 41236) with seps `0 0`. The next return borrows through the three digit-0
levels and turns at (211, 16721); from then on the seven seps right of that block run through

```
1111111 → 1111101 → 1111100 → 1110111 → 1110101 → 1110100 → 1110011 → 1110010 → 1110000 → 1101111 → ...
```

which is a base-3 count down with digit 2 = `1 1`, 1 = `0 1`, 0 = `0 0` per level. The lengths follow: when a
level's digit goes 2 → 1 or 0 → 2 one pair moves from its W to its g (the last level's W gains instead), and 1 → 0
changes no length (the last level's W gains 2). Every trip adds two pairs. The parities ride along: digit 2 has g and W
odd, digits 1 and 0 have them even.

### Proved (kernel, `lean/Counter.lean`)

The symbolic runs of `symtm.py` split one trip into eight pieces (`lean/Rules.lean`): `first0` and `walk0`, the walk
over a level with digit 0 (it leaves the level in a transit form `01 (0101)^c 0100 (1010)^(d+1) 1001` on the right of
the head and eats two pairs of the block before); `turn2`, `turn1`, the turn at a digit 2 or 1; `sweep0`, the reset of
a transit level on the way back; `sweepLast`; and `last2`, `last1`, the whole trip when the last digit is not 0. None
of them looks at the tape left of the turning level. `Counter.lean` chains them by induction on the number of borrows
(`walkN`, `sweepN`) and proves the tape equalities that bring the result back to the level form (`rep_rotate` and
friends, about 30 lines). The four theorems:

```
counter_last2   lv 1 1 (2a+1) (2b+1) L           →  lv 0 1 (2a+2) (2b+2) L
counter_last1   lv 0 1 (2a+2) (2b+2) L           →  lv 0 0 (2a+2) (2b+4) L
counter_borrow2 lv 0 0 (2c+2) (2d+2) (Zs zs (lv 1 1 (2a+1) (2b+3) L))
                                                 →  lv 1 1 (2c+3) (2d+3) (Rs zs (lv 0 1 (2a+2) (2b+2) L))
counter_borrow1 lv 0 0 (2c+2) (2d+2) (Zs zs (lv 0 1 (2a+2) (2b+2) L))
                                                 →  lv 1 1 (2c+3) (2d+3) (Rs zs (lv 0 0 (2a+2) (2b+2) L))
```

each from `⟨_, 0 1 0^inf, C⟩` to `⟨_, 0 1 0^inf, C⟩`, where `lv s s' g W L` is a level in front of the tape L, `Zs`
a list of digit-0 levels (g = 2c+2, W = 2d+4) and `Rs` the same levels reset (2c+3, 2d+3). Kernel only.

### Checked against the run (computed)

`python3 py/counter.py check 1e11`: 151,240 trips; 150,680 covered by a theorem, all of them ending as the theorem
says (comparing tapes, since two token lists can spell the same tape: `100 1 0 20` is `121`); 321 whose borrow runs
past the levels into the irregular left end (`left`); 239 that meet a level too short or of the wrong parity
(`short`). All 560 change the tape only in its first 24 tokens (the leftmost six levels'
worth), out of about 30 to 45. So everything the counter does is proved, and
what is left is its top: the left end, where the walls of the first entry live.

### Leads

1. **The top of the counter.** The `left` and `short` trips as maps on the block lengths: the left end is a short
   irregular prefix (`92 0 6 0 4 0 8 1 37 1 37` at 10¹⁰) followed by levels; a borrow into it should be one cycle of
   the blank-run replay of the first entry. `py/counter.py events` lists them.
2. **The replay, restated.** In front of a wall the left end repeats the blank run; with the counter, the blank run
   itself is the counter started from nothing, so the replay may be the statement that the counter below a wall runs
   as if the wall were the tape's end.
3. **A level running out.** A level's W loses one pair for every two of its digit changes, and the level to its right
   changes three times as often, so a level with a small W runs out. Catalogue what the run does then (`short`).
4. **Halting.** A halt needs `E>0 1 1`. The counter trips never halt (proved); a halt has to come from the top.

## 3 November 2026: the top of the counter

### Cutting a top trip (`py/top.py`)

A trip that `counter.predict` does not cover (`left` or `short`) is cut into three parts: the counter's walk over
the last level and the digit-0 levels before it, as long as each is standard (seps `0 0`, even lengths, W at least
2 or 4) and has two pairs in the block to its left (`first0`, `walk0`); then a *top piece*, from the moment the
walk arrives on the tape T left of those levels, in the walk's phase `T C> 1 1 0 0 1 0 1 R`, to the moment the
head leaves T to the right in the sweep's phase `T' 0 1 1 0 C> R`; then the counter's sweep back over the walked
levels (`sweep0`, `sweepLast`). If the last level itself is not standard, the piece is the whole trip, from
`T C> 0 1 0^inf` back to the same. The piece is run with `bbtools.symtm` on the shortest stretch of T it needs (the
rest of the tape a context L), or on all of T down to blank tape. Then each block of the piece is made symbolic,
longest first, as `(10)^a (1010)^x (10)^b` with the fewest literal pairs a + b that still let the symbolic run close;
blocks for which no such form closes stay concrete. The result is a *shape*: a map on block lengths valid for all
values of its variables. `py/top.py events STEPS` prints every trip with its shape and values.

To 10¹¹ steps the 560 top trips go through 43 shapes (`lean/top_shapes.txt`), except 47 trips (below). A few:

```
turn  L 1 2x                          ->  L 0 2x+2                          a level of the wrong parity: its sep goes to 0
turn  0^inf 2x+1 1 2y+1 0 2z+2        ->  0^inf 2x 1 2y+3 1 2z+3           the walk reaches the first level
turn  0^inf 2x+2                      ->  0^inf 3 1 2x+3                    a new level at the left end
turn  0^inf                           ->  0^inf 3 1 1                       (the walk used up the first block)
turn  0^inf 2x 1 2y+2 1 2z+4 0 2w+2   ->  0^inf 2x+4 1 2y+2 1 2z+3 1 2w+3   the wall grows by 4 (also with 0, 3, 4,
                                                                            5, 6, 8 blocks `1 2y+2`)
last  0^inf 2x+3 1 1 0 8 0 2y+2       ->  0^inf 15 1 2x+9 1 2y+2           one of the fixed steps of the replay
```

(`turn`: after a walk, the walked levels then reset as in the counter; `last`: the whole trip. Lengths are in pairs;
`0^inf` is the left end of the tape.) Several shapes are one rule at different depths (the wall grows by 4 across 0, 1, 3,
4, 5, 6 or 8 levels `1 (10)^(2y+2)`), which an induction would merge.

### Proved (kernel, `lean/Top.lean`, `lean/Counter.lean`)

`counter_borrow_any` (Counter.lean): for any tape T and any piece `h` that takes `T C> 1 1 0 0 1 0 1 R` to
`F3 X C> R` for every R (X starting with a 0), the borrow through the last level and any list `zs` of digit-0
levels in front of T is a trip from the right end and back, ending with the levels reset exactly as in
`counter_borrow2`. It is `counter_borrow2` with the turn made a parameter.

`Top.lean`, generated by `lean/top_gen.py` from `top_shapes.txt`: for each shape i, `top_piece_i` is the symbolic run
as `symtm.prove` writes it (cells and repeats of `1010`, `0101`), and `top_i` is the whole trip with the tape written
as blocks (`rep n P` and seps, as `lv` does): for a `turn` shape

    ∃ n, steps n ⟨lv false false (2c+2) (2d+2) (Zs zs START), R0, .C⟩ = some ⟨lv true true (2c+3) (2d+3) (Rs zs END), R0, .C⟩

for all values of the shape's variables, all c, d, zs and (where the shape has one) all tapes L; for a `last` shape
the same without the levels. The bridge from the raw cells to blocks is one `simp only` with the rewriting lemmas
of Counter.lean (`rq`, `pq`, `rotf`, `rep_add`, `rep_succ`). One change to what `symtm.prove` writes: the step count
is stated as the sum of the segments instead of a closed form checked by `omega`, which took minutes on the longer
runs (one took 386 s); the theorems only use that some n exists. The file is 1.8 MB (the longest pieces have about
700 segments) and checks in about 90 s. Kernel only (`Axioms.lean`).

So every trip through these shapes is a theorem, for every length and any number of borrows, and none of them halts.

### Checked against the run (computed)

`python3 py/top.py check 1e11` (2 minutes): of the 560 top trips, 513 go through a shape of `top_shapes.txt` and end
as it says (comparing tapes), 0 differ, and 47 are `long` (no piece closes within 2,000 symbolic moves).
`tests/test_machine.py` checks this to 10⁹ steps (235 and 20) and runs every shape, with random lengths, a random tape
L and a random borrow, on the reference simulator `bbtools.machine.Run`, independently of symtm.

A first try counted 233 long trips: concrete blocks were written `(10)^n`, which the sweeps, crossing two pairs at a
time, cannot cross as a whole, so the simulator stepped through them. Writing long concrete blocks as `(1010)^m`
and letting the literal pairs of a symbolic block sit on both sides of it (`10 (1010)^x 10`) brought it to 47.

### The 47 long trips: walls (heuristic)

Every long trip begins the same way: the walk arrives at a level `0 (10)^X 0 (10)^Y` with a single pair to its left
(`... 1 (10) 0 (10)^X 0 ...`), so it cannot take its two pairs and goes on into the prefix. Then the head shuttles
between the left end and X: each round moves one pair out of X and adds two to a block at the left end
(at step 38,720: the left block runs 2, 4, 6, ..., 86 while X runs 45, 44, ..., 0, one round every 200 to 500 steps).
In 42 of the 47 the trip ends with a new first block of 2X − c pairs, c from 1 to 26 (48 → 88, 130 → 259, 884 →
1752, 5396 → 10766, 10786 → 21564); in the other 5 the doubled block joins a long block already there (11118 → 43798 =
21564 + 2·11118 − 2). These are the walls of the first entry: a wall is made by doubling a level that has run down
to a single pair on its left. Because the number of rounds is X, no single symbolic run covers them: they need an
induction on the rounds, with the tape between the left end and X (fixed during the loop) as a parameter.

### Leads

1. **The doubling loop.** State one round (left block 2i → 2i+2, X → X−1, the blocks between unchanged) as a
   symtm piece with the in-between blocks symbolic, prove it, and iterate by induction in Lean; with an entry and an
   exit piece this would cover the 47 long trips.
2. **Merge the families.** The shapes that differ only in how many `1 (10)^(2y+2)` levels they cross (wall grows by
   4) are one theorem by induction on that list, as `walkN` is for digit-0 levels. Likewise the replay steps
   (`3 1 13 → 3 1 3 1 12`, ...) are the same few shapes at every wall size.
3. **The top as a recursion.** With the shapes and the doubling, the left end is a finite set of maps on the first
   few block lengths plus the counter below; following the run as a sequence of these maps (rather than of steps)
   is the next simulator, and the place to look for a mark right after a boundary.

## 3 November 2026: an accelerated run, and what the top does to a wall

### Counter trips in closed form (`py/accel.py`)

The steps of one counter trip are a linear function of the lengths it touches, read off the pieces in `Rules.lean`:
last level digit 2: 4g+4W+18; digit 1: 4W+16; a borrow costs 8g+4W+23 at the last level and 8g+4W+8 at each other
digit-0 level, plus 4g+4W+3 (digit 2) or 4W+1 (digit 1) at the turn. This matches all 14,129 counter trips of the
first 10⁹ steps. Level p is touched by the trips whose counter value is a multiple of 3^p, its digit cycles 2, 1, 0,
and its lengths change by one pair at the 2s and 0s, so n trips of the counter can be applied at once, with their
exact step count, as long as no level runs short. `py/accel.py` does that and runs the other trips (the top) on the
macro simulator, built from the token list with long blocks still run-length coded. Every state it passes through
is a return of the plain macro run at the same step (checked to 10¹¹; `tests/test_machine.py` checks 10⁹).

Result (trusted Python): **no halt in the first 1.05·10¹⁵ steps** (1.6·10⁷ trips, 1,741 at the top), in five
minutes. The tape then has 53 tokens.

### The slow part: a wall drains into the left end

Most of those minutes go into a few top trips that last up to 10¹² steps without the head coming back to the right
end. Inside one (from step 1.42·10¹³, tape `92 0 6 0 4 0 8 1 37 1 1485 0 220 ... 107543 1 1 0 339982 0 ...`), after a
short rearrangement the head shuttles between the left end and the wall of 339,982 pairs; all levels in between have
seps `1 1` and do not change. Each round trip moves two pairs from the wall to the leftmost block (`94 1 8 ...`,
`96 1 8 ...`, `98 1 8 ...`), and takes 3,658,888, 3,658,920, 3,658,952, ... steps (32 more each time). That goes on
for about 170,000 round trips, until the wall is used up. This is the replay of the first entry seen from the other
side: the wall is spent at a fixed rate while the left end grows. A cycle accelerator (proved with `symtm.py`, then
applied k times at once) would make these phases instant; that is being built in the shared tools.

### Cycle jumps inside the top trips

`tools/py/bbtools/cycles.py` (PR #183) finds such cycles in a macro run (samples where the head faces blank tape,
same words, counts moving by a constant amount), proves the one-cycle rule symbolically with `symtm.py` for all
values of the counts, and applies it k times at once with the exact step count k·S + D·k(k−1)/2. `accel.py` now runs
its top trips through it (`concrete_trip`), keeping the proved rules from trip to trip. The returns it passes through
are the same as without jumps, at the same steps: all 3,697 returns to 10¹⁴ (`tests/test_machine.py` checks 10¹¹).
To 10¹⁴ the run takes 47 s instead of 259 s; 255 rules, 121 jumps.

Result (trusted Python): no halt in the first 10²³ steps (1.2·10¹¹ trips, about 10,000 of them at the top) in 17
minutes, and in the first 1.48·10³³ steps in two hours:

## 5 November 2026: the wall-building trips, proved

### One round of the copy loop (`lean/Wall.lean`, kernel)

At the start of each round of the loop that builds a wall the head is in C at the left end, blank tape to its left, and
to its right

    0 (10)^(2a+1)  0 (10)^(2t1+2) ... 0 (10)^(2tm+2)  0 0 (10)^X  R              (`Rd a ts X R`)

(the first block, the blocks between, the level being copied; all seps between the blocks are 0 at this moment, and
the blocks between are even). A round is five symbolic pieces, now in `Rules.lean` (written by `rules.py`):
`wall_start` (C crosses the first block in E), `wall_right` (E crosses one block between and marks its boundary),
`wall_turn` (at `0 0 (10)^(x+1)` the head takes one pair and turns in D), `wall_left` (D/B cross a marked block back
and clear the mark) and `wall_end` (back at the left end, the first block two pairs longer). Each is proved for all
values and any tape around it. `wall_rightN` and `wall_leftN` cross any list of blocks by induction on the list,
and

* `wall_round`: `Rd a ts (x+1) R → Rd (a+1) ts x R`, for every a, ts, x, R;
* `wall_loop`: k rounds, by induction on k; `wall_jump`: `Rd a ts X R → Rd (a+X) ts 0 R`.

So the loop is one theorem for any length of the level, any first block and any tape between and beyond; that is
the induction the 47 long trips needed.

### The long trips as shapes (`py/wall.py`, `lean/top_gen.py`)

`wall.run` is `symtm.run` with one more move: when the configuration is `blank C> Rd a ts X R` with X symbolic (or
at least 4), it jumps to `Rd (a+X) ts 0 R`. `top.py` uses it for a trip only when the plain symbolic run does not
close, so the 43 earlier shapes are unchanged. Two mistakes on the way, both caught by comparing with the run: the
tape after the level was first rebuilt with its literal pairs folded into a repeat, which let a shift rule jump over
the moment the head is back at the right end (3 trips ended two pairs late); and repeats of `0101` were rebuilt as
`(10)^2n`, which the sweeps cannot cross whole (one trip ran out of moves). Now every block keeps its parts.

`python3 py/top.py check 1e11`: of the 560 top trips, 513 go through the old shapes and **all 47 wall trips** go
through 28 new ones, every one ending as the run does. The new shapes are appended to `top_shapes.txt` (so the
numbering of `top_0` to `top_42` is kept); `top_gen.py` proves each as plain symbolic stretches (`top_piece_i_k`,
by `symtm.prove`) chained with `wall_jump`, the tape at each jump matched with `Rd` by one `simp only` (constant
counts expanded, `(10)^2n` as `(1010)^n`, literal cells moved right across the repeats they can cross:
`mv10`, `mv01`, `mvQ`, `mvQ0`). A wall piece needs the borrow theorem with the piece's step count depending on the
tape to its right, `counter_borrow_ex`. Examples:

```
turn  0^inf 2x+1 1 1 0 2y+4 0 2z+2              ->  0^inf 4y+7 1 2x+7 1 2z+2       the plainest wall: X = 2y+4
turn  0^inf 2x+2 1 2y+1 1 3 1 2z+1 1 1 0 2w+8 0 2v+2  ->  0^inf 4w+8 1 2x+4 1 2y+4 1 1 1 2z+7 1 2v+2
turn  ... 1 1 0 2w+8 0 2v+6 1 2u+5 1 1 0 2s+16 0 2r+2 -> 0^inf 4s+12 1 4w+... +32 ...  two loops in one trip
```

A first generalisation left one shape with blocks of 606, 1791 and 5382 pairs concrete; its proof did not fit in
15 GB. For wall trips the literal pairs of a symbolic block may now sit anywhere around its repeat, and the move
budget is 6,000: then every block of every wall shape is symbolic. The wall theorems are split over
`TopWall1.lean` to `TopWall10.lean` (about 1 MB each), one Lean process each, since one file of all of them ran out
of memory. Split so, they compile in about 4 minutes on two cores, at under 3 GB, and `AxiomsWall.lean` shows all 28
rest on `propext` and `Quot.sound` only. They are checked by `lean/check_wall.sh` in their own CI workflow
(`wall.yml`), next to the main Tests job.

So **every trip from the right end and back in the first 10¹¹ steps is an instance of a theorem in Lean** (the
counter theorems, or one of the 71 top shapes), all kernel only, and none of them halts. What is computed is only
that the run's trips are those instances (`py/counter.py`, `py/top.py check`, trusted Python).

### Leads

1. **Shapes past 10¹¹.** `accel.py` runs the top trips concretely; classify them with `top.cut` instead, to see
   whether the 71 shapes keep covering the run at 10¹⁵, 10²⁰, ... or new shapes keep appearing, and how fast.
2. **Merge the families.** Many shapes are one rule across a different number of levels `1 (10)^odd` between the
   left end and the copied level (the wall shapes too: `... 1 2z+1 1 1 0 2w+8 0 2v+2 -> 4w+8 ...` at several depths).
   An induction on that list, as `wall_rightN` does for the blocks between, would make them one theorem each.
3. **The top as a recursion.** With the families merged, the left end is a finite set of maps on block lengths; then
   whether they close up forever, and whether any can put a mark right after a boundary in E's way.

So **every trip of the first 10¹² steps is an instance of a kernel-checked theorem** (82 top shapes). The rate of new

## 6 November 2026: families of top shapes, by induction on a list of blocks

Lead 2 above. Many top shapes are one rule with a different number of copies of some blocks: the ladders
`0^inf (1010)^x0 1 10 10 (1010)^x1 1 10 10 (1010)^x2 ... 1 10 10 10 (1010)^y 10 0 10 10 (1010)^z` with one rung
`1 10 10 (1010)^t` more each time were 11 of the 82 shapes, and 4 of the 11 new ones of 10¹².

### Sequences in the symbolic runs (`tools/py/bbtools/seqtm.py`, `lean/Seq.lean`)

A *sequence* `[ 1 10 10 (1010)^a ]^ts` is a list `ts` of blocks of one form, each with its own value of the
parameter `a`, any length, any values. `seqtm.run` is `symtm.run` with the sequence as a context the run may not
look into, and one more move: when the head is about to step onto it, one block is run on its own between two
contexts (with up to three literal cells next to the head carried along, as in `symtm`'s shift rules); if the
head comes out on the far side in the state it went in with, the block changed to some `w'`, then the whole list
is crossed, and the sequence is on the other side as `[w']^ts`, in reverse order. In Lean the list is `sq f ts X`
(first element next to the head) or `sqr f ts X` (last element next to the head), and `seqL`, `seqR`, `seqL'`,
`seqR'` of `Seq.lean` prove the crossing for every list from the run of one block, by induction on the list. A
sequence may also have several kinds of block (`[ w1 | w2 ]^ts`, elements `(kind, parameter)`, the one-block
hypothesis proved by cases); no family uses that yet.

The ladder's run crosses its list of rungs four times (B to the left, E to the right, B to the left, C to the
right), each time block by block in one state, and closes; the theorem, for every list of rungs, takes Lean's
kernel about a second.

### The families (`py/family.py`, `lean/top_families.txt`, `lean/TopFam.lean`)

`python3 py/family.py find` makes candidates from each shape: a stretch of its start from a separator to just
before one, with one or two variables, together with the copies of it that follow, written as a sequence. A
candidate is kept when its run closes; it covers a shape when, on random values, the shape's piece is an instance
of the family (`family.match`, on blocks and separators) and the family's end is the shape's end, cell by cell.
Chosen greedily, **7 families cover 24 of the 48 plain shapes**:

| family | shapes it replaces |
|---|---|
| `0^inf (1010)^x0 [ 1 10 10 (1010)^a ]^ts 1 10 10 10 (1010)^x2 10 0 10 10 (1010)^x3` (the ladders) | 11 |
| `L 1 10 (1010)^x0 1 10 (1010)^x1 10 10 0 (1010)^x2 10 10 [ 0 10 (1010)^a 10 0 (1010)^b 10 10 ]^ts 0` | 3 |
| five more, each with the two-level block `[ 0 10 (1010)^a 10 0 (1010)^b 10 10 ]` or a shorter ladder | 2 each |

`top_gen.py` proves each as `fam_i` in `TopFam.lean` (the run `fam_piece_i`: stretches by `symtm.prove`, the
lists by `seqL`/`seqR`, the whole trip with `counter_borrow_ex`); all rest on `propext` and `Quot.sound` only, and
the file compiles in seconds. The shapes they cover are dropped from `top_shapes.txt` (58 shapes are left, 24 of
them plain, 34 wall-building; `top_i` is renumbered), and Top.lean goes from 3.0 MB to 0.7 MB (plus 0.26 MB of
TopFam.lean).

`top.check` now tries the families first: a trip is `family` when its piece is an instance of a family and the
family's end, with the walked levels reset, is the run's tape. `tests/test_machine.py` also runs every family with
lists of 0 to 4 random blocks on the plain simulator.

`python3 py/top.py check 1e12`: of the 703 top trips, **114 are instances of the families and 589 go through the
58 shapes**, none wrong, none unlisted. The 5 plain shapes that were new at 10¹² are all covered now (4 by the ladder
family, 1 by the family of `[ 0 10 (1010)^a 10 0 (1010)^b 10 10 ]`), as any longer member of these families will be.

### The wall shapes, not yet

The 34 wall-building shapes are `0^inf B0 (s B)* 1 (2y+1) 1 1 0 X 0 W`, a list of blocks of mixed kinds (separator 0
or 1, either parity) in front of the copied level. A sequence of several kinds (`seqtm`, proved by cases) would
cover any such list if the passes crossed the blocks one by one in one state, but they do not: before the copy loop
the head converts the blocks next to the copied level one at a time, with short zig-zags whose state depends on the
block before (a carry), and only then sweeps over all of them. So the wall families need an induction over that
conversion, not only a crossing; that is the next lead, together with the third lead above.
